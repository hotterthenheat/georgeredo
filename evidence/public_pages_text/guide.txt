 How to read any alert
 Every alert is one card with the same parts. Learn them once.
 What you get: Read any card in five seconds: find the ▶ line and you know whether there's a trade. 
 Every alert is one card.
 This is one, exactly as it lands in Discord. Scroll, and each part lights up in turn.
 The first word tells you what to do.
 SURGE, WATCH, READ: a heads-up. BUY NOW, FULL SIZE: an entry. GET OUT: close it.
 Then the contract.
 The index, the strike, C for call or P for put, and when it expires. 0DTE means today.
 WHAT happened.
 One sentence, with the numbers.
 The ▶ line is the instruction.
 PLAY or ENTER means there's a trade. NO PLAY or WAIT means there isn't, yet. EXIT or CLOSE means get out. Read this line before anything else.
 Find the ▶ first. Every time. 
 WHY is the evidence.
 Useful. It never overrides the ▶ line. Neither does the joke some cards end with.
 Four words you'll see on every card.
 C is a call: it pays when SPX goes up. P is a put: it pays when SPX goes down. 
 The strike is the price the contract is about. $7725C is the 7725 call. 
 Lots are contracts. 2,175 lots is 2,175 contracts. 
 Prices are per share, and one contract is 100 shares. A $7.55 price costs $755 for one contract. 
 Four kinds of first word
 Know which pile a word is in and you know what the card wants from you.
 What you get: The first word tells you buy, manage, wait or practice before you read anything else. 
 Buy something.
 BUY NOW, FULL SIZE, HALF SIZE, SIZE UP, LOTTO, BUY & FORGET. The card names the contract and the price.
 Manage what you hold.
 STAY IN, LOCK HALF and LOCK MOST manage an open play. GET OUT means thesis invalidation; profit, risk, plan-clock and session exits have their own labels.
 Wait, or lean.
 WATCH, PLAN, SURGE, STAND ASIDE: nothing to buy yet. READ is a lean: buy only if its ▶ line says PLAY.
 No real money.
 PAPER is a practice book. Never size off it.
 Read to the arrow.
 A card can name puts and still lean up. The word after → is the lean, and the contract after “play” is the ticket. On a WATCH the arrow is only a lean: WATCH still means not yet.
 Big size doesn't tell you which way
 A SURGE card means an unusual number of contracts (lots) just traded on one strike. It never says whether SPX goes up or down. Here is what adding them up looked like on September 25.
 What you get: You'll stop reading a pile of put surges as “it's going down”.

 If you remember four things
 What you get: Four rules that keep you out of the most expensive mistakes. 
 Read the ▶ line first.
 PLAY, NO PLAY or EXIT (and their cousins ENTER, WAIT, CLOSE). That line is the instruction.
 Size is not direction.
 SURGE, WATCH and most prints show where money is, not which way price goes.
 Only entries are trades.
 BUY NOW, HALF SIZE, FULL SIZE, SIZE UP, LOTTO, BUY & FORGET, or a ▶ PLAY line. Everything else is a heads-up.
 GET OUT means out.
 Thesis invalidated: close it. TAKE PROFIT, PROTECT PROFIT, RISK EXIT, PLAN COMPLETE and SESSION CLOSE still end a play for their stated cause. PAPER means no real money.

 Live Names a side BUY NOW / FULL / HALF SIZE The title is the order: contract, price, size. The exit rules are on the card, so you know them before you buy. Read it in Apex 
 Live Names a side BUY & FORGET Buy the named contract at the reference price, then follow its exit message: GET OUT for source invalidation, RISK EXIT for the loss boundary, or SESSION CLOSE before the bell. When the title says HALF SIZE, buy half your normal size. Read it in Buy & Forget

A dip alone is not permission to add; follow the current card and any separately authorized ADD. HALF SIZE means small size or skip. If the card says near the high, near the low or don't chase, it is telling you to wait. Read it in Other

 Live Names a side MOC WICK Buy only on CONFIRMED or BUY NOW. Stop = a 1-minute close below the wick low. Take the rip; don't hold to the bell. Read it in Other

 Every word, in plain English
 Every term on this page and on the cards, with an example.
 Filter the glossary @here A Discord ping to everyone online in the channel. George uses it for the urgent ones. e.g. FULL SIZE and GET OUT alerts usually ping @here. 
 % of range Where price sits between today's low (0%) and today's high (100%). e.g. at the high (100% of range) = buying calls here is buying the top so far. 
 ^NDX and ^RUT The Nasdaq-100 and Russell 2000 indexes. QQQ and IWM are the funds that track them. e.g. An ^NDX read leans the same way for QQQ. 
 ^SPX and SPY ^SPX is the S&P 500 index. SPY is the fund that tracks it at about one tenth of the price. e.g. SPX 7700 ≈ SPY 770. 
 × ambient / × normal How many times its usual volume a contract traded, compared with the same time of day. e.g. 12.3× ambient = this strike traded 12 times its normal volume for 9:48 AM. 
 0DTE An option that expires at today's close. e.g. ^SPX $7735C 0DTE is worth something at 4:00 PM ET only if SPX is above 7735. 
 Accelerator A read of how fast dealer positioning is changing, and which way. e.g. accelerator flipped +6 → −18 = dealers switched from supporting dips to selling rallies. 
 Accumulation Steady buying over time, often by one large player. e.g. PUTS bought across 3 sessions → lean SHORT. 
 Aggressor (bought / sold) Who started the trade: a buyer who paid the ask, or a seller who hit the bid. Most alerts can't tell, and say so. e.g. BOUGHT (84% ask) = 84% of the size paid the asking price: buyers were in a hurry. 
 Apex print / big print One of the largest option positions of the session on a single contract, often 40,000+ contracts. e.g. 41,731 PUTS on the 7700P on 9/25. 
 Ask The lowest price a seller will take right now. e.g. Buying at the ask means paying up to get filled now. 
 Bid The highest price a buyer will pay right now. e.g. SOLD (80% bid) = sellers hit the bid to get out or to write the option. 
 Book One of George's own tracked records: every play a set of alerts called, priced off real quotes. Practice (PAPER) books are marked as practice and are never an entry for you. e.g. “The live and practice books” on /trades = George's live record and his practice ones. 
 Call (C) An option that gains when the price goes up past the strike. e.g. $7735C = a call with strike 7735. 
 Call wall / put wall The strike with the most dealer exposure above (call wall) or below (put wall) price. e.g. Approaching 7,650 call wall — expect fade/reject. 
 Chart it first Look at the price chart and wait for it to turn your way before acting on the card. e.g. A read that flips SHORT at the day's low says “chart it first”: don't sell into the low. 
 Chop day A two-sided day that goes nowhere. Breakouts fail and both calls and puts bleed. e.g. CHOP DAY — two-sided tape / no directional 0DTE entries, size down. 
 Clearing The official overnight count of open interest, published the next morning. e.g. 2026-09-24 clearing = Thursday's positions, read Friday morning. 
 Contract One specific option: ticker, strike, call or put, and expiry. One contract controls 100 units. e.g. ^SPX $7725C 0DTE is one contract line. Buying 2 means 2 contracts. 
 Conviction How strong a read is, from 0 to 100. e.g. read leans LONG (44) = a modest long lean. 
 Crescendo Volume on one contract rising minute after minute. e.g. 8m crescendo, 5.2× normal volume. 
 Dark pool Stock trades done off the public exchanges, usually by large players. e.g. DARK POOL SELLING — 71% sell-initiated. 
 Dealer The firms on the other side of option trades. They hedge by buying or selling the index, which can push price. e.g. dealers long (strong) = dealer hedging leans toward supporting price. 
 Delta (Δ) How much an option's price moves for a $1 move in the stock. e.g. Δ0.42 = about 42 cents per $1. 
 DTE (1DTE, 3DTE…) Days to expiration. 1DTE expires tomorrow, 3DTE in three trading days. e.g. ^SPX $7600C 1DTE bought on Thursday expires Friday. 
 EMA A moving average that weights recent prices more. Price below a fast EMA = momentum fading. e.g. premium rolled off peak, below fast EMA. 
 Event-week (σ) This week's build is at least twice as unusual as that name's normal. It means louder, not which way. e.g. ≥ 2σ on META = META's weekly build is far bigger than its usual. 
 Expected move How far options are pricing the stock to move by expiry. e.g. 2.4 expected moves out = a strike very unlikely to be reached. 
 Floor / cap A strike that holds price up (floor) or holds it down (cap). George never calls one from price alone: a strike not reached is distance and clock, and the follow-up says how often a strike that far is still reached. e.g. $7750 NOT REACHED → no lean. 
 Flow Option trades as they happen, especially large ones. e.g. flow buying $920K = about $920K of calls bought recently. 
 Flush / rip A fast drop / a fast rise. e.g. puts loading into the rip = puts piling up while price shoots up. 
 GEX (gamma exposure) A map of how hard dealers must hedge at each strike. Big strikes act like magnets or walls. e.g. 7745 (GEX resistance) = a strike where rallies tend to stall. 
 Hedge A trade that protects another position. It is not a bet on direction. e.g. A fund long stocks buys the 7650P far below spot as insurance. 
 Hold Advisor The part of George that watches a play after you're in and sends STAY IN, LOCK HALF, LOCK MOST or GET OUT. e.g. After the 11:05 SIZE UP, the Hold Advisor sent the 13:37 GET OUT. 
 ICT The chart-reading method George's main book uses: key levels, sweeps and market structure. e.g. ICT turned BEARISH = the main book's chart read now leans down. 
 IV and IV rank Implied volatility: how much movement option prices expect. IV rank compares today's IV with the past year. e.g. IV rank 22 = options are cheaper than usual. 
 Lean The side George favours. A lean is not an order to buy. e.g. calls, buyer/seller unclear → no lean = no side at all. 
 Limit order An order where you set the most you will pay. It fills at that price or better, or not at all. e.g. ENTER with a limit no higher than $5.40. 
 LOCK HALF / LOCK MOST Sell half, or most, of the position to lock in the gain and let the rest run. e.g. LOCK MOST — ^SPX $7730C +166%. 
 LONG / SHORT Up or down. LONG means calls; SHORT means puts. e.g. → lean SHORT = George favours puts. 
 Lots Number of option contracts traded. Lots and contracts mean the same thing here. e.g. 9,964 lots = 9,964 contracts changed hands. 
 Lotto A tiny, cheap bet that usually expires worthless and occasionally pays many times over. Bet only what you'd be fine losing all of. e.g. A $1.00 lotto costs $100 for one contract (prices are per unit, ×100), and can go to zero by the close. 
 Max pain The strike where the most options would expire worthless. Some days price drifts toward it. e.g. Max pain — ^SPX (yesterday's OI). 
 Mirror The opposite side near the same price. When calls were SOLD, the play is puts. e.g. play $7645C (mirror of the SOLD $7640P). 
 MOC (market on close) Orders that execute at the 4:00 PM close. The last 20 minutes often flush, then snap back. e.g. MOC WICK CONFIRMED = a late flush got bought and held. 
 Momentum ignition A sudden burst of price and flow in one direction. e.g. PUTS igniting → SHORT. 
 Normal, full and half size Your normal size is the amount you are willing to risk on one trade. George doesn't know your account, so you pick that number once and stick to it. FULL SIZE means that amount; HALF SIZE means half of it. e.g. If your normal size is $500 of premium, FULL SIZE is $500 and HALF SIZE is $250. 
 Notional (~$573K) Dollars that changed hands: contracts × price × 100. e.g. ~$573K = the 9,964 lots cost about $573,000 in total. 
 Open interest (OI) How many contracts of a strike are still open after the day's trades settle. e.g. calls +56,889 = 56,889 more call contracts open than the day before. 
 Opening range The high and low of the first minutes of the session. ORH and ORL are its high and low. e.g. Opening range 7691.19 – 7699.02 (the first 15 minutes of 9/21). 
 OTM / ATM / ITM Out of, at, or in the money. A call is out of the money while price is below its strike. e.g. With SPX at 7700, the 7750C is OTM and the 7700C is ATM. 
 Paper A practice book with no real money behind it. e.g. 📄 PAPER — ^SPX $7730 C — ORB Trail FIRED. 
 PDH / PDL Previous day's high / previous day's low. e.g. pump breached PDH = price opened above yesterday's high. 
 Pin A strike that price gets pulled toward into the close. e.g. LIKELY PIN 7750: SPX closed 7742 and touched 7752 on 9/25. 
 Premium The price of one option, per unit. Multiply by 100 for one contract. e.g. @ $7.55 = $755 for one contract. 
 Put (P) An option that gains when the price goes down past the strike. e.g. $7650P = a put with strike 7650. 
 Read George's current view of direction, with how strong it is. e.g. READ — FLIPPED to SHORT · lean puts. 
 Rotation Money moving out of one group of stocks and into another. e.g. USO ← SPY = money leaving the S&P and going to oil. 
 Runner The part of a position you keep after taking the first profit (T1). e.g. T1 banked · CALLS runner rides. 
 Session One trading day, 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM ET. e.g. the session low = the lowest price so far today. 
 Settle The final price an expiring option is worth at the close. e.g. settles at zero unless price crosses the strike before the bell. 
 Shakeout A dip that scares weak holders out before the move resumes. e.g. Expect a −22% dip first, bottoming ~24 min in. 
 Share % How much of the flow sat on one side. e.g. 93% share = $836K of about $898K was on the put side. 
 SMT divergence Two related indexes disagree: one makes a new high or low and the other doesn't. e.g. ^SPX made a higher high, ^NDX did not. 
 Spot The underlying's price right now. e.g. spot 7772 (−2) on a 7770 card = SPX is at 7772; the 7770 strike is 2 points below it. 
 Straddle Buying a call and a put at the same strike, to profit from a big move either way. e.g. SPY 640 straddle — buy BOTH sides, no lean. 
 Strike The price in the contract's name. The option pays from there. e.g. In ^SPX $7710P the strike is 7710. 
 Structure The pattern of highs and lows. LONG structure = higher lows. e.g. LONG structure → lean CALLS · ride until it changes. 
 Surge / burst A sudden pile of size on one contract, far above its normal rate. e.g. 4,842 lots in 5 minutes on the $7770P. 
 Sweep Price pokes through a key level (like yesterday's low) to trigger stops, then comes back. e.g. SPY PDL $665.80 swept, then reclaimed → lean LONG. 
 Tape The live stream of trades. Reading the tape = watching who is buying and selling. e.g. tape confirmed BEARISH = sellers dominated the recent trades. 
 The desk The large institutional traders whose option flow George tracks. e.g. the desk has been loading this strike. 
 Theta (time decay) The value an option loses as time passes. It is fastest on 0DTE. e.g. theta eats both sides on a pinned tape. 
 Ticket The exact contract a card tells you to buy. e.g. On an ALIGNED SURGE the ticket is the contract after “play”. 
 Trail / breakeven floor An exit that follows the price up. Once armed, it won't let a winner turn into a loser. e.g. +15% arms a breakeven floor, 35% giveback trail. 
 Two-way Buying and selling roughly even, so the trade carries no side. e.g. two-way (50% ask-side) = half bought, half sold. No lean. 
 Unwind A position being closed, or a price-based inference that it may be closing. SPX unwind cards do not verify selling or remaining position and stay WATCH only. e.g. 41,731 $7700 PUTS fully unwound → LONG. 
 VWAP The day's average price, weighted by volume. Above it, buyers have been paying up. e.g. held 6 bars above VWAP = price stayed above the average for 6 minutes. 
 Whale A very large trader or position. e.g. whales still in = the big holder has not sold yet. 
 Wick The thin line on a candle. A long lower wick means price dipped and got bought back up. e.g. undercut the session low to $7,695.80 and closed back at $7,706.59.
