# Filters, regimes and position sizing for short 0DTE premium: what has published or testable support

Scope: evidence on whether event-day exclusion, VIX level and term structure, intraday realized-volatility forecasts, day-of-week effects, dealer gamma and tail-aware sizing improve the *risk-adjusted* outcome of short 0DTE premium, with effect sizes, sample periods and in-sample / out-of-sample status. Written 2026-10-13 to extend what the repository has already measured (`COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §1 and §8; `research/thetadata/index_tests/tb_momentum/RESULTS.md`; `research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md`; `research/thetadata/straddle_nbbo/RESULTS.md`; `research_notes/Finding a 0DTE options edge/empirical_gex_regime_and_exits.md`).

Evidence tiers used below: **A** = peer-reviewed journal; **B** = academic working paper or thesis; **C** = vendor or practitioner study with a stated sample and method; **D** = vendor commentary without a stated method; **O** = our own measured data (ThetaData NBBO, day-clustered bootstrap). Anything marked **O-post-hoc** was computed for this note on 2026-10-13 from committed CSVs and is *not* a pre-registered result under plan §1; it is descriptive and must be re-run on a fixed holdout before it is used as a rule. The post-hoc computations use `research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/tc_straddles.csv` (339 SPXW sessions, 2025-06-02 → 2026-10-09), `event_days.csv` beside it, and the sibling dataset `research/thetadata/vol_filters/out/daily.csv` (2,353 SPX sessions 2017-06-01 → 2026-10-09, 2,333 valid after removing early closes; built by `build_daily.py` in that folder, which was still being extended when this note was written). Definitions: `rv_0930_1300` and `rv_1300_1600` are square-root sums of squared one-minute log returns over the window; `range_pm` is (max − min)/price at 13:00 over 13:00–16:00; `absmove_pm` is |close/price(13:00) − 1|; "VIX-implied 3-hour sigma" is VIX(13:00)/100 × √(3/(6.5 × 252)), a crude normalisation that ignores the intraday U-shape and the overnight share of the VIX, so the *level* of the realized/implied ratio is not meaningful, only its variation across regimes.

Older evidence (pre-2022, before daily SPX expirations) is labelled "older" throughout.

## 1. Event days (FOMC, CPI, NFP, opex, constituent earnings): realized vs implied moves and the P&L of short 0DTE premium on those days

### Takeaway
Every source agrees that FOMC afternoons move 2–3× more than normal afternoons and that 0DTE premium on those days is priced 2–5× higher; the evidence that *excluding* event days improves per-trade expectancy of short premium is absent, and our own two NBBO-priced samples show the opposite: event days carried a higher mean, a higher win rate and a higher credit, and the catastrophic days were non-event days. The only event-day rule with (vendor-grade) support is a *timing* rule, not an exclusion: sell after the 14:00 FOMC release rather than before it.

### Inferences
- In both of our NBBO samples the event-day subset had a *higher* mean per straddle and a higher win rate because the credit was 45 % larger and the realized afternoon move, while larger, did not scale as much; the blanket exclusion lowered the mean and did nothing for the lower confidence bound. Event-day exclusion is therefore a tail-budget decision (it removes one −$5,719 FOMC day out of 11), not an expectancy improver. The remaining lower bound below zero in T-A and T-C is driven by non-event days (2026-06-11, 2026-06-09, 2025-07-31).
- The published FOMC evidence is consistent with a timing rule rather than a skip: pre-14:00 FOMC mornings are abnormally quiet (0.51 % vs 0.90 % range) and the implied event variance is a good forecast of post-release realized variance (R² 78–85 %), so a 13:00 short straddle on FOMC day is short the correctly-priced event at roughly 2.8× the normal afternoon range. The tastylive and GreeksLab numbers (13:05 / 13:55 entries, 84 % and 23/23) are vendor-grade, cost-free, with profit targets and stops, and should be treated as hypotheses to pre-register (e.g. "enter 14:05 on FOMC days" as a separate lane), not as results.
- CPI and NFP are 08:30 releases; by 13:00 their variance has already been realized, which is why a 13:00 entry sees higher credits (post-release IV still elevated) and quieter afternoons. The plan's T-E pre-registration (H13) fixes the exclusion in advance; the figures above argue for pre-registering the *inclusion* of CPI/NFP days and the exclusion of pre-14:00 FOMC windows only.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study compares the realized post-release move with the 0DTE straddle-implied move on CPI or NFP days; Rhoads uses 1DTE straddles from the prior close.
- Johannes, Kaeck and Seeger (2026) could not be retrieved; its statement-window straddle result is known only through Song.
- No evidence at all on index 0DTE outcomes around constituent earnings; the T-E window (2022-01 → 2025-05) would allow a test using mega-cap earnings dates.
- All vendor FOMC backtests are cost-free and use profit targets or stops; none reports NBBO fills.

## 2. Does the VIX level, VIX term structure (VIX9D/VIX, VIX/VIX3M) or VVIX predict the rest-of-day realized/implied ratio and short-premium outcomes?

### Takeaway
The VIX level is the strongest single predictor of afternoon realized volatility in our 2017–2026 SPX series (Spearman 0.74), and the realized/VIX-implied afternoon ratio *rises* with the VIX (median 0.25 in the lowest tercile vs 0.39 in the highest; 1σ breaches 4.6 % vs 13.1 %), but in the T-C straddle sample the P&L as a percentage of credit is uncorrelated with the VIX (Spearman −0.03) and win rates are flat across terciles (62.8–64.6 %): the higher credit at high VIX pays for the larger realized move and nothing more. Published work supports the term-structure *shape* as a scaling signal for longer-dated straddles (Lehnert 2025) and implied-over-realized overstatement in every VIX regime (tastylive), but no study of VIX9D/VIX or VVIX as a 0DTE predictor was found.

### Inferences
- In our own sample the VIX changes the *size* of both the credit and the tail roughly in proportion: dollar P&L per straddle is highest in the lowest tercile (+$151 vs +$60) only because the s.d. is half as large ($939 vs $1,730), and the per-credit P&L shows no gradient. A VIX ceiling would therefore reduce the dollar drawdown but, per the evidence, not the per-trade expectancy in units of credit; that is a sizing statement, not a skip statement.
- The realized/implied afternoon ratio rising with the VIX (13.1 % vs 4.6 % breaches of the VIX-implied band) is the one regime fact with both published (fattail.ai, dev.to worst day) and own-data support, and it is consistent with Muravyev–Ni's "intraday long-option returns highest when VIX is high". It argues against *increasing* size in high VIX, which is what a fixed-dollar-credit rule implicitly does.
- Term-structure shape (Lehnert) is the only VIX-family signal with a peer-reviewed straddle result, but it is for listed-maturity straddles and scales exposure continuously; its transfer to 0DTE is untested. VIX9D/VIX and VVIX have no retrievable 0DTE evidence.

### Gaps
- No study tests VIX9D/VIX, VIX/VIX3M or VVIX as predictors of the same-day realized/implied ratio for 0DTE; the Lehnert paper's sample, out-of-sample status and transaction-cost treatment could not be read (HTTP 503 on both the handle and the PDF).
- The tastylive VIX-regime studies are undated backtests on 30–45-day options with unreported methodology and are not independently audited.
- Our own VIX-tercile split uses within-sample terciles on 339 days (113 per bin; standard error ≈ $90–160 per bin), so the +$151 vs +$60 difference is within noise.

## 3. Intraday volatility forecasting: how well does the morning range predict the afternoon range, and can it set entry time or strike width?

### Takeaway
Morning realized volatility is an excellent predictor of afternoon realized volatility (own data: Spearman 0.82, log-log R² 0.69 over 2,333 SPX days; the first hour alone 0.76; published older equity evidence: first-hour variance explains ≈ 68 % of the day's), but it is already in the 13:00 option price: in the T-C straddle sample the P&L as a fraction of credit is slightly *negatively* related to morning RV (Spearman −0.04 executable, −0.13 held to settlement) and the morning-range terciles are non-monotone (mid best, high worst). The forecast is useful for stop width and lot size, not for deciding whether the afternoon is cheap to sell; and on FOMC days the relation inverts (quiet morning, 2.8× afternoon), so any morning-range rule must exclude 14:00 events.

### Cited Findings

## 4. Dealer gamma as a volatility (not direction) gauge: do negative-gamma afternoons have larger ranges, and is that tradable as a skip rule?

### Takeaway
Negative dealer gamma coincides with larger absolute moves in every dataset (our IC −0.21 on 30-minute |moves|; Amaya–Pearson's maximum 30-minute impact +6.4 vol points; the 2017 GEX quartile spread 0.55 % vs 0.85 %), but the only study that controls for implied volatility finds the gamma signal vanishes (SPY 2018–2026: ρ −0.36 raw → −0.03 after VIX and ATM IV), and the peer-reviewed 0DTE papers find the average effect of 0DTE gamma is to *lower* volatility because dealers are usually long gamma. Gamma therefore looks like a concurrent volatility proxy that the 13:00 option price already contains; it has no published support as a skip rule, and our own partial IC (−0.17) was computed against trailing realized volatility, not against IV.

## 5. Sizing a 65–75 % win-rate, fat-left-tail strategy: Kelly, fractional Kelly, drawdown constraints and the effect of one 6× loss per N trades

### Takeaway
On our measured T-C series the growth-optimal (full-Kelly) capital is about $28,000 per one-lot straddle, which would have produced a 59 % drawdown and a 31 % bootstrap chance of ending a 339-day run below the start; quarter-Kelly ($112,000 per lot) cuts the probability of a > 30 % drawdown from 0.99 to 0.08 while the chance of ending below the start only falls to 0.19, because that probability is governed by the mean's noise, not by the fraction. Published theory (MacLean–Thorp–Ziemba; Busseti–Ryu–Boyd) says the same thing: fractional Kelly trades growth for drawdown roughly as f(2−f) and estimation error in the mean is the main danger; a drawdown-constrained bet dominates fractional Kelly at equal drawdown risk. Arithmetic on the "one 6× loss" question: with wins of one full credit, a 6× loss is break-even only at an 85.7 % win rate (one loss per 7 trades); with 50 %-of-credit take-profits it needs 92.3 % (one per 13).

**Arithmetic (no source needed; stated for the record).** With a win of w credits at probability p and a loss of L credits otherwise, expectancy is pw − (1 − p)L and the break-even win rate is p* = L/(w + L). For w = 1: L = 2 → p* = 66.7 %; L = 3 → 75 %; L = 4 → 80 %; L = 6 → 85.7 %. For w = 0.5 (50 % take-profit): L = 2 → 80 %; L = 3 → 85.7 %; L = 6 → 92.3 %. At p = 0.70, w = 1: L = 2 gives +0.10 credit/trade (Kelly f* = 0.10 of bankroll per unit of loss), L = 3 gives −0.20, L = 6 gives −1.10. At p = 0.75, w = 1: L = 3 is exactly zero, L = 6 is −0.75. So one 6× loss per 10 trades at 90 % wins of a full credit is +0.30 credit per trade; per 7 trades it is zero; per 5 trades it is −0.40.

**Published.**

### Inferences
- Sizing is the only lever in this note that changed the shape of our measured equity curve without changing per-trade expectancy. - The bootstrap probability of finishing below the start barely responds to the fraction (0.31 → 0.18 → 0.18): a positive-but-noisy mean cannot be turned into a usable equity curve by sizing alone; only more days (the T-E window) or a higher per-trade mean can. - No published Kelly or drawdown-constrained sizing study uses short 0DTE premium distributions; the closest is Verdad's lognormal Monte Carlo, which the author says understates the tails.
- Our Kelly computations use the realized 339-day sequence (in-sample), ignore margin requirements and intraday mark-to-market, and bootstrap days independently (no volatility clustering); a block bootstrap and the T-E window are needed before any fraction is fixed.
- MacLean–Thorp–Ziemba's half-Kelly variance claim was not read in the primary text; only the growth column of the table is quoted.

### Inferences
- The weekday pattern in the long SPX series is mostly the FOMC/CPI calendar (FOMC statements are Wednesdays, CPI often Tuesday–Thursday) plus the Wednesday expiry that pre-dates daily 0DTEs; with 66–71 straddle-days per weekday the T-C differences (±$150 around a mean of $72 with s.d. $1,350) cannot be distinguished from zero. A weekday filter would be a pure trade-count reducer.
- Month-end is the one calendar effect that appears in both the nine-year SPX series (47 vs 36 bp, breach 14.7 % vs 7.5 %) and the 15 T-C sessions (−$864, 40 % wins), and has a plausible mechanism (rebalancing flows into the close); it is nominal, post-hoc and based on 15 trades, so it belongs in the pre-registration list for T-E, not in a rule.
- The published 1DTE Monday premium is a weekend-variance effect (Jones–Shemesh; Muravyev–Ni overnight) and is unavailable to a lane that is flat at 16:00; it is relevant only if an overnight 1DTE lane is ever considered, where spreads were the stated obstacle.

### Gaps
- No academic or vendor study reports 0DTE short-premium P&L by weekday with costs; the dev.to and OptionMetrics work are Wednesday-only and 1DTE respectively.
- Our weekday and month-end splits are post-hoc on 339 days; the plan's criteria (≥ 150 trades per cell, lower bound > 0) are not met by any cell.
- Holiday effects: only pre/post-holiday afternoon ranges on the SPX series; no straddle P&L (T-C has too few such days to report).

## 7. Which "filters" merely reduce trade count without improving per-trade expectancy, and which have support as risk inputs?

### Takeaway
On the evidence assembled, none of the candidate day-level skip rules (event days, VIX ceiling or floor, morning range, dealer-gamma sign, weekday) has published or own-data support for raising the per-trade expectancy of short 0DTE premium measured in units of credit; each changes the *dollar* size of credits and tails together. The things with support are (i) sizing to a drawdown or max-loss budget, (ii) an FOMC *timing* rule (sell after 14:00, not before), and (iii) using volatility forecasts (VIX, morning RV, gamma sign) to set stop distance and lot size. Exit-side spread engineering (H14) and the hold-to-settlement variant (H13) remain the only candidates that moved the lower confidence bound.

### Cited Findings
- Event-day exclusion: T-A +$8.60 → +$5.92 and T-C +$72.4 → +$45.3 (both lower bounds stay below zero); event days held 45 % of T-C P&L on 12 % of days with a 72.5 % win rate; the three largest losses were non-event days — [tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md); [straddle_nbbo/RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/straddle_nbbo/RESULTS.md)
- VIX ceiling/floor: win rates 62.8–64.6 % across terciles; Spearman of VIX with P&L per credit −0.03; tastylive: trading only when VIX > 20 underperforms always-trading (older, 45-day options) — this note §2; [tastylive.com](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/only-trade-in-high-iv-10-22-2018)
- Morning range: predicts afternoon RV (ρ 0.82) but P&L per credit ρ −0.04 to −0.13 and non-monotone terciles — this note §3
- Dealer gamma: raw IC −0.21 on absolute moves, but −0.03 after IV controls in the only controlled study; 0DTE dealers are long gamma on average and lower volatility (Adams et al.; Amaya et al.) — this note §4
- Weekday: per-cell n = 66–71, differences inside noise — this note §6
- Stops: the 2.0× credit stop lowered the T-C mean from $72.4 to $53.2 and the drawdown from −$21,180 to −$15,020; the plan's exit grid found 0 of 576 configurations positive after costs and the same ordering on random entries — [tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md); [empirical_gex_regime_and_exits.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research_notes/Finding a 0DTE options edge/empirical_gex_regime_and_exits.md)
- Sizing: quarter-Kelly cut P(drawdown > 30 %) from 0.99 to 0.08 at unchanged per-trade expectancy; P(end < start) only 0.31 → 0.19 — this note §5
- FOMC timing: post-14:00 entries 84 % / +$278 (GreeksLab, cost-free, with stops) and 23/23 (tastylive); pre-release morning entries ≈ $4 average on a $5.80 credit (tastylive) — [greekslab.com](https://greekslab.com/blog/trading-0dte-spx-fed-day); [tastylive.com](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/weekly-0-dte-breakdown-trading-into-fomc-post-fomc-achieves-100-win-rate-in-the-past-three-years-12-15-2025)
- Structural ceiling on filters: the 0DTE variance risk premium is present but "economically small", mean strategy returns are insignificant because of distribution width (Vilkov), and Sharpe ratios are "economically insignificant" after costs and estimation risk (Chong); Muravyev–Ni's intraday short-premium return is the *wrong-signed* half of the daily premium (+0.3 % to long options intraday vs −1.0 % overnight, 2004–2013) — [hulltactical.com](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/); [bm.hkust.edu.hk](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2026/04/are-0dte-options-mispriced); [ideas.repec.org](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v136y2020i1p219-238.html)

### Inferences
- The right test for any proposed filter under plan §1 is not "is the mean higher after the filter" but "is the per-credit mean higher *and* the lower bound higher on the fixed holdout, with the filtered-out days reported as a separate cell". Every filter examined here would fail that test on current data; most would also reduce the trade count below the 150-trade minimum within a 16-month window.
- Items worth pre-registering for the T-E window (2022-01 → 2025-05), in this order: (1) sizing rule fixed as a max-loss budget from a drawdown constraint (α, β stated), (2) FOMC-day rule "no entry before 14:05; entry at 14:05 as its own cell", (3) month-end exclusion as a separate cell (nominal in two samples), (4) a stop or lot size scaled by the 13:00 VIX or by morning RV (continuous, not a skip), (5) intraday gamma sign as a stop-width input with ATM IV as the control.
- Nothing in the published literature suggests a day-level filter can lift a short 0DTE lane whose unconditional per-credit mean is inside its confidence interval; the published premium is small, is largely paid overnight, and is consumed by spreads, which is why H14 (exit-side cost) and H13 (no exit at all) are the levers that moved our bounds.

### Gaps
- No controlled study exists of any skip rule for short 0DTE premium at NBBO costs; the vendor backtests are cost-free and use profit targets and stops that bias win rates upward.
- The per-credit statistic used here (P&L / credit) is a proxy; the plan's promotion metric is dollars per straddle after spread with a day-clustered bound, and the two can rank filters differently when credits vary by 45 % between regimes.
- The sibling `daily.csv` was still being built when these splits were computed; all O-post-hoc numbers should be regenerated from the frozen file before they are cited anywhere else.
