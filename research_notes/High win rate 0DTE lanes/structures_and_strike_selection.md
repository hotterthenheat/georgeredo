# Defined-risk structures and strike/delta selection for high-win-rate 0DTE index/ETF option selling

Scope: how structure (credit spread, iron condor, iron butterfly, broken-wing butterfly, wide strangle) and strike selection (5/10/16/25/ATM delta, expected move, realized vol, dealer gamma) set the win-rate / expectancy / tail trade-off, and the SPXW-vs-SPY/QQQ/IWM economics. Written against `COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §1 (promotion gate: day-clustered lower 95 % bound > 0 under the spread model, ≥ 150 trades / 3 months OOS, NBBO-validated fills) and §8 (measured: SPY/QQQ entry half-spread 0.6 % of credit, 15:55 buy-back of near-worthless legs 5–6 % of leg value and 3.3 % of credit, SPXW entry half-spread 0.87 %, SPXW 13:00 straddle worst day −$8,499, hold-to-settlement variant +$156.8/day [+5.8, +305.0] all sessions but [−18.5, +288.6] ex-events).

Evidence tiers used below: **T0** = this repository's own measured results (ThetaData NBBO, 2025-06-02 → 2026-10-09); **T1** = exchange/clearing/regulator specifications and SEC or academic working papers; **T2** = data-vendor or fund studies with stated data and method (Cboe Insights, OptionMetrics, Volos, FlashAlpha); **T3** = practitioner/educational backtests with incomplete methodology (tastylive episode summaries, Option Alpha, Option Omega user posts, Lambda Fin); **T3-sec** = a T3 result known only through a secondary write-up. Dates: 2022–2026 unless marked *older*.

## Key question 1: For each structure, what win rate does the payoff shape imply at each delta, what loss-to-win ratio results, and where do published backtests land on expectancy after bid/ask costs?

### Takeaway
At fair pricing the structure fixes the arithmetic: a credit spread sold at delta *d* with credit/width ratio *c* has close-based win probability ≈ 1−d, max loss-to-win (1−c)/c, and expectancy = width × (realized win rate − (1−c)); an iron condor has win ≈ 1−2d and loss-to-win (1−2c)/(2c). Published realized hit rates sit 3–12 points above the delta-implied rate (SPY 1σ band 71.0 % vs 68.3 % fair; SPX 20Δ/$20-wide condors ≈ 72 % unmanaged vs ≈ 60 % delta-implied), which is the whole 0DTE variance premium — and the only cost-corrected academic study (Vilkov 2026, SPXW 2016–2026, half-spread per leg + 0.5 bp) finds no structure, conditional or unconditional, keeps a positive net Sharpe, with the 4-leg iron butterfly/condor the worst (net SR −2.67 after the August-2026 cost correction).

### Cited Findings

**Delta as the hit-rate anchor (definition).**

(T3; no Cboe/OIC page surfaced.)

**Realized hit rates vs delta-implied (close-based).**
- SPY, 193 Wednesday sessions 2022-07-06 → 2026-04-01, 10:00 ET ATM straddle → 1σ band: close inside the band **71.0 %** of sessions vs ≈ 68.3 % if fairly priced (i.e. a ≈16Δ strangle both-OTM-at-close rate); by year 69.2 % (2022 H2, n=26), 69.2 % (2023, 52), 70.0 % (2024, 50), 71.2 % (2025, 52), 84.6 % (2026 Q1, 13); median realized/implied move **0.589** vs ≈ 0.674 fair; median implied 1σ 0.72 % of spot, median |realized| 0.37 %; worst miss 2025-04-09, +8.72 % vs 3.51 % implied (2.5×); quote mids, no fees — [FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study) (T2).
- Counter-evidence on the *mean*: tastylive's expected-move study finds "one-day options have no long-term overstatement due to large outside moves" even though IV is "frequently" overstated — [tastylive, Aug 2023](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/expected-move-overstatement-for-zero-dte-08-10-2023) (T3). - SPX "peg" rates (Option Alpha, 180 trading days to 2024-08-19): SPX closed within **0.2 % of its 14:00 price 65.6 %** of days and within **0.3 % of its 10:30 price 53.3 %**; a 0.3 %-OTM condor entered 13:00 had a 66.1 % historical max-profit rate; a 0.2 %-OTM $5-wide condor at 14:44 collected $196 against $304 risk (≈ 68 % max-profit rate) and Option Alpha's own EV = 0.68×196 − 0.32×304 = **+$36** assuming every loser is a max loss — [Option Alpha, updated Apr 2026](https://optionalpha.com/learn/top-0dte-options-strategies) (T3; no fees/slippage, no trade count).

### Inferences
- **Structural identities (derived; no data needed).** Credit spread with width W, credit C, c = C/W, short delta d: P(win at close) ≈ 1−d; max loss-to-win = (1−c)/c; breakeven win rate if every loser is a max loss = 1−c; expectancy per trade = W × (w − (1−c)). Iron condor with per-side credit ratio cₛ: P(win) ≈ 1−2d, loss-to-win (1−2cₛ)/(2cₛ), breakeven 1−2cₛ, max return on risk 2cₛ/(1−2cₛ). At fair pricing (c = d) the loss-to-win ratios are: credit spread 5Δ 19:1, 10Δ 9:1, 16Δ 5.25:1, 25Δ 3:1, ATM 1:1; iron condor 5Δ 9:1 (win 90 %), 10Δ 4:1 (80 %), 16Δ 2.1:1 (68 %), 25Δ 1:1 (50 %); max return on risk per trade 11 % (5Δ), 25 % (10Δ), 47 % (16Δ), 100 % (25Δ). Every point of win rate above 1−c is worth W per trade; every point of credit above fair is also worth W. The "high win rate" lanes (5–10Δ) therefore need realized win rates above 90/80 % *and* a credit above fair just to cover the 4-leg spread, which is why Vilkov's corrected condor Sharpe is the most negative of all structures.
- **The tastytrade 20Δ condor numbers imply partial losses, not max losses.** With $500 credit on a $2,000 width, breakeven is 75 % if losers hit max loss; a 72 % win rate can only be profitable (as reported) if the average loser is < 0.72×500/0.28 ≈ $1,290, i.e. most losers expire *partly* ITM. That is also why 0.5× stops halve the win rate: the stop converts recoverable partial excursions (48 % recover) into realized losses.
- **Iron butterfly held to settlement ≈ 64–67 % win.** The straddle component wins when |close − strike| < credit; our SPXW T0 straddle-to-settlement win rate is 67.3 %, so a $20-wing iron fly's held-to-settlement hit rate is bounded by that and by the wing debit; the tastylive 78 % comes from a 10 % profit target (exit when the straddle has decayed 10 %), not from the payoff shape.
- **Where the published expectancy lands after costs.** The only cost-explicit multi-leg academic result (Vilkov, corrected) is negative for every structure at a 2.2 bp-of-spot half-spread per leg; the two practitioner results that *state* costs (Option Omega $1.70/$0.70 + $0.05 slippage; OptionMetrics sold at the bid, fees not modelled) are positive but on short or single-leg samples; the tastylive/tastytrade condor and fly results state no costs at all. Nothing published meets the plan §1 gate as written.

### Gaps
- No published source gives win rate and average loss *by delta* (5/10/16/25) for 0DTE condors or spreads on SPX or SPY; the tastylive "profit targets by delta" episode exists but its page is JS-rendered and the summary only mentions 25–35Δ — [tastylive, Dec 2024](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/profit-targets-by-delta-for-0-dt-es-12-19-2024).

## Key question 2: How much does a 4-leg iron condor cost to open and close at NBBO on SPXW vs SPY, what do complex-order fills add, and which rules for leaving wings to expire reduce the exit cost?

### Takeaway
Measured SPXW ATM half-spreads are one to two ticks ($0.05–0.07 per leg, 0.87 % of a 13:00 straddle credit) and SPY/QQQ 0.5–0.6 %, but the exit of near-worthless legs is where the round trip blows up (3.3 % of credit median on SPY/QQQ at 15:55; Volos: expiring-ITM NDX legs cost > 2× the average closing spread and letting them cash-settle cut roll costs by > 50 %); the SEC/DERA OPRA study shows midpoint limit orders on SPXW fill ≈ 60–70 % of the time and cut the net effective spread to less than half a marketable order's, but no study measures 4-leg complex-order fills, so the complex-vs-per-leg question must be measured in T-F.

### Cited Findings

**Quoted and effective spreads on SPXW 0DTE (single-leg).**
- SEC DERA working paper (Fu, Li, Musto, Pearson, March 2025), OPRA/MIDAS SPXW trades and quotes 2020-07-01 → 2023-09-28: for ATM options around 13:00 the BBO was one tick wide 0.5 % of the time and ≥ 3 ticks 76 % in July 2020; by July 2023 **one tick 64.7 %, two ticks 35.3 %**, rarely wider — [SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-hope-reasonable-prc-2503.pdf) (T1).
- Midpoint non-marketable limit orders on two-tick BBOs fill within one second ≈ 60 % (< $3 options) and ≈ 70 % (> $3) in the late period; the net effective spread of a midpoint order is "less than half" a marketable order's ($0.04 vs $0.10 for > $3 options); for three-tick BBOs the one-tick-improving order's fill rate rose 22 % → 36 % and net effective spread fell 5.5 → 3.8 ¢, the two-tick order 84 % → 90 % and 3.5 → 3.2 ¢ vs 7.5 ¢ marketable — same source.
- Realized spreads (counterparty profit) are "always less than half the quoted spread, generally less than half a tick"; the largest late-period realized spread for picked-off customer orders is 2.8 ¢, i.e. 0.14 bp of the delta-exposure of an ATM contract at SPX 4,000 and 0.28 bp at 25Δ — same source.
- On 2022-07-01, 37.7 % of SPXW call trades and 38.0 % of put trades printed strictly inside the *apparent* BBO and 18.4 % / 20.3 % at the midpoint (before the paper's resequencing fix) — same source, Table 3.
- Multi-leg share: late period (Apr 2022 → Sept 2023) average daily 0DTE SPXW volume: single-leg LOB 567,788 + auction 43,716 + floor 3,134 contracts; **multi-leg LOB 461,886 + auction 17,973 + floor 38,655**, i.e. complex orders ≈ 46 % of 0DTE volume; PIA trades between customers rose 10 % → 18 % with an average spread of $0.10 vs $0.18 for other PIA trades — same source, Table 2 and §2.1.

**Our measured T0 costs (2025-06 → 2026-10).**
- SPXW 13:00 ATM: entry half-spread per leg mean $0.072 (call) / $0.072 (put), p50 $0.05, p95 $0.25 / $0.20; entry half-spread 0.87 % of straddle credit (mean), 0.81 % (median) — [tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md).
- SPY/QQQ 13:00 ATM: entry half-spread median 0.60 % of credit (mean 0.62 %); **round trip median 3.89 %, mean 4.89 %**; the 15:55 buy-back of two near-worthless legs costs a median 3.30 % of credit (mean 4.27 %); realized round-trip cost $12.93 vs the modelled $8.30 per straddle — [straddle_nbbo/RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/straddle_nbbo/RESULTS.md).
- Realized half-spreads by root (GeorgeFi NBBO repricing): SPY/QQQ 0.5 % of mid, SPXW 0.9 %, IWM 0.7–0.9 %, **RUTW 4.5 %, NDXP 7.5 %**, single names 3.5–4 % — [COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md §8](/home/user/georgeredo/COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md).

**Exit-side cost of near-worthless and expiring legs.**
- Volos (NDX 0DTE, April 2025 paper): mid-to-bid spreads are highest near the close, driven by expiring ITM options (≈ 6 bp of spot, > 2× the full-sample closing average); entering and exiting daily costs ≈ 300–350 bp/yr at 25 % slippage of the mid-to-bid spread, exiting expiring ITM legs alone > 350 bp/yr; letting expiring ITM index options **settle in cash** would have cut roll costs by > 50 % (> 85 bp/yr) — [Volos](https://www.volossoftware.com/insights/improving-0dte-trading-returns) (T2).
- Roundhill (manager of XDTE/QDTE/RDTE, 252 trades/yr): buying back far-OTM 0DTE calls intraday means buying "nearly worthless" options "often with no bid"; repurchasing doubles transaction costs; the funds do not buy back — [Roundhill, Apr 2025](https://blog.roundhillinvestments.com/0dte-to-buy-back-or-not-to-buy-back) (T2, qualitative).
- Minimum tick: SPX/SPXW "0.05 ($5.00)" below $3.00 and "0.10 ($10.00)" otherwise — [Cboe SPX specs](https://ww2.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/specifications) (T1). A leg quoted 0.00 × 0.05 therefore costs ≥ $5 plus fees to close.
- Broker fees still apply on $0-commission closes: tastytrade "$0.00 commission to close" but "applicable exchange, clearing, and regulatory fees still apply to all opening and closing trades" — [tastytrade fee schedule](https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt7dc2e3d4a7071563/blt2b752fef372188fe/commissions-and-fees) (T1 broker doc).

**Complex-order fills (what exists).**
- Spread orders rest on a complex order book with "no public Bid/Ask"; the implied NBBO from the legs is not guaranteed; fills can occur inside the implied NBBO but sometimes not even at it — [Options AI](https://learn.optionsai.com/limit-price-execution/) (T3).
- tastylive: an iron condor "may be more difficult to fill than a strangle" because the market maker's transaction costs are higher — [tastylive, 2016](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/best-practices/episodes/getting-good-fills-11-15-2016) (*older*, T3).
- Single-leg broker execution quality differs across brokers by ≈ 89 % of the quoted spread, ≈ 90 % of it attributable to wholesaler auction instructions — SSRN 5399225 as cited in an [SEC comment letter](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-887/4887-753447-2321514.pdf) (T1-cited, single-leg only).
- *Older* price-improvement benchmark: BOX PIP marginal improvement averaged 1.49 % of option price (2006–07 data) — [Khoury, Perrakis, Savor, EFM 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/eufman/v16y2010i2p211-228.html).
- DERA notes that LOB marketable volume includes "legs of complex orders" but does not separate complex-order execution costs; it cites a companion paper, "Costs of executing complex options trades" (Li, Musto, Pearson 2023), which this session could not retrieve — [SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-hope-reasonable-prc-2503.pdf).

### Inferences
- **A 4-leg SPXW condor at measured ATM-class spreads costs 4 × $0.05–0.07 = $0.20–0.28 per side at mid-to-touch**, i.e. $0.40–0.56 round trip plus ≈ $9.76 in tastytrade fees (see Q4) ≈ $50–65 per 1-lot condor if closed, $30–40 if left to settle. Against a 10Δ condor credit of (fair) 0.20 × width — e.g. $8 on a $40-wide SPXW condor at a 1σ ≈ 0.7 % day — that is 4–8 % of credit for the spread alone, versus 0.87 % for the ATM straddle. Wing quotes at 5–10Δ are not measured here and the Vilkov 2.2 bp-of-spot average (≈ $1.3 per leg at SPX 6,000, dominated by 2016–2020 and interpolated grid quotes) would make it several times worse; the per-delta half-spread must be measured from the ThetaData chains before any condor lane is costed.
- **On SPY the same condor pays four $0.01-tick legs twice plus the exit penalty**: the T0 round trip of 3.9 % median on an ATM straddle is almost entirely the 15:55 buy-back (3.3 %), and condor wings expire far more often near-worthless than ATM straddle legs do, so the SPY condor round trip should be assumed ≥ 5 % of credit until measured.
- **Exit rules that follow from the evidence**: (a) on SPXW never buy back — cash settlement has no assignment risk (Q4) and removes the entire exit-side spread (Volos > 50 % of roll cost; our T0 hold-to-settlement +$156.8 vs +$72.4 per day); (b) on SPY/QQQ leave *long* wings to expire unconditionally (they cost nothing to abandon), and buy back *short* legs only when the ask ≤ one tick above zero ($0.01 on SPY, $0.05 on SPX) or when the strike is inside the after-hours risk band (Q4); (c) use midpoint resting orders rather than marketable orders on entry — DERA's 60–70 % midpoint fill rate and < half-spread net cost is the best measured number, and the 1-second rule understates the saving for patient orders. This is the mechanical content of plan H14/T-F.
- The share of 0DTE SPXW volume executed as complex orders (≈ 46 %) means the complex book is deep enough that a 1–10-lot condor should fill near the implied mid most of the day; but no fill-rate statistic exists, so T-F must log complex-order NBBO (not available on OPRA for the package) against the leg-implied NBBO.

### Gaps
- No measured per-leg vs package fill comparison for SPXW or SPY condors exists in any source found; the Li–Musto–Pearson "Costs of executing complex options trades" (2023) paper is the likely source and was not retrievable.
- No source gives SPXW or SPY quoted half-spreads by delta (5/10/16/25) for 0DTE; Cboe says only that 0DTE options have "very tight bid-ask spreads" — [Cboe 0DTE page](https://www.cboe.com/tradable-products/0dte); a one-day 2023 Schaeffer's snapshot found SPY/QQQ 0DTE percentage spreads of 5.8 % vs ≈ 1 % for other expirations — [Schaeffer's](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/bgs/2023/09/22/0dte-options-are-higher-spreads-imminent) (T3, single day).
- Cboe's complex-order auction (COA) price-improvement statistics were not found on a public page.

## Key question 3: What are the realized tail losses of short 0DTE structures on event days and large-gap/large-range days, and how often does a max loss occur per 1,000 trades?

### Takeaway
Published evidence gives tail sizes (expected shortfall 0.58–1.58 % of underlying per day; worst realized/implied miss 2.5×; our SPXW straddle −$8,499 on a non-event day) and shows that scheduled events keep the *hit rate* (83 % on FOMC days in a 3-year tastylive sample) while destroying the *expectancy* ($4 average on a $5.80 credit); nobody publishes a max-loss count per 1,000 trades, so it has to be derived from the long-strike delta and measured in-house.

### Cited Findings
- Vilkov (pre-correction summary; the tail metrics are cost-independent): ES 1 % of 0.58–1.58 % of underlying across the seven SPXW 10:00→16:00 structures; worst days "severe for all strategies"; realized skewness explains 20–40 % of PnL variation for directional spreads and risk reversals; IV/skew add 2–7 pp of R² — [results-summary.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/docs/agent-context/results-summary.md), [paper-annotated.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/docs/paper/paper-annotated.md) (T1).

### Inferences
- **Max-loss frequency, derived.** For a short strike at delta d with the long strike at delta d_L, the close-based probability of a *full* max loss ≈ d_L (the long strike finishing ITM). A 16Δ/5Δ spread therefore reaches max loss ≈ 50 times per 1,000 trades at fair pricing; a 10Δ short with the long at ≈ 3Δ ≈ 30; a 20Δ short with $20 SPX wings (long ≈ 10–13Δ on a 0.7 %-σ day) ≈ 100–130. Realized rates should be a few points lower given the 3–12-point VRP in hit rates (Q1), and intraday stops *raise* the realized-loss count (tastytrade: 0.5× stop halves the win rate). None of the published studies reports the count.
- **Per-$1,000-of-margin tail.** Vilkov's ES 1 % of 0.58–1.58 % of underlying is, at SPX ≈ 6,500, $3,800–$10,300 per short straddle-class contract on the worst 1 % of days — the same magnitude as our measured −$8,499 worst day — so the T0 window is not unusually benign. For a defined-risk 10Δ condor the tail is capped at W(1−2cₛ) ≈ 0.8 W, which converts the ES problem into a frequency problem: with a 1,000-trade sample the lane must survive ≈ 30–130 max-loss days whose combined cost is 24–104 widths against 1,000 × 0.2 W = 200 widths of max credit.
- **Event days**: the tastylive FOMC sample says the hit rate is unaffected (83 %) and the expectancy is zero; the FlashAlpha and our T0 figures say the worst days are *not* scheduled-event days. Excluding FOMC/CPI/NFP (plan T-E) removes ≈ 12 % of sessions and in T0 lowered the mean while leaving the worst day untouched. The pre-registered exclusion is justified on the expectancy evidence, not on tail evidence.

### Gaps
- No published distribution of 0DTE short-structure P&L on CPI or NFP days was found (only FOMC); no published "max loss per N trades" count for any 0DTE structure.
- Vilkov's per-structure worst-day and drawdown numbers (Table 3) are not public.
- No study separates large-gap days (overnight) from large-range days (intraday) for 0DTE sellers; plan H5 (intraday gamma as a vol gauge) is the only in-house lead.

## Key question 4: SPXW mechanics (PM settlement, settlement timing, fees, margin for defined vs undefined risk) versus SPY/QQQ (early assignment, after-hours exercise, dividends, tax)

### Takeaway
SPXW is European, cash-settled on the *component closing prices* (the closing auctions, published after 4:00 pm ET) the business day after expiry, stops trading at 4:00 pm ET, has $0.05/$0.10 ticks and no position limits, is a Section 1256 contract, and costs ≈ $0.36–0.45 exchange fee plus a $0.60 broker index fee per contract; SPY/QQQ are American, physically settled, exercisable until 5:30 pm ET after a 4:15 pm trading close, auto-exercised at $0.01 ITM, carry quarterly ex-dividend assignment risk on third-Friday expiries, and are not 1256. Reg-T margin is max loss for both when defined-risk; for undefined risk SPX requires 15 % of index less OTM (min 10 %), which portfolio margin cuts roughly 5–6×.

### Cited Findings

**SPXW contract and settlement.**
- Exercise style "European - SPX/SPXW options generally may be exercised only on the expiration date"; SPXW exercise-settlement value "is calculated using the closing sales price in the primary market of each component security on the expiration date" (SPX AM-settled uses opening prices); "Exercise will result in delivery of cash on the business day following expiration"; SPXW trading "will ordinarily cease on the day of expiration, 3:00 pm CT" (1:00 pm ET on half days); ticks 0.05 below $3.00, 0.10 otherwise; strikes $5/$10/$25/$50/$100/$200; "No position and exercise limits are in effect" — [Cboe SPX specifications](https://ww2.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/specifications) and [current page](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/specifications/) (T1).
- Component primary markets close at 4:00 pm and "disseminate closing prices of the component securities, which are used to calculate the exercise settlement value" — [SEC filing 34-84480](https://www.sec.gov/file/34-84480) (T1). The SPXW settlement is therefore the official S&P 500 close built from closing-auction prints, not the 4:00:00 index tick.
- Settlement-timing risk magnitude (local sibling note, from the earlier literature round): closing prices deviate from the 16:00 midquote by ≈ 8 bp on average (3.6 bp for SPY/QQQ) and the deviation half-reverts shortly after the close — [zero_dte_microstructure_and_exits.md §5](/home/user/georgeredo/research_notes/Finding%20a%200DTE%20options%20edge/zero_dte_microstructure_and_exits.md). Our T0 variants: SPXW 13:00 straddle ex-events, exit at 15:55 +$45.3/day, at 15:59 +$60.5, at the 16:00 last quote +$19.6, held to settlement +$139.7 — [tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md) (T0).
- Share of SPX volume: 0DTE ≈ 2.4 mm contracts/day and a record 62.4 % of SPX volume in Aug 2025, retail ≈ 53 % — [Cboe, Sept 2025](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/spx-0-dte-options-jump-to-record-62-share-in-august/); 61 % in May 2025 with retail 54 % — [Cboe, June 2025](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/spx-0-dte-options-jump-to-61-share-on-retail-resurgence) (T2).

**Fees per contract.**

- tastytrade: options on stock/ETF $1.00/contract to open, $0.00 to close, capped at $10 per leg; broad-based index options $1.00 open / $0.00 close, **excluded from the cap**, plus clearing $0.10/contract, ORF $0.02/contract, FINRA TAF $0.00329/contract on sales (waived 2026-10-01 → 12-31), and a "Single-Listed Exchange Proprietary Index Options Fee" of **SPX $0.60, XSP $0.00/$0.07, NDX $0.25, RUT $0.18, VIX $0.35** per contract; exercise/assignment $5 — [tastytrade fee schedule](https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt7dc2e3d4a7071563/blt2b752fef372188fe/commissions-and-fees) (T1 broker doc). **Margin.**

**SPY/QQQ mechanics.**

### Inferences
- **Fee economics per unit of notional (tastytrade schedule).** SPX: open ≈ $1.00 + $0.10 + $0.60 + $0.02 = $1.72, close ≈ $0.72 (fees only) → a 4-leg condor ≈ $6.88 to open, $2.88 to close, $9.76 round trip, $6.88 if left to settle. SPY: open ≈ $1.12, close ≈ $0.12 → $4.48 / $0.48 / $4.96. Because one SPX contract is ≈ 10 SPY contracts, matching one SPX condor with SPY costs ≈ $49.60 vs $9.76 (5×); the plan's $0.70 per side per contract is below the SPX figure ($1.72 open) and above the SPY close figure. XSP ($0.00/$0.07 index fee) is the SPY-sized cash-settled 1256 alternative but its spreads were not measured here.
- **Margin for defined vs undefined risk.** Reg-T buying power for a defined-risk SPXW condor is exactly max loss (width − credit), so return-on-capital is the 2cₛ/(1−2cₛ) identity of Q1 (11–47 % per trade at 5–16Δ fair credit). Undefined-risk SPX strangles under Reg-T need ≈ 15 % of index less OTM per side (≈ $97,500 at SPX 6,500 for a near-ATM side, ≥ 10 % floor ≈ $65,000), i.e. 15–25 % of notional; Cboe's examples put portfolio margin at ≈ 3 % of notional for a short straddle (≈ 5.9× less) and *below max loss* for the 50-wide condor ($39,839 vs $65,100). A high-win-rate wide-strangle lane is therefore a portfolio-margin product; under Reg-T the defined-risk condor returns more per dollar of buying power even after paying for the wings.
- **Settlement timing.** With SPXW the last tradeable second is 16:00:00 but the settlement is the auction close; our T0 variants (exit 15:59 +$60.5 vs 16:00 last quote +$19.6 vs settlement +$139.7, ex-events) show the exit-spread cost in the final minute is large relative to the auction-gap risk, which the sibling note sizes at ≈ 8 bp. For a short straddle at SPX 6,500 an 8 bp gap is ≈ $5.2 per contract — negligible against a $4–6 per-contract exit spread plus fees, but it is uninsurable after 16:00.
- **SPY after-hours exposure is asymmetric and uncapped.** A short SPY leg that closes $0.02 OTM can be exercised on a 5:00 pm headline; the holder's loss is the overnight gap on 100 shares, not the option premium, and the position is only known the next morning. That is the concrete reason the "leave legs to expire" rule (H14/T-F) is clean on SPXW and conditional on SPY (buy back short legs within the after-hours band; abandon long wings).

### Gaps
- The exact publication time of the official SPXW settlement value (and how often it is corrected) is not stated on any page found; Cboe has issued settlement-price corrections before — [Cboe release note, 2019](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/release_notes/2019/Corrected-Closing-Settlement-Prices-for-Certain-SP-Indices-II.pdf).
- Cboe's current margin worked examples are at 2021 index levels; brokers (tastytrade, IBKR, Schwab) may require more than the exchange minimum and their index-option margin pages were not retrieved.
- XSP quoted spreads for 0DTE were not measured and no source reports them.

## Key question 5: Does strike selection using realized intraday volatility, the expected move (ATM straddle), or dealer-gamma levels improve the hit rate relative to fixed delta?

### Takeaway
The expected move and a fixed ≈16Δ are the same strike (1σ band ≈ 16Δ), and the only measured gain from using it is the generic 3-point VRP (71.0 % vs 68.3 %); conditioning on *volatility state* (skew below median, contracting intraday IV, VIX terciles) improves single-leg expectancy in two vendor studies but has not been shown to raise multi-leg hit rates after costs (Vilkov's conditional rules reverse sign after the cost fix); dealer-gamma strike selection has no published evidence and the in-house tests (H4, H6) rejected it.

### Cited Findings
- 1σ-band strikes from the 10:00 ATM straddle: 71.0 % inside at the close vs 68.3 % fair; stable 69–71 % 2022–2025; realized/implied median 0.589 — [FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study) (T2). The FlashAlpha screener page defines "wing" options as beyond ≈ 10Δ and measures spread as % of mid but reports no SPX results — [FlashAlpha liquidity](https://flashalpha.com/concepts/liquidity).
- Skew filter on 0DTE 25Δ call sales (SPX, sold at bid, 2022–2026): sell only when 10:00 0DTE skew < 3 % (median) → CAGR 16.5 % → 19.8 %, Sharpe 1.04 → 1.14, half the trades — [OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/blog/0dte-covered-calls-arent-special-timing-them-is/) (T2).
- Vilkov: conditioning on terciles of 10:00 integrated 0DTE implied variance uses full-sample (ex-post) cutoffs; downside structures earn more in high-VIX states, upside spreads in low-VIX; realized-variance regressions explain at most ≈ 4.5 % of PnL; ML conditional rules reached net Sharpe ≈ 1.0 before the cost correction and negative after — [paper-annotated.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/docs/paper/paper-annotated.md), [KNOWN-ISSUES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/KNOWN-ISSUES.md) (T1).
- tastylive: 0DTE success "is higher when IV contracts ... expanding IV ... leads to poor performance" — [tastylive, Sept 2024](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/intraday-iv-and-0-dte-success-09-24-2024); "selling into a rising VIX proved profitable" — [tastylive, Oct 2024](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/anticorrelation-and-0-dte-10-15-2024) (T3, qualitative and mutually tension-laden).
- Option Alpha's peg tables (SPX within x % of the price at time t) are the "realized intraday move" alternative to delta; a 0.2 % band at 14:00 held 65.6 % of days vs 53.3 % for 0.3 % at 10:30 — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/learn/top-0dte-options-strategies) (T3).
- Dealer gamma: Cboe finds market-maker gamma hedging "de minimis" (≤ 0.2 % of SPX liquidity) and customer 0DTE flow "very balanced" — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact) (T2). In-house: dealer-gamma direction filter rejected (POS−NEG slope +0.03 [−0.12, +0.19]); wall/magnet pin fades indistinguishable from distance-matched random levels; intraday gamma as a *volatility* gauge is open (IC −0.21 vs absolute 30-min move) — [COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md §4](/home/user/georgeredo/COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md) (T0).
- Practitioner note on why delta fails around events: delta-based probabilities "can be misleading around events" and the expected move is "often quoted and rounded" — [Saxo, July 2026](https://www.home.saxo/content/articles/options/where-the-strikes-go-a-meta-earnings-case-study-28072026) (T3, single-name earnings).

### Inferences
- **Expected-move strikes are fixed-delta strikes.** A strike at ±1 straddle-implied σ is ≈ the 16Δ strike by construction, so "expected move vs delta" is not a test of two methods; the useful variable is the *multiple* of the expected move (0.5σ ≈ 30Δ, 1σ ≈ 16Δ, 1.5σ ≈ 7Δ, 2σ ≈ 2–3Δ) chosen at the entry time, and the FlashAlpha table says the realized inside-1σ rate is 69–71 % every year, i.e. the implied σ is a slightly conservative scale for the *body*. For the hit-rate target this gives a calibrated mapping: a 1σ strangle/condor at 10:00 ≈ 70 % both-OTM at the close; a 1.5σ pair (≈ 7Δ each) would be ≈ 86 % at a normal and higher under the measured 13 % body-overstatement.
- **Realized intraday vol as the scale.** Option Alpha's peg numbers (65.6 % inside ±0.2 % from 14:00) are the empirical analogue of a realized-vol band for a late entry; because they are unconditional on implied vol they would be the natural control arm for a "realized-vol-scaled strikes vs implied-vol-scaled strikes" test on the ThetaData chains. No published study runs that comparison.
- **What improves expectancy is day selection, not strike selection.** The two positive vendor results (OptionMetrics skew filter; tastylive contracting-IV) and the one negative academic result (Vilkov after costs) all operate on *which days to sell*; none shows a strike rule that moves the hit rate beyond the 3-point VRP without giving back credit. For the plan this means a high-win-rate lane should fix the strike rule in advance (a σ-multiple at the entry minute), and spend the pre-registration budget on a single day-selection filter (skew or 0DTE IV-change) with the H5 gamma-volatility gauge as a size input, not a strike input.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head backtest of fixed-delta vs expected-move vs realized-vol strike selection for 0DTE condors/spreads on any index was found.
- No published evidence that dealer-gamma strike levels (walls, flip points) change 0DTE short-structure hit rates; the only tests are in-house and negative.
- The tastylive IV-conditioning episodes give direction only, no effect sizes.
