# SPXW at measured NBBO: pre-registered test T-E (13:00 straddle held to settlement) and a pre-registered high-win-rate short-premium grid

Scope and provenance. Every number below was computed in this study from the local ThetaData dataset: 1-minute NBBO of the same-day SPXW expiry for strikes within ±2 % of the 09:31 SPX price, plus SPX index minutes, 1,106 sessions 2022-05-13 → 2026-10-09, of which 1,097 are regular sessions (the 9 early closes are excluded). No ThetaData login was made. Design, splits, seeds and pass rules were fixed in [PREREGISTRATION.md](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/PREREGISTRATION.md) before any run; its SHA-256 and the times are in [prereg_sha256.txt](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/prereg_sha256.txt) (written 2026-10-13 23:53 UTC; Amendment 1 at 00:04 UTC; TRAIN selection hashed at 00:24 UTC, before the TEST stage ran). Code: `sim.py`, `run_grid.py`, `te.py`, `analyze.py`, `bandcheck.py`, `diag.py`, `report.py` in [research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/README.md). Generated tables: [RESULTS.md](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/RESULTS.md). Per-trade data (≈ 2.1 M rows) are in `$SP/hwr_grid/spxw/` (not in git).

Conventions: P&L in $ per 1-lot (one contract per leg, ×100); sell at the NBBO bid, buy at the ask; $1.00 per contract per side; settlement = SPX price stamped 16:00 (an approximation: the official SPXW settlement uses the components' closing-auction prices); 95 % intervals are day-clustered percentile bootstraps (5,000 draws, seed 7). Periods: **T-E window** 2022-05-13 → 2025-05-29; **holdout** 2025-06-02 → 2026-10-09; grid **TRAIN** 2022-05-13 → 2024-12-31 (656 sessions); grid **TEST** 2025-01-02 → 2026-10-09 (441 sessions). "LB" = lower 95 % bound of the mean.

Deviations and interpretations, all written down before the runs unless stated: (1) T-E window starts 2022-05-13, not 2022-01-03 as in plan §8, because daily SPXW expirations only exist from that date; (2) "FOMC entry only at 14:05" was implemented as "on FOMC days enter at 14:05 instead of the configured time" (so for 14:05 entries it equals no filter; 144 duplicate configurations are dropped, leaving 2,736 distinct); (3) stops and take-profits are triggered on the executable cost to close (shorts at ask, longs at bid), not on mids as T-C did; (4) the literal "a leg with bid 0 at entry → skip" rule was applied to wings too (it removed one trade in total); (5) Amendment 1 (00:04 UTC, before any TEST or T-E statistic): the reality-check denominator is each configuration's fixed bootstrap s.e.; the within-draw studentised version is reported alongside; (6) the band-truncation check (`bandcheck.py`) is post-hoc and is labelled as such.

### Inferences
- The positive T-C/H13 observation does not carry back to 2022-05 → 2025-05: three years of the same rule produce a mean indistinguishable from zero, a −$41,419 drawdown and one day (2025-04-09, the tariff-pause rally, not an FOMC/CPI/NFP day) that erases about two years of income. Excluding scheduled events does not remove unscheduled tails.
- In both windows the 13:00 entry earns less than a random entry time held to settlement. Whatever premium exists is not tied to 13:00.
- Holding to settlement beats buying back at 15:55 by ≈ $38 per straddle in the T-E window (+$26.4 vs −$12.2) and ≈ $97 in the holdout (+$152.9 vs +$56.1). This is consistent with §8's exit-spread finding, but it moves the mean, not the interval.

### Gaps
- Return on capital uses the Reg-T formula, not a broker's 0DTE house margin, and portfolio-margin figures were not computed per day.
- The T-E window contains only one extreme unscheduled day (2025-04-09). The tail estimate rests on very few observations, and the 2017–2022 stress move proved too small.

## Grid: which train-selected configurations combine a TEST win rate ≥ 70 % with a TEST lower bound > 0, and how many such bounds would chance produce?

### Takeaway
Selection on TRAIN produced two pre-specified configurations. **S1** (14:05 short straddle, 1× credit stop, held to settlement, skip FOMC + month-end) fails on TEST: LB < 0 and win rate 59 %. **S2** (14:05 short strangle at ±0.5·EM, no TP/stop, held to settlement, skip FOMC + month-end) is the one train-selected configuration that combines a TEST win rate ≥ 70 % (71.5 %) with a positive cost-realistic TEST lower bound (+$63.2). It still fails the plan's random-entry criterion: the same strangle entered at random minutes earned as much or more. Over the full grid, the number of TEST lower bounds above zero (135 of 2,736) is within the range chance produces under a global null (mean 51, 95th percentile 232). A fixed-s.e. max-t reality check does reject the global null, but the 13:00 survivors of that check depend on a data-band artifact. **No iron condor or iron butterfly configuration qualifies.**

### Inferences
- The TEST period (2025–2026) was more favourable to premium sellers than TRAIN: TEST medians are higher in every win-rate bin. Many configurations look good in TEST without having looked good in TRAIN, and the count of positive lower bounds is what correlated configurations produce by chance.
- S2 is the only result that meets the two headline criteria after honest selection, and it survives the band check under a realistic credit. The random baseline shows its P&L comes from "sell a ±0.5·EM strangle and hold to settlement, skip FOMC and month-end", not from the 14:05 timing. That broader rule was not pre-registered and has not been tested out of sample.
- The fixed-s.e. reality-check rejections should not be read as discoveries. The TEST survivors are 13:00 strangles whose positive record excludes 2025-04-09 only because the dataset did not store their strikes that day. The within-draw version, which accounts for rare-loss tails, rejects nothing.

- The ±2 % storage band forces skips on exactly the days that matter for short strangles. A complete answer needs the full chain on large-move days (in particular April 2025), which the dataset does not hold.
- TEST overlaps the T-C window, whose 13:00 straddle results were known before this study, so TEST is not unseen for the 13:00 straddle family.
- Random-entry baselines are themselves band-truncated on large-move days.

## Win rate versus expectancy at measured NBBO

### Takeaway
At SPXW NBBO a higher win rate does not buy expectancy. On TRAIN, median expectancy is highest at 50–70 % win rates for naked structures and turns negative above 70 %. Configurations with ≥ 90 % wins had no positive lower bound on TRAIN, and every winged (condor/butterfly) bin has a negative median in both periods. TEST is flatter and more positive across the board, consistent with a favourable regime rather than a stable trade-off.

### Inferences
- The "≥ 70 % win rate" target is reached mainly by moving strikes out (k ≥ 1) or by adding take-profits. Both cut the credit and the mean faster than they cut losses, so at these costs high win rates are mostly a description of the payoff shape.
- Wings are a net cost at SPXW NBBO in this sample: four legs pay more spread and fees than the protection returns. The literature's cost-corrected finding that 4-leg structures are worst after costs is reproduced here with measured quotes (see [structures_and_strike_selection.md](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research_notes/High%20win%20rate%200DTE%20lanes/structures_and_strike_selection.md)).

### Gaps
- Win-rate bins mix entry times, filters and structures; the table is descriptive, and its bins are not independent samples.

## Effect of the exit rule, stops, take-profits, FOMC timing and month-end

### Takeaway
Matched over all other rules, holding to settlement is the most reliable improvement: +$6.5 per trade over E1 on TRAIN and +$29.3 on TEST. Leaving legs with ask ≤ $0.10 adds a small but consistent +$2.7 over buying everything back. Take-profits raise the win rate by 7–18 points but cost $28–45 per trade. Stops lower the win rate by 5–13 points and narrow the interval; their effect on the mean flips sign between TRAIN and TEST. Moving FOMC entries to 14:05 is equivalent to skipping FOMC days. Skipping month-end on top of FOMC is the only filter that helps in both periods (+$6.4 TRAIN, +$20.0 TEST per trade).

### Inferences
- The exit result answers H14 (plan §8): leaving cheap legs is worth a few dollars per trade, and settling everything is worth more. Both are small next to the day-level dispersion (s.d. ≈ $1,000–2,300 per lot).
- Take-profits are the textbook way to raise win rate, and at measured costs they lower expectancy in essentially every matched pair. A ≥ 70 % win rate obtained this way is not evidence of edge.
- The month-end skip that `empirical_vol_filters_spx.md` flagged as a post-hoc candidate helps in both TRAIN and TEST here. FOMC handling by itself does not, and the CPI/NFP exclusion of T-E has no consistent value.

### Gaps
- Effects are averages over heterogeneous configurations and are not each tested for significance; they describe the grid, not single rules in isolation.

## Sizing for configurations whose TEST lower bound is > 0

### Takeaway
For the 135 TEST-positive configurations, quarter-Kelly (estimated on TRAIN) needs a median $73,000 of capital per 1-lot, and an 8 % max-loss budget needs $80,000. On TEST paths these produce median drawdowns of 15–17 % and P(end below start) of 1–2 %. ## Gate verdict (COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md §1 and §8)

### Takeaway
**Nothing is promotable.** T-E fails its pre-registered rule in the T-E window, so H13 is rejected; the holdout "confirmation" comes from the window that generated the hypothesis. In the grid, S1 fails §1.1. S2 passes §1.1 (TEST LB > 0, n = 397, 21 months), the adverse-fill check and the ≥ 70 % win-rate objective, but fails §1.2 because it does not beat its random-entry baseline by more than one round trip. Recommended register change: close H13; record "short ±0.5·EM SPXW strangle held to settlement, skip FOMC and month-end, any entry 10:00–14:30" as a new hypothesis that needs its own pre-registration on data with full strike coverage of large-move days.

### Inferences
- Measured NBBO removes the cost objection for SPXW (entry half-spreads ≈ 0.9 % of credit, exit avoided by settlement). The binding problem is the tail: one unscheduled day per few hundred decides the sign of the mean. Win-rate engineering (TPs, wings, far strikes) does not change that.
- The one robust regularity across both periods is that afternoon (13:00–14:05) naked premium held to settlement, skipping FOMC and month-end, had positive means. Its out-of-sample status is "promising but not distinguishable from random timing, and not tested on complete large-move-day data".
