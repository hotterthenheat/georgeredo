# Empirical: high-win-rate short-premium grid on SPY / QQQ / IWM 0DTE at measured NBBO (pre-registered, TRAIN → TEST)

Scope and method (every number below was computed in this study from the local ThetaData dataset; code, pre-registration and CSV outputs are under `/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/`, README there). No ThetaData login was used. Data: 1-minute NBBO of the same-day expiry for strikes within ±2 % of the 09:31 underlying print, and 1-minute underlying bars (`research/thetadata/dataset`). Split fixed in advance: TRAIN 2022-11-11 → 2024-12-31 (530 SPY/QQQ sessions after excluding 9 early closes), TEST 2025-01-02 → 2026-10-09 (441 sessions); IWM TRAIN 2024-04-19 → 2024-12-31 only (short). Grid per ticker: 7 structure-k cells (ATM straddle k = 0; strangle k = 0.5/1.0/1.5 × the ATM-straddle expected move EM; iron condor k = 0.5/1.0/1.5 with wings at (k+1)·EM) × 4 entries (10:00, 11:00, 13:00, 14:05) × 3 take-profits (none, 25 %, 50 % of credit) × 3 stops (none, 1×, 2× credit loss) × 2 exits (E1: buy back at the ask at 15:55, leaving legs with ask ≤ $0.05 to expire; E2: hold to expiry, valued at intrinsic against the 16:00 close) × 4 event filters (none; skip FOMC; on FOMC days enter at 14:05; skip FOMC or month-end) = **2,016 configurations per ticker (1,890 distinct)**. Fills: sell at the bid, buy at the ask, per leg; $0.70 per contract per side; a leg with bid 0 at entry → no trade; TP filled at that minute's ask, stop filled at the next minute's ask. All $ are per 1-lot (one contract per leg). CIs: 95 % bootstrap over trading days, 5,000 draws, seed 7. Selection on TRAIN only by the lower confidence bound (LB); the pre-registered selections were written to `selection_train.json` before any TEST trade was computed and were never revised (post-hoc analyses are labelled as such). Scopes: SPY, QQQ, IWM and SPY+QQQ pooled.

## Key question 1: Across the pre-registered grid, what are win rate, mean P&L per 1-lot, day-clustered 95 % CI, profit factor, max drawdown and worst 5 days, in TRAIN and in TEST?

### Takeaway
The grid is mostly a win-rate machine with negative or zero expectancy: across the 1,890 distinct SPY+QQQ configurations the median win rate is 71.0 % (TRAIN) and 72.9 % (TEST) while the median mean P&L is −$2.34 (TRAIN) and −$1.39 (TEST) per trade, the median profit factor is 0.85 / 0.94, and iron condors never have a positive lower bound when bought back at 15:55. The configurations with a positive lower bound are short straddles/strangles, overwhelmingly the hold-to-expiry (E2) variants, and their edge is the last five minutes of option premium on days the trade is still open (Key question 3).

### Inferences
- A ≥ 70 % win rate is cheap on this grid (more than half the configurations reach it) and says almost nothing about expectancy: it is bought by distance (k), take-profits or condor wings, each of which lowers the mean (Key question 3), consistent with the literature note's point that the win rate of a short-premium structure is mostly its payoff shape ([short_premium_management_evidence.md](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research_notes/High%20win%20rate%200DTE%20lanes/short_premium_management_evidence.md), [structures_and_strike_selection.md](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research_notes/High%20win%20rate%200DTE%20lanes/structures_and_strike_selection.md)).
- Four-leg condors on SPY/QQQ lose to the extra two legs' spread and fees ($2.80 more per round trip plus wing half-spreads against a credit of $11–$77): their E1 means are negative in every cell.
- The coverage loss is concentrated on high-volatility days (spot moved > 2 % before entry, or strikes beyond the band), so every number is conditional on "the market is inside the ±2 % band at entry". That flatters late entries, which are the ones skipped once the market has already broken out (for SPY S1 the 19 days it could not trade cost random-time entries −$458.56 per trade; Key question 4), and far-OTM/condor cells, which are skipped most on large-EM days.

### Gaps
- The ±2 % stored band prevents pricing entries on days when the underlying had already moved > 2 % (9 of 678 published T-A rows; 39 % of QQQ condor k=1.5 TRAIN entries); a wider band would be needed to remove this conditioning.
- No quote-size or queue modelling: every fill is at the displayed NBBO for a 1-lot.

## Key question 2: Which configuration is best on the TRAIN lower bound, and what does it do in TEST? Same for the best TRAIN configuration among those with TRAIN win rate ≥ 70 %.

### Takeaway
The TRAIN winners are all the same family — a 14:05 ATM straddle (or k = 0.5 strangle) with a 1× or 2× stop, held to expiry (E2), skipping FOMC and month-end days — and they stayed positive in TEST with lower bounds of +$12 to +$24 per trade; but a random entry time between 10:00 and 14:30 with the same rules did as well or better, so the entry time adds nothing, and two of the eight pre-registered selections (SPY S1, win 68.2 %; IWM S2, win 64.5 %) clear every gate item as written. IWM selected the same family on a short TRAIN window and kept a positive TEST lower bound (+$6.28 for S2) at about a third of the SPY/QQQ size. With the realistic 15:55 buy-back (E1, post-hoc), the best TRAIN configuration's TEST lower bound is about zero (−$2.63 to +$0.39 across SPY, QQQ and the pool; IWM −$1.50).

### Inferences
- The TRAIN→TEST survival is real but it belongs to "hold an afternoon short straddle/strangle to the close", not to a timing or filter rule: the random-time baseline with the same structure and rules earns the same, and the TRAIN-best E1 versions are at the edge of significance in TEST and negative in 2026.
- The stop rules (1×/2×) that TRAIN selected cut the worst days (pooled S1 worst day −$706 vs −$1,229 for S2) without changing the mean materially; that is a risk-shaping choice, not an edge.

### Gaps
- TEST is 21 months of one broadly favourable regime for sold volatility (2025–2026, both higher premium and higher realized moves; Key question 3); one TRAIN/TEST split cannot show whether the family survives a 2022-style trending market (the dataset's SPY/QQQ daily expiries only start 2022-11-11).

## Key question 3: How much do take-profit rules, stops, the leave-cheap-legs exit rule, hold-to-expiry, and the FOMC/month-end filters move win rate vs expectancy?

### Takeaway
Take-profits raise the win rate by 5–12 points (median, matched pairs) and cost $5.6–$8.0 per trade (median) in both periods; stops lower the win rate by 4–13 points and leave the mean about unchanged in TRAIN (median +$0.63 / +$0.76 for 1× / 2×) and slightly worse in TEST (−$2.75 / −$1.20); the leave-cheap-legs rule adds a small, consistent +$0.3 (median) per trade; event filters move either number by well under one point / one dollar; holding to expiry (E2) is the only rule that raises both, by +1.5–2.9 points and +$1.7–4.5 (median), up to +$30 per ATM straddle — and post-hoc diagnostics show that most of the E2 gain is the price of SPY/QQQ's after-hours exercise risk, which the E2 valuation ignores.

### Inferences
- Within this grid the win-rate/expectancy trade-off is monotone and costly: every lever that raises the win rate (TP, wider k, condor wings) lowers the mean, and the lever that lowers the win rate (stops) does not raise the mean out of sample; the literature claim that early small profit targets "work" is a win-rate claim, not an expectancy claim, on measured NBBO ([short_premium_management_evidence.md](file:///home/user/georgeredo/research_notes/High%20win%20rate%200DTE%20lanes/short_premium_management_evidence.md)).

- The 15:55 ETF option prices look rich against the 16:00 close but roughly fair against the after-hours price, i.e. the "premium" E2 collects is mostly payment for after-hours exercise/assignment risk that a 16:00 valuation does not charge. ### Gaps
- After-hours prices are available here for SPY only (ThetaData EOD records at 17:15); the QQQ and IWM E2 numbers could not be re-valued.
- The real assignment process (exercise-by-exception at $0.01 ITM, contrary instructions until 17:30, overnight exposure on delivered shares until the next open) is approximated by a single 17:15 price; the next-morning gap risk on assigned shares is not modelled.

### Inferences
- The grid does contain a genuine, multiple-testing-robust positive expectancy in TEST — short ATM-to-0.5-EM premium entered in the afternoon and held to the close — but it is unconditional with respect to entry time (random baseline), it is largely the after-hours exercise premium of American ETF options when valued at 16:00, and the version that buys back at 15:55 (E1) is at the zero line in TEST. The two formal PASSes (SPY S1, IWM S2) are fragile, single-ticker results with < 70 % win rates whose margins over the random baseline are partly or wholly an artifact of the ±2 % band (the lane cannot trade the days the market had already broken out); SPY S1 is at most a candidate for a new pre-registered test with after-hours/assignment costs modelled and a wider stored band, not a promotable lane.
- For a high-win-rate lane the most defensible numbers in this study are the far strangles bought back at 15:55 (k = 1–1.5, E1, 13:00, skip FOMC+month-end: TEST 81–88 % win, +$12–$15 per trade with LB ≈ +$5), but they were not TRAIN-selected (TRAIN LB < 0) and so cannot be promoted from this run.

### Gaps
- One fixed split; no second out-of-sample window exists for SPY/QQQ daily expiries before 2022-11.
- The gate's "one round-trip cost" for E2 lanes is small by construction (no exit spread), which makes the random-baseline criterion easier for E2 than for E1 lanes.
