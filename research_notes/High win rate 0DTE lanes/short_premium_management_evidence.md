# Managing short-premium 0DTE positions for high win rate without negative expectancy: evidence note

Scope: management rules for short 0DTE premium (straddles, strangles, credit spreads, iron condors/flies on SPX/SPXW, SPY, QQQ), 2022–2026 evidence first; pre-2022 weekly-option evidence is labelled **pre-0DTE**. Written against `COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §1 (promotion only on a day-clustered lower 95 % bound > 0 under the spread cost model, out of sample, ≥ 150 trades, NBBO-validated fills) and §8 (measured: SPY/QQQ entry half-spread 0.6 % of credit; buying back near-worthless legs at 15:55 costs 5–6 % of leg value, realized round trip 3.9 % of credit; SPXW entry half-spread 0.87 %; T-A/T-C failed; H13 hold-to-settlement and H14 exit-spread engineering open).

Evidence-tier labels used below: **[PR]** peer-reviewed/journal; **[WP]** academic working paper (SSRN); **[EX]** exchange, clearing or regulator rule text; **[VN]** vendor/exchange research (Cboe Insights, OptionMetrics, ORATS); **[PB]** practitioner backtest or trade-log study (tastylive, Option Alpha); **[AN]** anecdote/commentary; **[OWN]** this repository's measured results (§8) or arithmetic done for this note. Cost treatment is stated per source where the source discloses it; "not disclosed" means the page does not say.

Access note: SSRN itself blocked every fetch (bot check), so Vilkov, Beckmeyer–Branger–Gayda, Almeida–Hizmeri–Vilkov and Chong are cited through secondary pages that quote them; every such number is flagged. The seven tastylive 0DTE episode pages are a JavaScript shell with no server-rendered text; their numbers come from tastylive's own news-insights write-ups and from search-engine/podcast summaries of the episodes, flagged accordingly.

## Key question 1: Which take-profit and stop-loss rules have been tested on 0DTE short premium, and what did they do to win rate, average P&L, profit factor and drawdown? Which sources used bid/ask fills?

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study tests 0DTE take-profit/stop rules; the evidence is practitioner-tier. It points one way on profit targets (early, small targets of roughly 10–35 % of max profit raise win rate, and tastylive reports that unmanaged 0DTE short premium loses consistently) and the other way on stops (tastylive's 0DTE strangle test found a loose 100 %-of-credit stop beat 25 % and 50 % stops on both win rate and average daily P&L, because tight stops are hit by noise at roughly twice the expiry-ITM rate). None of the sources reports profit factor or max drawdown numerically, only Option Alpha's trade logs are real fills, and no source reports a confidence interval, so none of them would pass the §1 gate as published.

### Cited Findings

Underlying, sample, pricing and the actual win-rate numbers are not on the public page — [tastylive episode page](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/stop-losses-for-0-dte-strangles-04-30-2024) (numbers via search-engine summary of the episode description; in-sample; cost treatment not disclosed).
- **[PB] tastylive, "Success in Zero DTE: Early Management Key" (10 Sep 2024).** Research "favors frequent management, particularly at 25% of max profit", and "unmanaged 0DTE positions lose money consistently" — [tastylive episode page](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/success-in-zero-dte-early-management-key-09-10-2024) (episode description; no numbers public; cost treatment not disclosed).
- **[PB] tastylive, "Profit Targets by Delta for 0DTEs" (19 Dec 2024).** Conclusion: managing at 25–35 % of max profit "yields better outcomes across different delta ranges" — [tastylive episode page](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/profit-targets-by-delta-for-0-dt-es-12-19-2024) (description only).
- **[PB] tastylive, "Zero Hour Breakdown" (1 Dec 2025).** Two-week 0DTE study: 25 % profit target won 89 % of trades vs 80 % for the 50 % target, with lower max-loss percentages; the 50 % group was hurt by "one extreme directional session"; hosts cautioned it was only 2–3 weeks of data — [tastylive episode page](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/zero-hour-breakdown-12-01-2025) (via search summary; tiny sample).
- **[PB] tastylive, 0DTE iron flies (7 Aug 2025).** A 10 % profit target on $20-wide iron flies had a 78 % win rate; a 20 % target won 61 % with larger average profits — [tastylive episode page](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/zero-dte-winners-and-losers-for-iron-flies-08-07-2025) (via search summary; average P&L not public).
- **[PB/AN] tastylive viewer-question episode (May 2025, third-party summary).** Closing a 0DTE strangle "at twice the credit received yields similar results to adjusting or holding"; holding to the close "can be profitable but may not be optimal because of intraday swings" — [Podwise summary](https://podwise.ai/episodes/3898838) (secondary; no numbers).
- **[PB] tastylive "Best of 2024" (2 Jan 2025).** 20-delta strangles at the open vs 45 DTE: "early profit-taking is key for both"; 20- and 30-delta strangles placed early in the day produced "significantly higher average P&L"; "holding to expiration increases risk and volatility". No figures — [Podwise summary](https://podwise.ai/episodes/2630734).
- **[PB] Option Alpha, 230,000 live 0DTE bot trades (483 trading days from Sep 2021).** 49.64 % of 0DTE traders were profitable. Real broker fills (autotrading platform), but the "profitable trader" cut is survivorship-selected and in-sample.

Profit targets and stops are not quantified on the page.
- **[PB] Option Alpha live bot statistics (all DTE, not 0DTE-specific).** Iron condor 144,976 live trades, 65.06 % average win rate, average entry 17 DTE; iron butterfly 200,679 trades, 65 % win, average entry 1 DTE and 0-day hold (i.e. effectively a 0DTE book); bear call spread 73.41 %, bull put spread 69.82 % — [Option Alpha stats page](https://optionalpha.com/stats) (no P&L shown; "live stats from bot trades").

- **[AN] Numerix white paper.** Backtesting problems (data quality, overfitting, trading costs) "are amplified by the ultrashort timeframe"; recommends high-frequency data and scenario analysis — [Numerix 0DTE white paper](https://www.numerix.com/sites/default/files/file/2026-01/Numerix_White_Paper_0DTE_Trading_Strategies.pdf).
- **Probability-of-touch heuristic (why tight stops cut win rate).** Practitioner sources state probability of touch ≈ 2 × delta and delta ≈ probability of expiring ITM — [Options Trading IQ](https://optionstradingiq.com/probability-of-touch-in-options/); tastylive's **pre-0DTE** SPY test found touch rates about double the expiry-ITM rate and that IV overstated both — [tastylive, Testing Probability of Touch (2016)](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/testing-probability-of-touch-11-22-2016).
- **Pre-0DTE background (45 DTE SPY, tastylive 2016/2019).** Managing strangles at 50 % of max profit won 90 % vs 82 % held to expiry — [tastylive 2016](https://tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/why-manage-at-50-not-25-for-short-strangles-09-13-2016); stop losses on 16-delta strangles reduced strategy volatility — [tastylive 2019](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/stop-losses-in-strangles-03-07-2019). Labelled pre-0DTE; the mechanism (early profit-taking converts path variance into wins) is the same one tastylive invokes for 0DTE.
- **[OWN] Cost treatment summary for this question.** Real fills: Option Alpha (bot executions). Modelled bid/ask: ORATS (fraction of spread). Not disclosed: every tastylive 0DTE episode. Mid only: none of the above state mid, but none of the tastylive pages rule it out.

### Inferences
- Small profit targets raise the reported win rate mechanically (the position is closed before the path can reverse), but every take-profit exit is a buy-back and therefore pays the exit spread that §8 measured at 5–6 % of leg value for near-worthless legs; a 25 %-of-max-profit target on a credit c closes legs worth 0.75c, where the spread as a fraction of leg value is lower than at 0.05–0.10, so targets taken while legs still have value are cheaper per dollar than the 15:55 buy-back of near-worthless legs. This is an inference from §8's measurement, not from any external source.
- The tastylive stop result (100 % stop > 25 %/50 % stops) is consistent with touch ≈ 2 × delta: a stop placed at 25 % of credit on a 0DTE strangle is hit by ordinary intraday noise far more often than the strike is breached at expiry, so tight stops convert many eventual winners into realized losers; a stop at 1× credit sits roughly where the position's intrinsic value exceeds the credit, which is near the breakeven anyway.
- Option Alpha's 63–72 % win rates for SPY 0DTE condors/flies with +4.6 % to +7.9 % of premium average return are the only 0DTE management figures on real fills; expressed per dollar of credit, those averages are 1–2 times the §8-measured SPY round-trip cost of 3.9 % of credit, i.e. the live edge is of the same order as the spread. No profit factor or drawdown is given, so the §1 lower-bound test cannot be evaluated from the published pages.
- The ORATS 200 %-stop anecdote (loss doubled again after the stop) shows that stops at 2× credit limit single-day losses only if the fill is close to the trigger; the QuantConnect report of a 1.9-point adverse fill on SPX suggests the stop model should be adverse-fill NBBO, which is what §1 rule 3 requires.

### Gaps
- No source publishes a 0DTE grid of (profit target × stop) with win rate, average P&L, profit factor and max drawdown together; tastylive's grids exist only in video; Option Alpha's are in images.
- No source reports whether the 25 %/50 % targets were priced at mid or at the ask; none reports a confidence interval or an out-of-sample split.
- No evidence was found on delta-based stops (e.g. close when short-strike delta reaches 30) for 0DTE; the only delta-based result is a target-by-entry-delta study with no public numbers.
- No public, bid/ask-aware test of 1×/2×/3× credit stops on SPX 0DTE condors for 2023–2024 was found (Option Omega public write-ups cover 2018–2022 with no stops; ORATS covers QQQ).

## Key question 2: What is the evidence on entry time of day, and does afternoon theta decay beat the morning volatility-risk premium after costs?

### Takeaway
The academic evidence tests fixed 10:00 (and 13:00/15:00) entries held to expiry and finds the unconditional 0DTE variance premium "statistically real, economically irrelevant" after costs at any of those times; practitioner evidence is split by structure: tastylive finds morning OTM strangles (open, closed after 90 minutes) "somewhat profitable" at 64–68 % win rates while last-30-minute premium selling loses net despite win rates up to 82 %, whereas Option Alpha finds SPY iron condors opened more than two hours after the open averaged +37 % of premium vs −0.36 % for condors opened within two hours, and that SPX decay accelerates after 15:30. The repository's own T-A found 13:00 the only non-negative entry for SPY/QQQ straddles and T-C found 13:00 SPXW positive only when held to settlement; no external source isolates "afternoon decay minus afternoon exit cost".

### Cited Findings
- **[WP] Vilkov, "0DTE Trading Rules" (SSRN 4641356; Nov 2023 and Jan 2024 versions).** SPX 0DTE, Sep 2016 to May 2023 (Jan 2024 version to 11 Jan 2024), 30-minute prices interpolated to moneyness 0.98–1.02 in 0.00125 steps; strategies: calls, puts, ATM straddles, strangles, bull call/bear put spreads, call/put ratio spreads, risk reversals; "positions open at a fixed time, 10:00 in the main analysis and 13:00 and 15:00 in additional tests, and are held to expiration at 16:00"; variance risk premium measured as implied minus realized variance while open — [CXO Advisory summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/0dte-strategy-outcomes) (paywalled beyond the setup). Abstract via search: "0DTEs deliver significant variance risk premium for the whole sample period"; at the median, buying deep ITM calls and selling OTM calls and puts can be statistically profitable; median returns of most popular strategies are mostly negative; realized P&L is well explained by realized skewness of the index — [Hull Tactical commentary quoting the paper](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/) and [SSRN listing (blocked to fetch)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4641356).
- **[WP, via secondary] Vilkov numbers as quoted by a newsletter.** VRP "about 0.001 %" of index value from 10:00 to close, "statistically real, economically irrelevant", transaction costs "swallow it whole"; risk reversals about +0.01 % of index value mean return, "trivial even before costs"; straddles, condors, spreads, butterflies "showed no consistent profitability when held to expiry"; "static rules (fixed entry, hold to expiry) failed across the board"; P&L symmetric but fat-tailed, "a few large losses erase weeks of small gains", Sharpe near zero; conditional rules would require forecasting realized skewness — [Breaking Nasdaq, "The 0DTE illusion"](https://breakingnasdaq.beehiiv.com/p/the-0dte-illusion). Secondary; the 0.001 % and 0.01 % figures could not be checked against the paper.
- **[WP] Vilkov, 2026 version "0DTE Trading Rules: Tail Risk, Implementation, and Tactical Timing".** SPXW, Sep 2016 to Jan 2026, 10:00 ET entry held to 16:00; seven strategy families (strangle/straddle, iron butterfly/condor, risk reversal, bull call, bear put, call ratio, put ratio); "unconditional 0DTE is weak after costs and tail risk"; conditional out-of-sample rules on selected strategies and baskets achieve Sharpe 1.0–1.3; the option panel includes mid prices and bid-ask spreads — [replication-repository summary](https://tomevault.io/tome/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies). The specific conditioning variables and the cost model are not on the public page.
- **[PB] tastylive 52-week open-vs-close study (14 May 2024; Jacob Perlman).** Short OTM strangles at the open, closed after 90 minutes: win rates 64 % ($10 OTM), 65 % ($20), 68 % ($30), average P&L and CVaR "relatively stable", "somewhat profitable with controlled risk". Short premium in the last 30 minutes: straddles 40 % win, $10 OTM 57 %, $20 OTM 82 %, yet "all late-afternoon strategies produced net losses, mainly from outlier moves"; Sosnoff: "Don't sell zero DTE premium with a half hour to go." Underlying, dates, deltas, dollar P&L and pricing basis not stated — [tastylive news-insights write-up](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/0dte-options-opening-vs-closing-trades).
- **[PB] tastylive, "Trading the Close with 0DTEs" (2 Jul 2024).** 0DTE strangles opened at 11:00 and 12:00, then closed for profit or rolled; closing beat rolling "with high win percentages but considerable risks"; hosts note SPX IV < 10 % made profitable trades difficult — [tastylive episode page](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/trading-the-close-with-0-dt-es-07-02-2024) (description only; no numbers).
- **[PB] tastylive, 20-delta 0DTE strangles vs 45 DTE (8 May 2024).** One year of 0DTE data; the write-up gives two different answers for the 0DTE "peak earning period" (the second two-hour window in one passage, the final hour in another) and says "the initial hours carried significant risk and volatility" — [tastylive news-insights](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/How-To-Manage-Risk-and-Profit-in-Zero-DTE-Trades).
- **[PB] Option Alpha, entry time within live 0DTE positions.** SPY iron condors opened within two hours of the open averaged −0.36 % of premium; condors opened more than two hours after the open (759 positions) averaged +37 %, and 67 % of those expired worthless; iron butterflies were nearly unchanged by entry time; condors were opened on average 1 h 43 min after the open and closed with 2 h 13 min left — [Option Alpha 0DTE report](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte) (real fills; in-sample; the >2 h cohort is a small, self-selected subset).
- **[PB] Option Alpha, profitable-trader timing.** "Consistently profitable traders opened positions at approximately 10:15 AM ET and closed shortly after 12:00", about two hours average hold; Monday most profitable, Thursday consistently negative for profitable traders — [Option Alpha performance study](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance).
- **[PB] Option Alpha, 10:30 vs 14:30 SPX iron condor backtest video.** Reports a $15,000 P&L difference between the two entry times without stating which is higher on the public page — [Option Alpha video page](https://optionalpha.com/videos/morning-vs-afternoon-0dte-backtested-options-strategies).
- **[PB] Option Alpha, intraday decay of a 10-point OTM SPX put spread.** 30 calendar days, late Jul–Aug 2024, minute quotes: decay is "non-linear" with "a noticeable acceleration in the afternoon, particularly after 15:30"; early entries "don't capture significantly more premium" and add directional exposure; on volatile days premium stays elevated "until the final moments"; on 5 Aug 2024 spreads "widened significantly" and the spread "held its value a lot longer than we'd expected" — [Option Alpha time-decay study](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-time-decay) (single 30-day sample).
- **[PB] Option Alpha "peg" statistics (last 180 trading days).** SPX closed within 0.20 % of its 14:00 price 65.6 % of the time; within 0.30 % of its 10:30 price 53.3 %; within 0.20 % of its 13:30 price 56.1 %; within 0.50 % of its post-13:30 price 90.6 %; "roughly 1 in 10 trades moves beyond 0.50 % by the close" — [Option Alpha intraday SPX prices](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-intraday-spx-historical-prices).
- **[VN] OptionMetrics, daily 25-delta SPX call selling at 10:00 held to expiry (IvyDB Intraday, mid-2022 to present).** Calls priced at the bid (spread paid); 0DTE version CAGR 16.5 %, Sharpe 1.04 vs SPX buy-and-hold Sharpe 1.01, negative skew; 5DTE worst of 0/5/10/30; a filter selling only when 10:00 25-delta skew < 3 % (the median) removes about half the days and lifts CAGR to 19.8 % and Sharpe to 1.14; authors call the daily version's marginal gains "likely not worth" the 21× trading volume; bull-market sample caveat — [OptionMetrics blog](https://optionmetrics.com/blog/0dte-covered-calls-arent-special-timing-them-is/).
- **[PB] FlashAlpha, 10:00 ET SPY ATM straddle, 193 Wednesdays, 6 Jul 2022 to 1 Apr 2026, quote mids, no fees.** Median implied 1σ move 0.72 % vs median absolute realized move 0.37 %; close inside the implied 1σ band 71.0 % of sessions vs ~68.3 % if fairly priced; median realized/implied 0.589 vs ~0.674 benchmark; hit rate 69–71 % every year 2022–2025 (84.6 % in Q1 2026, 13 sessions); worst session 9 Apr 2025: implied 3.51 %, realized +8.72 %; conclusion "selling it naked is a leverage decision, not an edge decision" — [FlashAlpha study](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study).
- **[WP] Almeida–Hizmeri–Vilkov, "0DTE Asset Pricing" (SSRN 4701401).** Investors "demand a high compensation to bear variance risk over the day, which is mainly due to compensation for upside risk"; the intraday VRP negatively predicts intraday market returns — [Tinbergen seminar abstract](https://tinbergen.nl/event/2025/11/06/13089/0dte-asset-pricing); [Liverpool seminar abstract](https://liverpool.ac.uk/institute-for-financial-and-actuarial-mathematics/past-seminars/rodrigo-hizmeri).
- **[AN] SpotGamma.** Hedging flows from 0DTE positions "intensify throughout the afternoon, peaking in the final two hours" ("0DTE Power Hour"), with dealer re-hedging and pinning — [SpotGamma 0DTE page](https://spotgamma.com/0dte); [SpotGamma strategy guide](https://spotgamma.com/0dte-options-strategy-guide/). SpotGamma's own deck on Cboe's 2023 report asserts, without data, that 0DTE "is likely opened in the AM, and closed in the PM" and that delta (not gamma) exposure into the close matters — [SpotGamma deck](https://spotgamma.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/CBOEs-0DTE-Report.pdf).
- **[OWN] Repository measurements (§8).** T-A (13:00 short ATM straddle, SPY+QQQ, n = 678, 17 months, measured NBBO): +$8.60 per straddle, 95 % bound [−6.07, 22.45]; "every other entry time negative"; T-C (13:00 SPXW, close 15:55, 339 sessions): +$72.4/day [−75.8, 214.2], PF 1.16; the same T-C held to the 16:00 settlement: +$156.8/day [+5.8, +305.0], but [−18.5, +288.6] ex FOMC/CPI/NFP — `COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §8.

### Inferences
- The two practitioner results that look contradictory (tastylive: morning better, last-30-minutes loses; Option Alpha: >2 h after open better) are reconcilable: both say the last half hour is where tail losses live, and both say roughly 10:00–14:00 entries closed before the close are where the positive samples are; neither tests a 15:00 entry held to settlement with a cash-settled product, which is the only variant the repository found positive (T-C hold-to-settle).
- The OptionMetrics result is the only external 0DTE test that (a) prices at the bid, (b) holds to expiry (no exit spread), and (c) is positive after that cost; it is a one-sided 25-delta call overwrite in a bull market, so it says nothing about the put side or about two-sided structures, but it is structurally the same "no buy-back" design as H13.
- FlashAlpha's 71.0 % vs 68.3 % is the cleanest statement that the ATM straddle's win rate is essentially the payoff-shape number (lognormal 1σ containment is 68.3 %); the edge is the 2.7-point excess, not the 71 %.
- Afternoon entries shrink the premium collected (Option Alpha's decay curve) while the exit spread in dollars does not shrink proportionally, so the afternoon's higher containment rate (65.6 % within 0.2 % of the 14:00 price) only pays if the position is not bought back; this is why afternoon-entry rules and hold-to-settlement rules are coupled for SPXW (H13) and why for SPY/QQQ (physical delivery) the afternoon lane is structurally harder.

### Gaps
- No external source reports an entry-time grid (open, 10:00, 11:00, 13:00, 14:00, 15:00) with win rate, average P&L and drawdown under bid/ask fills for a two-sided 0DTE structure; Vilkov's 13:00 and 15:00 results are in the paper (not reachable) and Option Alpha's grids are in images/video.
- The Vilkov VRP (0.001 % of index) and risk-reversal (0.01 %) magnitudes are secondary quotations; the paper's tables could not be fetched.
- No source separately measures afternoon theta capture net of afternoon exit cost; the repository's §8 figure (5–6 % of leg value at 15:55) is the only measurement.

## Key question 3: Holding to expiry versus buying back — cost of buying back near-worthless legs, pin risk, SPXW cash settlement vs SPY/QQQ assignment, and "let it expire" rules

### Takeaway
The rule texts make the asymmetry concrete: SPXW stops trading at 15:00 CT on expiration day, settles in cash on component closing prices, is European, and quotes in $0.05 ticks below $3 (so a $0.05–$0.10 leg has a spread floor of 50–100 % of its value), whereas SPY quotes in $0.01 for all series, trades until 16:15 ET, is physically delivered, and is subject to exercise-by-exception at $0.01 ITM with holder exercise decisions allowed until 17:30 ET on after-hours prices. Practitioner data show that condors held to expiry are overwhelmingly full winners (94 % in Option Alpha's SPY log) but that only 20 % of condors were held to expiry; no external source quantifies the buy-back cost of near-worthless legs, so the §8 measurement (5–6 % of leg value) is the only number.

### Cited Findings
- **[EX] Cboe SPX/SPXW specification.** "Trading in SPXW options will ordinarily cease on the day of expiration, 3:00 pm CT"; exercise-settlement value "calculated using the closing sales price in the primary market of each component security"; European style; cash delivered the business day after expiration; multiplier $100; minimum tick 0.05 for series below 3.00 and 0.10 otherwise; legs of complex orders may trade in $0.01 increments but the net package must be in $0.05 — [Cboe SPX specifications](https://ww2.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/specifications).
- **[EX] SPY tick size.** SPY options trade in $0.01 increments for all series regardless of premium — [SEC/C2 rule filing 34-78012](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/c2/2016/34-78012.pdf); the $3 breakpoint was removed for SPY and IWM — [SEC notice 34-61281](https://www.sec.gov/file/34-61281). Non-penny classes use $0.05 below $3 and $0.10 at or above — [Cboe Penny Interval Program notice](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/product_update/2023/Penny-Interval-Program-Annual-Review-and-Rebalancing.pdf).
- **[EX] OCC exercise by exception.** OCC automatically exercises equity and index options that are in the money by $0.01 or more at the underlying's closing price unless contrary instructions are given; "your firm may have a different threshold"; exchanges set a 4:30 pm CT cut-off for exercise notices and most brokers set earlier cut-offs — [OIC exercise FAQ](https://www.optionseducation.org/referencelibrary/faq/options-exercise).
- **[EX] Holder exercise deadline.** Option holders have until 5:30 pm ET on expiration day to make a final exercise decision; firms may set earlier cut-offs but may not accept instructions after 5:30 pm ET — [FINRA information notice (Feb 2021)](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2021-02/information-notice-020321.pdf); same 5:30 pm ET rule in exchange rulebooks — [Nasdaq ISE Options 6B](https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/rulebook/ise/rules/ise-options-6b).
- **[PB] After-hours exercise risk, in practitioner terms.** Long holders can exercise until 5:30 pm whether the option stopped trading at 4:00 or 4:15 pm; if the ETF closes OTM but moves past the short strike after hours "you will not know whether you will be assigned until the next morning"; assignments are processed overnight — [Option Alpha backtesting best practices](https://docs.optionalpha.com/tools/backtesting/best-practices).
- **[PB] Partial-ITM assignment on SPY 0DTE spreads.** SPY options expire 4:15 pm ET and are physically settled; SPX 4:00 pm ET cash; if only the short put finishes ITM the trader is assigned 100 shares per contract at the short strike and the position is "no longer risk-defined"; worked example 558/554 put spread with SPY at 556 leaves the trader long 100 shares at 558, a gap to 550 costing about $800 before credit; brokers charge assignment fees — [Option Alpha, partial ITM assignment](https://optionalpha.com/learn/0dte-partial-in-the-money-assignment).
- **[PB] How often positions are held to expiry and what happens.** Option Alpha SPY 0DTE log: 20 % of iron condors held to expiration; "94 % of iron condors held to expiration were full winners (expired worthless)"; iron butterflies cannot be full winners because one side always finishes ITM; condors were closed with 2 h 13 min left on average and butterflies with 3 h 13 min left — [Option Alpha 0DTE report](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte). In the 230k study "the majority of these trades were not held until expiration" and fewer than 0.5 % hit the 3:50 pm expiration protocol — [Option Alpha performance study](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance).
- **[PB] tastylive on holding to the close (0DTE).** Holding to expiration "increases risk and volatility"; closing beat rolling for 11:00/12:00 strangles — [Podwise summary of 2 Jan 2025 episode](https://podwise.ai/episodes/2630734); [tastylive 2 Jul 2024](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/trading-the-close-with-0-dt-es-07-02-2024).
- **[VN] Cboe, how 0DTE positions are structured (May 2025).** Over 95 % of SPX 0DTE trades are limited-risk (long outright or spreads); only about 4 % are naked short options; customer activity "extremely balanced"; net market-maker gamma hedging at most about 0.2 % of SPX daily liquidity — [Cboe, 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact).
- **[WP, via press] Retail execution costs on SPX 0DTE.** Effective spreads for retail trades were 6.1 % (calls) and 4.9 % (puts) vs 13.1 % and 10.1 % for non-retail trades; 72.2 % of retail orders traded a single option; "about 60 % of daily losses came from transaction costs" — [WealthManagement.com on Beckmeyer–Branger–Gayda](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options).
- **[EX/VN] Broker commission asymmetry that favours buy-backs.** tastytrade: $1 per contract to open (max $10 per leg for stock/ETF options), $0 to close for stock/ETF and broad-based index options; exchange, clearing and regulatory fees still apply to closes — [tastytrade pricing](https://tastytrade.com/pricing/). (No source found for a "$0.05 or less" closing policy; that is a different broker's historical practice and was not verified.)
- **[OWN] Repository measurements.** Buying back near-worthless legs at 15:55 costs 5–6 % of leg value, taking the SPY/QQQ round trip to 3.9 % of credit against a 0.6 % entry half-spread; SPXW straddle held to the 16:00 settlement: +$156.8/day [+5.8, +305.0] over 339 sessions, [−18.5, +288.6] ex event days (post-hoc, logged as H13); H14 = leave near-worthless legs to expire or buy back only when ask ≤ $0.05 — `COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §8.

### Inferences
- For SPXW the "let it expire" rule is cheap to specify and has no assignment risk: the only residual risks are (i) the settlement print being computed from component closing prices rather than the 15:00 CT index level (a last-print gap), and (ii) the $0.05 tick, which makes buying back a $0.10 leg cost at least 25–50 % of its value at the half-spread, so H14's "buy back only when ask ≤ $0.05" is really "never buy back a leg already at the minimum tick", and the economically meaningful version is a threshold in cents of the ask relative to the credit.
- For SPY/QQQ, holding a short leg within a few cents of the money through 16:00 converts a defined-risk spread into an undefined overnight share position whenever a partial-ITM finish or an after-hours move through 17:30 ET triggers exercise; the penny tick means the buy-back of a far-OTM leg is cheap in absolute terms (a $0.01 ask), so the SPY/QQQ analogue of H14 is "let expire only legs whose strike is beyond the after-hours move you are willing to carry", not "let all near-worthless legs expire".
- Option Alpha's 94 % full-winner rate among condors held to expiry is selection (traders held the ones that were comfortably OTM), not a property of holding; it does show that the exit-spread saving is available on the majority of days if the rule is applied to legs already far OTM.

### Gaps
- No external source quantifies the bid/ask on $0.05–$0.15 SPXW or SPY legs in the last 10 minutes, or the P&L difference between buying back and expiring; the only numbers are the repository's own.
- No source quantifies how often SPY/QQQ short legs that closed OTM at 16:00 were assigned after hours; the 17:30 ET window is documented in rule text only.
- No source documents the distribution of the SPXW settlement print minus the 15:00 CT index level.

## Key question 4: What do the published 0DTE studies say about short-premium profitability, hit rates and tails after costs?

### Takeaway
The academic record is consistent: 0DTE options carry a measurable variance risk premium (mainly for upside risk), it is small relative to the width of the daily P&L distribution, and unconditional "sell at a fixed time, hold to expiry" rules are not profitable after costs; retail as a group lost money as net buyers, with about 60 % of the losses being transaction costs, and the short side of retail trades is reported as profitable after fees; Cboe's own data say more than 95 % of 0DTE trades are defined-risk and flow is balanced, which removes a "dumb-money buyer" explanation for a durable seller edge. The only study claiming a strategy that survives costs is Vilkov's 2026 conditional out-of-sample rules (Sharpe 1.0–1.3), whose conditioning variables are not public.

### Inferences
- Across tiers, the measured gross edge of unconditional short 0DTE premium is of the same order as the round-trip spread (Vilkov's "costs swallow it"; §8's 3.9 % round trip vs Option Alpha's 4.6–7.9 % of premium average on real fills), so any rule that raises win rate at the cost of an extra buy-back is likely to be net-negative unless the exit is free (expiry) or taken when legs still have substantial value.
- Beckmeyer et al.'s "short positions earned 48–56 % after fees" is the retail short side of a tape where retail is net long; because the paper does not condition on structure, time or management, it cannot be read as a management rule, but it is the only tape-based evidence that the seller side of 0DTE was positive after fees in 2021–2023.

## Key question 5: What win-rate / expectancy combinations are realistic — theoretical win rate for a given delta, and the tail loss that makes expectancy zero?

### Takeaway
For a short leg at delta δ held to expiry the theoretical win rate is about 1 − δ (a strangle about 1 − 2δ) by construction, so any reported 80–95 % win rate for 5–20-delta selling is the payoff shape, not skill; the arithmetic that matters is the break-even tail: at a 90 % win rate with full credit kept on wins, the average loss that zeroes expectancy is 9× the credit, and for a width-w credit spread the break-even hit rate is (w − c)/w, i.e. 90 % for a $1 credit on a $10-wide spread before costs. Empirical hit rates line up with the theory (FlashAlpha 71 % vs 68.3 % for an ATM straddle; tastylive 82 % for $20-OTM last-30-minute premium that still lost net), which is why the §1 gate on the lower bound of expectancy, not the win rate, is the right test.

### Cited Findings
- **Delta as probability of expiring ITM (practitioner convention).** Delta "can be thought of as an estimate of how likely the option will be in-the-money at expiration"; probability of touch is about 2 × delta — [Options Trading IQ](https://optionstradingiq.com/probability-of-touch-in-options/); [tastylive, delta probability approximation (2025)](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/from-theory-to-practice/episodes/3-ways-to-use-delta-probability-approximation-10-02-2025); [tastylive touch test (2016, pre-0DTE)](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/testing-probability-of-touch-11-22-2016).
- **[PB] Win rate vs average P&L by delta (not 0DTE).** 5-delta strangles reach a 50 % profit target "with a 98% win rate" but average only $18 vs $77 for 40-delta; "sweet spot" 16–20 delta — [tastylive Options Jive (6 May 2025)](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/can-delta-help-you-time-profits-in-a-strangle-05-06-2025).
- **[PB] 0DTE credit-spread payoff asymmetry.** SPY short 35Δ/long 25Δ put spread: average credit $27 at 0DTE vs $217 at 45 DTE (about 8×); P&L as % of credit for a $2 adverse move: −111 % (0DTE) vs −7 % (45 DTE); "0DTE exposure is roughly 10x greater as a percentage of credit" — [tastylive, Vertical spreads in 0DTE options](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/vertical-spreads-in-0dte-options) (illustrative table; not a historical sample).

- **[OWN] Arithmetic for this note (lognormal, zero drift, no costs; delta = P(ITM)).**
  - Theoretical win rates: one short leg at δ = 0.05/0.10/0.16/0.20/0.25/0.30 → 95/90/84/80/75/70 %; strangle (both legs) → 90/80/68/60/50/40 %; probability of touching either strangle strike ≈ 4δ → 20/40/64/80/100/100 %, which is why a touch-based stop at 16Δ turns a 68 % expiry win rate into roughly a 36 % no-touch rate.
  - Credit spread break-even hit rate p* = (w − c)/w: $5 wide for $0.25/$0.50/$1.00 credit → 95/90/80 %; $10 wide for $0.50/$1/$2/$3 → 95/90/80/70 %; $20 wide for $2 → 90 %.
  - Break-even average loss L* = p/(1 − p) × W (W = average win per $1 credit): at p = 0.80, W = 1 → 4.0× credit; p = 0.90, W = 1 → 9.0×; p = 0.90 with a 25 %-of-credit target (W = 0.25) → 2.25×; p = 0.95, W = 0.25 → 4.75×.
  - Cost drag: with the §8-measured SPY/QQQ round trip of 3.9 % of credit, a gross edge of 4 % of credit nets +0.1 %; the same edge held to SPXW settlement (0.87 % entry half-spread only) nets +3.1 %. A 1σ straddle contains the close 68.3 % of the time under the normal; E|Z| = 0.798σ and the median |move| is 0.674σ, so the FlashAlpha 0.589 median ratio corresponds to realized moves about 13 % below the fair-value benchmark.

### Inferences
- Any lane whose target is "win rate ≥ 90 %" is, by construction, selling ≤ 10-delta per side; at that delta the credit is small enough that a 3.9 % round-trip cost is a large fraction of the gross edge and the break-even average loss is 9× the credit if wins keep the full credit, or 2.25× the credit if a 25 % target is used (because W falls with the target). The target therefore trades a higher win rate for a sharply lower tolerable tail, which is the win-rate-versus-expectancy trade-off in one line.
- A stop at 1× credit on a 10-delta strangle is hit at roughly the 40 % touch rate, so the realized win rate under a 1× stop is closer to 60–70 % than 80 %, matching the tastylive observation that loose stops beat tight ones on both win rate and P&L.
- The only management rule that improves the cost term without lowering W is "do not buy back legs that will expire worthless" (H14 for SPXW; delta-threshold variant for SPY/QQQ), which is why the H13/H14 pair is where the published evidence (OptionMetrics bid-priced hold-to-expiry; Option Alpha 94 % full winners among held condors; §8 settlement result) is most favourable, and where the pre-registered T-E/T-F tests should decide.

### Gaps
- No source reports the empirical distribution of losses conditional on a loss (average loss as a multiple of credit) for 0DTE condors/strangles under bid/ask fills; that is the quantity the §1 lower bound depends on, and it must come from the ThetaData dataset.
- No source provides the touch-rate (stop-hit) frequency for 0DTE specifically; the 2 × delta heuristic is from longer-dated tests.
- The delta ≈ P(ITM) approximation ignores drift and skew; at 0DTE with intraday skew (Almeida–Hizmeri–Vilkov: upside premium dominates) the realized put- and call-side hit rates differ, and no source quantifies that split.
