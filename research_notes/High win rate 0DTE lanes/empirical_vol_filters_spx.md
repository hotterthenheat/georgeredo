# Empirical volatility and regime filters for the SPX afternoon range (skip/size rules for short 0DTE premium lanes)

Scope and method (all numbers below were computed in this study; code and CSV outputs are under `/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/vol_filters/`, README there).
Data: locally cached ThetaData SPX and VIX 1-minute index prints, SPX EOD and SPY EOD, 2017-06-01 to 2026-10-09 (2,353 trading days; 20 early closes excluded -> 2,333 days; fit 2017-06..2022-12 = 1,395 days, test 2023-01..2026-10 = 938 days; split fixed before any analysis). No ThetaData login was used. Ranges are (max - min)/reference price of 1-minute prints; RV is the root sum of squared 1-minute log returns; "afternoon" = 13:00-16:00 prints, "morning" = 09:31-13:00. Event days: FOMC decision days from the Federal Reserve meeting calendars (77 in sample, incl. the two unscheduled March-2020 actions), CPI and Employment Situation (NFP) release days from the BLS news-release archives (cross-checked: every CPI date is an ALFRED CPIAUCSL vintage date; every NFP date equals the BLS reference-week rule), 2025-06 onward from the existing `straddle_nbbo/event_days.csv`; opex = third Friday. Stylized payoff: at 13:00 sell a straddle whose premium is k x (09:30-13:00 range); outcome per traded day = k - |13:00->16:00 move| / (09:30-13:00 range) (units of the morning range), 0 on skipped days; k_fair = 0.5053 (train mean of the ratio, so the trade-all payoff has zero in-sample mean; test mean of the ratio is 0.4757). "Delta" = change in mean outcome per trading day vs. trading every day. All CIs are 95 % moving-block bootstraps over trading days (block 20 days; 1,000 resamples for ICs, 2,000 otherwise; seed 20251013). Because the morning range is a realized proxy for an implied scale, every rule was re-run under an alternative scale (trailing 20-day geometric mean of the afternoon range, k_alt = 0.6734) to separate "the filter finds bad afternoons" from "the filter finds days on which the morning range is a bad yardstick".

## Key question 1: Which predictors carry the most information about the 13:00->16:00 |move| and range (Spearman IC with CI; out-of-sample R^2 of a linear model on log ranges)?

### Takeaway
The volatility level already realized or priced by 13:00 (morning realized variance, VIX at 13:00) carries almost all of the information: the afternoon range is moderately predictable (OOS R^2 0.44 for the full model and 0.29 for morning RV alone vs. a constant; 0.34 and 0.15 vs. a trailing 20-day mean), the afternoon |move| is barely predictable (OOS R^2 0.11), and calendar, gap, prior-day and VIX-change variables add little or nothing once the vol level is known. The afternoon move scales sub-linearly with the morning range (log-log elasticity 0.76 in-sample, 0.51 out-of-sample), so a premium or wing set 1:1 to the morning range is too tight on quiet mornings and too wide on wild ones; this fact, not any regime filter, dominates the payoff results below.

### Inferences
- Morning realized variance and the 13:00 VIX are near-substitutes (ICs within a few hundredths of each other); together with prior-day vol they describe the vol level, and all remaining variables are second-order. Information at the open (VIX, prior-day vol, gap, calendar) already gives 0.36 of the 0.44 full-model OOS R^2 for the afternoon range.
- Prior-day range, 5-day and 20-day RV do not beat a trailing 20-day average of the afternoon range out of sample (R^2 vs. that baseline all within [-0.17, +0.10]); the morning RV does (0.15 [0.05, 0.25]). A trader who already conditions on a trailing average gains only from intraday information.
- The afternoon |move| magnitude is mostly noise after conditioning (OOS R^2 about 0.1), which caps what any skip rule can do: rules reshape the distribution of the ratio, they do not forecast single afternoons.
- An elasticity of 0.5-0.8 means the ratio |move|/morning range mechanically falls as the morning range rises (quintile table: 0.75 -> 0.32), so under a 1:1 morning-range scale quiet mornings look like the worst days and wild mornings like the best. This is a property of the yardstick, not evidence that quiet mornings precede large afternoons in absolute terms (absolute afternoon |move| still rises with the morning range: IC +0.38).
- The gamma-sign effect is small and its CI nearly includes zero on a 2-year out-of-sample-only window; it should not be used as a rule yet.

### Gaps
- No option-implied data in the cache, so the "implied scale" is proxied by the realized morning range; whether the 13:00 implied afternoon move also has elasticity below 1 to the morning range (which would make quiet mornings genuinely under-priced) cannot be tested here.
- SPX 1-minute prints are index values rather than OHLC bars, so intra-minute extremes are missed (consistently across all series).

## Key question 2: How much larger are afternoon moves on FOMC/CPI/NFP/opex days (ratio and CI), and does excluding them raise the hit rate more than it reduces the opportunity count?

### Takeaway
Only FOMC days have larger afternoons: the 13:00-16:00 |move| is 2.08x [1.56, 2.68] and the range 2.10x [1.67, 2.58] that of event-free days, and relative to the (smaller) morning range the ratio is 2.93x [2.27, 3.68]. CPI, NFP and opex afternoons are not larger (0.94-0.96x with CIs spanning 1) because the 08:30 release is absorbed in the morning, and skipping them costs premium. Skipping FOMC (3.2 % of days) raises the out-of-sample hit rate by 1.3 points and the mean outcome per trading day by +0.034 [+0.016, +0.058] morning-range units under both scales; skipping all three events (12.3 % of days) adds nothing beyond FOMC. Month-end is a second, smaller, robust skip (1.33x [1.06, 1.65] |move|; +0.010 [+0.002, +0.017]).

### Inferences
- FOMC is a clean, cheap skip: it removes 3.2 % of opportunities and about 2.1x-sized afternoons whose hit rate at the fair premium is 32.5 %; the gain survives both scales, all k values and 7-9 of 10 years. The pre-announcement morning lull (morning range 0.65-0.84x) makes any morning-range-scaled premium especially thin on FOMC days, which is why the ratio effect (2.9x) exceeds the raw effect (2.1x).
- CPI/NFP days should not be skipped by a 13:00 lane: the release is at 08:30, the morning range is larger (NFP 1.22x), and the afternoon is ordinary; excluding them lowers the opportunity count by 4.5-4.7 % each and lowers the mean outcome.
- The Wednesday effect in the raw data is mostly FOMC (72 of 77 FOMC decision days are Wednesdays; ex-FOMC Wednesdays are 1.07x with a CI spanning 1).
- Month-end afternoons are larger (rebalancing flows are the usual explanation, not tested here) and the skip is small but positive under both scales.

### Gaps
- The CPI/NFP/FOMC lists before 2025-06 were extracted from the BLS and Fed pages via a summarising fetch tool because bls.gov blocked direct downloads; they were validated programmatically against ALFRED vintage dates (CPI, 101/101) and the BLS reference-week rule (NFP, 101/101), and against the existing 2025-26 event file at the overlap, but the raw HTML was not retained.
- Only one month-end-adjacent definition (last trading day) was tested; quarter-end and the first trading day were not separated.

## Key question 3: Does a VIX filter (skip when VIX > its 60-day 80th percentile, or when VIX rose > 10 % since the open) improve the out-of-sample hit rate and mean?

### Takeaway
No, under the morning-range scale: skipping days with VIX(13:00) above its rolling 60-day 80th percentile removes 22.8 % of test days, lowers the hit rate by 0.9 pt and the mean outcome per trading day by -0.020 [-0.035, -0.005] (-0.029 % of SPX per day), and the "VIX up more than 10 % since the open" rule is also negative (-0.0057 [-0.0091, -0.0028]). High-VIX afternoons are about 2x larger in absolute terms, but so is the morning range (1.94x), so the ratio is unchanged (0.96 [0.86, 1.06]). Under a trailing-afternoon-range scale the VIX filter turns positive in-sample (+0.082) but is not significant out of sample (+0.023 [-0.004, +0.057]).

### Inferences
- Whether a VIX filter helps depends entirely on whether the premium collected already reflects the day's vol. If the premium scales with the morning range (or, presumably, with a live implied move), high-VIX days are fair and skipping them forfeits 23 % of opportunities; if the premium is anchored to a trailing average, high-VIX days are the loss-makers. A lane that prices off live implied vol is closer to the first case, so the VIX filter is unlikely to add value as a skip; it is better used for sizing in index terms (losses in index % on high-VIX days are 2x larger).
- The intraday VIX change carries too little information (ICs near zero with the ratio) to be a skip rule at either threshold.

### Gaps
- No VIX term-structure (VIX9D/VIX3M) or VVIX data in the cache; the "VIX rose since the open" test could not be repeated with 0DTE implied vol, which is what an actual lane would observe.

### Inferences
- For a lane whose premium or wing is set as a multiple of the realized morning range, the best skip is to not trade when the morning has been unusually quiet relative to recent days (or, equivalently, to widen the scale on quiet mornings, since the afternoon move only shrinks about half as fast as the morning range). Treated as a sizing rather than a skip, half size keeps roughly half the gain.
- For a lane whose premium reflects live implied vol (the realistic case), the robust skips are FOMC and month-end only; VIX-level, VIX-change, gap, prior-day-range, CPI/NFP/opex and day-of-week rules either cost more opportunities than they save or are not distinguishable from zero out of sample. The strongest two-rule recommendation that survives both scales is "skip FOMC decision days and month-end sessions" (7.6 % of days; test hit rate +2.5 pts; +0.044 per trading day on both scales), although that pair was identified after seeing the single-rule results and should be treated as a post-hoc candidate for pre-registration.
- Year-to-year instability of the quiet-morning rule (large gains 2020-2024, nothing in 2017 and 2025-2026) coincides with the fall in the morning-to-afternoon elasticity from 0.82 to 0.54 and with the much lower 2025-2026 ratio levels (trade-all hit rate 73-74 % vs 56-59 % in 2020-2023), i.e. the afternoon has become relatively calmer and the denominator effect weaker.

### Gaps
- 31 single rules and 465 pairs were screened; selection was done on the training half, but the alternative-scale reversal shows that the train-selected winners encode the scale choice, so a lane-specific re-test with the lane's actual premium is needed before use.
- The stylized payoff ignores transaction costs, intraday stop-outs and the fact that daily SPX expirations only exist for all weekdays since 2022-05; the afternoon move statistics are structural, but the mapping to lane P&L is not.
