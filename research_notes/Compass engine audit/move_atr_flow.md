# Compass Modules 7, 8 and 11: expected-move bands and king scanner, ATR exhaustion filter, flow / dark-pool / rotation engine

Scope: an audit of the three modules under the owner's guardrails (no invented weights, ledger vs causal, full session 09:35–15:58, friction-adjusted, picked on 2022–2024 and read on 2025–2026, stress tests on every headline). Every number below comes from a script in `research_notes/Compass engine audit/move_atr_flow/` (`engine.py` → `out/*.parquet`; `analyze_em.py`, `analyze_atr.py`, `analyze_flow.py`, `daily.py`, `summarize.py` → `out/*.csv`, `out/summary.txt`). The data is the local ThetaData store (same-day chains at 1-minute NBBO, per-contract 1-minute bars within ±2% of the 09:31 price, 1-minute underlying bars, SPX prints) and the committed Unusual Whales lake. No vendor API was called for this note.

**Data and conventions actually used.** Sessions with a full day: SPY 971 and QQQ 971 (2022-11-11 → 2026-10-09), IWM 615 (2024-04-19 →), SPXW 1,097 (2022-05-13 →); the 9 (6 for IWM) half days are skipped. Minute m = 0 is 09:30; an ETF bar stamped t closes at t+1, so the spot at minute m is the close of the bar stamped m−1; an SPX print stamped m is the price at m; quotes stamped m are the NBBO at m; the close is minute 390. Every option trade: buy at the ask, sell at the bid, $0.65 a contract each way, none on a worthless expiry; the exit is a 50% trailing stop on the bid (from the highest bid since entry) else the bid at 15:58 (a hold-to-15:58 variant is also kept). "pick" = 2022-2024, "read" = 2025-2026 (IWM's pick period is 2024-04 → 2024-12 only). Stress = a 2,000-draw bootstrap that resamples sessions, the mean without the best 5 sessions, and the mean by year. The 1-minute underlying files before 2017-06 hold no prices for SPY and IWM (every field NaN), so the daily bars run 2017-01-03 → 2026-10-09 for those two and 2012-06 → for QQQ; SPX daily bars are built from the index prints (2017-06 →).

## Q1. Expected move: M = straddle vs M = 1.2533 × straddle (Module 7)

### Takeaway
The ATM straddle is a fair 1-SD ruler at the open in the root-mean-square sense (the RMS of |close − spot| / straddle at 09:35 is 1.15–1.29, close to the theoretical 1.2533), but the 68.3 % quantile is only 1.09–1.19 × straddle at the open and falls to 0.70–0.95 × in the last 90 minutes for the ETFs, so the 1.2533 multiple over-widens the band for its stated purpose. Neither boundary is a fade signal: the probability that the close is back inside the touch is 0.46–0.52 at both multipliers, the underlying continues 0.2–1.6 bps against the fade over the next 30 minutes, and the friction-adjusted nearest-OTM fade loses 11–29 % a trade in every product, period and multiplier, with every 95 % day-bootstrap range at or below zero. Changing M to 1.2533 × straddle does not improve fade accuracy (differences of ±0.02 in the back-inside rate and no consistent sign in $/contract).

### Inferences
- The 0.7979 rule holds for the second moment (RMS 1.15–1.29 ≈ 1.2533) but the distribution of the remaining-day close is fat-tailed, so the 68.3 % quantile is nearer 1.1 × straddle at the open; 1.2533 × straddle contains 71–75 % of closes, more than its nominal 68.3 %. If the band is meant as "where 68 % of closes land", M = straddle × 1.10–1.15 at the open, tapering to about 0.85–0.95 × straddle after 14:00, is the empirical constant for SPY/QQQ/IWM; SPXW keeps a constant near 1.15–1.2 all day (see the Gap on its last-minutes figure).
- The band is a reachability ruler, not a reversal level: price that reaches +M closes above it more often than not (P back inside 0.42–0.43 on upper touches in 2022–2024) and the option fade loses at both multipliers. The owner's test is answered: 1.2533 × does not improve fade accuracy; nothing about the boundary does. Keep the bands for sizing and target distance (Q2); drop any "fade at upper_1/lower_1" trigger.
- Verdict, Module 7 bands: **change** — M = straddle (not × 1.2533) as the default band, labelled "~63 % of closes inside at the open"; no fade signal at the boundary. If a 1-SD band is wanted, use the measured quantile (≈ 1.1 × at the open, ≈ 0.9 × after 14:00) rather than the theoretical constant.

### Gaps
- Touches are detected on minute closes (SPX prints); a bar-high touch would fire a few percent more often and slightly earlier, which can only make the entry price worse for the fade. The straddle uses the quoted mid; a straddle bought at the ask is 2–8 % wider at the open.
- SPXW's exceedance of ±1.0 × straddle at 15:50 is 58–60 % of sessions against 29–37 % for the ETFs, and 40–43 % of its closes land outside the band from 15:50. This is either the 16:00 SPX print (the closing auction's jump) against a 15:50 straddle that is quoted on the last trade before it, or stale/erratic index prints in the last minutes; it was not resolved here and the SPXW late-day containment figures should not be used until the 16:00 print is checked against the official settlement.

## Q2. King / target reachability (Module 7 scanner)

### Takeaway
With the king proxied by the strike with the most same-day contract volume so far (gross gamma by strike is not on disk historically), a target with |dist_em| ≤ 1 is reached before the close 63–67 % of the time and one at 1–2 M 14–21 % of the time — but the mirror level on the other side at the same distance, and any level at that distance, are reached at the same rates. The ≤ 1 rule is the distance (M itself) doing the work; the king adds nothing to reachability.

- The king proxy sits above spot 39–69 % of the time (SPXW 49–69 %, the ETFs 39–59 %), at a median distance of 0.31–0.43 M within the ≤ 1 band — [king_reach.csv](research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/king_reach.csv)

### Inferences
- The |dist_em| ≤ 1 gate is a sound reachability filter — a target 1–2 M away is reached only 14–21 % of the time, 0.5–1 M away 41–45 %, and under 0.5 M 65–87 % — but it validates the distance, not the king. Verdict: **keep** |dist_em| ≤ 1 as a target-reachability gate; **do not** present it as evidence for the king; state the reach odds by distance band instead of a pass/fail.
- A volume king is not a gamma king. The test says nothing about a gross-gamma strike; it says a heavily traded strike at distance d is reached like any level at distance d.

### Gaps
- The true king (max gross gamma by strike through the day) cannot be rebuilt from disk (no per-strike open interest or 1-minute gamma history); UW's `greek-exposure/strike` is a one-year daily (end-of-day) series and `spot-exposures/strike` answers the current day. A recorded per-minute king (Q6) is the only way to test the real rule.

## Q3. ATR exhaustion (Module 8)

### Takeaway
On the underlying's path the rule has support: a counter-trend fade of the session extreme on a day already extended more than 1.5 ATR closes back inside only 30–44 % of the time (47–53 % otherwise) and the underlying runs a further 8–35 bps to the close — but the option fade loses in every bucket anyway, so the filter removes a loss-maker from a loss-maker. The "negative gamma" qualifier is untestable at the available sample (5–47 events, 2–14 sessions a cell, signs inconsistent), and net gamma on UW's series is negative on 64–68 % of days, so the filter would in practice be "|move_atr| > 1.5". For swings, "pullback over chase" has no stable edge: it held in 2012/2017–2024 on the ETFs with ranges that include zero, reversed in 2025–2026, and is absent in the 3,500-name cross-section.

### Inferences
- On the underlying, the exhaustion filter is directionally right: an extended day (ext > 1.5) continues to the close after a sweep of its extreme in 56–70 % of cases on SPY/QQQ (the literature's intraday-momentum effect, Gao et al. 2018; Baltussen et al. 2021, is the same mechanism at the day scale). The gamma qualifier cannot be read off the available year; it also would not change the filter's application much because UW's net gamma is negative about two days in three.
- Verdict, 0DTE filter: **change to** "block counter-trend fades of the session extreme when |move_atr| > 1.5, whatever the gamma sign" — stated as a ledger finding (hit rate 30–44 % and −8 to −35 bps to the close, N 41–84 sweeps a product and period), not a causal rule; and note that the fade it blocks loses in every bucket anyway, so the filter's value is only in a desk that fades at all.
- Verdict, swing/LEAPS "prefer pullback over range_position > 0.95": **untestable as an edge / drop as a rule**. In 2017–2024 the ETFs' pullback and near-mean entries beat chase entries on 20-day returns, but every range includes zero except SPY's near_mean, 2025–2026 reversed it on SPY (chase_20 +162 bps, in the top of the range), and the cross-section shows no pullback premium (t −1.0) and a weak 5-day reversal after 20-day-high closes (−24 bps, t −2.2) that a swing with 8–28 DTE would not capture after the spread. State it as a preference for entry price, never as expected return.

### Gaps

## Q5. Literature (what was found, magnitude, costs, replicability here)

### Takeaway
The published option-flow evidence is about signed, opening, delta-aware order flow at the daily-to-weekly horizon in the cross-section of single stocks (40 bps next day in Pan–Poteshman; 0.34 % a week in Johnson–So), none of it about 0DTE index flow at the minute scale; the 0DTE literature disagrees on whether dealer hedging raises or dampens intraday volatility and finds retail 0DTE buyers lose on net; the straddle-to-sigma constant is Brenner–Subrahmanyam's 1.25; the intraday-momentum papers are the published basis for a trend-day filter. None of these can be replicated on the local data except the intraday-momentum and straddle results.

### Cited Findings
- Pan and Poteshman (2006, Review of Financial Studies 19:3, "The information in option volume for future stock prices"): put–call ratios from buyer-initiated opening volume (CBOE account-type data); stocks with low ratios outperform high ones by more than 40 bps the next day and more than 1 % over the next week, stronger where informed trading is concentrated and in higher-leverage contracts; attributed to private information. Not replicable here (needs opening/closing and buyer/seller flags by account type) — [NBER w10925](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10925), [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/355439)
- Hu (2014, JFE 111:3, 625–645, "Does option trading convey stock price information?"): the part of stock order imbalance induced by option market makers' hedging (delta-adjusted option order flow) predicts cross-sectional stock returns controlling for past stock and option returns; the imbalance independent of options has only a transitory impact. Not replicable here (needs signed option trades and stock order imbalance) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v111y2014i3p625-645.html), [SMU repository](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/3607)
- Muravyev (2016, Journal of Finance 71:2, 673–708, "Order flow and expected option returns"): decomposes option trades' price impact into inventory-risk and asymmetric-information components; the inventory component dominates and order imbalances attributable to inventory have about five times the impact on option prices previously thought; a one-SD rise in the inventory-related imbalance corresponds to about 1 % higher expected option return the next day (from the working-paper abstract); a Chilean replication on eight stocks found information, not inventory, dominant. Not replicable here (needs trade-signed data with market-maker identities) — [OptionMetrics summary](https://optionmetrics.com/?p=1033), [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/359812), [Universidad de Chile thesis](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/handle/2250/176841)
- Ge, Lin and Pearson (2016, JFE 120:3, 601–622, "Why does the option to stock volume ratio predict stock returns?"): with signed option volume, opening call purchases are the strongest predictor of stock returns, then sales closing purchased calls; no evidence that synthetic-short trades carry more information; the embedded leverage is the main reason option trading predicts returns — [Monash abstract](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/why-does-the-option-to-stock-volume-ratio-predict-stock-returns/), [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v120y2016i3p601-622.html)
- Johnson and So (2012, JFE 106:2, 262–286, "The option to stock volume ratio and future returns"): the lowest O/S decile outperforms the highest by 0.34 % a week (19.3 % annualized) in the published abstract (1.47 % a month risk-adjusted in the working paper); stronger when short-sale costs are high; O/S predicts firm-specific earnings news. A cross-sectional weekly effect, not an intraday signal; a daily O/S could be built from UW's stock screener and option volume but was not here — [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356813), [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i2p262-286.html), [Quantpedia on the working paper](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=527)
- Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda (2023, SSRN 4404704, "Retail traders love 0DTE options… but should they?"): 0DTE options drive most of the growth in S&P 500 option volume; end customers buy more 0DTE than they sell, leaving market makers net short (a secondary summary); retail's share of S&P 500 option trading is 3–4 %; retail lost about $184,000 a day on average from February 2021 to February 2023 and $358,000 a day after the daily-expiration calendar of May 2022, losing on a net basis every month since; buying or selling 0DTE tended to be profitable only in the first ten minutes, two-thirds of the gains in the first minute. The SSRN page was not reachable (403); figures are from press and summaries — [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@y125789/retail-traders-love-0dte-options-paper-summary-6da4a7ff7d72), [BNN Bloomberg, 21 Apr 2023](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634), [Bonus.com excerpt](https://www.bonus.com/news/zero-days-to-expiration-options-investment-or-gambling/)
- Brogaard, Han and Won (University of Utah, "How does zero-day-to-expiry options trading affect the volatility of underlying assets?"): a one-standard-deviation increase in 0DTE volume is associated with a jump of almost 14 % in the S&P 500's daily volatility and higher intraday volatility (press report; the SSRN listing was not found in this search) — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/zero-day-options-are-reordering-the-way-the-stock-market-behaves-1.1921930)
- The opposite finding: Adams et al. ("Do S&P500 options increase market volatility? Evidence from 0DTEs", SSRN 5641974): S&P 500 volatility is about 60 annualized bps lower on days with 0DTE trading; a 1 bp rise in market makers' hedging need is associated with a 4 bp fall in realized volatility; intraday, higher net gamma predicts lower volatility over the next 10 minutes, fading after about an hour; market makers' hedging needs were about 1 % of S&P shares per 1 % move before 2022 and rose about 0.15 points on 0DTE days — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/). Dim, Eraker and Vilkov (2024) likewise find higher 0DTE open-interest gamma is not linked to higher index volatility — [Harbourfront Quant](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-0dte-options-cause-for). A 2026 Princeton thesis finds the canonical delta-hedging channel adds no meaningful predictive power for intraday variance — [Princeton theses](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/61115cd1-cb09-4a8b-9230-ff488fc2a386)
- Cboe (Mandy Xu, "0DTEs decoded", 2 May 2025): SPX 0DTE volume up more than five-fold in three years to almost 2 million contracts a day; retail about 50–60 % of SPX 0DTE trading (57 % → 47 % in early April 2025, back to about 60 %); over 95 % of 0DTE trades are limited-risk structures and only 4 % naked short options; customer activity "extremely balanced", net market-maker gamma hedging at most 0.2 % of SPX daily liquidity. Cboe's earlier note (2023): 0DTE from 5 % of SPX volume in 2016 to over 40 %, averaging 50 % in August 2023; market-maker hedging flows 0.04–0.17 % of S&P futures liquidity, up to 1.3–1.9 % of daily notional under extreme conditions. Cboe has a commercial interest in the benign reading — [Cboe, 0DTEs decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact), [Cboe, Much ado about 0DTEs](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- Dark pools: Comerton-Forde and Putniņš (2015, JFE 118:1, 70–92, "Dark trading and price discovery"): dark trades are less informed than lit trades; low levels of non-block dark trading are benign or beneficial for informational efficiency, high levels harmful; block trades in the dark do not impede price discovery — [Edinburgh research portal](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/dark-trading-and-price-discovery/), [open manuscript (UTS)](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/121967/1/manuscript.pdf). Reed, Samadi and Sokobin ("Shorting in broad daylight"): exchange short sales are more informative than dark-pool short sales — 20-day underperformance 0.89 % on exchanges vs 0.53 % in dark pools — [Novus summary](https://www.novus.com/blog/shorting-in-broad-daylight-short-sales-and-venue-choice). Brogaard and Pan (2022, RFS, "Dark pool trading and information acquisition"): more dark trading is associated with more information acquisition around earnings (Tick Size Pilot identification) — [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/351982). Zhu (2014, RFS, "Do dark pools harm price discovery?") is a theory paper: dark pools concentrate informed traders on the exchange and improve price discovery — [MIT copy](https://www.mit.edu/~zhuh/Zhu_darkpool_RFS.pdf). No paper found tests a print-level "at the ask = buy" dark-pool signature on returns.
- Straddle vs 1σ: Brenner and Subrahmanyam (1988, Financial Analysts Journal, "A simple formula to compute the implied standard deviation"): ATM option ≈ 0.4 × S × σ√T, so straddle ≈ 0.8 × S σ √T and σ√T ≈ 1.25 × straddle / S, exact at zero rate and dividend; Stefanica and Radoičić (2016) show it is much less accurate away from the money — [Gundersen](https://gregorygundersen.com/blog/2023/08/19/atm-approximation/), [Options Analysis Suite](https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/calculators/expected-move), [IDEAS: Stefanica & Radoičić 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/ijfexx/v03y2016i01ns242478631650002x.html). Our data: RMS 1.15–1.29 at the open (Q1) — the constant holds in the second moment.
- ATR / trend-day evidence: Gao, Han, Li and Zhou (2018) and Baltussen et al. (2021) as in Q3 (intraday momentum from the first half-hour and from the rest-of-day return into the last 30 minutes, tied to gamma hedging); Zarattini and Aziz (2023) use an ATR-sized stop in an opening-range breakout whose net result did not replicate on other indices — citations in Q3. I found no academic paper on ATR-defined "trend-day exhaustion" or on fading expected-move boundaries; the only sources are practitioner notes (e.g. [Pepperstone](https://pepperstone.com/en-af/market-analysis/modeling-intraday-volatility) on using the straddle-implied range to spot a "tired" move, anecdotal) — a Gap.

### Inferences
- Every option-flow result that survives peer review uses signed, opening, account-typed flow at daily-or-longer horizons in single stocks; magnitudes of 40 bps a day to 0.34 % a week are far above any intraday index-option spread but are not what Module 11 measures. The local proxy (unsigned prints by close-vs-mid) is closer to the "noise" part of those papers' decompositions, which is consistent with its tiny, contrarian reading.
- The two sides of the 0DTE hedging debate (Brogaard et al. vs Adams et al. / Cboe) agree that customer 0DTE positioning is nearly balanced and dealer hedging is a small share of liquidity; a "negative gamma amplifies" filter has at best weak published support at the 10-minute scale (Adams et al.: higher net gamma → lower next-10-minute volatility) and none at the trend-day scale.

### Gaps
- SSRN pages (4404704, the Brogaard paper, 5641974) were not readable through the proxy (403); figures are from press and secondary summaries and should be checked against the papers before quoting in a public document. The SEC DERA paper on 0DTE (`dera-hope-reasonable-prc-2503.pdf`) is encrypted and could not be read.
- Hu's exact delta-adjusted imbalance formula, Ge–Lin–Pearson's magnitudes and Muravyev's published (vs working-paper) coefficients were not available from the abstracts reached.

## Files

Runtime: the engine 2–7 minutes a product single-threaded; the analyses under 3 minutes each. The `out/flow_*.parquet` tables are about 100 MB in all and can be rebuilt from the engine.
