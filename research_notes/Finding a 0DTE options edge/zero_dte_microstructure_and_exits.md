# 0DTE option economics, intraday seasonality and exit engineering (evidence note)

Scope: published, working-paper, exchange and vendor evidence on (1) who wins/loses in 0DTE index/ETF options and the size of the 0DTE variance risk premium (VRP) by time of day, (2) intraday return seasonality in SPX/SPY, (3) implied-vs-realized and vol-of-vol patterns intraday, (4) stop/exit engineering for very short-horizon option longs, (5) implications and concrete tests for a long- vs short-premium 0DTE strategy on SPY/QQQ/IWM/SPX. Source tiers are labelled in each bullet: [peer-reviewed], [working paper], [exchange research], [vendor/practitioner], [press]. Sample periods are stated where the source states them. Several SSRN pages returned HTTP 403 in this session, so some paper-level numbers are taken from secondary summaries and are flagged as such.

## Key question 1: What do Beckmeyer/Branger/Gayda, Brogaard/Han/Won, Dim/Eraker/Vilkov, Vilkov, Cboe and 2025-2026 papers find about who makes/loses money in 0DTEs and about 0DTE returns by holding period and entry time?

### Takeaway
Every independent dataset agrees on direction: retail long-premium 0DTE buyers lose on average (about 60% of the loss is bid-ask/transaction cost), sellers/market makers collect a small positive premium, and the unconditional 0DTE VRP is statistically real but economically tiny (median ~0.001% of the underlying from 10:00 to the close) with severe tails (1% expected shortfall 0.6-1.6% of the underlying per day). Entry-time-specific return tables exist (Vilkov tests 10:00, 13:00, 15:00 entries) but the 13:00/15:00 numbers are behind paywalls and could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?" [working paper, SSRN 4404704, March 2023]: Cboe SPX trade data with trader-type codes, 0DTE sample January 2021-February 2023; ~75% of retail SPX option trades are 0DTE; >40% of all SPX contracts traded expire the same day; retail lost on average $184,000/day over the full sample and $358,000/day after daily expirations began on 16 May 2022, >$70m in total; ~60% of daily losses are attributable to transaction costs and ~60% to put positions; long positions "lost 53% of their value" (61% since May 2022) while short positions "returned 48%" (56%) over the same periods (units as reported by the article, likely % of premium); retail effective spreads 6.1% (calls) / 4.9% (puts) vs 13.1% / 10.1% for non-retail; 72.2% of retail orders are one contract; retail keeps trading with under an hour to expiry even as spreads widen, and most positions are closed before expiry — [WealthManagement summary of the paper](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options); [SSRN listing](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4404704); [CXO Advisory summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance)

### Inferences
- The counterparty structure is: retail (and some institutions) net pay bid-ask spread and a small variance premium; market makers and systematic short-premium sellers collect it. The cost share (~60% of retail losses) means a long-premium strategy must first overcome effective spreads of ~5-6% of premium per side before any directional edge counts.
- The academic consensus has moved from "0DTEs destabilize markets" (Brogaard et al.) to "net dealer gamma is small and, if anything, dampens volatility on average" (Cboe, Dim et al., Adams et al.); the live disagreement is about conditional episodes when customer flow becomes one-sided.
- Vilkov's conditional results (net Sharpe ~0.4-0.9 for selected short-premium structures, negative for long directional spreads) are the only entry-time-fixed, cost-adjusted, out-of-sample numbers found; they point to short-premium/ratio structures, not long calls/puts.

### Gaps
- Vilkov's 13:00 and 15:00 entry results and the 2024-draft per-strategy tables are paywalled/403; the exact P&L of a 10:00 long straddle vs short straddle could not be quoted directly from the paper.
- The Brogaard/Han/Won paper itself (method, sample dates) was not retrievable; the 14% figure is from press coverage.
- No source gives 0DTE returns by holding period (e.g. 30-min, 1-hour, 2-hour holds) for long options; every academic study holds to expiry.

## Key question 2: What is the average P&L of buying (or selling) an ATM 0DTE SPX/SPY straddle at 09:35, 10:30, 13:00 and holding to the close?

### Takeaway
No source reports a straddle P&L table at those exact times. The best available evidence is for a 10:00 entry: SPY ATM straddle implied moves over-forecast realized close-to-close moves by ~13% (median realized/implied 0.59 vs 0.67 if fair), the 1-sigma band contains the close ~71% of the time vs 68% fair, and Vilkov's SPX 10:00 short strangle/straddle rule earns ~1.2 bps of index per day net with a 72% hit rate only after conditional filtering. Practitioner backtests favor early-morning short-premium entries closed by midday or at 10-25% of max profit.

### Inferences
- A 10:00 long SPY straddle held to the close has lost on roughly 70% of sessions since mid-2022 (close inside implied band), with the small number of >2x-implied days (e.g. April 2025) providing the only offset; the short side's premium is in the low single bps of index per day, which is comparable to one SPY option tick on a ~$2-3 straddle.
- Because realized moves cluster in the first hour and the last half hour (U-shape, see Q3), a 09:35 straddle captures the opening-range move while paying the highest IV of the day; a 13:00 straddle buys the cheapest decay window but has the least realized variance left except the close. There is no published P&L for these entries; this is the first test to run (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No published or exchange study reports straddle P&L at 09:35, 10:30 or 13:00; the FlashAlpha study is Wednesday-only and SPY-only, Vilkov is SPX-only and 10:00-only in the retrievable material.
- No breakdown by weekday (Mon/Wed/Fri vs Tue/Thu) of implied vs realized moves for SPX/SPY 0DTE was found in any source.

## Key question 3: Which intraday seasonality effects (market intraday momentum, Bogousslavsky, Lou/Polk/Skouras) are robust after 2022, and what is the effect size per half-hour?

### Takeaway
Market intraday momentum (rest-of-day return predicts last-half-hour return) is the most robust index-level effect: R-squared ~2% on SPY, last-half-hour timing Sharpe ~1.7 across equity index futures 1974-2020, present only on days when option market makers are net short gamma, and re-confirmed through early 2024 in a net-of-cost SPY strategy (Sharpe 1.33, 2007-2024). The strong cross-sectional end-of-day reversal (3.8-6.9 bps/day, t>10) is a stock-level effect and does not apply to the index. Post-2022 SPY-specific half-hour tables are not published; opening-range breakouts on QQQ reproduce gross but not net of costs.

### Cited Findings

- Index-level overnight vs intraday: SPY 1993-2017 overnight advantage "not stable across arbitrarily set subperiods" — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=26654); Robot Wealth (data to May 2024) finds most SPY returns and most large negative returns still come overnight — [Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com/revisiting-overnight-vs-intraday-equity-returns/); over-intra outperformance in S&P futures 2000-2021 is "hard to trade because of the extreme turnover required" — [arXiv 2507.04481](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04481v1)
- Opening-range breakout (Zarattini & Aziz 2023) [working paper]: QQQ 5-minute ORB, Jan 2016-Feb 2023, stop at far side of first bar, 10R target, close at 16:00, 1% risk per trade, 4x max leverage, $0.0005/share commission, no spread/slippage — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy); hit rate ~24%, +0.13R per trade, ~33%/yr at 4x on QQQ per a blog summary; an independent 2015-2026 replication on five index CFDs reproduced the gross result on NQ (~23% hit, +0.131R) but found "no market is distinguishable from zero net of costs" — [MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- Simple SPY ORB [vendor backtest]: 2016-2019, 304 trades, 51% win rate, net loss ~4.5%, negative Sharpe — [QuantConnect search result](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_f6ae92d1dddc1879d137f5a4da8f835b.html)
- Overnight gap-fill statistics [vendor]: SPY since 1993, up-gaps of 0.1-0.25% fill same day 78% (n=1,204), 91% within five sessions; 1-2% up-gaps 26% same day — [thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/gap-analysis); 2017-2026 (~2,400 sessions) aggregate same-day fill 60-70%, 80-85% for gaps <0.25%, 30-50% for gaps >1%, VIX>30 roughly doubles fill rate for 0.5-1% gaps (33%→67%) — [StockWirex](https://stockwirex.com/?p=39795); 0.15% gap-down fills intraday 92%, 0.35% gap 69% — [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/sp-500-spy-es-gap-fill-strategy-and-statistics/)
- Intraday realized-volatility shape [peer-reviewed]: Todorov & Zhang estimate the deterministic intraday volatility component from SPX 0DTE/1DTE options sampled every 5 minutes 09:35-15:55 and find "the familiar U-shape with volatility being higher at market open and market close relative to the middle of the trading day," with a much higher 15:55 estimate in 2020 than the two prior years; the available 0DTE strike range "starts to shrink gradually around 14:30," mostly deep OTM puts — [Todorov & Zhang PDF](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/todorov/htm/papers/odp.pdf)
- Hourly SPX statistics (tastylive research summarized by StockWirex) [vendor]: first hour sets the day's high or low ~33% historically (~32% in the last two years); midday 11:30-12:30 CT shows the largest volatility increase vs 2012-2019 and more positive drift; morning more volatile than the close; average VIX 15.55 (2024), 18.93 (2025) — [StockWirex](https://stockwirex.com/?p=49715)
- Option expiration week effect [peer-reviewed]: Stivers & Sun (JBF 2013) find high S&P 100 stock returns in third-Friday weeks tied to delta-hedge rebalancing and falling IV; earlier draft: S&P 500 index expiration-week returns high in 1983-2008, not before 1983 — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v37y2013i11p4226-4240.html); [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=2463); practitioner follow-ups: long-only SPX in opex weeks ~2% CAGR invested ~18% of the time — [Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/the-options-expiration-week-effect-aa2); since 2021 SPX has performed poorly in expiration weeks, especially quarterly (small sample) — [Schaeffer's](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/analysis/2023/03/08/caution-potentially-volatile-week-ahead)
- VIX settlement days: VIX/VXX changes abnormally negative the day before VIX option expiration (56-57 of 77 OE-1 returns negative, 2009-2015) — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/vix-calendar-effects/); statistically and economically significant volume spikes in the OTM SPX options used in the VIX calculation at the exact settlement time (Griffin & Shams) — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/is-vix-index-manipulated/); practitioner note of weakness after monthly VIX futures expiration — [VolVibes](https://volvibes.substack.com/p/vix-piration)

### Inferences
- Per-half-hour effect sizes that survive after 2022: last-half-hour index momentum conditional on negative dealer gamma (order of 1-3 bps/day mean, Sharpe 1.3-1.7 before costs in futures), opening-gap reversal within 30 minutes for large gaps (practitioner), and the first hour setting the day's extreme ~1/3 of the time. The lunch window is the lowest-variance segment (U-shape), which is a theta-bleed window for long premium and the window practitioners use to close short premium.
- Because the end-of-day reversal is cross-sectional (stocks vs the market), it cannot be harvested with index options; the index-level last-half-hour effect is momentum, not reversal, and only when dealers are short gamma.
- The ORB evidence says the breakout signal has positive gross skew (24% hit rate, 10R targets) but is cost-fragile; translating it into 0DTE options adds a 5-13% premium spread, so the underlying (ES/SPY shares) is the cheaper vehicle for that signal.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study reports SPY half-hour-by-half-hour mean returns or momentum R-squared for 2022-2026 specifically; the post-2022 robustness evidence is the Zarattini et al. net-of-cost SPY strategy (to April 2024) and the COVID-period replication.
- No index-level "lunch reversal" effect size was found in any source for the post-2022 period.
- No study separates SPX Monday/Wednesday/Friday (and Tue/Thu) expiration days by realized vs implied move or by last-half-hour behaviour.

## Key question 4: What does the evidence say about trailing stops vs fixed holds for positive-skew instruments, and are "breakeven after +X%" rules documented to help or hurt?

### Takeaway
Theory (Kaminski & Lo) says stops subtract expected return under a random walk or mean reversion and add value only under positive autocorrelation; empirically, stops help in momentum portfolios (Han/Zhou/Zhu) and trailing beats fixed stops in one Swedish stock study. For 0DTE options the only exit evidence is practitioner: profit targets of 10-25% beat hold-to-expiry for short premium, and the profitable retail cohort uses a noon time-stop. No academic or vendor study of breakeven stops on long options was found; the intraday index evidence (momentum only on short-gamma days, reversal otherwise, U-shaped variance) implies a breakeven stop will convert many +25% excursions into scratches on positive-gamma/lunch-window days.

### Cited Findings
- Kaminski & Lo, "When Do Stop-Loss Rules Stop Losses?" [peer-reviewed, Journal of Financial Markets 2014; MIT working paper 2007]: defines the stopping premium (probability of being stopped out times the gap between the stop-out asset return and the strategy return while stopped out); under the Random Walk Hypothesis simple 0/1 stop-loss rules "always decrease a strategy's expected return"; under mean reversion stops reduce expected return; with momentum (positive serial correlation) stops can add value; in regime-switching models they add value only if the low-mean-regime return is below the risk-free rate; empirically (US equities 1950-2004, stop-out into long-term bonds) certain rules add ~50-100 bps/month during stop-out periods — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876); [SIFR abstract](https://swopec.hhs.se/sifrwp/abs/sifrwp0063.htm); [BSIC review](https://bsic.it/how-to-define-a-stop-loss/)
- Han, Zhou & Zhu, "Taming Momentum Crashes: A Simple Stop-Loss Strategy" [working paper 2014/2016]: 10% stop-loss on US momentum portfolios 1926-2011/2013 cuts the worst month from about -49.8% to about -11.3% (equal-weighted) and from about -65% to about -24% (value-weighted); average returns and Sharpe ratios more than double; reviewers caution that extra trading costs could erase much of the gain — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/); [BSIC](https://bsic.it/how-to-define-a-stop-loss/)
- Snorrason & Yusupov [thesis, Lund 2009]: OMX Stockholm 30 stocks 1998-2009, three-month holding periods; both traditional and trailing stops beat buy-and-hold on expected and risk-adjusted returns; trailing stops outperform fixed stops at almost all stop levels — [Lund University](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/publication/1474565); [BSIC](https://bsic.it/how-to-define-a-stop-loss/)
- Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon [working paper]: the net-Sharpe-1.33 SPY intraday momentum result uses a dynamic trailing stop (opposite noise band or VWAP, checked each half hour) plus a mandatory close-of-day exit — [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=45565); the authors state they "add dynamic trailing stops to limit downside while leaving upside open" — [SFI listing](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)
- Short-premium 0DTE management [vendor]: 10% profit target 78% win rate vs 20% target 61% on $20-wing SPX iron flies (2 years) — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/zero-dte-winners-and-losers-for-iron-flies-08-07-2025); 10% targets beat hold-to-expiry over ~2.5 years — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/the-liz-and-jny-show/episodes/0-dte-spx-winning-streaks-outperform-losing-ones-10-01-2025); 25% target 89% win vs 50% target 80% (2-3 weeks) — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/zero-dte-weekly-breakdown-12-08-2025)
- Profitable 0DTE retail cohort exits [vendor]: 15% profit target, -25% stop, exit by 12:00 ET, ~2-hour hold — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- SpotGamma risk framing [vendor, no data]: "if the thesis hasn't played out by 2:00 PM ET, risk-to-reward shifts heavily toward the house"; traders "often close 'zombie' positions before the final 90 minutes" — [SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/?p=19793)
- Breakeven stop evidence: forum and vendor material only; a BabyPips thread asking for studies found none and one reply argued there are "too many variables"; a NexusFi post argues breakeven moves "have no market rationale" — [BabyPips](https://forums.babypips.com/t/statistics-of-setting-break-even-vs-letting-run/126971); [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=355842)
- Trailing stop measurement pitfall for options: a 10% trailing stop measured against the current bid rather than open profit can close a trade at a loss after a small rebound — [useThinkScript thread](https://usethinkscript.com/threads/trailing-stop-loss-doomed-to-fail.17332/post-134881)
- Intraday serial correlation facts that govern stop value: index last-half-hour momentum present only on negative-NGE days and absent on positive-NGE days — [Baltussen et al. 2021](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); higher dealer net gamma predicts stronger order-flow reversals and more negative high-frequency momentum over ~1 hour — [Adams et al. via Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)

### Inferences
- Applying Kaminski & Lo to a long 0DTE option: the option's P&L path inherits the underlying's intraday autocorrelation. On positive-gamma days (the majority: 3,158 of ~6,100 days in Baltussen's proxy) the index mean-reverts at high frequency, so a stop or breakeven lock is expected to reduce the mean (it harvests the reversal into a zero/negative exit). On negative-gamma trending days a trailing stop keeps the convexity and is expected to add value. A single static exit rule across regimes should therefore underperform a gamma-regime-conditional rule.
- For positive-skew payoffs, a breakeven stop is a short put on the path: it caps the right tail by nothing but removes the small set of outcomes where the trade first dips and then runs. With hit rates of ~24% (ORB) to ~30% (straddle breach), the expected value lives in the tail; a breakeven lock that triggers on +25% excursions (which are common given 0DTE gamma) will raise the win rate and lower expectancy unless the excursion is itself informative of continuation.
- The practitioner evidence is uniform that short-premium 0DTE should be closed early (10-25% of max profit, or by midday); this is the mirror image of the long-premium problem: the same early-afternoon window is where a long option's remaining variance is lowest and its theta highest.

### Gaps
- No academic or vendor backtest of breakeven stops, trailing stops or partial profit-taking on long 0DTE (or any short-dated) options was found; this must be measured on Slayer Terminal's own 1-minute option bars.
- The Kaminski & Lo full text was rate-limited (HTTP 429); the trailing-vs-fixed detail comes from the BSIC review and the Lund thesis, not the paper.

## Key question 5: What is documented about 15:30-16:00 behaviour (MOC imbalances, gamma unwind) that a "MOC wick" or power-hour lotto relies on, and is there evidence?

### Takeaway
Documented facts: the closing auction is ~7.5% of daily volume (2018), closing prices deviate from the 16:00 midquote by ~8 bps on average (3.6 bps for SPY/QQQ) and that deviation reverts half shortly after the close and fully overnight; last-minute NYSE imbalances cause larger auction-price reversals than on Nasdaq; index last-half-hour returns continue the day's direction only when dealers are net short gamma (median 15:30 net 0DTE gamma is +$173mm, i.e. usually long); and 0DTE strike coverage thins from ~14:30. The "gamma unwind at 16:00 produces a wick" narrative is vendor/working-paper commentary without published effect sizes; the peer-reviewed evidence on options pinning for SPY finds none since 2016 and instead ~16% wider ranges on high-ATM-open-interest days.

### Cited Findings
- Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, "Who Trades at the Close?" [working paper, Dec 2020; earlier title "Should We Use Closing Prices?"]: closing auction 7.48% of aggregate dollar volume in 2018 vs 3.11% in 2010 (0.49% in Smith 2006); $15.2bn/day traded in the auction; closing prices deviate from the 16:00 midquote by 8.12 bps on average (20.6 bps small stocks, 2.66 bps large), >0.63% on 1% of stock-days; large ETFs (SPY, QQQ, S&P sectors) average 3.63 bps deviation, 99th percentile 16.32 bps; deviations "revert by half shortly after the close and fully overnight," one-third to one-half of the reversal within the first half hour after the close; reversal complete for large stocks even after the half-spread; ETF/passive ownership drives auction volume; they cite Wu & Jegadeesh (2020) that reversal strategies based on market-on-close imbalances are profitable; auction imbalance information contributes to price discovery for small stocks, only weakly for large — [AEA preliminary paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)
- Hu & Murphy, "Vestigial Tails? Floor Brokers at the Close" [peer-reviewed, Management Science]: NYSE closing-auction price changes reverse more than Nasdaq's; larger last-minute abnormal imbalances explain the stronger reversals; NYSE allows late orders via floor brokers (d-Quotes) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3600230)
- Cushing & Madhavan (Journal of Trading 2001, 1997-98 NYSE MOC data) [peer-reviewed]: the final five minutes explain a disproportionate share of daily return variance and reversals follow imbalance publications, especially on index-expiration days — [IIJ listing](https://guides.iijournals.com/content/iijtrading/2001/1/12)
- NYSE mechanics and impact [exchange research]: MOC/LOC cutoff 15:50, d-Quotes modifiable to 15:59:50; large auction orders entered in the last five minutes have limited impact on standard days, rising when entered very late — [NYSE Data Insights](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights-blog/closing-auction-order-impact-9rbi8f9wcu)
- Cboe 0DTE dealer gamma into the close [exchange research, Sept 2023, ~1 year of 30-minute data]: net market-maker 0DTE gamma averages 0.04%-0.17% of daily S&P futures liquidity ($170mm-$670mm); interquartile range at 15:30 -$1.1bn to +$2.4bn with median +$173mm; whisker range -$5bn to +$7.7bn = 1.3%-1.9% of daily futures notional; customer flow "remarkably balanced" (15 Aug 2023 4440 put: 52k bought vs 55k sold, dealers ~3k short = 3% of gross) — [Cboe Volatility Insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options/); May 2025 update: net gamma hedging "at best 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity" — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)
- Baltussen et al. 2021 [peer-reviewed]: last-30-minute index return continues the rest-of-day return only on negative-net-gamma days; no significant momentum on positive-NGE days; LETF rebalancing historically 16.8% (50.2%) of last-half-hour volume on cited estimates — [paper PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Adams et al. [working paper]: higher dealer net gamma predicts lower volatility over the next 10 minutes and stronger E-mini order-flow reversals, fading after ~1 hour — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)
- Baltussen, Da & Soebhag [working paper]: the 15:30-16:00 cross-sectional reversal (3.8-6.9 bps/day) comes mainly from intraday losers bouncing and is not explained by stock, index or LETF gamma hedging; it survives controlling for hedging demand and is present in non-optioned and non-index stocks — [paper PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)
- Elms, "From Pinning to Amplification" [working paper, SSRN 6564078, 2026]: 1.6m near-expiry SPY contracts, 2,294 trading days 2016-2025; "no evidence of options pinning" across five tests; high near-expiry ATM open interest associated with ~16% wider daily ranges (p<0.001), framed as a regime shift from pinning (Golez & Jackwerth, pre-2009) to amplification — [Harbourfront summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/from-pinning-to-amplification-evidence)
- Todorov & Zhang [peer-reviewed]: 0DTE OTM strike availability shrinks from ~14:30, mostly deep OTM puts; 15:55 volatility estimate much higher in 2020 — [paper PDF](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/todorov/htm/papers/odp.pdf)
- Iannucci (Stratos Research), "Zero Days to Expiration: How they are reshaping equity markets" [vendor working paper, May 2026, no data tables]: describes a "3:30 PM effect" where market quality deteriorates in the final 30 minutes, ES volume peaks at 16:00 when SPX options settle and hedges are unwound; forced unwind volume ≈ net gamma × final-hour drift × multiplier; attributes sharp acceleration or reversal into the close to short-gamma unwinds; cites Baltussen et al. as the empirical support — [PDF](https://fyuvmxmqdvuplkmvwixh.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/article-pdfs/9e5879ad-db85-421c-849a-8261a033e77a/1779011027297-uzckzg.pdf)
- RTGamma [vendor, no sample stated]: negative open gamma correlates with higher first-hour true range; the correlation between open gamma and realized volatility "drops significantly" after midday; two case studies only — [RTGamma](https://rtgamma.substack.com/p/open-gamma-vs-real-time-gamma-which)
- SpotGamma [vendor, no data]: 0DTE gamma "2-5x higher than weekly options, potentially 10x near the close"; recommends closing before the final 90 minutes — [SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/?p=19793)
- Cboe settlement facts: SPX 0DTE (PM-settled SPXW) settle on the 16:00 closing print; SPY/QQQ/IWM options are physically settled and trade to 16:15 ET — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/the-rise-of-spx-0dte-trading-analyzing-volume-trends)

### Inferences
- A "MOC wick" long-lotto has a documented mechanism only on the subset of days when dealers are net short 0DTE gamma and the day has trended (then last-half-hour momentum of a few bps and auction deviations of ~4 bps on SPY). On the median day (net long gamma, +$173mm at 15:30) the documented behaviour is reversal and volatility damping, so an unconditional power-hour lotto buys the most expensive gamma of the day into a mean-reverting window.
- The index-level reversal into the auction is ~3.6 bps for SPY with full overnight reversal; 0DTE options expire at 16:00 (SPX) or 16:15 (SPY), so the post-close reversal is not capturable by options, only by holding the ETF/futures through the auction.
- Because strike coverage and quote quality in 0DTEs degrade from ~14:30, late-day fills on long premium carry wider effective spreads than the 5-6% retail average measured over the whole day.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study quantifies SPX/SPY last-30-minute return or range conditional on 0DTE net gamma after May 2022; Baltussen's gamma proxy ends May 2020 and uses all listed options, not 0DTE.
- No study quantifies "wick" frequency (e.g. probability that the 15:30-16:00 range exceeds X times the implied move) or MOC-imbalance predictability for SPY itself post-2022.
- The Wu & Jegadeesh (2020) MOC-imbalance reversal paper was cited but not retrieved.

## Key question 6: What does this imply for a long-premium 0DTE strategy on SPY/QQQ/IWM/SPX versus a short-premium one, and which concrete tests should Slayer Terminal run?

### Takeaway
The evidence points to short-premium (conditional, early-entry, early-exit, ratio/strangle structures) or to the underlying (ES/SPY shares for intraday momentum and gap reversal) rather than to unconditional long premium. A long-premium 0DTE book can only be positive-expectancy if it is (a) conditioned on the negative-dealer-gamma/trending regime where last-half-hour momentum is documented, (b) entered where realized variance is concentrated (first hour, last half hour) rather than at lunch, and (c) exited by rules that preserve right-tail convexity (time stops and trailing on the underlying, not premium-based breakeven locks). Everything else in the literature says the long side pays ~60% of its loss in spread and the rest in VRP.

### Cited Findings
- Long side loses, short side earns, and ~60% of retail loss is cost — [WealthManagement summary of Beckmeyer et al.](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)
- 0DTE VRP real but tiny (≈0.001% of underlying 10:00-close) with ES1% 0.6-1.6%/day; conditional short-premium rules reach net Sharpe 0.4-0.9, long directional spreads negative — [Vilkov results summary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/docs/agent-context/results-summary.md)
- 0DTEs "expensive but not mispriced" after costs — [HKUST/Chong](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2026/04/are-0dte-options-mispriced)
- Index last-half-hour momentum Sharpe ~1.7 pre-cost, only on negative-gamma days, positive net Sharpe in ES at one-tick cost — [Baltussen et al. 2021](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- SPY intraday momentum with trailing stops: 19.6%/yr, Sharpe 1.33 net, 2007-2024 — [SFI/Zarattini et al.](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)
- Upside variance premium dominates intraday; VRP negatively predicts intraday returns; bound-violation profits dissipated after 2022 — [Almeida & Hizmeri](https://tinbergen.nl/event/2025/11/06/13089/0dte-asset-pricing)
- Profitable retail 0DTE cohort: 10:15 entry, noon exit, 15% target, -25% stop, Mon/Wed; Thu consistently negative — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- Implied move over-forecasts realized by ~13% on SPY Wednesdays 2022-2026, 1-sigma breach 26% — [FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study)
- Net dealer 0DTE gamma median +$173mm at 15:30, IQR -$1.1bn to +$2.4bn — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options/)

### Inferences
- Verdict: no documented unconditional long-premium edge; a small, cost-fragile, tail-heavy short-premium edge; the cleanest documented intraday edges (momentum, gap reversal) live in the underlying, where SPY/ES round-trip cost is ~1 bp vs ~5-13% of premium in 0DTE options.
- Concrete tests to run on Slayer Terminal data (1-minute underlying bars, 1-minute option bars with bid/ask, daily straddle-implied moves):
  1. Straddle P&L surface by entry time: for each of SPY/QQQ/IWM/SPX, buy and sell the ATM straddle at 09:35, 10:00, 10:30, 11:30, 13:00, 14:30, 15:30 at the ask (long) / bid (short), mark to the 16:00 (16:15 for ETFs) settlement; report mean, median, hit rate, ES1%, by weekday, by VIX tercile, and by sign of estimated dealer gamma; compare with the FlashAlpha 10:00 benchmark (median realized/implied 0.59, breach 26%).
  2. Realized-vs-implied by hour: compute the ratio of realized half-hour variance to the straddle-implied remaining variance at each half hour; the literature predicts a U-shape with the lunch ratio lowest and the 15:30 ratio highest only on negative-gamma days (Baltussen) and lower on positive-gamma days (Adams).
  3. Last-half-hour conditional momentum in the underlying: regress 15:30-16:00 return on prior-close-to-15:30 return, split by net-gamma sign and by weekday/expiration type (Mon/Wed/Fri vs Tue/Thu, opex, VIX-settlement Wednesdays); target R-squared ~0.02 and timing Sharpe >1 pre-cost as in Baltussen; then re-express the same signal as a 15:30 long 0DTE ATM/10-delta option bought at the ask and test whether the option version survives the spread.
  4. Opening-range tests: 5-minute and 30-minute ORB on SPY/QQQ at the underlying with Zarattini's rules (stop at far side, 10R target, EOD close) and with the noise-band trailing stop; benchmark hit rate ~24% and +0.13R; then the option version (buy the 09:35 breakout-direction 0DTE at ask, exit on underlying stop) to measure how much of the R-multiple the spread and theta consume.
  5. Gap-fill and gap-fade: condition on gap size buckets (<0.25%, 0.25-0.5%, 0.5-1%, >1%) and VIX; benchmark same-day fill 78% for 0.1-0.25% gaps and 26% for 1-2% gaps; test the 30-minute gap-reversal overlay (>2% gaps) from Concretum.
