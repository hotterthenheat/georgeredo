# Dealer positioning (GEX, vanna, charm, gamma flip, walls, pinning) as a short-horizon predictor for US index ETFs: evidence and testability with Unusual Whales data

Scope note: evidence tiers are labelled throughout as **[peer-reviewed]**, **[working paper]**, **[exchange research]**, **[vendor/practitioner]**. Anything whose sample ends before May 2022 pre-dates daily SPX expirations (Tue/Thu SPX added 2022; daily SPY/QQQ expirations in the same period) and is flagged **[pre-2022]**. Where I could not open a primary document (SSRN returned HTTP 403 for Dim/Eraker/Vilkov and Adams et al.; the Brogaard/Han/Won paper has no public listing I could find), the findings are reported from the sources named and marked as unverified against the original.

---

## Key question 1 — What do the named papers (and the exchange/vendor studies) actually find about the mechanism and its size?

### Takeaway
The best-identified evidence says dealer net gamma is (a) a statistically strong, economically modest *volatility* predictor (one s.d. of gamma imbalance ≈ 5–25 bps lower absolute daily return for stocks, >20 bps for indices; 2010–2020) and (b) a conditioner of *last-half-hour momentum* (the rest-of-day return carries ~6.6 % into the last 30 minutes on negative-gamma days vs ~0.8 % and insignificant on positive-gamma days; 1996–2020). The post-2022 0DTE literature finds market makers are, if anything, net *long* gamma on expiry days, that 0DTE gamma lowers intraday realized volatility over roughly a 10–60 minute horizon, and that the hedging flow is small relative to futures liquidity; the one study claiming 0DTE raises volatility (Brogaard/Han/Won) could not be verified from a primary source.

### Cited Findings

**Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum", JFE 142 (2021) 377–403 [peer-reviewed] [pre-2022]**
- Sample: intraday returns on 60+ futures (17 equity index, 16 bond, 21 commodity, 8 currency), December 1974–May 2020; the last-30-minute return (rLH) is positively predicted by the return from previous close to 30 minutes before close (rROD); "economically and statistically highly significant, and reverts over the next days" — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- S&P 500 cash index (Panel A, 16:00 close, April 1982–May 2020): βROD = 5.98 (×100, i.e., 6 % of the ROD return carries into the last 30 min), Newey-West t = 4.78, adj. R² = 3.28 %; for the last 15 minutes β = 2.83 (t = 3.99), last 5 minutes β = 1.94 (t = 5.03). For S&P futures with the 16:15 close, the last-30-min β = 3.28 (t = 5.09) but last-15 and last-5-minute coefficients are insignificant (−0.37, −0.28), which the authors read as hedgers completing hedges before the 4:00 pm cash close — [Baltussen et al. PDF, Table 12](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Dealer-gamma proxy (NGE) is OI-based and assumes "(ii) call options are sold by investors, and bought by option market makers, (iii) put options are bought by investors and sold by option market makers, and (iv) option market makers hedge precisely their option deltas"; NGE = Σ Γ·OI·100·P for calls + Σ Γ·OI·(−100)·P for puts, divided by S&P 500 market value; computed from OptionMetrics 1996–2017 and extended with SqueezeMetrics data to May 2020 ("We have verified the computations of SqueezeMetrics using OptionMetrics data over the 2002 to 2017 period, yielding virtually identical results"); 2,930 negative-NGE days vs 3,158 positive — [Baltussen et al. PDF, §4.1](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Conditional result (Table 7, Jan 1996–May 2020, S&P 500 futures): on NGE ≥ 0 days βROD = 0.82 (t = 1.03), R² = 0.05 %; on NGE < 0 days βROD = 6.63 (t = 4.78), R² = 3.58 %. Table 8 interaction: I(NGE≤0)·rROD = 5.05 (t = 3.04) on top of rROD = 1.41 (t = 1.72); continuous NGE·rROD = −123.04 (t = −3.42); R² up to 4.63 % — [Baltussen et al. PDF, Tables 7–8](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Reversal: next-day return on today's rLH for equity index futures = −14.51 (t = −1.70), two-day −29.05 (t = −3.16), three-day −27.98 (t = −2.61) — "consistent with the price pressure caused by market makers' hedging activities" — [Baltussen et al. PDF, Table 11](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Costs: "we do not consider transaction costs... the strategy as presented might not be exploitable to many investors after accounting for transaction costs"; asset-class Sharpe ratios 0.87–1.73 gross; "exploiting the effect in the S&P 500 futures yields a positive net Sharpe ratio when we assume transaction cost equal to a tick"; effect "especially pronounced" in Feb–May 2020 — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- The paper also attributes part of the effect to leveraged-ETF rebalancing (t-stat of βROD rises with LETF market share, Fig. 4) — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)

**Barbon & Buraschi, "Gamma Fragility" (working paper, first version Jan 2019, this version 18 Mar 2021) [working paper] [pre-2022]**
- Data: options trade data by participant category from three Nasdaq option exchanges (10–20 % of worldwide option dollar volume on US equities), Jan 2010–May 2020; OptionMetrics greeks/OI; CRSP/TAQ; final panel 2,700 stocks + 4 indices (S&P 500, Dow Jones, Nasdaq 100, Russell 2000) — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Stock-level proxy is *trade-side tagged*: "Hedgers Inventory = BrokerDealers Inventory − Customers Inventory" following Ni, Pearson, Poteshman & White; gamma-weighted and scaled by price/ADV (ΓIB = dollar hedging flow for a 1 % move as % of average daily share volume). For *indices* the exchange data has no index options, so they "fall back to" the OI assumption "that most investors have short positions on calls and long positions on puts (collar strategy)... broker/dealers are mostly long call options and short put options", ΓIB1 = (Γ$Call − Γ$Put)/ADV × 100, citing Garleanu, Pedersen & Poteshman (2008) — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF, §II.B and eq. 4](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Volatility (Table II, 3,666,122 stock-days): |daily return| on lagged ΓIB, controlling for lagged IV: −5.07 (t = −4.04) with no FE to −24.8 (t = −16.6) with time FE; "one standard deviation in the gamma imbalance is associated to a decrease between 5 and 25 basis points in the absolute value of the daily return... absolute daily returns average at 180 basis points"; 70 % of stock-level time-series t-stats are negative — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Indices (Table III, 21,434 index-days, OI-based proxy): coefficients −14.79 (t = −4.42) to −5.49 (t = −3.33); "one standard deviation increase in the gamma imbalance is associated to a decrease in absolute return of the underlying index of more than 20 basis points (about 20 % of a standard deviation)"; survives adding 1–3-day lagged |returns| (Table IV) — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Intraday autocorrelation (Table VI): the day's sample autocorrelation of h-minute returns (h = 5, 10, 20, 30, 60) regressed on lagged ΓIB is "negative and significant for all the frequencies"; "A unitary standard deviation increase in the gamma imbalance proxy is associated to a decrease in autocorrelation between 1.5 % and 2 %"; effect largest at h = 5 and h = 60 — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF, §III.C](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Unconditionally, 5-minute autocorrelation averages zero; 13 % (8 %) of stock-days have autocorrelation > +0.20 (< −0.20) — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Flash crashes (1997–2015, 10,572 events, mean 30-min drop −2.06 %): one s.d. lower ΓIB raises flash-crash probability "by roughly 5 % relative" and is "associated to a 20 to 200 basis point larger price drop"; authors say they "do not interpret these results as evidence that delta-hedging of option dealers can cause flash crashes" — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF, §IV](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Status: listed as a working paper, last revision 2021-03-19; no trading-strategy or cost analysis — [abarbon.com paper page](https://abarbon.com/papers/gamma-fragility)

**Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi, Moerke, "Liquidity Provision to Leveraged ETFs and Equity Options Rebalancing Flows: Evidence from End-of-Day Stock Prices" [working paper] [pre-2022 sample]**
- "a large and negative (positive) aggregated gamma imbalance, relative to the average dollar volume, gives rise to an economically and statistically significant end-of-day momentum (reversal)"; LETF effect "economically larger"; effects "quickly revert at the next day's open"; 2022 revision: "gamma effects are persistent throughout our sample, while LETFs effects have decreased over time" — [SSRN 3925725](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3925725); [SFI 22-40](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-22-40-liquidity-provision-to-leveraged-etfs-and-equity-options-rebalancing-flows-evidence-from-end-of-day-stock-prices); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=75929)

**Pearson, Poteshman, White / Ni, Pearson, Poteshman, White, "Does Option Trading Have a Pervasive Impact on Underlying Stock Prices?" [peer-reviewed, RFS 34(4) 1952–1986] [pre-2022]**
- Finds "a significant negative relationship between stock return volatility and the net purchased option positions of likely hedgers" (i.e., when hedgers are net long options → lower volatility) — [2006 draft](https://ou.edu/dam/price/Finance/CFS/paper/pdf/pearsonPoteshmanWhite.pdf); publication record — [Pearson faculty page](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/profile/neil-pearson)

**SqueezeMetrics, "Gamma Exposure (GEX): Quantifying hedge rebalancing in SPX options" (March 2016, revised Dec 2017; Prior Analytics LLC) [vendor]**
- Four assumptions: all options are facilitated by delta hedgers; "Call options are sold by investors; bought by market-makers"; "Put options are bought by investors; sold by market-makers"; market makers hedge precisely to delta. GEX = Γ·OI·100 for calls, Γ·OI·(−100) for puts, summed over all strikes/expiries (dollar-denominated for SPX) — [GEX white paper (mirror)](https://slopeofhope.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/GEX-white_paper.pdf)
- Only empirical claim: next-day SPX return standard deviation is 0.55 % after the highest GEX quartile vs 0.85 % after the second-highest, compared with 0.51 % vs 0.66 % for the lowest vs second-lowest VIX quartiles; "exponential increase in volatility as GEX trends below zero". No sample period, no regression statistics, no out-of-sample test; the paper also asserts a near-zero-GEX stock screen "yielded excess returns" without numbers — [GEX white paper (mirror)](https://slopeofhope.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/GEX-white_paper.pdf)

**Cboe, Mandy Xu, "Much Ado About 0DTEs" (7–8 Sept 2023) [exchange research]**
- Cboe sees "for every transaction whether it's customer or market maker, buy or sell, opening or closing" and tracks MM net position per strike; customer flow is "remarkably balanced" (e.g., 15 Aug 2023 4440 put: 100k+ contracts traded, MMs net short only ~3k, "3 % of the gross volume") — [Cboe note](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options); [PDF mirror](https://spotgamma.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/cboeqqq.pdf)
- SPX 0DTE MM net gamma over the prior year in 30-min buckets: average $170 mm–$670 mm per 1 % move = 0.04–0.17 % of ~$400 bn/day S&P futures liquidity; at 3:30 pm median +$173 mm, IQR −$1.1 bn to +$2.4 bn, whiskers −$5 bn to +$7.7 bn (1.3–1.9 % of futures notional); outside estimates of positioning ranged "from 'record short' to long $50bn gamma" — [Cboe note](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- No change in close-to-close minus intraday (Garman-Klass) realized-vol spread (2.7 vol pts YTD vs 10-year average) and no increase in 2-sigma 1-minute moves (vs preceding 30-min RV), including the last hour; on 15 Aug 2023 MMs were ~+$2 bn gamma when the 3:00–3:30 pm selloff (−0.4 %) started and only −$500 mm short by 3:30 pm when the index had stabilised — [Cboe note](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- Caveat stated by Cboe: the analysis covers SPX 0DTE only; "Market makers may have offsetting positions in other expiries, or other equivalent products (e.g. E-mini or SPY 0DTE options)" — [Cboe note](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)

**Cboe, Mandy Xu, "0DTEs Decoded" (2 May 2025) [exchange research]**
- SPX 0DTE ~2 M contracts/day; retail ~50–60 % of 0DTE volume (dipped to 47 % in the April 2025 vol spike); >95 % of 0DTE trades are limited-risk structures (long options or spreads), ~4 % naked short; customer activity "extremely balanced"; net MM gamma hedging "de minimis... at best, just 0.2 % of the SPX daily liquidity" — [Cboe 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)

**Dim, Eraker, Vilkov, "0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation" (SSRN 4692190, 2024) [working paper] — primary not retrievable (SSRN 403); reported from the Oxford seminar abstract and two Harbourfront summaries**
- "High open-interest gamma in 0DTEs does not propagate past volatility"; "Intraday 0DTE volume shocks do not amplify recent past index returns"; 0DTE and underlying markets have become more integrated with "a marginally stronger link" between index volatility and 0DTE trading — [Oxford seminar abstract](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/node/67396)
- Secondary summaries add: higher 0DTE/short-dated OI gamma "is associated with lower realized volatility within the day" and does not spill into overnight or lagged intraday volatility; the later-vs-earlier-sample difference in the return response to 0DTE volume is ~0.1 s.d. of absolute returns; 0DTEs carry the largest volatility risk premium — [Harbourfront (1)](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/impact-of-zero-dte-options-on-the); [Harbourfront (2)](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-0dte-options-cause-for)

**Adams et al., "Do S&P500 Options Increase Market Volatility? Evidence from 0DTEs" (SSRN 5641974, Oct 2025) [working paper] — primary not retrievable; reported from Quantpedia's summary**
- Uses the Ni/Pearson/Poteshman/White hedging-needs measure (fraction of S&P 500 shares MMs must buy to hedge a 1 % decline); identification from pre-May-2022 Tue/Thu-only 0DTE availability; SPX volatility ~60 annualized bps lower on 0DTE days pre-2022 (gap disappears once 0DTEs trade daily); MM hedging needs ~1 % baseline, +0.15 pp on 0DTE days; two-stage multiplier ≈ −4 (1 bp more hedging need → 4 bp lower RV); pre-existing positions that *become* 0DTE add ~24 bps of hedging need on expiry day vs ~3 bps from positions opened that day — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)
- Intraday: "Higher net gamma predicts lower S&P500 volatility over the next 10 minutes. This effect fades after about one hour"; higher net gamma predicts stronger *reversals* in E-mini order flow and "negative momentum in high-frequency returns"; MMs "keep a positive portfolio net gamma... throughout the expiration day"; "Hedging a positive-net-gamma portfolio at high frequency can be profitable on average when transaction costs are low" — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)

**Brogaard, Han, Won, "How Does Zero-Day-to-Expiry Options Trading Affect the Volatility of Underlying Assets?" (Univ. of Utah) [working paper] — no primary listing found**
- Press coverage: a one-s.d. increase in 0DTE volume is associated with "a jump of almost 14 % in the S&P 500's daily volatility"; effect shows up intraday (5-minute intervals over the past decade, variance-ratio test) while day-to-day movement changes little — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/zero-day-options-are-reordering-the-way-the-stock-market-behaves-1.1921930) (article body not retrievable; figures from search snippets, unverified)

**Beckmeyer, Branger, Gayda, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?" (SSRN 4404704, 2023) [working paper]**
- Cboe SPX trade data with trader-type codes, Jan 2021–Feb 2023; retail lost ~$184k/day Feb 2021–Feb 2023 and ~$358k/day after daily expirations (May 2022); >75 % of retail SPX volume is 0DTE; retail groups "buy and sell options with roughly equal frequency, slightly favor puts over calls, and focus on at-the-money and slightly out-of-the-money 0DTE options with 3–24 hours to maturity" — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634); [Bonus.com](https://www.bonus.com/news/zero-days-to-expiration-options-investment-or-gambling/); [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance); [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@y125789/retail-traders-love-0dte-options-paper-summary-6da4a7ff7d72)

**Vilkov, "0DTE Trading Rules" (SSRN 4641356) [working paper]**
- S&P 500 index options Sept 2016–11 Jan 2024; static daily rules held to expiry (e.g., buying deep-ITM calls, selling OTM calls/puts); "some trades look profitable at the median, but the realized return distributions are very wide, and the average returns of most strategies are not statistically significant"; a same-day variance risk premium exists; P&L largely explained by realized skewness — [Hull Tactical summary](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/)
- Related seminar abstract (Nov 2025): 0DTEs show "a high variance risk premium, which is mainly driven by compensation for upside risk" — [Tinbergen Institute](https://tinbergen.nl/event/2025/11/06/13089/0dte-asset-pricing); 0DTEs were ~60 % of SPX option volume in 2025 — [HKUST summary](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2026/04/are-0dte-options-mispriced)

### Inferences
- The mechanism is real but second-order: in the best peer-reviewed test the gamma sign explains ≤ 3.6 % of last-half-hour return variance, and the volatility effect is tens of bps per s.d. of exposure. Any 0DTE long-premium edge built on it must survive a signal-to-noise ratio that low.
- Post-2022 evidence (Cboe, Adams, Dim/Eraker/Vilkov) converges on dealers being *long* gamma on expiry days and on 0DTE gamma *compressing* 10–60-minute volatility — the opposite regime to the one that produced Baltussen's momentum. That favours short-premium or mean-reversion-in-the-underlying trades on days with positive dealer gamma, not long premium.
- The one dissenting study (Brogaard/Han/Won) is unverifiable; its 14 % figure concerns the volume-volatility link, not the sign of dealer gamma, so it does not directly contradict the gamma-sign results.

### Gaps
- Could not open the SSRN texts of Dim/Eraker/Vilkov (4692190) or Adams et al. (5641974); sample periods, data vendors and exact coefficients for those two are from secondary summaries only.
- No public primary source for Brogaard/Han/Won; the regression specification and sample are unknown.
- No peer-reviewed study tests dealer gamma on SPY/QQQ/IWM *ETF* option books specifically; Barbon's index results use SPX/DJX/NDX/RUT index options, Baltussen's use SPX.

## Key question 2 — Is there evidence that dealer-gamma sign predicts the next 15/30/60-minute return conditional on the past return (intraday momentum vs reversal)? What statistics were used?

### Takeaway
Yes, but at the half-hour-before-close horizon and only for the *negative*-gamma state: Baltussen et al. show rROD→rLH momentum of 6.63 (×100, t = 4.78, R² 3.58 %) when OI-based NGE < 0 and nothing (0.82, t = 1.03) when NGE ≥ 0; Barbon & Buraschi show the within-day autocorrelation of 5–60-minute returns falls 1.5–2 pp per s.d. of gamma imbalance; Adams et al. report higher net gamma predicting *negative* high-frequency momentum with a ~10-minute effect that fades within an hour. All results are regressions of later returns on earlier returns (or of daily autocorrelation coefficients) with Newey-West or double-clustered standard errors; none reports an options-based P&L.

### Cited Findings
- Specification used by Baltussen et al.: rLH,t = α + βROD·rROD,t + ε, estimated separately on NGE_{t−1} ≥ 0 and < 0 subsamples (Table 7) and pooled with I(NGE≤0)·rROD and NGE·rROD interactions (Table 8); Newey-West t-stats; sample Jan 1996–May 2020; unreported checks show the result also holds with rONFH (overnight + first half hour) and rM (first half hour to last half hour) as predictors, and "when only considering puts (and no calls)" — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Horizon detail for the cash S&P 500 (1982–2020): predictability exists for the last 30, 15 and 5 minutes before the 16:00 close (β = 5.98, 2.83, 1.94; t = 4.78, 3.99, 5.03) — [Baltussen et al. PDF, Table 12](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Specification used by Barbon & Buraschi: ρ^h_{j,t} = α_h + β^h_0·ΓIB_{j,t−1} + FE + ε for h = 5, 10, 20, 30, 60 minutes, where ρ^h is the day's sample autocorrelation of non-overlapping h-minute returns; standard errors double-clustered by asset and month; β negative and significant at every h, largest at h = 5 and h = 60 ("two frequency of portfolio rebalancing"); one s.d. of ΓIB → autocorrelation lower by 0.015–0.02 — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF, §III.C](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Adams et al. (per Quantpedia): higher MM net gamma predicts lower SPX volatility over the next 10 minutes, stronger E-mini order-flow reversals, and "negative momentum in high-frequency returns", effects fading after ~1 hour — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)
- Dim/Eraker/Vilkov (abstract): "Intraday 0DTE volume shocks do not amplify recent past index returns" — [Oxford seminar abstract](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/node/67396)
- End-of-day cross-section: Barbon/Beckmeyer/Buraschi/Moerke find negative (positive) stock-level gamma imbalance → end-of-day momentum (reversal), reverting at the next open — [SSRN 3925725](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3925725)
- Next-day return reversal of the last-half-hour move in equity futures: −14.51 (t = −1.70) one day, −29.05 (t = −3.16) two days — [Baltussen et al. PDF, Table 11](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)

### Inferences
- The documented conditional momentum is a *close-of-day* phenomenon tied to hedgers squaring up by 16:00; there is no published evidence of a 15/30/60-minute *mid-day* momentum conditional on gamma sign. For a 0DTE long-premium strategy, the actionable window is 15:30–16:00 on negative-net-gamma days, where the conditional expected move is ≈ 0.066 × rROD (e.g., ~6.6 bps for a 1 % rest-of-day move).
- The 2022+ regime evidence (long dealer gamma, reversal in order flow) means negative-gamma days are the minority state; the Baltussen effect may be rarer today than in its 1996–2020 sample (2,930 of 6,088 days negative).
- Statistically, the right test is Baltussen's split-sample / interaction OLS with Newey-West SEs plus Barbon's day-level autocorrelation panel; both are directly reproducible with 1-minute SPY/QQQ/IWM bars.

### Gaps
- No study reports the conditional momentum at 15/30/60-minute horizons *away from the close* for index products; the Barbon autocorrelation result is pooled over the whole day and over stocks.
- No study reports hit rates or option-level P&L for a conditional-momentum trade.

## Key question 3 — Do "gamma flip", "call wall", "put wall", "gamma magnet"/pinning and OPEX effects have documented out-of-sample predictive value, or only descriptive value?

### Takeaway
Pinning toward strikes on expiration days is peer-reviewed (Ni/Pearson/Poteshman 2005; Golez & Jackwerth 2012) with modest magnitudes (≥ 16.5 bps return alteration per expiration for optionable stocks; ≥ $115 m notional futures shift per expiration), and an OPEX-week return effect exists for S&P 100 stocks (Stivers & Sun 2013), all pre-2022. "Gamma flip", "call wall" and "put wall" as vendor levels have *no* published out-of-sample test that I could find; the only independent backtests (FlashAlpha 2026 on SPY; Maurer 2026 on overnight gaps) test aggregate GEX, not the levels, and show the raw GEX–volatility relation is mostly a volatility-regime proxy that largely disappears after controlling for VIX/ATM IV.

### Cited Findings
- Ni, Pearson, Poteshman (JFE 78, 2005, 49–87) [peer-reviewed] [pre-2022]: closing prices of optionable stocks cluster at strikes on expiration dates; returns "altered by an average of at least 16.5 basis points" per expiration, ~$9 bn aggregate; attributed to "hedge rebalancing by option market makers and stock price manipulation by firm proprietary traders" — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v78y2005i1p49-87.html); [HKBU record](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/stock-price-clustering-on-option-expiration-dates-3/)
- Golez & Jackwerth (JFE 106, 2012, 566–585) [peer-reviewed] [pre-2022]: S&P 500 futures "gravitate toward the at-the-money strike" on serial-option expiration days, with "anti-cross-pinning" (repulsion from the cost-of-carry-adjusted ATM strike) just before SPX index-option expiry; mechanism is delta-hedge decay over time (i.e., charm) plus early exercise/resale of ITM options; notional shift "at least $115 million per expiration day"; pinning occurs only on expiration dates — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p566-585.html); [Konstanz WP record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/knz/dpteco/1012.html)
- Stivers & Sun (J. Banking & Finance 37, 2013, 4226–4240) [peer-reviewed] [pre-2022]: option-expiration (third-Friday) weeks show higher S&P 100 stock returns; earlier WP (1996–2008): 0.45 % average OE-week return vs 0.12 % other weeks; explanations include "delta-hedge rebalancing by option market makers, with a reduction in short-stock hedge positions over the OE week"; Quantpedia's long-only OE-week strategy Sharpe 0.61, max DD −15.1 % — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v37y2013i11p4226-4240.html); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/Screener/Details/102)
- SpotGamma's definitions [vendor]: Zero Gamma/Gamma Flip = "the spot price where aggregate GEX crosses zero", recalculated over hypothetical spots and dependent on inventory assumptions; Call Wall = strike with largest call gamma (resistance), Put Wall = largest put gamma (support); "Volatility Trigger" is proprietary and undisclosed; the page "says GEX does not predict market direction" and reports no backtests or hit rates — [SpotGamma, What is GEX](https://spotgamma.com/what-is-gex-gamma-exposure/)
- FlashAlpha (23 Apr 2026, practitioner; SPY 2018-04-16 to 2026-04-02, 1,972 EOD snapshots): Spearman ρ of GEX with next-day RV −0.36 raw (p 4.6e-60), −0.14 after VIX, −0.03 (p 0.18) after VIX + ATM IV; top-minus-bottom GEX quintile RV spread −10.63 vol pts raw (t = −13.0), −0.99 (p 0.25) after controls; in high-VIX days (n = 493) ρ = −0.02; DEX→next-day return ρ −0.03 (p 0.19); CHEX→return −0.05 raw, 0.00 after controls; gamma flip, walls, intraday momentum/reversal and pinning "not tested"; conclusion: GEX is "a regime descriptor, not a standalone forecasting signal" — [FlashAlpha backtest](https://flashalpha.com/articles/gex-dex-vex-chex-8-year-backtest-spy-vix-control)
- Maurer, "Dealer Gamma Exposure and Overnight Gap Risk" (May 2026, SSRN via OptionMetrics) [working paper]: dealer gamma in S&P 500 options improves overnight-gap-magnitude forecasts against a Corsi HAR baseline only in low-VIX regimes; pooling regimes "washes out the effect"; ~15 % lower calm-regime quantile loss at the 0.99 quantile; robust to "stress threshold, training cutoff, tenor, sign convention" and a Holm correction over 100 Clark-West tests — [OptionMetrics abstract page](https://optionmetrics.com/research/dealer-gamma-exposure-and-overnight-gap-risk-incremental-information-in-low-volatility-regimes/)
- Unusual Whales' own docs warn that on dense chains zero-gamma crossings "often cluster within a dollar or two, which means no single level is decisive" and that `gamma_flip` "is a cumulative zero-crossing that can be `null` or far from spot, so do not use it for regime detection" — [UW gex-levels docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-levels.md)
- Practitioner claims on vanna/charm ("primary drivers of the slow, grinding rallies and end-of-day pins", charm "the dominant force on expiration days") come only from vendor pages with no statistical test — [SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/vanna-and-charm-explained/); [FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/why-gex-isnt-enough-vanna-charm-exposure-predict-market-moves)

### Inferences
- Pinning/magnet behaviour is the best-supported *level* effect, and the mechanism (charm-driven delta decay into expiry) is the academic analogue of vendor "charm flows". With daily SPY/QQQ expirations, every session is an expiry day, so the Golez-Jackwerth pin should be testable daily — but their magnitude (tens of bps, concentrated in the last hour) is small relative to 0DTE option spreads.
- Gamma-flip/wall levels are descriptive constructs; their predictive content, if any, is unmeasured. Any test must beat a VIX/ATM-IV control, since FlashAlpha shows the raw GEX–RV link is mostly the IV regime.
- Maurer's result suggests dealer gamma matters most in calm regimes — which is also when 0DTE premium is cheapest, so a long-premium strategy would be buying cheap options precisely when gamma predicts *smaller* gaps.

### Gaps
- No published out-of-sample test of gamma flip, call wall or put wall for any index/ETF.
- No post-2022 academic pinning study for SPY/QQQ/IWM daily expirations was found.
- Tier1Alpha methodology pages returned no search results; its construction could not be described.

## Key question 4 — What are the documented problems with OI-based dealer-position estimates (customer vs dealer side, 0DTE flows not in OI, intraday changes, multi-leg)?

### Takeaway
Every OI-based GEX (SqueezeMetrics, Baltussen's NGE, Barbon's index proxy, UW's `oi` series) rests on the unverifiable "dealers long all calls, short all puts" assumption; Cboe's position-level data shows customer 0DTE flow is nearly balanced so that gross OI/volume vastly overstates net dealer gamma, and that vendor estimates ranged from "record short" to "+$50 bn"; OI is a once-a-day snapshot that misses same-day 0DTE flow (though Adams et al. find most expiry-day hedging need comes from pre-existing positions that are in OI); trade-side "dir" variants depend on a bid/ask quote rule that cannot identify customer vs dealer and ignores multi-leg structure.

### Cited Findings
- Baltussen et al. acknowledge: "we assume option market makers are short all puts and long all calls. We do not have data on, for example, OTC option positions and precise end-user option positions. This means that our sample misses many long puts and short calls typically done OTC by institutional investors"; NGE is "on average positive for most days" — [Baltussen et al. PDF, fn. 8](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Barbon & Buraschi use the OI assumption only where they lack participant-tagged data (indices) and call it "imperfect"; for stocks they use broker-dealer minus customer inventories from exchange trade categories — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Cboe: "What matters is the balance of the volume between buys vs. sells, not the total size of the volume"; "those are just estimates based on assumptions. Since 98 % of the volume in SPX 0DTE options are traded electronically, most outside observers have very little visibility"; customer flow is "remarkably balanced"; outside estimates ranged "from 'record short' to long $50bn gamma in SPX alone" — [Cboe note](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- Cboe: MMs "may also be hedging their 0DTE positions with other 0DTE options, rather than with linear instruments such as futures", and may hold offsetting positions in SPY 0DTE or E-mini options — [Cboe note](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- SpotGamma (vendor) lists the same caveats: "Open interest is a snapshot... published overnight and static during the session"; "Public OI does not identify holders"; "GEX is a model output, not an exchange statistic, so different methodologies produce different results"; 0DTE volume (59 % of 2025 SPX volume) "is not the same as net dealer exposure" — [SpotGamma, What is GEX](https://spotgamma.com/what-is-gex-gamma-exposure/)
- Adams et al. (per Quantpedia): on expiration day ~24 bps of hedging need comes from pre-existing longer-dated positions that have become 0DTE vs ~3 bps from new 0DTE positions opened that day — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)
- Unusual Whales' construction: GEX "assumes that market makers are part of every transaction and that the bulk of their transactions are buying calls and selling puts"; per-contract exposure = greek × 100 × 0.01 × price × (price for gamma), summed "determined by the daily open interest or by volume"; the daily `greek-exposure` series "updates once per day at market open" — [UW gex-exposures-per-1min docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-exposures-per-1min.md); [UW greek-exposure docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/greek-exposure.md)
- UW "dir" variant: "the bid/ask spread is used... When a trade is made closer to the ask, the Market Maker would be selling the contract and when a trade is closer to the bid then the Market Maker would be buying"; `call_gamma_ask` is negative because ask-side trades are assumed MM-short; a strike recalculation "with a negative call gamma or a positive put gamma is discarded"; a strike is recalculated only on new greeks/trade/OI load ("A price move alone does not recalculate a strike"), so strikes within one response "can differ in `time`, possibly by hours"; a past date rebuilt from end-of-day data "recalculates every strike at the close, while the per-minute values were recorded during the session" — [UW spot-exposures/strike docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-exposures-by-strike.md)
- UW `gex-levels` defaults to `source=vol` ("directionalized volume... the exposure dealers took on from the day's ask/bid sided flow, updated through the session") with `oi` as the alternative; the response carries a `time` of the snapshot — [UW gex-levels docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-levels.md)
- The per-strike `greek-exposure/strike` fields (`call_gex`, `call_charm`, `call_vanna`, `call_delta`, put equivalents) are documented as "The sum of the gamma [charm/delta] values of all call transactions that executed" — i.e., the field descriptions describe transaction sums, not OI — [UW greek-exposure-by-strike docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/greek-exposure-by-strike.md)
- Practitioner guides concur that "dealer positioning is assumed, not measured" and "identical open interest can produce opposite market behavior depending on a sign nobody publishes" — [Curved Trading](https://curvedtrading.com/articles/en/education/options-positioning-data/); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/gamma-exposure/)
- Vendor disagreement in practice: FlashAlpha advises "where vendors disagree materially, treat the zones as soft context rather than hard lines" — [FlashAlpha Q&A](https://flashalpha.com/articles/options-flow-questions-answered-dealer-positioning-qa)

### Inferences
- The "dir" quote-rule series is closer in spirit to Barbon's trade-tagged measure than the OI series, but it still cannot see who is on each side, treats mid-price prints as ambiguous, and will mis-sign spreads (the >95 % limited-risk structures Cboe reports) whose legs print on opposite sides.
- Because `oi` is as-of-open and most expiry-day hedging need is in pre-existing OI (Adams), the OI series may be *less* wrong on expiry days than the "0DTE is not in OI" critique suggests — this is itself testable by comparing `oi` vs `vol`/`dir` explanatory power.
- Per-strike intraday history rebuilt at the close is not usable for intraday as-of tests; only the per-1-minute ticker-level series records true as-of values.

### Gaps
- UW does not document how `gex-levels` for a past `date=` chooses the snapshot (final of day vs as-of a time); this creates look-ahead risk that must be checked empirically before any level-based backtest.
- No independent audit of UW's directionalization accuracy versus exchange-tagged data was found.

## Key question 5 — Which concrete, falsifiable tests can we run with daily `gex-levels`, daily `greek-exposure/strike`, 1-minute `spot-exposures` and 1-minute SPY/QQQ/IWM bars, and what would count as an edge after a 1–2 % half-spread on a ~$1 premium?

### Takeaway
Seven tests map directly onto the literature; the strongest priors are (i) Baltussen's last-30-minute momentum on negative-gamma days, (ii) Barbon/Adams-style volatility and autocorrelation compression on positive-gamma days (10–60-minute horizon), and (iii) Golez-Jackwerth pinning into the 16:00 close. Each should be run with the `oi`, `vol` and `dir` variants side by side, with VIX/ATM-IV controls, Newey-West or clustered SEs, and a pre-registered out-of-sample split. The bar for a long-premium 0DTE edge is high: the conditional expected underlying move must exceed the option's time decay over the holding window plus 2–4 % of premium round-trip, which the published effect sizes (≈ 6.6 bps per 1 % rest-of-day move; ≤ 25 bps per s.d. of gamma in |daily return|) do not obviously clear, whereas the same evidence maps cleanly onto short-premium or underlying-only trades.

### Cited Findings (what the data can and cannot support)
- `spot-exposures` returns, per minute during the cash session, `gamma_per_one_percent_move_{oi,vol,dir}`, `vanna_...`, `charm_...`, `price`, `time`; per-strike equivalents exist via `spot-exposures/strike` (500 strikes/page) — [UW gex-exposures-per-1min docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-exposures-per-1min.md); [UW spot-exposures/strike docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-exposures-by-strike.md)
- `gex-levels` returns `call_wall`, `put_wall`, `gamma_magnet`, `gamma_flip`, `nearby_flips` (≤ 5), `source`, `date`, `time`; UW recommends taking the regime from the sign of `call_gamma + put_gamma` in `greek-exposure`, not from `gamma_flip` — [UW gex-levels docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-levels.md)
- For Cboe index tickers (SPX) the index price is returned as `null` ("missing_periscope") without provisioned access, consistent with the constraint that we have no SPX index bars — [UW gex-levels docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-levels.md)
- Historical depth may be limited: the error code `historic_data_access_missing` "means a date in the request predates the earliest date the token may query", and the self-serve Startup tier advertises a 90-day lookback — [UW gex-levels docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-levels.md)
- Cboe's measured SPX 0DTE MM gamma is small (0.04–0.17 % of futures liquidity on average), and MMs may offset in SPY 0DTE — so ETF-level signals may be contaminated by cross-product hedging — [Cboe note](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- Baltussen's cash-index effect sizes (β = 5.98 for last 30 min; 1.94 for last 5 min) and conditional sizes (6.63 on NGE < 0, 0.82 on NGE ≥ 0) are the benchmarks any SPY replication should be compared with — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Barbon's benchmark: 1 s.d. of gamma imbalance → −1.5 to −2 pp in daily autocorrelation of 5–60-minute returns and −20+ bps in |daily index return| — [Barbon & Buraschi PDF](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)
- Adams' benchmark: higher net gamma → lower RV over the next 10 minutes, fading by ~60 minutes; MMs long gamma through expiry day; high-frequency hedging of long gamma profitable at low cost — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)
- FlashAlpha's benchmark for controls: GEX→next-day RV partial correlation falls from −0.36 to −0.03 once VIX and ATM IV are included — [FlashAlpha backtest](https://flashalpha.com/articles/gex-dex-vex-chex-8-year-backtest-spy-vix-control)
- Same-day variance risk premium in 0DTEs means long premium pays a structural cost ("implied volatility tends to exceed subsequent realized volatility even at same-day horizons"; most static rules not significant) — [Hull Tactical summary of Vilkov](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/); retail 0DTE buyers/sellers lost ~$358k/day on average after May 2022 — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634)

### Inferences — proposed tests (each falsifiable, each with the published prior)

1. **Conditional last-30-minute momentum (Baltussen replication).** For SPY/QQQ/IWM: rLH = r(15:30→16:00), rROD = r(prev 16:00→15:30). Regress rLH on rROD × I(net gamma < 0) using (a) `greek-exposure` OI sign as of open, (b) `spot-exposures` `oi`/`vol`/`dir` gamma at 15:30. Prior: β ≈ 0.05–0.07 (t > 3) on negative-gamma days, ≈ 0 otherwise. Falsified if the interaction t < 2 or the sign flips after 2022. Also test last-15 and last-5-minute windows (prior β ≈ 0.028, 0.019).
2. **Gamma-regime autocorrelation panel (Barbon replication).** Each day compute the autocorrelation of 5/10/15/30/60-minute returns from 1-minute bars; regress on prior-day net gamma (standardised), with ticker and month FE and VIX/ATM-IV controls. Prior: −1.5 to −2 pp per s.d. Falsified if the coefficient is ≥ 0 or loses significance after IV controls (FlashAlpha's pattern).
3. **Realized-range compression.** Regress daily log(high/low) and 1-minute realized variance (whole day; 10:00–15:30; 15:30–16:00) on morning OI gamma and on the 09:35 `vol`/`dir` gamma, controlling for VIX and ATM IV. Prior: −20 bps |return| per s.d. (Barbon index result); expect most of it to be absorbed by IV. Edge-relevant output: the *residual* RV vs the 0DTE straddle's implied move — a long-premium trade needs positive residual RV on identifiable days.
4. **10–60-minute horizon test (Adams replication).** With 1-minute `dir`/`vol` gamma as the state variable, regress next-10/30/60-minute realized variance and the product r(next 10 min)·r(last 10 min) on current gamma. Prior: higher gamma → lower RV and negative return momentum over 10 minutes, fading by 60. This is the most direct test of whether the *intraday* UW series carries information beyond the morning OI.
5. **Gamma-flip crossing event study.** Identify minutes where the 1-minute close crosses `gamma_flip` (and each `nearby_flips` entry), separately for `oi` and `vol` sources. Compare realized vol and |return| over the following 15/30/60 minutes with matched non-crossing minutes at the same time of day and same VIX decile. Prior: none published (descriptive only); UW itself warns flips cluster. Count it as information only if the vol expansion below the flip survives the match and both sources agree.
6. **Pin/magnet and wall tests (Golez-Jackwerth / Ni et al. analogue).** (a) Pin: distance of the 16:00 close from `gamma_magnet` (and from the nearest strike) versus a bootstrap benchmark drawn from the same day's 15:00–16:00 realized volatility; prior: measurable but small pull (tens of bps), concentrated in the last hour, possibly with Golez-Jackwerth's repulsion in the minutes before expiry. (b) Walls: conditional probability and size of 30-minute reversal when price is within 0.1 % of `call_wall`/`put_wall` versus elsewhere, matched by time of day. Check look-ahead: confirm what snapshot `gex-levels?date=` returns for past dates before using it.
7. **Charm/vanna end-of-day drift (practitioner claim, exploratory).** Regress r(15:00→16:00) on the sign/size of `charm_per_one_percent_move_{oi,vol,dir}` at 15:00, and on vanna × same-day ATM-IV change. Prior: vendor claims only; FlashAlpha found CHEX→next-day return not robust. Treat a result as real only if it survives VIX/IV controls and holds out of sample.

**Cost hurdle and what counts as an edge.** With a 1–2 % half-spread on ~$1 premium, a round trip costs 2–4 % of premium before theta. For an ATM 0DTE option at 15:30 (delta ≈ 0.5), Baltussen's conditional expectation of ≈ 6.6 bps of underlying per 1 % rest-of-day move translates to ≈ 3.3 bps of spot in option value, against (i) the option's remaining time decay over 30 minutes and (ii) the same-day variance risk premium documented by Vilkov and the Tinbergen abstract. The edge criterion should therefore be stated on the option, not the underlying: mean P&L per trade after a 4 % round-trip haircut > 0 with t > 2.5 in a held-out period, *and* the conditional realized variance over the holding window must exceed the straddle-implied variance (otherwise the long-gamma leg loses the VRP even when direction is right). Under the published effect sizes this is unlikely to be met by a pure long-premium implementation; the same signals are far better matched to (a) short-premium structures on positive-gamma days (Adams, Dim/Eraker/Vilkov, Cboe), or (b) a last-30-minute underlying/futures momentum trade on negative-gamma days, where Baltussen found a positive net Sharpe at one-tick costs.

**Data-adequacy caveats for the tests.** SPY is a proxy for SPX hedging flow and ETF-option dealer books may be hedged cross-product (Cboe); only the per-1-minute ticker series is true as-of (per-strike history is rebuilt at the close); the 90-day/`historic_data_access_missing` limit may cap the sample well below what is needed to detect a 1.5–2 pp autocorrelation shift (Barbon used 3.67 m stock-days; Baltussen used ~6,000 index days), so results on a few hundred ETF-days will have wide confidence intervals and multiple-testing control (Holm/Benjamini) must be applied across the seven tests and three variants.

### Gaps
- Whether `gex-levels?date=` and `spot-exposures?date=` return as-of or end-of-day snapshots for past dates is undocumented; must be verified empirically (e.g., compare the `time` field and intraday `price` against bars).
- How far back the account's token can query (`historic_data_access_missing`) is unknown to me; sample length determines whether tests 2–4 are powered.
- No source quantifies SPY/QQQ/IWM 0DTE bid-ask spreads intraday; the 1–2 % half-spread assumption should be validated from the 1-minute option-contract bars before setting the hurdle.
- No study gives the fraction of post-2022 days on which ETF-level dealer gamma is negative, which determines how often test 1's actionable state occurs.
