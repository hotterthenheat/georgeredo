All numbers below come from our own Unusual Whales 1-minute data and the code in `/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/` (`common.py`, `run_tests.py`, `summarize.py`, `t3_variants.py`; outputs in `csv/`, full tables in `summary.md`, ORB variants in `t3_variants.md`). The run reuses `/home/user/georgeredo/backtest/data.py` (client + cache), `exits.py` (unchanged `trail`/`hold` engines) and `costs.py` (half-spread and fee constants); nothing under `backtest/` or `evidence/` was modified. Re-run: `python3 -I run_tests.py && python3 -I summarize.py && python3 -I t3_variants.py` with `UW_API_TOKEN` and `BT_CACHE` set (about 2 minutes from the warm cache, about 12 minutes cold; 11,355 option contract-days, of which 5,261 were fetched fresh for this study).

## Common definitions, cost assumptions and statistics (apply to every test)

### Takeaway
339 full sessions x 3 tickers = 1,017 ticker-days (the three half-days 2025-07-03, 2025-11-28, 2025-12-24 are skipped). Every decision uses only bars that started before the decision minute; every fill is the volume-weighted price of the first option bar after the decision; P&L is for one contract per leg; CIs are date-cluster bootstraps.

### Cited Findings
- Sample: 342 trading days 2025-06-02..2026-10-09 from `/home/user/georgeredo/backtest/trading_days.json`, minus 3 half-days (210 RTH bars) = 339 sessions x SPY/QQQ/IWM = 1,017 ticker-days — [run_tests.log](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/run_tests.log)
- Look-ahead rule: a decision at minute T uses the close of the last underlying bar with start < T (`px_before`); the entry fill is the UW `avg_price` (volume-weighted) of the first option bar whose start minute is in [T, T+3), i.e. the bar that trades during the minute after the decision (same convention as `backtest/run.py`: signal bar closes at T, entry on the next bar) — [common.py](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/common.py)
- Combo stops (straddle/strangle) are evaluated once per minute on the combined volume-weighted mark of the two legs (forward-filled when a leg does not trade that minute) and are filled at the next bar's vw of each leg; the hard close is the vw of the first bar with start >= 15:55 (fallback: last traded price, flagged in the `ffill` column; 0 of 6,102 straddle decisions failed to fill both legs, 30,510 rows = 6,102 x 5 variants) — [common.py `sim_combo`](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/common.py); [t1_straddles.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/csv/t1_straddles.csv)
- Single long options (tests 3-5) are run through the unchanged `backtest/exits.py` `trail` engine (stop -25 %, breakeven lock after +6 %, guardian trail 35 % give-back after +15 %, 30-minute time stop when flat/losing, 15:55 close; stops evaluated on bar highs/lows and filled at the stop level) or `hold` — [exits.py](/home/user/georgeredo/backtest/exits.py); [common.py `sim_long`](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/common.py)
- Cost models, per contract per side: `mid` = vw fills, no costs; `spread` = half-spread max(1 % of premium for SPY/QQQ, 2 % for IWM, $0.01) plus $0.70 fees; `adverse` = fill at the bar LOW when selling and the bar HIGH when buying, plus $0.70. A straddle round trip is 4 contract-sides, so fees alone are $2.80 — [common.py](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/common.py); [costs.py](/home/user/georgeredo/backtest/costs.py)
- Implied mean round-trip cost of the spread model (mid P&L minus spread P&L): $11.31 per straddle at 09:35 (3.6 % of the $3.18 mean credit), $10.65 at 10:00 (3.7 % of $2.89), $8.33 at 13:00 (4.5 % of $1.84), $7.60 per 10:00 strangle (7.5 % of the $1.02 mean credit) — [summary.md, "Mean round-trip cost"](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/summary.md)
- Statistics: mean $ per trade, 95 % CI from a 5,000-draw bootstrap that resamples DATES (clusters), so same-day SPY/QQQ/IWM trades are not treated as independent; win rate; profit factor; max drawdown of the cumulative 1-lot $ P&L in chronological order; breakdowns by ticker, weekday, month and tercile of the PRIOR session's UW 1-day IV percentile (terciles cut at 0.25/0.58 for the straddle sample) — [common.py `boot_ci`, `summ`, `breakdowns`](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/common.py)
- "Random-time baseline" for the combos = one straddle/strangle per ticker-day entered at a uniformly random minute in 09:35..14:30 (seed 42), same strikes rule, same exits; for the directional tests the baseline is the coin-flip expectation = the average of the call and the put bought at the same time and strike (exact, no RNG), plus for the ORB test a random-time (09:46..15:00) random-direction trade — [run_tests.py](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/run_tests.py)

### Inferences
- The spread model (1 %/2 % half-spread + $0.70) costs a 1-lot short straddle 3.6-4.5 % of credit per round trip, which is the same order as the mid-price edge measured below; conclusions about short-vol therefore hinge on real executable spreads, which we do not have (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- UW option bars carry no bid/ask quotes, so spreads are modelled as a percentage of premium rather than observed; real ATM 0DTE SPY/QQQ quotes are often $0.01-0.02 wide (0.2-0.5 % half-spread on a $2-3 option), which would put the truth between the `mid` and `spread` numbers; IWM quotes are typically wider than 2 %.
- Margin/buying power for short straddles is not modelled; everything is $ per 1-lot, not return on capital.

### Inferences
- There is an intraday variance-risk premium in these ETFs' 0DTE straddles (mid-price short P&L positive at all five times, 2-10 % of credit, and the random-time mid CI [2.49, 27.73] excludes zero), but it is about the size of one round-trip bid-ask spread; whether it is harvestable depends entirely on fill quality, which this data cannot resolve.
- The 13:00 entry looks best (highest % of credit, highest win rate, smallest drawdown, mid CI excludes zero) because fewer hours remain for a large move, but after modelled costs its CI still includes zero, and its second-half-sample mean is a quarter of the first-half mean.
- The QQQ-13:00 and Friday/Wednesday cells with CIs above zero are 1-of-15 and 1-of-5 selections; with ~25 variants x 15 cells x 5 weekdays x 3 IV buckets examined, a few such cells are expected by chance.
- Stops at 1.5x credit lower the mean (they are hit on ~35 % of days by intraday noise and then re-pay the spread) while cutting drawdown by roughly a third; 2.0x stops are closer to neutral. Neither turns the strategy positive after costs.
- The loss distribution is the familiar short-gamma shape: two days (2025-10-10 and 2025-11-20) cost more than the strategy made in sixteen months.

### Gaps
- Fill quality (see common gaps); exit at 15:55 closes both legs, including the near-worthless one, which costs about $1.71 per leg under the spread model; letting it expire would improve the spread-model mean by roughly $1-2 but adds pin risk, which was not modelled.
- No event filter (FOMC, CPI, NFP days are included); no sizing or margin; one regime (16 months) with two tail days.

### Inferences
- The 15-minute opening range is too narrow relative to 1-minute noise for a close beyond it to carry information; the "better side" the task asked us to pick does not exist in this sample (both sides are within noise of zero on the underlying, and both lose after option costs).
- The small positive MID means of the fade and even of the random baseline (+1.38 [0.71, 2.10]) are an artefact of the trail engine's optimistic fill assumptions (stop and breakeven exits filled exactly at the level, on bar highs/lows), not of a directional edge; the adverse model shows the other extreme (-13 to -24). The spread model, in between, is negative for every variant.
- The fade's advantage over the breakout (-3.37 [-7.14, 0.42]) is consistent with the 78 % back-inside rate, but it is not large enough to pay for the option's spread.

### Gaps
- The option expression is confined to the trail engine's parameters; a wider-stop or longer-hold variant was not run, but the underlying statistics give no reason to expect a different sign.
- Opening ranges of 5 or 30 minutes were not tested.

## Key question 4: Intraday seasonality (half-hour returns), first-30 vs last-30 momentum, and the 15:00 ATM option expression

### Takeaway
No half-hour of the day has a mean return with |t| >= 2 in this sample, the first-half-hour return does not predict the last half-hour (corr -0.036, sign agreement 46.7 %), and buying the ATM 0DTE at 15:00 in the direction of the morning return loses after costs (-$4.17 [-11.33, 4.21] held to 15:55). Verdict: no tradable session-momentum effect.

### Inferences
- The classic "market intraday momentum" (first half-hour predicts the last half-hour) is absent, if anything slightly reversed, in SPY/QQQ/IWM over 2025-06..2026-10; the only pooled |t| above 2 is the 15:00-15:30 -> 15:30-16:00 link, which cannot be traded with a 15:55 hard close and whose sign-conditioned mean is within noise.
- A long ATM 0DTE held from 15:00 to 15:55 bleeds roughly $5-6 per contract (coin-flip baseline) after spread costs; the momentum sign recovers about $3 of that (CI [-9, 15]), not enough to be positive.

### Gaps
- The first-30 return is measured from the 09:30 bar's open, not the official opening auction print; the last-30 return ends at the 15:59 bar close, not the closing auction.
- No overnight-return or volume conditioning (the published momentum effect is strongest on high-volatility/high-volume days); the |first-30| >= 30 bp filter was the only conditioning tried and it is post hoc.

## Key question 5: Gap fill (|open gap| > 0.3 %): touch frequency and the 09:35 ATM option toward the fill

### Takeaway
Gaps larger than 0.3 % (580 of 1,017 ticker-days, mean 76 bp) touch the prior close by 10:30 only 22 % of the time and by 15:55 only 38 %, falling to 20 % for gaps above 1 %; buying the ATM 0DTE toward the fill at 09:35 with the trail engine loses -$3.78 [-7.12, -0.29] after costs and is identical to buying in the gap direction (-$3.75). Verdict: gap fill is not a tradable tendency in this sample.

### Inferences
- In this period large gaps mostly did not fill the same day (62 % unfilled by 15:55; 80 % for gaps above 1 %), so a "fade the gap" rule has no base-rate support; the 83 % fill rate for sub-0.3 % gaps is just normal intraday range covering a small distance.
- The 09:35 ATM long loses about $3.8 per contract after costs regardless of direction on gap days and $3-6 on non-gap days: the loss is the option's spread plus the trail engine's stop churn, not a directional signal.

### Gaps
- Prior close is the 15:59 bar close rather than the official closing print (differences are usually a few cents); the 0.3 % threshold means more than half of all days qualify, so this is not a rare-event test; a partial-fill target (e.g. 50 % of the gap) was not evaluated.

## Overall verdict across all tests

### Takeaway
After the modelled costs, no pre-specified test has a 95 % lower confidence bound above zero. ### Inferences
- The short-vol side has a real but thin premium (roughly 2-10 % of the straddle credit per day at mid) that the modelled bid-ask spread consumes; the session-effect side (ORB, half-hour seasonality, morning-to-close momentum, gap fill) shows no directional information in the underlying at all, so no option expression of it can work.
- If anything is worth pursuing from this set, it is the afternoon (13:00) short straddle in SPY/QQQ with real quote data to establish the achievable spread, an event-day filter, and a sizing rule that survives the 2025-10-10 / 2025-11-20 type of day; nothing else here merits further work.

### Gaps
- Executable bid/ask quotes, margin, event calendars and a second volatility regime are all missing; those are the four things that would change the short-straddle conclusion in either direction.
