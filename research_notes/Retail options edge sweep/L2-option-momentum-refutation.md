# L2 — cross-sectional option momentum and reversal in single-name ATM straddles: evidence-quality refutation (2026-10-14)

Lens: evidence quality (is the cited effect real, replicated, not a data-mining artefact; checked against the primary text). Hypothesis under test: registry-sweep id **L2** as stated in `academic-anomalies.md` §2 and §11 — "equity ATM straddles with high past 2–12 month returns outperform low ones by +6.22 %/month (t 7.97), one-month reversal −4.09 %/month (t −6.18), 12-month momentum Sharpe 1.53, stable in every 5-year subsample 1996–2019; but every long-straddle quintile has a negative mean (best −1.55 %/month) and the long-short is about zero at the full quoted spread (−6.78 % main sample; +1.12 %, t 0.75 for low-spread deciles)". Proposition to refute: *a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates.*

Grades: **[PR]** peer-reviewed; **[WP]** working paper; **[EX]** exchange/regulator; **[PRAC]** practitioner; **[SEC]** secondary summary (not evidence); **[LOCAL]** this repository's measurements or this note's own arithmetic. Every number from a paper below was read from the primary PDF (converted with `pdftotext` in the session scratchpad, `l2_dl/`) unless marked [SEC]. Local context read first: `research/compass/02_coverage_matrix.md`, `07_coverage_report.md`, `05_results.md`, `registry.jsonl` (45 entries incl. GATE-v2, C1, H14, H16, H17, S03, S10/S10-rerun, S14, S15, S16a/b, DATA-1..3), and the sibling notes `academic-anomalies.md`, `execution-costs.md`, `our-evidence-ledger.md`. No paid API was called; no repository data was modified.

## 0. Verdict

**Refuted as stated — and the hypothesis text already concedes the decisive points.** The gross effect is real, large and well replicated (§1–2): quintile momentum +6.22 %/month with t 7.97 over 282 months, monotone across quintiles, present in every liquidity tercile, positive in every rolling five-year window, extended to 2021 by an independent team, with a stated mechanism (underreaction to past volatility shocks). Nothing found suggests a data-mining artefact. But the retail proposition fails on the authors' own Table 13 and on arithmetic they do not do:

1. **The long-short is zero at the quoted spread.** Main-sample momentum is −6.78 %/month (t −9.63) when the full quoted half-spread is paid; the cost-optimised version (extreme deciles, spreads < 10 % of mid) is +1.12 % (t 0.75) and −0.61 % (t −0.40) once the stock acquired by exercise is liquidated at half the closing spread. The authors' abstract: "Average option momentum returns are close to zero after paying the full bid-ask spread for options with below-median bid-ask spreads." GATE-v2 prices marketable orders at the NBBO; that is the "quoted" column.
2. **The t-statistic of 0.75 implies a monthly standard deviation of ≈ 25 % of notional** for the after-cost decile strategy, so a 95 % lower bound above zero would need ≈ 1,350 months (one-sided) — 113 years. Our window (first holding month 2013-07 after a 12-month formation period → 2026-10) is ≈ 159 months, 87 of them after the paper's sample ends. The gate cannot be met by any realistic test.
3. **The only positive part is the short leg**: the low-momentum quintile loses −7.77 %/month, the high quintile −1.55 %. Harvesting it means writing naked single-name straddles on ≈ 137–274 names a month (highest approval tier; Reg-T margin ≈ 3.5 × the straddle's value by FINRA 4210's 20 %-of-underlying rule, versus the paper's SPAN ratio of ≈ 2, which already halves the return). A retail account holding ten names per leg carries ≈ 26 %/month of idiosyncratic noise per leg from an 81 %/month single-straddle standard deviation.
4. **The long leg alone — the only expression inside the Compass long-option scope — is negative before costs** (−1.55 %/month, t −0.85 for the best quintile; every quintile of every sort in Table 2 is negative) and ≈ −9 to −10 %/month after the C1 single-name round trip at 10:00 (two legs × two sides × 3.5–4.1 % half-spread), ≈ −24 % if executed at the close (11.1 % median closing half-spread).

What survives is what `academic-anomalies.md` §2 already said: a *ranking* of which long single-name straddles lose least, worth at most the gap between the best quintile and the universe mean (−1.55 % vs −5.28 %: ≈ 3.7 % of premium per month, gross) — bounded, not a lane, and moot while nothing in the registry gives Compass a reason to hold a single-name straddle (S04/S06: entries carry no information; S13/S11 is the one open event cell). A gross replication at 10:00 marks on Tier A is cheap (§4) and would be the first test of this effect on intraday quotes, but it is a data-quality exercise, not an edge search.

## 1. The claim as cited versus the primary text (Heston, Jones, Khorram, Li & Mo, *Journal of Finance* 78(6) 2023, 3141–3192 [PR])

Primary: published DOI 10.1111/jofi.13279 (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13279; abstract confirmed at https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v78y2023i6p3141-3192.html); September 2021 draft read in full — https://www.acem.sjtu.edu.cn/sffs/2021w/pdfs/3.pdf — and the December 2021 post-acceptance draft ("We thank two referees, an associate editor, and the editor") — http://faculty.marshall.usc.edu/Christopher-Jones/pdf/opmom.pdf. Tables 2, 4, 12 and 13 are identical in the two drafts; the published text was not retrieved (Wiley paywall) and is assumed to match the December 2021 draft.

- **Data and construction (§2 of the paper).** OptionMetrics *end-of-day* bid–ask quotes, January 1996–June 2019, 282 months, common equity only, regular monthly expirations. On each expiration Friday, the call/put pair with call delta nearest 0.5 (discarded outside 0.25–0.75), positive open interest required only during the holding period ("by dropping the open interest filter in the formation period … we increase the sample size by up to 50 %"), weighted-average bid–ask spread ≤ 50 % of the straddle's mid. Zero-delta straddle: weights proportional to −Δ_P·C and Δ_C·P (fractional, not one-lot-each). **Entry price = the closing bid–ask midpoint; held to expiration; payoff from the split-adjusted stock price at expiry.** Early exercise ignored ("non-dividend paying stocks … show almost identical patterns"). 384,672 straddle-months with positive open interest, 1,369 per month on average. Table 1: straddle return mean **−5.28 %/month, SD 80.6 %, median −22.8 %**, 10th/90th percentiles −86 %/+94 %; option illiquidity (weighted percentage bid–ask spread, 12-month average) mean **17.2 % of mid**, median 15.5 %, 10th percentile 6.1 %, 90th 31.1 %. The momentum signal is the *average* (not compounded) monthly straddle return over the formation lags, because returns near −100 % are frequent.
- **Table 2, Panel A (quintiles, Newey–West t with three lags), High − Low:** lag 1 **−4.09 % (t −6.18)**; lags 1–2 −3.41 % (−4.41); 1–6 +1.93 % (2.32); 1–12 +4.02 % (4.94); **2–12 +6.22 % (7.97)**; 2–24 +5.96 % (7.28); 2–36 +5.80 % (6.51); 13–24 +3.14 % (3.99); 13–36 +3.98 % (4.18); 25–36 +2.61 % (3.35). Quintile means for lags 2–12: **−7.77, −6.17, −4.53, −3.55, −1.55 %** (t −4.80 … −0.85) — monotone, all negative. Panel B: IV − HV low quintile −1.86 % (t −1.10), High − Low −10.34 % (−9.48); IV term spread high quintile −2.78 % (−1.69), High − Low +7.17 % (7.92); idiosyncratic vol −2.49 % (−1.99); market cap +2.41 % (2.07); smirk slope +0.49 % (0.78). **No long-only cell in the table has a positive mean.** All numbers in the hypothesis statement are confirmed.
- **Table 4 (factor statistics, 1996–2019):** lags 2–12 individual-straddle factor mean 6.22 %, SD **14.1 %**, monthly Sharpe 0.441 (**1.53 annualized**), skew +0.27, excess kurtosis 0.46, maximum drawdown 55.7 %, Calmar 1.34; lag-1 reversal mean 4.09 %, SD 12.4 %, Sharpe 0.330 (1.14), max DD 73.8 %. Comparators: short SPX straddle mean 10.5 %, SD 70 %, Sharpe 0.149; Goyal–Saretto IV − HV factor Sharpe 0.646; Vasquez term-spread factor 0.538.
- **Stability.** Figure 2: rolling (overlapping) five-year moving averages of the momentum and reversal factors "are positive … at all times", and "the momentum return has been significantly positive in every five-year interval in our sample" (95 % bands). This is a rolling-window statement, before costs, on the gross factor.
- **Pervasiveness (Table 12, Panel B, 3×3 sequential sorts, terciles).** Momentum spread within option-illiquidity terciles: low 4.35 % (t 5.40), medium 5.01 % (6.33), high 5.07 % (5.48), difference 0.72 % (0.67); within stock-illiquidity terciles 3.95 / 4.84 / 5.85 %; within size 5.44 / 5.28 / 4.22 %; rated-but-downgraded 5.09 % (2.44); unrated 5.46 % (7.32). The authors' reading: "Across stocks, trading costs are unrelated to the magnitude of momentum profits" — which is why the low-spread subset keeps the gross effect (7.23 % in the low-cost deciles) but also why the long-short is a bet on the same illiquid single-name straddles everywhere else.
- **Table 13 (after-cost, one-way cost = fraction of the *closing quoted half-spread*: none / algo 20.3 % / adjusted 51.6 % / effective 75.8 % / quoted 100 %; following Muravyev–Pearson 2020 Table 5):**

The authors: "the main momentum and reversal strategies are not profitable if one assumes that the entire quoted spread must be paid"; survival rests on "transactions cost-optimized strategies [that] are standard in practice", on limit orders and the complex order book ("orders are often filled inside the quotes"), and on Muravyev–Pearson's algorithmic-trader effective half-spread of $0.026 against a quoted $0.128 on ATM options. **Margin (SPAN-style ±15 % price / ±10 % vol scenarios, Goyal–Saretto 2009):** "After accounting for margins, the returns shrink by 50 %–55 % … suggesting an average initial margin ratio slightly above 2" — reported only for the no-cost case; the paper never combines costs and margin.
- **Mechanism.** The precursor paper (Jones, Khorram & Mo, "Momentum, Reversal, and Seasonality in Option Returns", Nov 2020 [WP], same data — https://portal.northernfinanceassociation.org/viewp.php?n=2240023016): "Momentum is mainly explained by an underreaction to past volatility and other shocks". The JF paper: "more consistent with an explanation based on underreaction … At this point we also cannot rule out the hypothesis that momentum is driven by time-varying risk premia"; no long-run reversal because "options, as short-lived assets, cannot accumulate mispricing".

Cost benchmark the table relies on: Muravyev & Pearson, "Options Trading Costs Are Lower than You Think", *RFS* 33(11) 2020, 4973–5014 [PR] — https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i11p4973-5014..html (ISE/OPRA 2003–2006, pre-penny): "effective spreads of traders who time executions are less than 40 % of the size of conventional measures, and the overall average effective spread is one-quarter smaller than conventional estimates"; the 20.3 / 51.6 / 75.8 % fractions are Heston et al.'s reading of its Table 5. The "algo" column is an institution with an execution algorithm; the "effective" column (75.8 %) coincides with Hendershott–Khan–Riordan's 2020 effective/quoted ratio of 0.74 for marketable orders in continuous trading (`execution-costs.md` §2.1 [PR]), which is the most favourable column a retail marketable one-lot could claim — and at that column the low-cost decile momentum is 2.60 % (t 1.75) before margin.

## 2. Replications, extensions and critiques

- **Käfer, Mörke & Wiest, "Option Factor Momentum", *JFQA* 61(1) Feb 2026, 32–60 [PR, open access]** — https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109025000225 (PDF read). OptionMetrics IvyDB, **January 1999–December 2021**, *daily delta-hedged call* returns rather than straddles, above/below-median long-short (not quintiles). Table 5, Panel A, cross-sectional option momentum, lags 2–12: annualized mean **4.57 % (t 6.98), Sharpe 2.03**; lag 1 +2.88 % (4.46); lags 13–60 +1.85 % (4.60); time-series momentum lags 2–12 +4.45 % (4.04). Panel B, alpha on the Horenstein–Vasquez–Xiao factors: CSM 2–12 **1.27 % (t 1.82)**, TSM 2–12 3.34 % (1.96) — "insignificant mean returns for the longer-term horizon momentum strategies when performing a risk adjustment". Headline: "Option factor momentum fully subsumes option momentum, but not vice versa" — "Contrary to Heston et al. (2023)". Reading: the gross effect reproduces on a different return measure and two more years of data, by an independent team, but its *distinctness* from a factor-momentum premium is contested; both are gross results on closing quotes.
- **Wei, Cao & Dong, "Option Momentum: Further Evidence", SSRN 4657193 [WP; abstract page returned 403 — content from the search summary and a Harbourfront post, both SEC]** — https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4657193: S&P 100 constituents, 2000–2019, momentum in straddle and strangle returns at 2–12, 2–24 and 2–36 formation lags (2–24 strongest), absent in butterfly spreads. Not verified against the paper.
- **Heston, Jones, Khorram, Li & Mo, "Seasonal Momentum in Option Returns", June 2022 [WP]** — https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Consortium2023/Paper_Seasonality.pdf: sample to November 2020, equity-VIX portfolios; quarterly-lag continuation with "an annualized pre-cost Sharpe ratio of 3.31". Same authors and data; pre-cost.
- **Da, Filippou, Goyenko, Zhang, Zhou & Zhou, "Intraday Option Return Predictability", 2 Oct 2026 [WP]** — https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/IntraOption.pdf (supersedes "Intraday Option Return: A Tale of Two Momentum", SSRN 5018430): half-hour straddle returns predict the same half-hour next day (open and close), with within-day reversal; attributed to delayed reaction to overnight volatility news and market-maker inventory. A different claim at a different horizon; it is the only intraday-quote evidence on straddle continuation and it does not test the monthly effect.
- **Measurement critiques that bear on the number, not the sign.** Goyenko & Zhang (McGill WP 2020 [WP], `academic-anomalies.md` §0.2 — https://ruslangoyenko.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Option-Returns-MAY-24-2020.pdf): closing mid-quotes are biased by end-of-day price pressure and the closing quoted spread is ≈ 50 bp wider than intraday. Heston et al. enter at the closing mid on expiration Friday and take the payoff from the stock, so the bias affects the entry price of both legs; for a long-short it cancels only if it is uncorrelated with past straddle returns, which no one has tested. Eberbach, Molnar, Schuster & Uhrig-Homburg, "Asset Pricing Results in Options Markets: True, Spurious, or Overlooked?", Oct 2025 [WP] — https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Derivatives2025/Molnar.pdf: the mean bias of *dynamically* delta-hedged returns grows with hedge frequency under microstructure noise; Heston et al.'s headline is the static held-to-expiry straddle (Table 2), with daily delta-hedging only as a robustness column (Table 4: the dynamic-hedge momentum spread is +4.78 %, t 3.37 in the VIX-return subsample versus +6.22 % static), so the critique touches the robustness row, not the main table.
- **Not found after extended search:** any post-2021 out-of-sample test of monthly option momentum; any replication on data other than OptionMetrics closing quotes; any after-cost replication by a third party. The Alpha Architect, CXO, Harbourfront, BSIC and Quantpedia pages are [SEC] summaries of the same paper and add no numbers.

Evidence-quality reading: a peer-reviewed, single-signal result with t ≈ 8 over 282 months (clears any multiple-testing threshold in use), monotone quintiles, stable across liquidity, size, coverage and rating subgroups and across rolling five-year windows, reproduced on delta-hedged calls to 2021 and on the S&P 100, with a mechanism. **The gross effect is as well established as anything in the single-name option literature.** Its two open questions — distinctness from factor momentum, and robustness to intraday rather than closing quotes — do not change what matters here, because the retail proposition fails on the authors' own after-cost table.

## 3. What a 2026 retail account would actually face [LOCAL arithmetic on the paper's numbers and C1]

- **Cost column.** GATE-v2 prices marketable orders at the NBBO and C1 disallows mid fills (`execution-costs.md` §3; 58 % of GeorgeFi rows priced better than the market). That is Table 13's "quoted" column: +1.12 % (t 0.75) for the low-cost momentum deciles, −0.61 % with stock-liquidation costs; +1.99 % (t 1.64) reversal; +1.98 % (t 1.47) composite, −0.30 % with exercise costs. Even granting the Hendershott EQ 0.74 price improvement on multi-tick single-name markets, the "effective" column is 2.60 % (t 1.75) — still inside zero.
- **Power.** t 0.75 on ≤ 282 months implies SE ≈ 1.49 % and a monthly SD ≈ 25 % (versus 14.1 % for the gross quintile factor; deciles are noisier). Months needed for a one-sided 95 % lower bound > 0 at 1.12 %/month: (1.645 × 25 / 1.12)² ≈ **1,357 months (113 years)**; for the 2.60 % "effective" case ≈ 354 months. The gross 6.22 % needs ≈ 20 months. Our data holds ≈ 159 holding months (2013-07 → 2026-10), 87 of them out of the paper's sample.
- **Margin.** The paper's SPAN-style ratio "slightly above 2" halves returns (3.47 % margin-adjusted gross for the low-cost deciles). Under FINRA 4210(f)(2)(E) a short equity straddle is margined at the greater side's premium + 20 % of the underlying plus the other side's premium (`execution-costs.md` §7 [EX]): for a $100 stock at 35 % IV the one-month ATM straddle is ≈ $8.08 per share, the requirement ≈ $28 per share, a ratio of ≈ **3.5**, i.e. a ≈ 70 % haircut. Applied to the quoted-cost 1.12 %: ≈ 0.3–0.5 %/month, t unchanged at 0.75. Portfolio margin (≥ $100–110 k) uses ±15 % equity shocks — the paper's scenario set — and is the only route to the 50–55 % haircut.
- **Approval and breadth.** The short leg is naked single-name straddle writing: Schwab's uncovered calls/puts and short straddles sit at its top level with $25,000 minimum equity for uncovered equity options ([PRAC] https://help.streetsmart.schwab.com/pro/4.36/Content/Option_Approval_Levels.htm; https://client.schwab.com/secure/file/P-2940524); TradeStation puts naked stock-option calls at Level 5 of 5 ([PRAC] https://help.tradestation.com/10_00/eng/tradestationhelp/elanalysis/row/option_approval_level_row_.htm). The paper's legs hold ≈ 137 (deciles) to ≈ 274 (quintiles) equal-weighted straddles rebalanced monthly; with a single-straddle SD of 81 %/month, a leg of 10 names carries ≈ 26 %/month of idiosyncratic noise, 20 names ≈ 18 %, versus ≈ 7 % at 137 names. A retail account cannot hold the paper's portfolio and cannot concentrate it without destroying the Sharpe.
- **Eligibility of today's single-name universe.** The paper's "low-cost" filter is a quoted spread ≤ 10 % of mid (half-spread ≤ 5 %). C1 and the S11 cache (`execution-costs.md` §2.2): closing near-ATM half-spread median **11.1 %** (p25 5.0 %, p75 28.6 %) across 254 event-selected roots, per-root median 10.8 % (p10 4.2 %, p25 6.1 %) — only about the top quartile qualifies at the close; the 10:00 intraday medians (3.5–4.1 % for the mixed book, 0.8–1.3 % for the mega-caps) qualify more broadly. The paper's own sample had a mean quoted spread of 17.2 % of mid (median 15.5 %), so its low-cost subset was likewise a minority.
- **Long leg only (the Compass scope).** Best quintile −1.55 %/month gross. Round trip at the 10:00 C1 half-spread (3.5–4.1 % × 2 legs × 2 sides ≈ 7–8 % of premium, plus $2.80 of fees on a $800 straddle ≈ 0.35 %) → ≈ −9 to −10 %/month; at the closing median (11.1 % × 4 ≈ 44 %) the long leg is destroyed. The registry's S10-rerun already shows defined-risk substitutes pay two spreads; an iron-butterfly proxy of the short leg pays four.
- **Mapping to GATE-v2.** Statistical validity (lower bound > 0, out of sample, multiplicity): fails by two orders of magnitude of sample size at quoted costs. Forward validation (≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 calendar months): seven monthly observations of a 25 %-SD series. Tail survival: naked single-name straddles across earnings months (the paper holds through all of them; Table 1's 10th percentile straddle return is −86 %, the short leg's worst is unbounded; Table 4's lag-2–12 factor max drawdown is 55.7 % gross). Product classification: a signal lane must beat random entry by more than one round trip — the long-short's gross edge over a random pair is the 6.22 %, and the round trip on the low-cost deciles is ≈ 6.1 % (7.23 − 1.12), i.e. the edge equals the cost.

## 4. Testability with our data [LOCAL]

- **What exists.** ThetaData option 1-minute NBBO, option EOD and open interest from 2012-06-04 (verified on SPY; single-name floors assumed equal, unverified per root — `02_coverage_matrix.md` §1); expirations per root for all history; stock EOD from 2012-06-01 for UTP-tape (Nasdaq) names but only **2017-01-03 for CTA-tape (NYSE) names**, so the hold-to-expiry payoff for roughly half the universe in 2012–2016 must be inferred from the chain by put–call parity (the method `execution-costs.md` §2.2 used), which adds noise exactly where the paper's return is defined. Unusual Whales adds nothing (option history from 2023-11). The 10:00 mark that Goyenko–Zhang recommend is available from the 1-minute NBBO at the same request cost as the chain.
- **Pull size.** One chain request per root per expiration month (3–6 s): Tier A ≈ 150 roots × 172 months ≈ 25,800 requests ≈ 4 h at 8 concurrent (32 h single-threaded); the paper's universe (800–1,700 names) ≈ 138k–292k requests ≈ 22–46 h at 8 concurrent on the single contested login (`DATA-3`; the stock-EOD backfill and S11 pull already queue on it). Open interest per contract-day is a separate endpoint (≤ 365 days per request) and the paper requires positive OI at entry.
- **What the test could show.** The gross quintile spread (6.22 %, SD 14 %) is resolvable in ≈ 20 months, so a Tier A replication at 10:00 marks over 2013–2026 would answer two questions the literature has not: whether the effect survives intraday quotes, and whether it held 2019-07 → 2026-10. Tier A (≈ 34–150 mega-cap names) is the liquid tail where Table 12 says the spread is 4.35 % (low-illiquidity tercile) rather than 6.22 %, and quintiles of 30 names carry ≈ 15 %/month of idiosyncratic noise per leg, so the first pass would need the full universe to match the paper's precision. None of this changes §3: the after-cost long-short is unresolvable with any sample we can assemble, and the long leg is negative before the test is run.

## 5. What remains, and what is closed

- **Closed by this note (as a lane):** L2 as "an option edge plausibly positive after costs" — refuted by the primary text (Table 13 quoted column, t ≤ 1.64 in every quoted cell, negative with exercise costs), by the margin and approval requirements of the short leg, and by the power arithmetic. The registry's S14 (stock 12-1 momentum, underpowered on 2024–2026) is a different asset and remains as recorded; no registry id covers option momentum and none should be opened as a lane.
- **Retained, bounded:** the *ranking* use already stated in `academic-anomalies.md` §2 — if Compass ever holds a long single-name straddle (S13/A5.2 pre-earnings cell via S11 is the only candidate), prefer names whose trailing 2–12-month straddle returns rank high and whose lag-1 return ranks low; the gross value is ≤ 3.7 % of premium per month relative to the universe and ≤ 6.2 % relative to the worst quintile, and it cannot turn a negative long-straddle cell positive. It belongs with L3/L4 as a selection filter, computed from the same per-root chain history, at zero execution cost.
- **Optional diagnostic, low priority:** a gross replication on Tier A at 10:00 NBBO marks (≈ 4 h of pull) to check intraday robustness and the 2019–2026 period, recorded as a data-quality result; not an edge search.
