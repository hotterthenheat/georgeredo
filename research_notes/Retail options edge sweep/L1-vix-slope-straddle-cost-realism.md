# L1 — VIX term-structure slope as a timing signal for 30-DTE SPY/SPX straddles: cost-realism lens (2026-10-14)

Hypothesis under test (from `academic-anomalies.md` §1.2, proposed screen L1): the VIX term-structure slope (Johnson 2017's second principal component) predicts S&P 500 straddle returns; low slope → high subsequent variance-asset returns; test the slope, not the level, at the 30-DTE tenor on SPY (and SPXW), monthly at 10:30, 21-session holds, long and short, cells by slope quintile, C1 costs. The stated reason to run it: "a positive long-straddle cell on inverted slopes would be the first long-option timing result".

Lens: cost realism — does the effect survive measured spreads and fees (registry `C1`, `execution-costs.md`), with the default of refuted when the source paper did not net out bid-ask. Grades: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[WP]** working paper, **[EX]** exchange/regulator publication, **[SEC]** secondary summary of a paper not read in primary form, **[LOCAL]** this repository's measurements, **[VEND]** vendor material (not evidence). Local context read first: `research/compass/02_coverage_matrix.md`, `07_coverage_report.md`, `05_results.md`, `registry.jsonl` (GATE-v2, C1, H9–H17, S03, S10, S12b, S15, S16b, DATA-1..3), and the sibling notes `academic-anomalies.md`, `execution-costs.md`, `our-evidence-ledger.md`.

## 0. Verdict

**Refuted as a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable under our gates.** Four independent reasons, any one of which is sufficient:

1. **The source never netted costs and cannot be run as published.** Johnson (2017) computes every straddle return "using midpoints of the bid–ask spread, which can be substantial in the options market, meaning they overstate returns available to real-time investors" (footnote 9, p. 2487), from OptionMetrics *closing* quotes 1996–2013, on a *daily-rebalanced* constant-maturity straddle. The default rule of this lens applies. The only investable form is the monthly buy-and-hold version in the hypothesis, which keeps the next-month regression (SLOPE adj. R² 3.8 % for the 1-month straddle, Table 4 Panel F) and discards the next-day sorts that produce the paper's headline positive low-slope returns.
2. **The paper's positive low-slope straddle return is inside the closing-quote bias and is 2008-loaded.** Johnson's lowest-SLOPE quintile earns +0.44 % per day on 1-month straddles (SE 0.19; Table 5 Panel C). Goyenko & Zhang (2020) show closing-midpoint straddle returns on S&P 500 options are biased upward by up to 100 bp per day relative to 10:00–12:00 marks. Johnson himself: "A substantial portion of the OOS improvement comes from late 2008" (p. 2487). Dew-Becker & Giglio (2025) date a break in traded-option alphas at 2012m5, with the traded-option information ratio moving from −0.6 to +0.09 and ATM-straddle delta-hedged alphas "approximately zero since 2010".
3. **Power under GATE-v2.** 172 monthly cycles split into slope quintiles give 28–42 observations per cell. A model-free proxy for the 21-session ATM straddle return on free Cboe data (§4) has a monthly standard deviation of 55–57 % of premium, so a cell's standard error is 8.5–9.6 % of premium; a positive 95 % lower bound requires a cell mean ≥ 16.6–18.8 % of premium per month, and ≥ 27–31 % after a Bonferroni correction over the 80 cells the suggested test pre-specifies (5 quintiles × 2 conditions × 2 structures × 2 directions × 2 roots). No source reports a long-straddle conditional mean of that size post-2010; Johnson's in-sample gross next-day +0.44 %/day (≈ +9 % per 21 sessions if it compounded, at midpoints, with 2008) would fail it. Daily overlapping entries do not help: 3,590 overlapping days are 171 independent 21-session windows.
4. **On 2012–2026 data the sign of the slope effect survives but the long cell does not.** The free-data proxy screen (§4; no option prices, no costs, biased *against* the long side by the VIX-versus-ATM-IV gap) finds low-minus-steep slope = +18 % of premium [+5, +31] on daily overlapping entries (t 2.7) and +19 % [−6, +44] monthly (t 1.5), consistent with Johnson's direction — and every long-straddle cell is negative in every cut: low-slope −16.5 % [−25.1, −7.8] daily, −10.3 % [−26.9, +6.3] monthly, −18.3 % ex the 2020 crash, −19.9 % in 2012–2019, −12.1 % [−26.9, +2.7] in 2020–2026. By year the low-slope long cell is negative in 13 of 15 years, including the two largest inverted-slope years, 2018 (−25 %, 97 days) and 2022 (−37 %, 84 days), and positive only in 2020 (+16 %, 61 days, one event). 90.8 % of low-slope days have VIX9D above VIX: the cell buys a near-term stress or event premium (registry H17: FOMC afternoons move 2.08× [1.56, 2.68]). The only cell with a positive gross mean is the *short* straddle in the steepest-slope quintile (+35 % of premium gross proxy daily, +30 % [+11, +48] monthly, before the ≈ 10–15-point proxy bias and before costs), which is the unconditional variance-risk-premium carry that Dew-Becker & Giglio show is now beta compensation rather than alpha, and which GATE-v2's product-classification gate and registry H15's scope note place outside the Compass long-option universe unless the owner puts short premium in scope.

Cost itself is not the binding constraint: the hypothesis's 2.1 %-of-premium round trip is small against a 55–69 %-of-premium monthly outcome and is probably an over-estimate for SPY at 30 DTE (§2). What kills L1 is that the long-option cell has no positive expectation to protect, and the gates cannot be met at n ≈ 34 per cell. Nothing in the registry needs reopening; S15's "the level has the wrong sign" at 0DTE is not contradicted (the level sort on 30-day horizons has the Johnson sign in §4), but the slope's information is a sizing input for a *carry* lane, not a long-option edge.

## 1. What the primary sources say

### 1.1 Johnson, "Risk Premia and the VIX Term Structure", *JFQA* 52(6) 2017, 2461–2490 [PR]

Read in primary form from the author's site: https://www.travislakejohnson.com/pdfs/Johnson%20VIXTS%202017%20(JFQA).pdf (publisher page: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/risk-premia-and-the-vix-term-structure/56572D1F060448571BD8F597C732D9C3).

- **Data and construction.** "Using closing quotes for S&P 500 index options and risk-free rates from 1996 through 2013 via OptionMetrics, I compute VIX_T,t for T = 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months at the close of each day t" (p. 2466). Straddle returns are "daily returns of constant maturity straddle strategies that use a mixture of the 2 maturity dates nearest a target maturity date that is always T = 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, or 12 months from the current date and compound them to compute monthly returns" (p. 2470; Appendix eq. A-2). 4,445 daily observations. Rationale given for straddles: "at-the-money options are the most liquid, and straddle portfolios have many fewer positions and therefore smaller transactions costs than option portfolios replicating variance swaps" — a relative statement; no cost is ever charged.
- **Unconditional straddle returns (Table 2 Panel C).** 1-month straddle: daily mean −0.32 %, SD 5.86 %, annualized Sharpe −0.88, skewness 3.65; monthly mean −5.29 %, SD 35.74 %, Sharpe −0.51. 2-month −3.10 %/month (SD 21.03 %); 12-month +0.13 % (SD 8.13 %). Synthetic variance swaps (Panel A): 1-month daily Sharpe −1.44, monthly mean −18.31 %, SD 126.52 %.
- **Predictive regressions (Table 4; SLOPE scaled to SD 1).** Next-day 1-month straddle: SLOPE −37.19 bp (SE 9.97), SLOPE-only adj. R² 0.38 %, OOS R² 0.30 %. Next-month 1-month straddle (Panel F, overlapping, Newey–West 32 lags): SLOPE −6.97 % (SE 1.47), SLOPE-only adj. R² 3.80 %, OOS R² 2.29 %; the single-factor hypothesis is *rejected* for next-month straddles at 1–6 months (PC5 enters at +3.81, SE 1.15), so at the monthly horizon the slope is not the only term-structure predictor.
- **Quintile sorts (Table 5 Panel C, next-day, 1-month straddle).** Low SLOPE +0.44 % (SE 0.19), Q2 −0.38, Q3 −0.61, Q4 −0.43, High −0.65 (SE 0.19); High − Low −1.09 % (SE 0.27). "When SLOPE is low, future variance risk premia are not just smaller, they actually change sign and become positive for 17 of 18 variance assets" (p. 2463).
- **Robustness (Table 6 Panel C).** Next-month High − Low for 1-month straddles −21.13 % (SE 4.83); crisis (2008–2009) removed −0.94 %/day (from −1.09); the exogenous two-point slope "SLOPE ≡ VIX²12 − VIX²1" gives −0.57 %/day: "statistically significant but muted predictability" (p. 2483). The hypothesis's VIX3M − VIX9D is a two-point slope at the short end; expect the muted version.
- **Trading strategy (Figure 2, p. 2487).** Unconditional: sell the 30-day straddle every day, hold one day, rebalance; short returns divided by Regulation-T margin of 20 % of the index. Conditional: buy straddles when SLOPE is in the bottom quintile of its expanding history, otherwise short; 2000–2013, 3,437 days; outperforms the unconditional short "by a factor of 4.8 over the 14-year window, 11.9 % per year"; "A substantial portion of the OOS improvement comes from late 2008". Footnote 9: "I compute the returns of both strategies using midpoints of the bid–ask spread, which can be substantial in the options market, meaning they overstate returns available to real-time investors." A daily-rebalanced constant-maturity straddle crosses the spread on part of the position every day; no retail account runs this.
- **What this leaves for a monthly retail test.** The next-month coefficient of −6.97 % per SD of SLOPE on a base of −5.29 %/month: a bottom-quintile month (mean SLOPE ≈ −1.4 SD if normal) would have expected gross return ≈ +4.5 % of premium at midpoints in 1996–2013, before the 2008 contribution is discounted and before any post-2010 decline.

### 1.2 Dew-Becker & Giglio, "The Decline of the Variance Risk Premium: Evidence from Traded and Synthetic Options", Chicago Fed WP 2025-17 (Sep 2025) [WP]

Read in primary form: https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2025/wp2025-17.pdf (also SSRN 5525882). "In the period since 2010, in fact, the alphas of the traded options have converged to zero, consistent with the synthetic options" (p. 4). Figure A.11: cumulative alphas for delta-hedged puts and straddles "have been approximately zero since 2010". Section 4.1: rolling 10-year information ratios of delta-hedged 5 % OTM puts and ATM straddles "have risen over time, converging to zero in 2020 (with a complete convergence for the ATM straddles coming even before Covid)"; "As of January, 2020, the difference in rolling information ratios … is no longer significant". Break test: 2012m5; information ratio of traded options "from −0.6 to 0.09". Their qualification matters for the short cell: "There is still a premium for variance risk, but the results imply that the premium is no larger than what would be expected from the CAPM beta." Sample 1987–2022 traded, 1926–2022 synthetic; returns at midpoints; their mechanism is a decline in dealer frictions and gamma inventory. Implication for L1: whatever Johnson measured on 1996–2013 straddles, 14 of the 18 years are pre-break; the hypothesis's window 2012-06 → 2026-10 is entirely post-break.

## 2. Cost realism

- **The hypothesis's cost number is an assumption, not a measurement.** The 2.1 %-of-premium round trip (two legs × two sides × 0.5 % half-spread + $2.80) uses `C1`'s SPY half-spread, which was measured on 0DTE contracts (2025-06 → 2026-10) and weeklies (2017–2026, SPY 4–7 DTE ATM 0.64 %). `execution-costs.md` §9: "30 DTE = the weekly ATM median for the index products, flagged as an assumption"; §12: "30-DTE index spreads: not measured locally … not evidence until a chain is pulled". `07_coverage_report.md` §3: "No spread has been measured on earlier chains, so an option result on 2012–2019 data would carry an unmeasured cost until the half-spreads are measured on those chains". A 30-DTE SPY ATM leg is ≈ $13 on a $777 underlying at VIX 15; the only snapshot of its quoted spread is vendor material ($0.02 full spread, TradeStation May 2025 [VEND], via `execution-costs.md` §2.1), which would make the half-spread ≈ 0.08 % of mid and the round trip ≈ 0.3 % + fees. Either way the cost is 0.3–2.1 % of a $2,685 straddle ($11–$56), one to two orders of magnitude below the monthly outcome dispersion (§4: SD 55–69 % of premium, ≈ $1,500 per straddle). **Execution cost does not decide L1.** It decides only that the signal must move the conditional mean by more than ≈ 1–2 % of premium per month, which is trivially true for the slope (§4: ≈ 18 % between extreme quintiles) and irrelevant to whether the long cell is positive (it is not).
- **The published strategy is not executable at any cost level.** Johnson's conditional strategy rebalances a constant-maturity straddle daily at closing midpoints (§1.1). Converting it to a monthly hold is what the hypothesis does; the conversion keeps 3.8 % of next-month variance explained (Table 4 Panel F) and loses the daily sort that produced "17 of 18 assets positive in the lowest quintile".
- **Short cell capital (GATE-v2 tail gate).** SPY 777, VIX 15: ATM 30-DTE straddle ≈ √(2/π) × 0.15 × √(21/252) × 777 ≈ $26.9 per share, $2,685 per lot. A ±10 % shock at expiry loses ≈ $77.7 − 26.9 = $50.9 per share = $5,085 per lot; ±15 % ≈ $8,970; the worst 21-session window in the sample (entry 2020-02-13, VIX 14.15, SPX −29 % simple) ≈ 8× premium ≈ $20,000 per lot. Capital for a 25 % stress budget: $35.9 k per lot at the 15 % shock, $80 k at the 2020 move; Reg-T for a short SPY straddle is of the order of 20 % of the underlying plus the other side's premium ≈ $16–17 k (`execution-costs.md` §7). On the proxy's bias-adjusted steep-slope short mean (§4, ≈ +18–25 % of premium gross, ≈ +16–23 % after 2.1 % costs, ≈ $430–620 per lot per qualifying month, ≈ 20 % of months qualify), the lane earns ≈ $1,000–1,500 per lot per year on $35.9 k of stress capital, ≈ 3–4 % a year, before a 2020-type loss. That is the arithmetic of H15's SPXW strangle (13.4 %/8.9 % a year on $261.6 k/$396.2 k), not an edge.

## 3. Power under GATE-v2 (the decisive constraint)

GATE-v2 requires a positive net expectancy with a day-clustered 95 % lower bound above zero, out of sample, after multiplicity accounting; signal lanes must also beat random entry by more than one round trip; carry lanes must be labelled carry. The suggested test has 172 monthly cycles (2012-06 → 2026-09, verified: 172 first-sessions-of-month in the Cboe data). Quintile cells of the realized slope distribution hold 28–42 months (§4: Q1 42, Q2 34, Q3 37, Q4 28, Q5 31 for VIX3M − VIX9D). With a monthly standard deviation of 53–57 % of premium (Johnson Table 2: 35.74 % for a daily-rebalanced 1-month straddle 1996–2013; §4: 56.6 % for the 21-session to-expiry proxy 2012–2026; BCJ's −15.7 %/month to-expiry mean implies the same order), the cell standard errors are 8.5 % (Q1) and 9.6 % (Q5) of premium. Required cell mean for a positive lower bound: 16.6 % (Q1) / 18.8 % (Q5) at z = 1.96; 27.0 % / 30.6 % at z = 3.19 (Bonferroni over the 80 cells: 5 quintiles × {slope, level} × {straddle, strangle} × {long, short} × {SPY, SPXW}). The unconditional 172-month cell has SE 4.3 %, so even a pooled long or short test needs |mean| > 8.5 % (13.8 % after Bonferroni). Daily overlapping entries give 3,590 observations but 171 independent windows (3,590/21); Newey–West standard errors in §4 are 3.7–6.6 % of premium per quintile for that reason. No positive long cell of ≥ 17 % per month is reported by any source at any date; Johnson's gross in-sample low-quintile next-day +0.44 % (≈ +9 % per 21 sessions if it compounded, at closing midpoints, with 2008 in sample) is the largest number in the literature and would fail. Conclusion: the long side of L1 cannot pass GATE-v2 on this window regardless of what the chains show; it is not "testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates" in the sense the gate requires.

## 4. Free-data proxy screen, 2012-06-04 → 2026-09-14 (screen, not a registry study)

Purpose: check, at zero cost and before any chain pull, whether the hypothesis's cells have the sign and size the gates need. Inputs: the Cboe CSVs in §1.7. Proxy for the long ATM straddle held 21 sessions to expiry, premium-denominated: r_long = |ln(S_{t+21}/S_t)| / (√(2/π) · VIX_t/100 · √(21/252)) − 1 (Brenner–Subrahmanyam ATM straddle ≈ √(2/π) σ√T S; payoff |S_T − K| ≈ S|r|). Variance-swap payoff proxy RV²_{t,t+21}/VIX²_t − 1 (Carr–Wu 2009; Dew-Becker & Giglio fn 26). Slope = VIX3M − VIX9D (also VIX6M − VIX, VIX3M − VIX); level = VIX − trailing 21-session realized vol. Quintiles cut on the 2012–2026 daily sample (Johnson's main design) and, separately, on an expanding window from 2011-01-04 (Johnson's OOS design). Standard errors: Newey–West with 21 lags on daily overlapping entries; plain on monthly non-overlapping entries (first session of each month, n = 172). No costs, no option prices. **Known bias:** VIX is a variance-swap rate and sits above 30-day ATM implied volatility by the skew premium (typically one to three vol points), so the proxy overstates the straddle premium and biases r_long *downward* by of the order of 10–15 points of premium (my estimate, unmeasured: (1 + r_true) = (1 + r_proxy) × VIX/IV_ATM); differences across quintiles are affected less. Read levels as "≈ 10–15 points too low for the long side, too high for the short side"; read differences as they stand. Script and outputs: session scratchpad `scripts/slope_screen.py`, `scripts/tails.py`, `out/slope_screen_cells.csv`, `out/slope_screen_year_by_quintile.csv` (reproducible in minutes from the formula above and the public CSVs).

Reading. (i) Johnson's direction replicates post-2012 on model-free data: steep contango is followed by larger straddle losses, inverted slopes by smaller losses, by ≈ 18 points of premium between extreme quintiles. (ii) The hypothesis's reason to run — a positive long cell on inverted slopes — is absent: after the ≈ 10–15-point proxy bias the low-slope long cell is between ≈ −5 % and ≈ +3 % of premium per month, i.e. zero, and its 95 % interval at n = 42 spans ± 17 points. (iii) The money is in the steep-slope short cell: ≈ +35 % gross proxy, ≈ +20–25 % after the bias, ≈ +18–23 % after the 2.1 % round trip, lower bound ≈ 0 after the bias — a carry cell with 2020-type exposure that the slope does not pre-empt, and its incremental value over the unconditional short (≈ +13 points of premium) is the slope's genuine content. (iv) The level sort (VIX − RV) carries the same information at this horizon, in the direction S15 found wrong at 0DTE; the 0DTE and 30-day results are not in conflict because the 0DTE "rich implied" days are event days (S15 reading) while a 21-session horizon averages over them.

- **H9, H11, H13, S15** closed unconditional and VRP-level-timed short ATM 0DTE straddles at the measured NBBO; **S03/S16b** found weeklies ≈ zero; **H15** holds the one open carry lane (SPXW 14:05 strangle) under forward-paper-only rules with the scope note "short premium is outside the Compass long-option recommendation universe unless the owner places it in scope"; **H16** says win rate follows strike distance; **H17** says VIX-level skips lower the carry mean and FOMC afternoons move 2.08×. Nothing here reopens any of them.
- **Do not open L1 as a long-option lane.** The long low-slope cell is ≈ 0 gross on 2012–2026 free data, −25 % and −37 % in the two large inverted-slope years (2018, 2022), and cannot reach a positive lower bound at n ≈ 34–42 under GATE-v2. The C1 cost is not the reason; the absence of a positive mean and the sample size are.
- **If the owner places short premium in scope,** the steep-slope (top-quintile VIX3M − VIX9D or VIX6M − VIX) 30-DTE short straddle/strangle is a *carry sizing input* to be tested under the H15 rules: measured 30-DTE SPY/SPXW half-spreads first (one pass over pulled chains; `07_coverage_report.md` §3), fills at the 1-minute NBBO, hold to expiry with H14's exit rule, FOMC/month-end treatment per H17, tail sizing to the 2020 window (≈ 8× premium per lot), labelled carry, forward paper ≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 months. - **What would reverse the verdict for the long side:** a pre-registered, pooled (not cell-by-cell) regression of 21-session long-straddle returns on the slope over the full 172 months showing a *positive* fitted mean at the lowest decile with a lower bound above the measured round trip — which requires a conditional mean no source reports — or a longer window (the 1996–2011 OptionMetrics era is not in the vendor floor; `02_coverage_matrix.md` option NBBO starts 2012-06-04).
