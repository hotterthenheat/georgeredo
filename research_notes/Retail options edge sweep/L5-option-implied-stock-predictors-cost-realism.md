# L5 — option-implied stock-return predictors (smirk, call–put IV spread, ΔCVOL) as a Compass screen: cost-realism lens (2026-10-14)

Hypothesis under test (`academic-anomalies.md` §5 and §11, sweep id **L5**): option prices predict stock returns — the steepest-smirk quintile underperforms the flattest by 16 bp/week raw and 21 bp/week FF3 alpha (10.9 %/yr), 1996–2005 (Xing–Zhang–Zhao); stocks with relatively expensive calls outperform those with expensive puts by 50 bp/week, declining over the sample (Cremers–Weinbaum); the decile spread on the past-month change in ATM call IV is 1.09 %/month raw, 1.38 % controlling for ΔPVOL, decaying to 0.34 % by month four, 1996–2011 (An–Ang–Bali–Cakici); Goncalves-Pinto et al. attribute the predictability mainly to one-day stock price pressure and its reversal rather than to option-market information. The hypothesis itself says "stock-side only; a long-option expression fails the premium/theta/spread arithmetic" and proposes an S07-style underlying screen (deciles of smirk, IV spread and ΔCVOL at the session close, entry next open, 5/10/21-session absolute and universe-adjusted returns against the A2 hurdle), with "long leg +10–25 bp/week gross in 1996–2011 samples, declining; clears a stock round trip (2–5 bp) if it still exists" as the expected size. Proposition to refute: *a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates.*

Lens: cost realism — does the effect survive the measured spreads and fees of registry `C1` and `execution-costs.md`, defaulting to refuted when the source did not net out bid–ask. Grades: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[WP]** working paper, **[EX]** exchange/regulator publication, **[PRAC]** practitioner document, **[SEC]** secondary summary (not evidence), **[LOCAL]** this repository's measurements or this note's computations, **[VEND]** vendor material (none used). ## 0. Verdict

**Refuted.** The proposition fails on every one of its four clauses:

1. **It is not an option edge, and the only retail-accessible option expression is negative at the measured costs.** The hypothesis concedes this ("stock-side only"); §3 prices it: a 0.5-delta capture of the long leg's *gross* 10–25 bp/week is 5–13 bp of spot against a single-name round trip of 14–16 bp (1-week ATM, C1 3.5–4.1 % half-spreads) or 28–32 bp plus ≈ 51–62 bp of theta (30-DTE held five sessions). Even on the most liquid single-name class (MSFT at the `execution-costs.md` §9 assumption of 1.5 % half-spread) the 30-DTE long call needs 18 bp of favourable move to pay spread and fees and another 62 bp to pay five sessions of theta, against a 5–13 bp expected capture. No configuration is positive; the sibling note's §5 arithmetic stands.
2. **None of the lead papers nets out any cost, on either side.** Xing–Zhang–Zhao's published text contains the word "cost" zero times and "transaction" zero times [LOCAL count on the full text]; returns are weekly portfolio returns from CRSP with options data used only for sorting. Cremers–Weinbaum's abstract says nothing about costs (the full text was not retrievable; the reference list cites Hasbrouck's trading-cost paper, use unknown). An–Ang–Bali–Cakici relegate costs to an internet appendix ("there may be some liquidity frictions involved in implementing a tradable strategy"; "screens related to liquidity and transactions costs, such as excluding the smallest, least-liquid, and lowest-priced stocks") and report no net-of-cost number in the main text. Under this lens's default rule the claim is refuted on the option side outright; on the stock side the cost is small (§5) but the paper evidence is gross.
3. **The effect the papers measure is old, declining within its own sample, and attributed by later work to one-day stock price pressure, not to information in options.** XZZ 1996–2005; AABC 1996–2011; CW: "The degree of predictability decreases over the sample period" (abstract, 1996–2005 per the sibling note). Goncalves-Pinto et al. (2020, abstract): the predictability "is strongest when stock price pressure is present, and it does not depend on options trading"; "stock price pressure [is] the primary driver of the option price-based stock return predictability"; the earlier IV-spread measures are transformations of the option-implied-minus-traded price gap. Muravyev, Pearson & Broussard (JFE 2013, abstract): "option price quotes do not contain economically significant information about future stock prices"; when option-implied and traded stock prices disagree, "option market quotes adjust to eliminate the disagreement" and the disagreements are typically triggered by stock price moves. A one-day stock reversal measured at the close is exactly what an S07-style screen (signal at the close, entry at the *next open*, 5–21-session hold) is built to miss: the close-to-open part of any reversal is never held.
4. **It is not testable to the gate standard with our data now, and the window that could test it is not on hand.** The daily lake holds 2023 → 2026 (2024-01 → 2026-10 usable: ≈ 145 weeks, 33 months). At the papers' own t-statistics the same effect at *full published size* would give t ≈ 1.2 (XZZ raw 16 bp/week), 1.6 (XZZ alpha), 1.4 (AABC raw 1.09 %/month) on this window, i.e. a 95 % lower bound of −11, −6 and −0.40 % respectively (§4): the A2/A5 requirement of a day-clustered lower bound above zero, the same sign in every year and a Bonferroni-adjusted interval cannot be met by a true effect of the published size, let alone a declined one. Extending to 2012-06 needs the stock-EOD lake years 2012–2022 (paused at 2022 part 003), the point-in-time universe for those years (task open), and ≈ 220,000 option-EOD requests at the vendor's observed throttled rate (≈ 15 days continuous), or ≈ 2 million 1-minute chain requests for the 15:45 version the hypothesis specifies (§4.2).
5. **The measures themselves are not reproducible from our data without known biases.** (a) The feasible pull is the *closing* option NBBO, which Goyenko–Zhang (sibling note §0.2) show is the biased quote and which on the S11 single-name cache has a median near-ATM half-spread of 11 % (`execution-costs.md` §2.2): at that width the implied volatility of a 30-DTE ATM mid is uncertain by ± 2.4 vol points at 20 % IV and ± 4.0 at 35 %, and measured on the cache itself the XZZ smirk carries a median noise of ± 6.3 vol points (OTM-put leg median closing half-spread 19.2 %, 45 % in the steepest quintile, where the noise equals the quintile's mean of 11 points) against a spacing of 1.2–3.8 points between XZZ-style quintiles; the smirk rank-correlates +0.38 with the put leg's half-spread, i.e. at the close the sort is largely a sort on quote width [LOCAL, §5.2]. (b) `engine/greeks.py` inverts prices with r = 0.04 and q = 0 (a recorded known defect); for a 30-DTE pair that manufactures a call-minus-put IV spread of −2.5 vol points in every 2012–2016 name (true r ≈ 0.5 %), +0.9 in 2023–2024 (r ≈ 5.3 %), and a *cross-sectional* difference of 2.2 vol points between a 3 %-yield name and a zero-yield name, with a mirror-image bias of +2 to +4 points in the XZZ smirk [LOCAL, §5.1]; CW's measure is a put-call-parity quantity, so this is not a nuisance but the signal's own units. (c) CW weight by open interest and control with stock-lending rebate rates; the S11 cache carries no open interest (the OI endpoint was throttled) and the repository has no borrow data. (d) XZZ use OptionMetrics binomial IVs with dividends and early exercise; the engine is European Black–Scholes.

What survives is a *stock-side diagnostic*, outside the options scope, that could be run on 2024 → 2026 for the record and would be underpowered by construction (§6).

## 2. Why the stock-side effect should be expected near zero on 2012 → 2026, before any cost

- **Decline inside the original sample.** CW report that predictability "decreases over the sample period" and read it as "mispricing that gradually diminishes over time" (abstract). XZZ's effect at a 4-week hold is already 40 % smaller than at 1 week (6.52 % vs 10.90 % annualized alpha); their SKEW has a first-order weekly autocorrelation of 66 % falling to ≈ 20 % at lag 8, i.e. the sorting variable itself is largely noise week to week.
- **Mechanism.** Goncalves-Pinto et al. and Muravyev–Pearson–Broussard (both [PR]) together say the option-implied price is the *anchor* and the traded stock price is the thing that moved: option prices "predict" the stock because the stock's closing price carries transient pressure that reverses. That is a one-day stock-reversal phenomenon, strongest where stock transaction costs are high (illiquid names — CW's own "stock liquidity low" condition), not an information flow from options to stocks over weeks. The AABC decay profile (1.38 % → 0.63 % → 0.34 %) and XZZ's 28-week persistence are the parts not explained by a one-day reversal, and those are the parts measured on 1996–2011 closing midpoints.
- **Closing-quote bias on the option side.** The sibling note's §0.2 (Goyenko–Zhang [WP]) documents that end-of-day option quotes are biased and that the closing quoted spread jumps ≈ 50 bp relative to the day; the smirk and the IV spread computed from closing mids therefore carry a component that is the dealer's closing inventory mark, not information. The hypothesis text's own remedy — the 15:45 1-minute NBBO — is the version that cannot be pulled at universe scale (§4.2).
- **No post-2012 replication of these three sorts was retrievable in this session** (search budget exhausted). This is stated so that nothing is invented: the note does not claim the effect is dead on 2012–2026 data; it claims the published evidence is gross, pre-2012, declining and mechanically attributed, and that the burden of showing it alive falls on a test the repository cannot yet power (§4).

## 4. Window, power and the gates

### 4.1 Power on the window we have [LOCAL arithmetic from the papers' own t-statistics]

The usable daily window is 2024-01-02 → 2026-10-13 (S07: 698 sessions ≈ 145 weeks ≈ 33 months). Scaling each paper's reported t-statistic by √(our weeks ÷ their weeks), i.e. assuming the effect persists at its full published size with the same return volatility:

| CW 50 bp/week | t not visible | — | not computable from what was retrieved |

Protocol A2 requires an absolute day-clustered lower bound ≥ +30 bp at 5/10/21 sessions **and** an adjusted lower bound > 0; A5 adds the same sign in every year and a Bonferroni-adjusted interval across the batch. On 33 months, the absolute part is met by the 2024–2026 drift alone (S13: an unconditional long earned ≈ +77 bp per 10 sessions), so only the adjusted part discriminates, and the table says a true effect of the published size would fail it in four of five cells. A null result on this window would therefore not be informative either way; S14 (cross-sectional momentum/reversal, 20–34 rebalance dates, intervals "hundreds of bp wide") is the registry's precedent and was left "un-resolvable on three years".

### 4.2 The 2012-06 → extension that the hypothesis names

- Stock side: the stock-EOD lake years 2012–2022 are not on hand (download paused at 2022 part 003 behind the S11 pull on the single ThetaData login, `our-evidence-ledger.md` §4.1); the point-in-time optionable universe for 2012–2022 is an open task; CTA-tape names have no stock EOD before 2017-01-03 (`02_coverage_matrix.md` §1), so 2012–2016 is a Nasdaq-tape-only universe.
- Option side, closing NBBO (the feasible version): one option-EOD request per root × expiration (a 10–60-DTE window needs two to three expirations per name-month); for ≈ 1,300 names × 12 months × 14.3 years × 1 request ≈ **220,000 requests**; the S11 pull ran at ≈ 0.17 requests/s under vendor throttling (876 of 7,166 chains in the first window) → ≈ 15 days of continuous single-login pulling, during which nothing else pulls. For 2024 → 2026 only: ≈ 44,000 requests ≈ 3 days.
- Option side, 15:45 1-minute NBBO (the version the hypothesis specifies to avoid the closing bias): one whole-chain request per root × expiration × session (3–6 s each): ≈ 1,300 × 2 × 700 sessions ≈ **1.8 million requests for 2024 → 2026** alone (≈ 12–17 days at 8 concurrent if unthrottled, ≈ 120–180 days at the observed throttled rate); ≈ 9 million for 2012 →. Not a near-term pull.
- Costs on 2012–2019 chains are unmeasured (no C1-type half-spread exists before 2025; `07_coverage_report.md` §3), which matters for the stock side only through the option-quote noise of §5, not through execution.

### 4.3 GATE-v2

A stock-side screen is not a lane and does not reach GATE-v2; the gate's forward-validation clause (≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 calendar months of live NBBO paper trading) applies to any option expression, and §3 shows there is none with a positive expectation to validate.

CW's measure *is* a put-call-parity deviation, and a deviation computed with the wrong r, q and no borrow rate measures the inputs, not the information. The fix (wire the ThetaData rate series, per-name dividend yields and ex-dates, and American-exercise pricing for puts) is engineering, not research, and it is not done.

(i) **At the close, the smirk sort is largely a sort on quote width.** The steepest quintile's put leg is quoted at a 45 % half-spread on a $0.75 mid; its measurement noise (± 11 points) equals its own mean (11.0); and the rank correlation between the smirk and the put leg's half-spread is +0.38. XZZ's quintile 5 is the one that "underperforms"; on our closing quotes it is the quintile whose signal is least measurable and whose contracts are least tradeable. (ii) For quintiles 1–4 the spacing between adjacent quintile means is 1.2–3.8 points against a median noise of ± 4–8.5 points: a name's quintile at the close is a draw from a distribution wider than the bins. (iii) The CW spread's cross-sectional SD (4.1 points) is 1.6× the r-constant artefact of §5.1 (2.5 points in 2012–2016) and 1.9× the dividend artefact (2.2 points): with the engine's constants, the measure's cross-section would be a mixture of carry errors and quote noise, not of parity deviations. (iv) The measure becomes usable (noise ± 1.5 points against a cross-sectional SD of 2.0) only on the ≤ 4 % decile — 135 roots on some dates, 19 roots reliably — which is a different, much smaller universe than the 840 firms/week of XZZ or the 1,100–1,500 names of S07, and for which the §4.1 power problem is worse, not better. (v) These are 2024–2026 closing quotes on event-selected names; the 15:45 quotes the hypothesis specifies would be tighter (C1 intraday medians: 3.5–4.1 % for the mixed single-name book, 0.8–1.3 % for the five most liquid names) and are the pull that §4.2 shows is not feasible at universe scale.

### 5.3 The stock-side cost from the lake (`l5_calc/stock_spread.py`)

The stock-EOD lake carries a bid and ask per symbol-day, but the row is stamped at the report's generation time (`created` 17:15–17:16 ET on every 2025 AAPL/MSFT/SPY/JPM/XOM row checked), so the quote is the after-hours NBBO, not the 16:00 close. Per-symbol median half-spreads from those quotes, 2025: SPY 0.38 bp, AAPL 1.8 bp, MSFT 2.9 bp, XOM 7.4 bp, JPM 7.9 bp; across an S07-like universe (same-year median dollar volume ≥ $50 M and price ≥ $5, a proxy for S07's prior-year rule; 1,220 / 1,520 / 1,780 symbols in 2024 / 2025 / 2026) the per-symbol median is 88–98 bp with p25 ≈ 26 bp and p10 ≈ 8–11 bp; for the ≥ $1 B names (62 / 101 / 131 symbols) 4.9 / 7.8 / 8.6 bp. These after-hours figures bound the regular-session cost from above; the regular-session closing NBBO for the universe is not in the repository (stock 1-minute quotes are listed from 2012/2017 and verified from 2020-06 but not pulled). The sibling note's "2–5 bp on liquid names" is therefore an assumption, not a measurement; for the top-liquidity names it is consistent with the after-hours upper bound, and for the median S07 name the regular-session spread is unknown here. Either way the stock-side cost is an order of magnitude below the option-side cost, which is why the stock row of §3 is the only one that can be positive.

## 6. What would change the verdict, and the only test worth recording

- A peer-reviewed or working-paper replication of the smirk, IV-spread or ΔCVOL sort on 2012 → 2024 data with a stated holding-period return net of stock costs, entered at the open after the signal, would be the evidence to bring; none was retrievable in this session.
- A *stock-side diagnostic* (not a lane, no option expression, outside the Compass options scope) can be run on 2024 → 2026 at the cost of ≈ 44,000 option-EOD requests: ΔCVOL and the smirk from the closing NBBO on the S07 universe, with r and q wired first (§5.1), the S07 engine's same-date universe adjustment and event-date clustering, deciles, and the 5/10/21-session horizons; report the per-year sign and the Bonferroni-adjusted interval, and read the result as §4.1 says: a positive lower bound would be surprising; an interval around zero is uninformative. The only cell with any statistical chance is AABC's ΔCVOL-controlling-ΔPVOL double sort. It would not reopen any option lane: the option arithmetic of §3 does not depend on whether the stock effect exists.
- If the owner places stock positions in scope, the same screen is a candidate for the stock book under the A2 hurdle, where the 2–5 bp round trip is the right cost — but that is a different project, and the registry's S14 precedent says three years cannot resolve a cross-sectional sort.

## 7. Relation to the registry and to closed items

- H2 (flow-following entries from UW aggregates, 0 of 62 cells) and H3 (per-strike bursts) are closed on a different claim (order flow, 2025-04 →); L5 is price-based and is not closed by them, but Goncalves-Pinto et al. and Muravyev–Pearson–Broussard place its mechanism in the stock market's own closing price pressure, which the registry's S07/S08 daily screens (signal at the close, entry next open) did not measure and would not capture.
- S07 and S08 ran the S07 engine on 2024 → 2026 and found breakouts and pullbacks followed by −31 to −97 bp of underperformance and no post-earnings drift; nothing in L5's stock-side screen is expected to be larger than those effects, and those effects did not earn an option test.
- L4 (sibling note) proposes excluding the top smirk quintile from any long-option book; that is the same measure used in the opposite direction (as a contract filter, not a stock predictor) and is unaffected by this note except for the §5.1 and §5.2 measurement caveats, which apply to it equally.
- S11-prelim (876 of 7,166 chains): the same single-name closing-NBBO cache that §5.2 uses; its short verticals lost ≈ $190 per spread in 2024–2026 regardless of the trigger, which is the period, not the signal.
