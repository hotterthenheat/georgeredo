# 0DTE and short-dated index options: what the literature measures (sweep of 2026-10-14)

Scope: Cboe, regulator and academic evidence on same-day and sub-week index options (SPX/SPXW/XSP) and on the ETF equivalents (SPY/QQQ/IWM): who trades them, what buyers and sellers realise by direction, time of day and strike, the gamma/pinning evidence, event-day behaviour (FOMC, CPI, NFP) and measured bid-ask costs. Every claim carries a grade: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[WP]** working paper (SSRN/NBER/university or regulator staff paper), **[EX]** exchange or regulator publication, **[VEND]** vendor or practitioner material (not evidence; listed to flag it). Local results are cited by registry id (`research/compass/registry.jsonl`) and are not re-derived here.

Access notes. ## 1. The short answer: what retail buyers and sellers of 0DTE earn after costs

**Index (SPX/SPXW), identified retail flow.** Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda [WP, version 15 Dec 2023, SSRN 4404704; Cboe SPX trade data with trader-type codes, Feb 2021–Sep 2023; retail identified through the SLAN/MLAT price-improvement auction codes, orders ≤ 10 contracts] measure hold-to-expiry P&L of every identified retail 0DTE trade:

| cell (Table 2, $ per average day, net of effective spread and fees / gross at mid) | net | gross |
|---|---|---|
| all retail 0DTE trades, Feb 2021–Sep 2023 | **−$241k** | −$57k |
| all retail 0DTE trades, 16 May 2022–Sep 2023 (daily expirations) | **−$350k** | −$106k |
| debit (buy) orders, full sample | **−$364k** | −$269k |
| credit (sell) orders, full sample | **+$122k** | +$213k |
| debit orders since May 2022 | −$805k | −$679k |
| credit orders since May 2022 | +$455k | +$573k |

Per trade (Table 3/4): a single long put loses $16.3 on average (median −$35), a single long call $20.7 (median −$35); margin-adjusted mean −5.6 % (put) and −9.4 % (call), median −0.7 %/−0.8 %, more than 25 % of single-leg trades expire at −100 %, the top 5 % return > 178 %. Put and call spreads: mean −$20.2/−$15.8, **median +$85** (+3.0 %/+3.3 % of margin); iron condors mean −$15.6, median +$135 (+5.5 %). Cumulative retail loss over the sample > $125 m, of which > $90 m is transaction cost; "roughly 60 % of daily losses are the result of transaction costs, 60 % are driven by investments in 0DTE put options, and retail buys show particularly poor performance" (p. 4). Gross-of-fee cumulative losses have been roughly flat since November 2022; the net loss keeps growing with the fees. Sellers earn the variance premium and are net positive after costs, but buyers outnumber them and lose more than sellers gain. The regression of margin-adjusted return on trade features (Table 5) gives an intercept of −0.141 % per trade that falls to an insignificant +0.066 % once a *Debit* dummy (−0.29 %) and a *Complex* dummy (+0.17 %, +0.08 % with day fixed effects) are included: the loss is the cost of paying premium up front plus the spread, not a stock-picking deficit.

**Index, all customers (not just PIA-identified retail), limit orders.** Fu, Li, Musto and Pearson [WP, SEC DERA Working Paper, March 2025; OPRA SPXW limit-order-book data Jul 2020–Sep 2023] find that customers supply liquidity with non-marketable limit orders: in slightly-OTM 0DTE series customer interest sits at the best bid more than half the time, at the best offer > 80 % of the time in far-OTM series; a quarter of all 0DTE trades are filled customer limit orders; 66–68 % of those are one-contract orders. A customer who rests at the midpoint of a two-tick market and converts to a marketable order after one second pays an expected $0.021–0.028 per contract against $0.05 for a marketable order (options < $3); for $0.10-tick options about $0.04 vs $0.10; realized spreads to the counterparty are below half a tick within a second and under a cent after a minute. Their conclusion: "the retail public may be losing money on these trades but they are not losing it to transactions costs but rather to the usual effect of high demand on supply" (p. 19). This is the only paper that prices the retail-accessible limit-order route, and it halves the cost side relative to the marketable-order assumption used by Beckmeyer et al. and by this project's own studies (C1).

**Index, unconditional structures held 10:00→close.** Vilkov [WP, SSRN 4641356, SPXW 0DTE Sep 2016–Jan 2026] with the August 2026 correction in the author's replication package (`KNOWN-ISSUES.md`): the half-spread had been charged at 1/100 of its true size (≈ 0.022 bp instead of ≈ 2.2 bp of spot). Corrected unconditional net Sharpe ratios on the shipped 2024-05 panel: put ratio spread +0.84 → **−0.61**, risk reversal +0.44 → +0.10, bear put spread +0.30 → −0.73, short strangle/straddle −0.51 → **−0.97**, iron butterfly/condor −0.96 → **−2.67**. The conditional (ML-timed) put-ratio rule goes from +0.93 to −0.75 and the top-three basket from +0.82 to −0.82. Author's statement: "No structure retains a materially positive net Sharpe ratio." The best gross structure (put ratio spread) earns +0.0251 % of spot per day against a quoted half-spread cost of 0.0343 %. Everything quoted from this paper before August 2026 (including the Sharpe 0.39–0.93 figures in the 2026-10-13 note) is superseded.

**Index, the VRP as a short-premium income.** Almeida, Freire and Hizmeri [WP, SSRN 4701401, version 23 May 2025; Cboe 1-minute SPXW quotes 6 Jan 2012–18 Mar 2025, 1,815 expiration days] measure the annualized 0DTE variance risk premium at 1.54–2.95 % depending on entry time, four times the 1DTE (0.55 %) and 22DTE (0.81 %) figures, and find all long-volatility 0DTE positions (delta-hedged ATM call, straddle, delta-neutral straddle) have negative average returns, most significant for 12:00–14:00 entries. But the strategy that harvests this — always sell the ATM delta-hedged call — has a per-entry-time Sharpe of −0.016 to +0.017 before costs and **−0.010 to −0.042 after paying the bid/ask** at every entry from 10:00 to 14:00 (Table 6, Panel B). Marketable-order harvesting of the 0DTE VRP on the index is negative after the spread in a 13-year sample.

**ETF options (SPY/QQQ/IWM), identified retail.** Bogousslavsky and Muravyev [WP, 29 Aug 2024; 5,182 traders, 0.9 m option trades, $15 bn, 2020–2022, trade-journal data from retail brokers; SPY and SPX alone 26 % of option trades] find the average retail option trade returns −0.9 % (t = −3.6), option purchases −3.95 %, naked sales +20 %; **index/ETF option trades −3 % vs +0.15 % for single-stock options; 0DTE trades −4.6 % per trade (−4.7 % relative to other trades, t = −10; −2.95 % with date and trader fixed effects) while non-0DTE trades earn 0.19 %.** 93.3 % of 0DTE trades begin with a purchase; average 0DTE trade size $1,068; median holding period half an hour. Bryzgalova, Pavlova and Sikorskaya [PR, Journal of Finance 78(6) 2023; OPRA single-leg auction trades Nov 2019–Jun 2021, ETF and equity options, index excluded]: retail prefers options with < 1 week to expiry (49.9 % of volume), quoted spread 12.6 %, effective spread 6.6 %; aggregate retail options P&L −$2.1 bn over 20 months at a 10-day horizon, −$1.6 to −$5.0 m per day net; buyers of sub-week options lose, writers of the same options earn "even on a net basis"; indirect costs (effective half-spread × size) $6.4 bn, about seven times the $0.9 bn of commissions. Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson and Vasquez [WP, April 2025, Cboe-funded] re-estimate the same SLIM trades with Cboe trade directions and expiration values and find customer SLIM trades on CBOE/EDGX were *profitable* (+$0.34 m/day, t = 0.29, Jan 2020–Jun 2023; +$2.07 m/day, t = 1.19, Jan 2020–Jun 2021) — statistically indistinguishable from zero, and a period in which the S&P 500 rose 33–38 % with most opening trades being call purchases; the paper is about equity/ETF SLIM trades, not 0DTE index options.

**This project's own measurements on the same question (registry):** long 0.45-delta same-day contracts at the NBBO with $0.70/side on SPY/QQQ/IWM lose −$4.19 per contract [−5.68, −2.81] on 2,886 trades 2022-11→2026-10 (S01), −$46.18 per SPXW contract (S09), and no parameter variant has a positive lower bound (S02). Unconditional short ATM 0DTE straddles at 10:00 lose −$15.46 per straddle [−32.68, +0.29] on 1,941 SPY/QQQ sessions (S15); 13:00 short straddles are +$8.60 [−6.07, +22.45] on SPY/QQQ (H9) and +$72 [−76, +214] on SPXW (H11) against measured round trips of $12.93 and $68.9; the only positive-lower-bound cell is the 14:05 SPXW strangle carry lane (H15, TEST +$151 per lot [+42, +252]) which fails the selection-correction and multiplicity tests and is not promotable. These numbers agree in sign and size with the literature: buyers lose the spread plus theta; sellers collect a premium that is smaller than the marketable-order round trip on the ETFs and roughly equal to it on SPXW.

**Stated plainly.** After costs, identified retail *buyers* of 0DTE index options lose on average every hour of the day, every weekday and every month since May 2022 (Beckmeyer et al. Table 2); identified retail *sellers* earn a positive net premium that is about a third of what buyers lose; on ETF options retail 0DTE trades lose about 4.6 % per trade. No peer-reviewed or working-paper result shows a retail-accessible 0DTE structure with positive expected return net of the quoted spread; the two that claimed one (Vilkov's conditional rules; Almeida's SSD-violation timing) have respectively been corrected to negative and shown to dissipate after daily listing in May 2022.

## 2. Who trades 0DTE (index)

**Volume share.** Cboe: 0DTE was 5 % of SPX volume in 2016, > 40 % after Tue/Thu expirations (May 2022), ~43 % in 2023, 61 % in May 2025, 57 % in Q3 2025, 65 % in May 2026 and a record 66.2 % in July 2026 [EX: Cboe Volatility Insights 8 Sep 2023; Cboe 2 Jun 2025; Cboe Q2-2026 presentation and July-2026 volume post, via press]. Amaya et al. [WP, Cboe trade records Jul 2020–Jun 2023, 442.6 m records]: 0DTE averaged 34.8 % of SPX+SPXW volume; 1–5 DTE 29.2 %; 6–21 DTE 18.3 %; > 21 DTE 27.1 % (Table 1 sums, averages of daily ratios). Bandi, Fusari and Renò [WP, OptionMetrics 2014–2023]: 0DTE over 43 % of volume, JPMorgan estimate ≈ $1 tn notional per day (Feb 2023). Doshi et al. [WP]: 80 % of SPX volume in 2023 was in options expiring within a month.

**Retail share — the estimates disagree by a factor of ten and none is verifiable.**
- Cboe's proprietary estimate: 50–60 % of SPX 0DTE volume; 57 % → 47 % in early April 2025 → 60 %; 54 % in May 2025; 53 % in April–May 2026 and 57 % in June 2026 after the pattern-day-trader rule change [EX, Cboe "0DTEs Decoded" 2 May 2025; Cboe 2 Jun 2025; Cboe Q2-2026 earnings call via press].
- OptionMetrics: 30–35 % [VEND, reported by FOW 2 Oct 2026; method not disclosed].
- JPMorgan (Peng Cheng, Nov 2022): ~5.6 % retail, ~95 % institutional [press; cited in the 2026-10-13 note].
- Beckmeyer et al.: PIA-identified retail is 2–4 % of all SPX volume and > 6 % of 0DTE volume [WP]; Amaya et al. call the 4–6 % proxy "implausibly low" because retail limit orders never reach the auction; Fu et al. show the limit-order book carries far more retail-sized flow than the auction. Almeida et al. (footnote 3) state that "around 94 %" of 0DTE SPX trading is institutional.
- Cboe [EX, 2025]: > 95 % of SPX 0DTE trades are limited-risk (long options or spreads), ~4 % are naked short options; customer flow "extremely balanced"; institutions open early and hold, retail is active at the open and the close.

**Positioning and who warehouses the risk.** Two findings look contradictory and are not. Beckmeyer et al. (Fig. 2, Open/Close data Feb 2021–Feb 2023): the aggregate option market maker's 0DTE order imbalance is consistently negative (−0.5 % to −1 % of daily volume before May 2022, below −1 % after), i.e. end users are net *long contracts* and market makers net short. Adams, Dim, Eraker, Fontaine, Ornthanalai and Vilkov [WP, 15 Jul 2026, SSRN 5641974; Cboe positions by participant type at 10-minute resolution, Jan 2018–Dec 2024]: customers begin the day short ATM/slightly-OTM 0DTE and long far-OTM (an iron-butterfly/condor profile), their net *gamma* is strongly negative all day because ATM gamma dominates, and market makers' net gamma is positive in 88 % of 10-minute intervals. Both are consistent: customers hold more long contracts (cheap wings) but are short the gamma-heavy strikes. Market makers' hedging needs average ~1 % of S&P 500 shares per 1 % move (0.88 % before May 2022, 1.42 % after); 0DTE positions contribute 0.23 % → 0.54 % of that; 0DTE daily volume ≈ 1 m contracts but the customers' cumulative ATM order imbalance is < 5,000 contracts by late day (Fig. 6) — market makers net the intraday flow almost completely.

**Who trades with whom.** Amaya et al. (Table 1): on an average day 68.7 % of SPX/SPXW volume is customer-vs-market-maker, 9.0 % market-maker-vs-market-maker, 2.2 % customer-vs-customer; within 0DTE, customers bought 17.15 % and sold 15.99 % of total SPX volume, and customer-to-customer trades are a much larger share of 0DTE than of longer-dated volume (1.66 % of total). Fu et al.: 66 % of market-turning and 68 % of passive customer limit orders are for one contract. Beckmeyer et al.: 74.2 % of PIA retail orders are one contract, 23.5 % two to five; retail and non-retail buy and sell with about equal frequency (39.9 %/42.3 % vs 38.4 %/45.3 %), both slightly favour puts, and both concentrate > 90 % of volume in ATM and slightly-OTM strikes with 3–24 hours to expiry (64.6 % of retail trades); non-retail trades more in the last hour (15.4 % vs 11.3 %). Single-leg orders rose from < 20 % of retail 0DTE volume in 2021 to 60–80 % since mid-2022; put and call spreads are ~70 % of multi-leg retail volume.

**Instrument substitution (ETFs).** Dalvi and LaFond [WP, 2026, Cboe trade-level SPY/QQQ/IWM 2012–2025; only a secondary summary retrieved, no magnitudes]: daily expirations raised 0–1 DTE share, drew volume away from 2–5 and 6–30 DTE, raised small-trade (retail) share and directional-trade intensity, especially after Tue/Thu expirations, and are associated with higher 30-day IV and a flatter 7d/30d term structure.

## 3. Realized returns by direction, time of day and strike — index options

### 3.1 By direction and hour (Beckmeyer et al., Table 2 Panel E, $ per average day, hold-to-expiry, trades grouped by the hour they were entered)

| entry hour | all net | debit net | credit net | all gross |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | −46k | −38k | −7k | −19k |
| 10:00 | −50k | −60k | +10k | −10k |
| 11:00 | −31k | −72k | +41k | −5k |
| 12:00 | −22k | −44k | +22k | −1k |
| 13:00 | −36k | −40k | +4k | −15k |
| 14:00 | −28k | −58k | +31k | −3k |
| 15:00 | −30k | −52k | +22k | −5k |

Buyers lose in every hour; sellers gain in every hour except the opening half hour. The gross (mid-price) loss is small from 11:00 on and the net loss is dominated by the spread: the retail buy-side problem is the cost of entry, not a wrong-time-of-day problem. By weekday (Panel D, net): Mon −243k, Tue −429k, Wed −136k, Thu −237k, Fri −236k; Thursday is the only day on which retail *buys* were net positive (+$1.61 m/day) and sells negative (−$1.85 m/day) — Thursday 0DTE existed only from May 2022, so that row is ~16 months of data and is not a stable fact.

### 3.2 By strike (moneyness)

- Beckmeyer et al. Table 1: retail effective spread at the money 3.0 % (calls) / 2.7 % (puts); 0.5–2 % OTM 12.8 % (calls) / 7.0 % (puts); > 2 % OTM calls 80 %; 0.5–2 % ITM 0.4 %. Hold-to-expiry losses are not tabulated by moneyness, but the spread table says that the slightly-OTM lottery strikes retail favours (16 % of call volume, 19 % of put volume) cost 2–4× the ATM strike to enter.
- Almeida et al. (Fig. 4–6): average 0DTE call returns are low overall and *decrease* with the strike, becoming more negative OTM; put returns are negative especially OTM; the implied intraday pricing kernel is U-shaped and higher for positive than negative returns, i.e. investors overpay most for upside (OTM calls) over intraday horizons. only ~6 % of ATM option prices satisfy the risk-averse (second-order stochastic dominance) price bounds and the violations are "mainly upper bound violations" (ATM too expensive); ~25 % of calls and 35 % of puts satisfy the bounds overall, ~97 % of one-month options do; OTM calls and puts are below the lower bound "reasonably often" — ATM is expensive and OTM is cheap relative to the realized intraday distribution; after May 2022 ATM calls are almost always upper-bound violations and OTM puts increasingly lower-bound violations.
- Bryzgalova et al. (ETF/equity, not index): slightly-OTM retail trades carry a 29 % quoted spread; "micro" trades ≤ $250 a 23.5 % spread.
- Fu et al. (Fig. 1–3): strike grid is $5 (≈ 0.125 % moneyness at SPX 4,000); just-OTM calls only reach the most actively quoted range in the last hour; customer presence at the bid peaks near 40 % of the day in the slightly-OTM bins.

### 3.3 Variance risk premium and short-premium structures by entry time (index)

- Almeida et al.: annualized 0DTE VRP 1.54–2.95 % by entry time (10:00–14:00, half-hourly); both the upside and downside components are positive and the upside is larger (the reverse of the one-month pattern); the VRP negatively predicts the rest-of-day return through its upside component; the VRP is smaller before May 2022 only in its downside component; excluding the 68 FOMC days lowers it. Relative bid-ask spread is at its minimum and stable 10:00–14:00 and highest at the open and the close (Fig. 3); the number of quoted strikes per standardized-moneyness bin falls from ~4 at 10:00 to ~2 at 14:00.
- Almeida et al., the one timing rule with a positive after-cost record: buy the delta-hedged ATM call when its price is below the SSD lower bound, sell it when above, otherwise hold cash. Sharpe after paying the bid/ask 0.10–0.16 per entry time over 2012–2025 (Table 6; units as reported, not annualized) against −0.01 to −0.04 for always selling; conditional on below-median realized variance 0.40–0.47 vs 0.00–0.08 above median, on below-median 0DTE volume 0.24–0.33 vs −0.04 to +0.04 above, on low Google attention 0.18–0.27 vs ≈ 0 (Table 7). Cumulative performance "mostly stagnates" after 2022 (Fig. 9); after May 2022 ATM calls are almost always upper-bound violations, so the rule degenerates into always-sell, which is net negative. The window in which it worked is the one the owner's chain cache does not cover.
- Vilkov (corrected): see §1; by the author's own correction no unconditional or conditional structure entered at 10:00 and held to 16:00 is net positive over Sep 2016–Jan 2026.
- Chong [WP, "Are 0DTE options mispriced?", 2020–2024, as summarised by HKUST]: volatility-arbitrage strategies that would have large Sharpe ratios if and only if 0DTEs were mispriced; "transaction costs and estimation risk render the Sharpe ratios ... economically insignificant"; 0DTEs are "expensive but do not appear to be mispriced". Full text not retrieved.
- Bandi, Fusari and Renò [WP, Mar 2024; Cboe 2 Jan 2014–11 May 2023]: an instantaneous volatility-risk-premium regression on leverage and vol-of-vol terms from a 10:30 0DTE panel has R² 21 % (34.8 % jointly) and the return premium R² 1.6 %; "suggestive" predictability of the subsequent 5.5-hour return and of the volatility premium; no trading-strategy P&L is reported.
- Muravyev and Ni [PR, JFE 136 (2020)]: delta-hedged S&P 500 option returns average −0.7 %/day, split −1.0 % close-to-open and **+0.3 % open-to-close**, for all maturities and moneyness. The intraday leg — the only leg a 0DTE holds — is the one with *positive* delta-hedged returns in that pre-2020 sample; this is the structural reason the 0DTE short-vol premium is small relative to overnight short vol.

### 3.4 Gross versus net: the cost share of the loss

Beckmeyer et al.: ~60 % of retail daily losses are transaction costs; gross cumulative losses flat since Nov 2022. Bryzgalova et al.: indirect costs seven times commissions. Fu et al.: with resting limit orders the cost halves and "they are not losing it to transactions costs". Our S01: gross −$2.79 and round trip $2.79 per contract — half the loss is cost, the other half is the entry (S04/S06: the rule fires after the move).

## 4. ETF options (SPY/QQQ/IWM) — what is specific to them

- Settlement and hours: SPY/QQQ/IWM options are American, physically settled and trade to 16:15 ET; SPXW is European, cash-settled on the 16:00 index close. H16 recorded that most of the "hold to expiry" gain on SPY/QQQ short premium is after-hours exercise risk (+$30.60 vs the 16:00 close shrinks to +$6.30 vs the 17:15 quote).
- Retail concentration: Option Alpha platform data [VEND]: SPY 81 % of ~230k 0DTE trades, SPY/QQQ/IWM 98 % of volume; 49.6 % of 0DTE traders profitable; profitable cohort enters ~10:15 ET and exits ~12:00 ET with 15 % target / −25 % stop; Mon/Wed net positive, Thu consistently negative. Platform-selected, no cost audit, not evidence.
- Bogousslavsky-Muravyev: index/ETF −3 % per trade vs single-stock +0.15 %; 0DTE −4.6 %; the put/call ratio is balanced for index/ETF options and 2:1 calls for single stocks; naked sales concentrate in index/ETF options.
- Pinning on SPY: Elms [WP, SSRN 6564078, 2026; 1.6 m near-expiry SPY contracts, 2,294 days 2016–2025, E-mini at 1-minute]: five pinning tests, none significant; days with the highest ATM open interest show ~16 % wider daily ranges (p < 0.001); one significant cell of five, no multiplicity adjustment, single-author preprint.
- Implied vs realized on SPY: FlashAlpha [VEND; 193 Wednesday sessions Jul 2022–Apr 2026, mid quotes, no costs]: the 10:00 ATM straddle over-forecasts the move to the close by ~13 % (median realized/implied 0.59 vs 0.67 if fair), close inside ±1σ 71 % of sessions.
- Costs (this project, C1, measured at ThetaData 1-second NBBO 2025-06→2026-10): median realized half-spread 0.5 % of mid on SPY/QQQ, 0.7–1.0 % IWM, 0.9 % SPXW; the buy-back of near-worthless short legs at 15:55 costs 3.3 % (median) of the credit; realized straddle round trip $12.93 vs modelled $8.30. Relative to the contract's dollar size the fee burden differs by an order of magnitude: $0.70 is 0.7 % of a $100 SPY contract and 0.035 % of a $2,000 SPXW contract, so the ETF all-in round trip (≈ 2 × 0.5 % + 1.4 %) and the SPXW round trip (≈ 2 × 0.9 % + 0.07 %) are similar in percentage terms; S09 confirmed that moving the long-0DTE rule to SPXW does not change the sign.
- XSP (mini-SPX, 1/10 SPX, cash-settled): Concretum [VEND, Cboe 30-minute snapshots Jan 2022–Aug 2026]: "XSP is less liquid than SPX, particularly during the final 30 minutes"; Cboe reported an XSP ADV record of 238k in July 2026 against ~2 m SPX 0DTE. No academic measurement of XSP costs was found; a broker snapshot [VEND, TradeStation May 2025] quoted ATM spreads of ~$0.02 (SPY) vs $0.12–0.13 (XSP) on one day — flagged, not evidence.
- No peer-reviewed or working-paper study reports SPY/QQQ 0DTE effective spreads or hold-to-expiry P&L by trader type comparable to Beckmeyer et al. for SPX. The ETF-specific evidence base is Bogousslavsky-Muravyev (journal sample, 2020–22), Bryzgalova et al. (sub-week options, 2019–21), Elms (pinning) and this project's S01/S02/S10/S12b/S15/S16b.

## 6. Gamma, dealer hedging and pinning

**Does 0DTE raise index volatility?** Three identified estimates, two signs.
- Adams, Dim, Eraker, Fontaine, Ornthanalai, Vilkov [WP, Jul 2026; subsumes Dim-Eraker-Vilkov SSRN 4692190 and Adams-Fontaine-Ornthanalai SSRN 4881008]: using the pre-May-2022 Tue/Thu exceptions as exogenous variation, 10-minute realized volatility is **61 annualized bp lower** (7 % of the 9.10 % baseline mean) on days a 0DTE expires; the weekday gap disappears once 0DTE expires every day. Market makers' hedging needs rise by 15 bp of S&P shares per 1 % move on 0DTE days, 25 bp from *stale* positions (longer-dated options that became 0DTE, ~90 % opened within the prior week) and only 3 bp from same-day 0DTE trades; non-0DTE needs fall 14 bp. 2SLS multiplier ≈ −4: 1 bp more hedging need → 4 bp lower realized volatility. One-standard-deviation higher 0DTE net gamma predicts ~23 bp lower volatility in the next 10 minutes, decaying over an hour; higher net gamma predicts stronger E-mini order-flow reversal (~30 minutes) and turns hour-to-hour momentum into reversal. The stale-position effect is ~20 bp in every hour and ~40 bp (wide CI) in the last hour.
- Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson, Vasquez [WP, Cboe-funded, Jan 2025; Cboe trade records Jul 2020–Jun 2023]: median impact of market-maker gamma on daily annualized volatility −0.08 pp; **maximum** impact +3.3 pp daily, +6.4 pp over 30 minutes — within the ordinary range (daily changes in realized vol have a 4.5 pp sd and exceed 3.0 pp on ~20 % of days; the 30-minute maximum is below the 95th percentile of ordinary changes).
- Cboe [EX, Sep 2023, one year of 30-minute data]: net market-maker 0DTE gamma $170–670 m per day, 0.04–0.17 % of S&P futures daily volume; 15:30 median +$173 m, IQR −$1.1 bn to +$2.4 bn, whiskers −$5 bn to +$7.7 bn (1.3–1.9 % of futures notional); no uptick in gap-move frequency in the last hour. Cboe [EX, May 2025]: net gamma hedging "at best 0.2 % of SPX daily liquidity".
- Brogaard, Han and Won [WP, SSRN 4426358, Apr 2023; **full text not retrieved; abstract as indexed**]: using the staggered introduction of weekly index options as an instrument, a one-standard-deviation rise in 0DTE trading raises volatility by 9.10 % relative to its mean, surviving controls for market-maker gamma hedging and "primarily driven by speculative retail investors"; press coverage of an earlier draft reported ~14 %. Adams et al. and Almeida et al. both cite this as the contrary result; Adams et al. argue it uses the 0DTE *share* of volume whereas the hedging effect comes from positions, not trading.

**Pinning.**
- Ni, Pearson and Poteshman [PR, JFE 78 (2005)]: optionable single stocks close near strikes on expiration Fridays; returns shifted ≥ 16.5 bp per expiration, ≈ $9 bn of market value; attributed to delta hedging and firm proprietary trading.
- Golez and Jackwerth [PR, JFE 106 (2012)]: S&P 500 *futures* pulled toward the ATM strike on serial-option expiration days (13.56 % settle within $0.25 of a strike vs 10 % expected, per secondary summary) and pushed away before index-option expiration ("anti-cross-pinning"); ≥ $115 m notional shift per expiration day.
- Elms [WP, 2026, SPY 2016–2025]: no pinning in five tests; +16 % range on high-ATM-OI days. FirmTape [VEND blog, 1,084 SPX sessions 2022–2026]: decomposes open interest into ATM concentration (elasticity of the forward range −0.28, t = −7.3) and total book size (+0.17, t = +4.3); cross-sectional association, author sells the data.
- Adams et al. (Fig. 6): customers' cumulative ATM 0DTE order imbalance < 5,000 contracts by late day against ~1 m contracts traded — the market maker's end-of-day ATM inventory, which is what a pin would need, is small.
- Local: H6 (wall/magnet pin fades) indistinguishable from distance-matched random levels; H4 (gamma regime as direction filter) sign flips in/out of sample; H10 (gamma-conditioned last-30-minute momentum) wrong sign (−3.07 bp/day on negative-gamma days). These agree with Adams et al.: dealer gamma is on average positive, dampens volatility and produces reversal, not momentum, and the sign of the regime is not a tradable direction filter at retail cost.

## 7. Event days

**FOMC (14:00 ET, 8 per year).**
- Song [WP, Sep 2026, HEC Montréal; SPX options Sep 2016–Dec 2023, ES futures at 5-second resolution; the drift break test uses announcements Apr 2011–Dec 2023]: option-implied event volatility rises monotonically from 0.60 % at 10:00 to 0.78 % at 14:00 (+30 %), collapses to 0.34 % within minutes (81 % of priced event variance), rebuilds to 0.42 % before the 14:30 press conference; **71 % of priced event variance resolves at the statement, 29 % at the press conference**; realized jump variation spikes to 94 % of the window at the statement vs 19 % at the start of the press conference; the implied uncertainty forecasts realized volatility only for windows containing the release (R² 78–85 %) and has no forecast power before it; implied skew is uniformly negative, stable, and does not predict returns. The pre-FOMC drift (Lucca-Moench, +49 bp over 24 h, 1994–2011) is gone: 2016–2023 open-to-release return −0.03 % (insignificant), the only significant piece is the overnight gap (+0.19 %); a structural-break test puts the collapse at the March 2016 announcement, the first after Wednesday SPX expirations were listed (23 Feb 2016); drift before the break +0.27 % (all) to +1.05 % (nearest four), after it −0.13 % to +0.20 %, insignificant. Kurov, Wolfe and Gilbert [PR, FRL 2021] independently date the disappearance to after 2015.
- Johannes, Kaeck and Seeger, "FOMC Event Risk" [PR forthcoming, Journal of Finance per SSRN 4484011; abstract only]: ex-ante FOMC event risk is large, time-varying and forecasts realized event volatility; a significant variance risk premium; "no evidence for an expected return premium related to FOMC event risk in the pre-FOMC period or spanning the announcement"; pre-FOMC drifts relate to the VIX, not to event-risk resolution. As quoted by Song: buying straddles loses significantly over the statement window but earns insignificant returns over the press conference.
- Londono and Samadi [WP, Fed IFDP 1376, Jun 2023; daily SPX expirations, Jan 2017–May 2023]: ATM implied volatility for expirations spanning releases is 44 bp (GDP) to 134 bp (FOMC) higher than neighbouring expirations, +2.5 % to +7.5 %; 10-delta put-call IV skew up to 58 bp wider (NFP, +6.3 %); **variance risk premia for FOMC-spanning expirations 63 bp, 44 % larger** than control; forward equity risk premia for CPI releases range from 0.54 % (May 2017) to 12.2 % annualized (Jan 2023); volumes in CPI/NFP/GDP-spanning expirations are higher than controls, FOMC-spanning volumes are not.
- Wright [PR, NBER 28306 (2020), J. Time Series Analysis 2025; Wednesday/Friday weekly options on E-mini and Treasury futures, "a bit more than 3 years" ending 2020]: average variance risk premia on event-eve options are positive and significant, larger for FOMC than for employment reports in stocks; roughly half of the FOMC-eve rise in implied variance is premium, half is realized.
- Almeida et al.: 68 FOMC days in 1,815; removing them lowers the average VRP and its components but changes no conclusion (Tables OA.8–OA.11).
- Greekslab [VEND; 32 FOMC days Jun 2022–Apr 2026, frictionless, one contract, 50 % target / 100 % stop]: SPX intraday range 1.41 % vs 1.05 % normal; 14:00→close 1.30 % vs 0.46 %; morning 0.51 % vs 0.90 %; pre-announcement OTM 0DTE premium ≈ 5.1× normal; a 13:55 short ATM straddle won 66 % with +$824/day and a worst day of −$2,743 (9:35 entry: 69 %, +$908, worst −$3,947). n = 32, no spread, no fees: not evidence; listed because it is the only time-of-day P&L table on FOMC days found.
- Local: H17 — FOMC afternoons move 2.08× [1.56, 2.68] event-free afternoons (SPX 2017–2026); the FOMC + month-end skip is the default filter for carry lanes; S15 skipping FOMC/CPI/NFP/month-end moved the unconditional 10:00 straddle from −$15.46 to −$11.78 per straddle (still negative). H13/H15 skip FOMC by construction.

**CPI and NFP (08:30 ET, before the open).** No academic or exchange study of 0DTE straddle P&L on CPI days was found. What exists: Londono-Samadi (above) on ex-ante pricing of CPI-spanning expirations (higher IV, higher skew, large forward equity premia in 2022–23); H17 locally — CPI and NFP *afternoons* move 0.94–0.96× event-free afternoons, i.e. by the 0DTE open the release is already in the price and skipping those days lowers a carry lane's mean. The 2022–23 CPI prints that moved the index several percent are in the sample; the measured afternoon ratio is still ≤ 1.

**Month-end and expiration weeks.** H17: month-end afternoons 1.33× [1.06, 1.65]. Stivers-Sun (JBF 2013) opex-week effects were covered in the 2026-10-13 note and are not 0DTE-specific.

## 9. Institutional-only versus retail-accessible

Institutional-only (cannot be replicated with marketable orders at the NBBO, $0.70/contract, no rebates): market-making revenue (Fu et al.: counterparty realized spread ≈ 1–2.5 ¢ per contract within a second, requires queue priority and inventory netting of ~1 m contracts/day); execution timing against the stock (Muravyev-Pearson, sub-second); the hedging-needs/net-gamma measures of Adams et al. (require Cboe trade-capacity data; the vendor GEX proxies tested in H4/H5/H10 agree with each other on the regime only 52 % of days); Vilkov's ML-conditioned rules (and they are negative net anyway); Almeida's SSD-violation timing (requires a daily re-estimated conditional physical distribution; positive 2012–2022, dissipated after May 2022); Londono-Samadi's release-premium measurement (a pricing fact, not a trade).

Retail-accessible and measured: paying the spread on long 0DTE (negative in every study and locally); selling premium at the NBBO (small positive gross, ≤ 0 net on ETFs; ≈ round trip on SPXW; H15 the one open carry cell); resting limit orders inside the SPXW spread (Fu et al.: ≈ half the cost, 45–70 % midpoint fills — untested locally, see L1); leaving near-worthless short legs to expire (H14, adopted); skipping FOMC and month-end afternoons (H17, adopted); choosing SPXW over SPY for fee burden and settlement (S09: does not change the sign of a long rule; H16: removes the ETF after-hours exercise problem).
