# L3 — stock and option characteristics as predictors of delta-hedged option returns (Goyal–Saretto, Cao–Han, Zhan et al., Bali–Beckmeyer–Mörke–Weigert, Vasquez): cost-realism lens (2026-10-14)

Hypothesis under test (`academic-anomalies.md` §3 and §11, sweep id **L3**): sorting single-name options on IV − HV, idiosyncratic volatility, price level, share issuance, analyst dispersion and the IV term slope predicts delta-hedged option returns — HV−IV long-short straddles 22.5 %/month gross falling to 7.5 % (draft) or 3.9 % (published) at the full quoted spread (Goyal–Saretto); delta-hedged returns fall monotonically in IVOL (Cao–Han); 7 of 12 characteristic sorts survive 100 % of the quoted spread (Zhan et al., 2016 draft); an ML ensemble earns 2.63 %/month at the mid, 1.33 % at 15 % and 0.47 % at 25 % of the quoted spread (BBMW). The claim's own reading: all profits sit in the short (expensive) leg; the retail use is a *negative filter* for long options, adopted if the gross spread between extreme quintiles exceeds 2 % of premium per month with a positive lower bound. Proposition to refute: *a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates.*

Lens: cost realism — does the effect survive the measured spreads and fees of registry `C1` and `execution-costs.md`, with the default of refuted when the source did not net out bid–ask. Grades: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[WP]** working paper, **[EX]** exchange/regulator publication, **[PRAC]** broker or practitioner document stating its own rules, **[SEC]** secondary summary (not evidence), **[LOCAL]** this repository's measurements or this note's own arithmetic, **[VEND]** vendor material (none used). Local context read first: `research/compass/02_coverage_matrix.md`, `07_coverage_report.md`, `05_results.md` (S01–S16b incl. the S11 preliminary row), `04_protocol.md` (§10 gates, amendments A1–A5.2), `registry.jsonl` (all 46 entries: GATE-v1/v2, RULE-separation, G1–G3, C1, H1–H17, DATA-1..3, S01–S16b, S11-prelim), and the sibling notes `academic-anomalies.md`, `execution-costs.md`, `our-evidence-ledger.md`, `L2-option-momentum-cost-realism.md`. ## 0. Verdict

**Refuted.** Three of the four lead papers did net out the bid–ask spread, and at the column a retail marketable order pays the surviving strategies are *writing* delta-hedged single-name options at 0.3–1.0 % per month of hedge capital before margin financing — an institutional short-vol book, not a retail long-option edge. The long-only expression, the only one inside the Compass scope, has a negative mean in every quintile of every sort. As a filter it is not an edge: on the contracts Compass actually trades its expected benefit is five cents per trade. Specifically:

1. **At the retail cost column the papers' own tables leave almost nothing, and what is left is short premium.** Goyal–Saretto Table 8 (1996–2005, effective = 100 % of quoted): long-short straddles 7.5 %/month (t 2.32); the liquid half 9.4 % (t 2.26), the illiquid half 5.9 % (t 1.37); delta-hedged calls and puts 0.6 % and 0.6 % (t 1.32, 1.44), which the authors call "statistically insignificant". Zhan et al. (published, 1996.1–2016.4) Table 6 at 100 % of the quoted spread: four of ten sorts (CH, ISSUE_1Y, TEF, PM) "are no longer profitable"; the survivors are 0.29–1.01 %/month; the margin cost, reported only at the 50 % column, cuts a further ≈ 14 %; the OPRA-measured effective spread of 55 % that the paper relies on is the execution-timed cost of the institutional trades in that tape. BBMW Table 6: 1.33 % (t 10.97) at 15 %, 0.47 % (t 4.41) at 25 %, and "if we increase the effective spreads further to 50 %, the unconditional strategy fails to generate positive returns"; at 25 % the 2016–2019 rolling returns "are zero or turn slightly negative"; nothing is reported at 100 %. Cao–Han compute returns at the closing midpoint; the only cost numbers available are from a secondary summary of their 2009/2010 drafts (2.33 % → 0.22 %, insignificant, at 100 %). Vasquez: no cost analysis retrieved. The published "3.9 %" in the hypothesis could not be verified from any accessible text (the JFE abstract carries no number; the draft says 7.5 %).
2. **The retail column pays the quoted half-spread, and the papers' survivors are conditional on paying less.** Zhan's and BBMW's after-cost claims both invoke Muravyev–Pearson's execution-timing discount ("investors who employ execution timing pay less than 40 % of the conventional effective spreads"; "the reduction can be increased to more than 60 %"). Muravyev–Pearson themselves reserve that discount for traders who can time executions on high-frequency stock moves; a retail marketable order at the NBBO pays the quoted half-spread, and `GATE-v2` disallows mid fills (`C1`'s fill-realism finding). The 2024–2026 single-name closing half-spread in our own cache is a median 11.1 % of mid near the money at 8–30 DTE (p25 5.0 %, p75 28.6 %), the same order as the ≈ 10 % half-spread implied by Zhan's Table 1 (mean quoted spread ≈ 20 %, median 15.6 %): the "100 % of quoted" column has not become cheaper for a single-name option executed at the close.
3. **Everything a retail account adds on top of the paper's cost model moves the short leg further down.** FINRA 4210 margin on a naked single-name call (premium + 20 % of the underlying, the same CBOE rule Zhan et al. apply); financing of the delta-hedge shares at the broker's margin rate — Fidelity 12.075 % under $25 k (base 10.825 % since 2026-09-18), Robinhood 5.25 % under $50 k (as of 2026-09-17) — against the LIBOR financing the papers assume: on the $5,000 of delta shares behind one ATM call on a $100 stock that is $50 (1.01 % of hedge capital) or $22 (0.44 %) per month against the paper's ≈ $17 (0.33 % at 4 %), i.e. +0.1 to +0.7 points per month of drag, the size of the surviving sorts; the highest options approval tier; American-style assignment on the short leg with the 16:00–17:30 exercise window and SPY-style ex-dividend exercise (`H16`, FINRA 2/3/21); and breadth — Zhan's decile portfolios hold ≈ 79 names each (792 stocks per month in the 2016 draft), a retail one-lot book holds five to ten, which multiplies the monthly standard deviation of the spread several-fold and destroys the t-statistics.
4. **The long-only expression is negative in every quintile.** Heston et al. Table 2, Panel B (1996–2019, zero-delta straddles): IV − HV quintiles −1.86 / −3.65 / −4.54 / −6.82 / −12.20 % per month (high-minus-low −10.34 %, t −9.48); idiosyncratic volatility −5.43 / −4.74 / −4.76 / −5.40 / −7.93 % (H−L −2.49 %, t −1.99); IV term spread −9.95 / −6.46 / −4.99 / −4.29 / −2.78 % (H−L +7.17 %, t 7.92). The best long quintile of any sort loses 1.9 % of premium per month before a single spread is paid. Goyal–Saretto's own Table 3 (1996–2005) has the top HV−IV straddle decile at +12.6 %, which Heston's longer-sample quintile replication does not reproduce. BBMW's full-sample delta-hedged return is −0.01 % mean, −0.34 % median per month; Zhan's returns are explicitly returns *to writing*.
5. **As a filter the benefit is bounded and, on Compass contracts, unmeasurable.** Dropping the worst IV − HV quintile raises the mean of the remaining long straddles from −5.81 to −4.22 % per month (+1.60 points); the worst IVOL quintile +0.57; the lowest-slope quintile +1.06 (Heston Table 2 arithmetic, §3.2). On S03's measured contracts (mean entry premium $222 per contract, mean hold 117 minutes) +1.60 points per month is **$0.05 per trade** against a $4.31 round trip and a $62.4 per-trade standard deviation: ≈ 6 million trades to put a 95 % lower bound above zero. On a 30–45-DTE single-name call held 21 sessions it is ≈ $7 per contract per month against a $36 round trip at `C1`'s intraday half-spreads ($103 at the cache's closing median) and ≈ $90–130 of theta. It lowers the expected loss of a long-option book whose entries carry no information (S04, S06, S07, S08); it cannot produce a positive expectancy, which the hypothesis text concedes.
6. **The measurement itself is now contested in ways that bear most on exactly these papers.** Goyenko–Zhang (closing-quote bias of delta-hedged returns, > 100 bp per day for daily returns); Eberbach–Molnar–Schuster–Uhrig-Homburg (Oct 2025): the mean bias of delta-hedged returns "increases with hedge frequency", standard tests "spuriously detect option-return premiums associated with underlying illiquidity", and "existing bias adjustments fail" — their list of affected designs names Cao and Han (2013), Zhan et al. (2022) and Bali et al. (2023); Duarte, Jones, Khorram, Mo & Wang, "Too Good to Be True: Look-Ahead Bias in Empirical Options Research", *RFS* forthcoming (DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhag061): some high-return option strategies look attractive only because of filters that use information unavailable at formation, and unusually high Sharpe ratios are the warning sign (abstract-level; text not retrieved, no finding attributed to a named paper). Zhan's annualized Sharpe of 2.4 and BBMW's 2.06 are the kind of number that paper warns about.
7. **The suggested test cannot run as written and could not resolve its own hurdle.** Issuance, dispersion, profitability, cash holding, external financing and distress (Zhan's sorts) need Compustat/IBES-type data we do not hold; the S03 "smoke test" on three index ETFs has no cross-section; the single-name option EOD on hand is 876 event-selected chains on 184 roots (2023-12 → 2026-10); the stock-EOD lake is 2023 → 2026; and with the papers' own monthly standard deviations (≈ 15–21 % on the quintile spreads) a 2 %-per-month spread needs ≈ 220–450 months for a positive lower bound against 172 months of vendor history (2012-06 → 2026-10) and 34 months on the lake today (§4).

## 1. What the primary sources say about costs

### 1.1 Goyal & Saretto, "Cross-section of option returns and volatility", *JFE* 94(2) 2009, 310–326 [PR]; March 2008 draft read — http://www.ruf.rice.edu/~jgsfss/goyal_041808.pdf

- **Design.** Sort on log(HV) − log(IV) (one-month ATM); straddles of one call and one put, and delta-hedged calls/puts using the IvyDB delta, "held until expiration and not rebalanced during the holding period"; returns "computed using the mid-point price as a reference"; sample 1996–2005 (every table). Table 3 Panel A: straddle decile returns "go from −9.9 % to 12.6 %", long-short 22.5 % with a 31.4 % monthly standard deviation, minimum month −40.5 %, monthly Sharpe 0.718; delta-hedged calls (puts) 10−1 = 2.7 % (2.6 %), SD 4.4 % (3.8 %).
- **§5.2 Transaction costs.** Effective spread assumed at 50 %, 75 % and 100 % of the quoted spread, charged "only at the initiation of the portfolio since we terminate the portfolio at the expiration of the option"; stock costs at exercise from TAQ effective spreads or Hanna–Ready. **Table 8 Panel A** (10−1 straddles, MidP / 50 / 75 / 100 %): all 0.225 (7.83) / 0.140 (4.27) / 0.107 (3.30) / **0.075 (2.32)**; low-spread half 0.182 / 0.136 / 0.114 / **0.094 (2.26)**; high-spread half 0.277 / 0.142 / 0.099 / **0.059 (1.37)**; low-volume 0.234 → 0.052 (1.38); high-volume 0.226 → 0.103 (2.18). **Panel B** (10−1 delta-hedged): calls 0.027 (6.68) / 0.010 (2.16) / 0.008 (1.74) / **0.006 (1.32)**; puts 0.026 / 0.013 / 0.009 / **0.006 (1.44)**. Text: "For effective spreads equal to the quoted spreads, the delta-hedged calls (puts) have statistically insignificant returns of around 0.6 % (0.6 %)." Margin: "Margin requirements for these options are relatively low and do not materially affect the execution of our strategies" — a statement, not a computation. Footnote 22 concedes the design trades only on Tuesday closing quotes.
- **Reading for the lens.** The paper did net out the spread and the retail column is 7.5 % for a long-short *straddle* book whose short side writes the highest-IV straddles on ≈ 10 % of the optionable universe. The long leg alone is not reported after costs, and in Heston's 1996–2019 replication it is −1.86 % per month gross. The published abstract (IDEAS/RePEc, SUFE listing, Crossref metadata) contains no after-cost number; "3.9 %" is unverified here.

### 1.2 Zhan, Han, Cao & Tong, "Option Return Predictability", *RFS* 35(3) 2022, 1394–1442 [PR]; published post-print read — https://utoronto.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/5c530340-f282-4a35-a281-76336e3757b1/content ; April 2016 draft — https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2016/annual-conference/investment-finance/226-option-return-predictability.pdf

- **Design.** OptionMetrics January 1996 – April 2016 (244 months); returns to writing delta-neutral calls, gain scaled by (Δ·S − C), midpoint quotes, one-month hold without rebalancing (daily-rebalanced version as a robustness check); stock-value-weighted 10−1 spreads average 1.56 % per month, monthly Sharpe 0.7, "annualized Sharpe ratio is 2.4". The 2016 draft's Table 1: mean quoted bid–ask spread of the ATM options "about 20 %, with a median of 15.6 %"; 792 stocks per month.
- **§2.4 and Table 6 (published).** Effective spread at 25 / 50 / 75 / 100 % of quoted, and an OPRA subsample (2003.5–2016.4) with actual effective spreads averaging 55 % of quoted. At **100 %**: CFV 0.29 (2.40), CH −0.06, DISP 0.40 (2.94), ISSUE_1Y 0.12 (1.05), ISSUE_5Y 0.33 (3.28), TEF 0.12 (0.77), −PM 0.26 (1.55), −Ln(PRICE) 1.01 (5.48), −PROFIT 0.38 (2.28), −ZS 0.45 (2.43). Text: "four option strategies (those based on CH, ISSUE_1Y, TEF and PM) are no longer profitable at 100 % effective option spread." Margin: CBOE naked-call rule, financed at option-maturity LIBOR; "further adjusting for option margin requirement reduces the stock-value-weighed (10-1) return spread by about 14 % on average" at the 50 % column (DISP 0.95 → 0.84, −Ln(PRICE) 2.80 → 2.68, ISSUE_1Y 0.34 → 0.22); no margin-adjusted number is reported at 100 %. The paper then leans on Muravyev–Pearson: the 32 % average reduction in the OPRA subsample "is likely an overestimate of the impact of transaction costs, since … investors who employ execution timing pay less than 40 % of the conventional effective spreads."
- **Reading for the lens.** The "7 of 12 at 100 %" of the 2016 draft became "6 of 10 at 100 %, before margin" in the published version; the strongest survivor is −Ln(PRICE), i.e. write calls on the lowest-priced stocks, and the paper's own footnote says "stocks with lower price tend to have higher percentage bid-ask spreads" — the sort is partly a sort on the spread it then charges at an assumed fraction. The margin adjustment is at LIBOR; a retail account finances at 5.25–12.075 % (§2).

### 1.3 Bali, Beckmeyer, Mörke & Weigert, "Option Return Predictability with Machine Learning and Big Data", *RFS* 36(9) 2023, 3548–3602 [PR]; CFR WP 21-08 read — https://www.cfr-cologne.de/download/workingpaper/cfr-21-08.pdf

- **Design.** 1996–2020, 215 months, delta-hedged returns with daily rebalancing; Table 1 full-sample delta-hedged return mean −0.01 %, median −0.34 % per month; nonlinear ensemble (N-En) high-minus-low decile at the mid 2.63 %, Sharpe 2.06 (sibling note).
- **§5.2.2 and Table 6.** "So far we have assumed that the investor can buy and sell each option at mid-point"; effective spread at 15 % and 25 % of the OptionMetrics quoted spread, charged in and out for options that outlive the month and in only for those that expire. Full sample **1.334 (10.97), SR 1.171 at 15 %; 0.474 (4.41), SR 0.431 at 25 %**; "If we increase the effective spreads further to 50 %, the unconditional strategy fails to generate positive returns." By bucket at 25 %: τ ≤ 90 ATM 1.007, OTM calls 2.057, OTM puts 2.725; τ > 90 ATM −0.291, ITM calls −1.624; footnote 19: OTM short-term puts keep significance at 50 %, ATM short-term and OTM calls are positive but not significant. Fig. 5 text: at 25 % "the notable exception of the years 2016 to 2019, where returns are zero or turn slightly negative"; significance at 25 % "is only observed for the beginning of the sample." The authors close the section with Muravyev–Pearson's 60 % execution-timing discount as the reason to be "confident that the signals N-En generates are valuable".
- **Reading for the lens.** No number at 100 %; the 0 → 15 → 25 → 50 path (2.63 → 1.33 → 0.47 → ≤ 0) puts the retail column below zero; the recent sub-period is zero at a quarter of the retail cost. The strategy is 1,747 names of monthly long-short delta-hedged positions rebalanced daily — a dealer's book.

### 1.4 Cao & Han, "Cross section of option returns and idiosyncratic stock volatility", *JFE* 108(1) 2013, 231–249 [PR] — https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v108y2013i1p231-249.html ; https://optionmetrics.com/research/j-cao-and-b-han-cross-section-of-option-returns-and-idiosyncratic-stock-volatility/

- Published abstract (verbatim segments): "delta-hedged equity option return decreases monotonically with an increase in the idiosyncratic volatility of the underlying stock"; "consistent with market imperfections and constrained financial intermediaries"; "Dealers charge a higher premium for options on high idiosyncratic volatility stocks due to their higher arbitrage costs"; limits-to-arbitrage proxies reduce the relation "by about 40 %". No cost figure in the abstract; the published tables were not retrieved (Rotman mirror 404, EFMA mirror 503, SSRN 403).
- Secondary [SEC], CXO Advisory on the July 2009 and January 2010 drafts — https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=2210 ; https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=2466 : ≈ 160,000 ATM delta-hedged positions on > 5,000 stocks, 1996–2006, daily rebalanced, held to maturity, executed at the midpoint of closing quotes; delta-hedged long calls lose 0.49 % of stock value (4.32 % of call value); the volatility-sorted spread 2.33 % per month at the mid, **1.25 % at 50 %, 0.73 % at 75 %, 0.22 % (not significant) at 100 % of the quoted spread**; the summary's conclusion is that only low-friction traders such as market makers can capture it.
- **Reading for the lens.** The mechanism the authors give — dealers' arbitrage costs — is the cost the retail account would have to pay to be on the dealer's side. If the 2020–2023 spread compression (`execution-costs.md` §2.3) lowered dealers' costs, the paper's own channel says the premium shrank with them.

### 1.7 Measurement critiques

- Goyenko & Zhang, "Price Pressures and Noise in Option Returns", McGill WP May 2020 [WP] — https://ruslangoyenko.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Option-Returns-MAY-24-2020.pdf : delta-hedged and straddle returns from end-of-day closing prices are biased upward, "more than 100 bps per day", with the closing quoted spread ≈ 50 bp wider than the rest of the day (sibling note §0.2). For a one-month hold the bias sits in the entry mark; for the daily-rebalanced designs (Cao–Han, BBMW) it compounds.
- Eberbach, Molnar, Schuster & Uhrig-Homburg, "Asset Pricing Results in Options Markets: True, Spurious, or Overlooked?", October 2025 [WP] — https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Derivatives2025/Molnar.pdf . Abstract: "the rebalancing frequency of delta-hedged option portfolios materially influences biases in measured average returns when underlying prices are subject to microstructure noise … the mean bias of delta-hedged returns increases with hedge frequency, leading standard asset pricing tests to spuriously detect option-return premiums associated with underlying illiquidity. Genuine return premiums related to underlying liquidity and volatility are obscured. Existing bias adjustments fail to correct this distortion." Their list of dynamically hedged designs: Bakshi–Kapadia (2003), Cao and Han (2013), Frazzini–Pedersen (2022), Bali et al. (2023), Vasquez–Xiao (2024); static: Goyal–Saretto (2009), Zhan et al. (2022).
- Duarte, Jones, Khorram, Mo & Wang, "Too Good to Be True: Look-Ahead Bias in Empirical Options Research", *RFS* advance article, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhag061 [PR; abstract not retrieved: OUP paywall, SSRN 403, ResearchGate 403, OpenAlex 429] — https://academic.oup.com/rfs/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/rfs/hhag061/8722307 ; SSRN 4590083; listed in the WFA 2024 program (read). Content available only from search-engine summaries of the SSRN listing and a paywalled CXO post [SEC]: some high-return option strategies look attractive only because of filters that use information not available at portfolio formation; unusually high Sharpe ratios may signal the bias; the two strategies re-examined are described only by type (a stock-characteristic sort, 2012-01 → 2020-06; an options-liquidity-provision strategy, 1996-01 → 2016-04). No finding is attributed here to a named paper.

## 3. Arithmetic

### 3.1 The short leg as a retail one-lot (why "not retail-accessible" is a cost statement, not a label)

Take Zhan's strongest survivor at the retail column, −Ln(PRICE): 1.01 % per month (t 5.48) of (Δ·S − C) after 100 % of the quoted spread, before margin. On a $100 stock, 35 % IV, 40 DTE: ATM call ≈ $4.60; Δ·S − C ≈ $4,540 per contract; 1.01 % = **$45.9 per contract-month** gross of margin financing. Against it: (a) the Reg-T margin ≈ $2,460 plus $5,000 of delta shares, ≈ $7,460 of capital per name, so the net return on capital is ≈ 0.6 % per month ≈ 7 % a year before the items below; (b) Zhan's own margin adjustment (LIBOR on the margin amount) takes ≈ 14 % of the spread at the 50 % column, and is not reported at 100 %; (c) if the delta shares are margined, retail financing at 12.075 % costs $50 per month on $5,000 — more than the whole $45.9 — and at Robinhood's 5.25 % $22; (d) the sort itself selects the lowest-priced stocks, whose options sit in the wide tail of our closing-spread table (p75 28.6 % half-spread), so the "100 % of quoted" charge for this decile in 2024–2026 is larger, not smaller, than the paper's sample average; (e) one lot per name on a $100 stock caps the book at ten names for $75 k, where the monthly standard deviation of the ten-name spread is several times the ≈ 2.2 % (1.56 % ÷ Sharpe 0.7) of the paper's 79-name deciles; (f) American assignment through the 16:00–17:30 window on every short call that finishes in the money, with the share leg to unwind on T+1. DISP (0.40 %, t 2.94) and −PROFIT (0.38 %, t 2.28) are a third of the PRICE number and need I/B/E/S and Compustat inputs we do not hold. For BBMW the retail column is not reported and the 0 → 15 → 25 → 50 % path (2.63 → 1.33 → 0.47 → ≤ 0) crosses zero well before 100 %. For Goyal–Saretto the 7.5 % (t 2.32) long-short straddle return at the retail column is a book of ≈ 150 naked straddles short against ≈ 150 long, which Reg-T margins at the greater side plus the other premium per name.

### 3.2 The filter on Compass contracts

Heston Table 2 (zero-delta straddles, monthly, 1996–2019) is the only source that reports every quintile of every L3 characteristic in one sample. Excluding the worst quintile and holding the rest equal-weighted:

| characteristic | mean of all five quintiles | mean of the four kept | improvement | per session (÷ 21) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IV − HV (drop the top quintile) | −5.81 % / month | −4.22 % | **+1.60 points** | +0.076 % |
| idiosyncratic volatility (drop the top) | −5.65 % | −5.08 % | +0.57 | +0.027 % |
| IV term slope (drop the lowest slope) | −5.69 % | −4.63 % | +1.06 | +0.051 % |

Those are percentages of premium on a one-month ATM straddle; a directional single-leg call carries the same volatility-premium drag on its time value, so the filter's benefit per unit of premium is at most this size and the directional component, which dominates a Compass trade, is untouched.

- **S03-type contract** (`results/v1/s03_weekly_index_etfs_baseline/trades.parquet`, 818 fills, read with polars [LOCAL]): mean entry ask $2.22 per share (median $1.86; SPY $2.32, QQQ $2.67, IWM $1.65) → **$222 per contract**; mean hold 117 minutes (median 46); measured round trip $4.31; per-trade P&L standard deviation $62.4. The IV − HV exclusion is worth $222 × 1.60 % = $3.55 per contract-month = $0.17 per session = **$0.05 per 117-minute hold**; the hypothesis's own 2 %-per-month adoption hurdle is $0.06 per hold. To put a 95 % lower bound above zero on $0.05 with a $62.4 standard deviation takes (1.96 × 62.4 / 0.05)² ≈ **6 million trades**; on $2 per trade, 3,740. The filter is a single-name effect in any case, and S03's contracts are index ETFs.
- **30–45-DTE single-name ATM call held 21 sessions** (A5.2-type): premium ≈ 4.6 % of spot, $460 per contract on a $100 stock; the IV − HV exclusion is worth ≈ $7.4 per contract per month; the round trip at `C1`'s intraday 3.5–4.1 % half-spread is ≈ $35 + $1.40 of fees, at the cache's closing median (11.1 %) ≈ $102 + $1.40; theta over the hold ≈ $90–130 (`our-evidence-ledger.md` §3.1 arithmetic). The filter recovers a fifth of one round trip and under a tenth of the theta; it is sub-dominant to every other term in the trade.
- **What the filter cannot do.** In the papers' sorts the best long quintile is −1.86 % per month (IV − HV low), −4.74 % (IVOL), −2.78 % (slope). A long-option book filtered on all three still loses on the volatility leg before costs; the only way it earns is a directional entry with information, which S04/S06 (index and 34 Tier A names), S07 and S08 have not found. The hypothesis says this itself: "It cannot make a zero-information entry (S04/S06) profitable."

### 3.3 Resolution of the suggested test against the papers' own dispersion

From Heston's t-statistics on 282 months, the monthly standard deviation of the quintile spreads is ≈ 18 % (IV − HV H−L), ≈ 21.5 % (IV − HV top quintile alone), ≈ 21 % (IVOL H−L), ≈ 15 % (term slope H−L) [LOCAL arithmetic]. A 2 %-per-month gross spread — the adoption hurdle in the hypothesis — needs ≈ 322 / 446 / 424 / 222 months for a 95 % lower bound above zero. The vendor history is 172 months (2012-06 → 2026-10); the stock-EOD lake that defines the universe is 34 months (2024-01 → 2026-10). The paper-sized spreads (−10 % IV − HV, +7 % slope) would resolve in ≈ 12–17 months, so the test as designed can only confirm what Heston already reports for 1996–2019; it cannot adjudicate its own 2 % hurdle, and a sub-period (2020 →, where BBMW's 25 %-cost returns are already zero) is hopeless at any hurdle.

## 5. Registry cross-check and disposition

- Nothing closed is reopened. `S15` (rich-implied 0DTE straddles lose more: the VRP level condition has the wrong sign), `S10`/`S10-rerun` (verticals pay two spreads), `S11-prelim` (single-name short 0.30/0.15 verticals held 21 sessions lost −$193 per spread in 2024–2026 with random-date controls at −$187: in this window a short single-name option position lost regardless of selection), `H14`/`H16` (exit-side spread and after-hours exercise on American short legs), `C1`/`GATE-v2` (NBBO fills, no mid), `H15`'s scope note (short premium outside the Compass universe unless the owner opens it), `S14` (cross-sectional stock sorts un-resolvable on 2024–2026 — the same window problem) all stand and all bear on L3 in the direction of the verdict.
- `execution-costs.md` §11 X4 (restrict single-name option tests to roots with closing near-ATM half-spread ≤ 4 % and price them intraday) is the only cost rule L3 adds a reason for; it is already proposed.
- **Disposition:** do not open an L3 hypothesis id; do not spend the pull budget on a characteristic sort ahead of the S11 completion and the S06 Tier A record; do not adopt the exclusion as a default contract rule on the papers' evidence alone — it is free to apply but, at $0.05 per Compass trade, unmeasurable, and a rule the engine cannot verify is a rule the gates cannot audit. If a cheap diagnostic is wanted once the S11 pull completes: tabulate the 21-session long-call P&L at the NBBO of the S11 *control* chains (random dates, ≈ 1,200 events) by IV − HV and price-level tercile, with the stated expectation that n ≈ 1,200 cannot resolve a tercile difference below ≈ 10 % of premium.

## 6. What would reverse the verdict

A pre-registered replication on the full optionable universe at 10:00 NBBO marks (not the close), 2012-06 → 2026-10, showing a *long-only* characteristic-filtered straddle or call portfolio with a positive mean after the measured half-spreads and fees (`C1`/C2) — which no paper reports and Heston's Table 2 contradicts in every quintile — or a long-short version whose short leg is sized to FINRA 4210 margin, financed at a retail margin rate, executed at the bid/ask with a stated loss under ±15 % single-name shocks, and still above ≈ +2 % per month net so that ≈ 170 months could give a positive lower bound at the papers' 15–20 % monthly standard deviation. The papers' retail-column numbers (0.3–1.0 % per month before margin financing) are a quarter of that.
