# L4 — lottery-like options as a hard exclusion rule (Boyer–Vorkink, Byun–Kim, Bali–Murray; the retail-loss leg): cost-realism lens (2026-10-14)

Hypothesis under test (`academic-anomalies.md` §4 and §11, sweep id **L4**): ex-ante total-skewness quintile spreads of 10–50 % per week (Boyer–Vorkink), calls on the most lottery-like stocks underperforming by 10–20 % per month (Byun–Kim), risk-neutral skewness negatively priced (Bali–Murray); retail is the buyer of these contracts and loses (Bryzgalova et al.; Beckmeyer et al.: ≈ 60 % of 0DTE retail losses are transaction costs; de Silva–Smith–So). Proposed use: a hard exclusion rule — drop contracts with ask < $0.10 or premium < 1 % of spot for ≥ 5-day expirations, the top quintile of the option smirk (OTM-put IV − ATM-call IV), the top quintile of MAX and of IVOL — measured as the gross return spread between excluded and retained contracts on the S03 cache and "Tier A chains" at the NBBO, adopted as a default rule (like H14/H17) if the excluded set underperforms with a positive lower bound; expected size "zero cost … does not create a positive lane". Proposition to refute: *a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates.*

Lens: cost realism — does the effect survive the measured spreads and fees of registry `C1` and `execution-costs.md`, defaulting to refuted when the source did not net out bid–ask. Grades: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[WP]** working paper, **[EX]** exchange/regulator publication, **[PRAC]** practitioner document, **[SEC]** secondary summary (not evidence), **[LOCAL]** this repository's measurements or this note's arithmetic, **[VEND]** vendor material (none used). Local context read first: `research/compass/02_coverage_matrix.md`, `07_coverage_report.md`, `05_results.md` (S01–S16b, S11-prelim), `04_protocol.md` (§10, A1–A6), `registry.jsonl` (all entries: GATE-v1/v2, RULE-separation, G1–G3, C1, H1–H17, DATA-1..3, S01–S16b, S11-prelim), and the sibling notes `academic-anomalies.md`, `execution-costs.md`, `our-evidence-ledger.md`, `retail-performance.md`, `L3-characteristics-cost-realism.md`, `L3-characteristics-delta-hedged-refutation.md`, `L3-characteristics-delta-hedged-data-and-prior-work-check.md`. ## 0. Verdict

**Refuted.** L4 is not an edge by its own statement, its thresholds do not measure what the papers measure, and on the only cache it names the "excluded" contracts do not underperform. Specifically:

1. **It has no expectancy to be positive after costs.** The hypothesis text concedes "does not create a positive lane". A rule that removes contracts from a long-option book whose entries carry no information (S04, S06 index and Tier A, S07, S08: nothing clears A1/A2) has an expected P&L that is at best a smaller loss. Under the proposition's wording — "plausibly positive after costs" — the claim fails before any cost is charged.
2. **None of the three lead papers nets out the bid–ask spread in any text retrieved; the default rule applies.** Boyer–Vorkink: returns "based on end-of-day bid-ask midpoints … assuming it is held to expiration", returns "ignore transaction fees for opening and closing", and "option bid-ask spread increases dramatically with skewness" so sellers near the bid "generally cannot capture the skewness premium" (CXO's reading of the 2010 draft [SEC]; the published JF text was not retrievable). Byun–Kim and Bali–Murray: the publisher abstracts contain no cost statement and the full texts were not retrievable. Frazzini–Pedersen (the moneyness/maturity dimension of the same result): "option returns are computed from the bid/ask midpoint", and "trading costs tend to be higher for options than the underlying spot markets" is given as a design motivation, not a netting [WP, read]. The only cost-netted numbers in this literature belong to the *short* side (writing the highest-skew, highest-gamma single-name contracts), which is outside the Compass long-option scope (`H15` scope note) and, per Boyer–Vorkink's own spread finding, not capturable at the bid.
3. **The "premium < 1 % of spot" threshold measures IV·√T, not skewness, and would delete most of Compass's liquid-index weekly book.** For a 0.45-delta call, premium ÷ spot ≈ 0.4·σ·√T: below 1 % for every ≤ 14-DTE contract at ≤ 15 % IV and for every ≤ 7-DTE contract at ≤ 20 % IV, while a 0.45-delta 5-DTE call on a 35 %-IV single name (1.36 %) and a 0.15-delta 30-DTE call on a 60 %-IV name (1.24 %) are retained (§3.1, Black–Scholes). On S03's 818 measured fills — the baseline's 0.30–0.60-delta contracts on SPY/QQQ/IWM weeklies, 2017 → 2026 — **80.1 % have entry premium < 1 % of spot** (SPY 85 %, QQQ 79 %, IWM 73 %; 91 % at 1 DTE, 58 % at 7 DTE; 98 % in 2017 and 2026, 43 % in 2022 when IV was 33 %), and **0 %** have an ask < $0.10 (median entry ask $1.56 in the "excluded" group). The rule as written removes the at-the-money index contracts that are cheapest to trade (`C1` 0.5–1.0 % half-spread) and keeps the single-name contracts that Boyer–Vorkink's sorts place in the high-skewness quintiles.
4. **On the S03 cache the excluded set does not underperform.** Excluded (n 655): mean **+$0.40 per contract [−3.60, +4.33]**, +0.73 % of premium [−1.27, +2.64]; retained (n 163): +$4.76, +1.55 % of premium; excluded minus retained **−$4.36 [−23.96, +14.83]** per contract and **−0.82 points of premium [−5.78, +4.14]** (session-clustered bootstrap). The adoption test the hypothesis sets itself — "the excluded set underperforms with a positive lower bound" — fails on the cache it names, and the registry already said why: S03's contracts are "zero, not negative" (+$0.02 vs random); there is no premium in them to avoid. Both groups are quoted at the same relative spread (median 1.25 % vs 1.29 % of mid), so the exclusion does not even buy a cheaper cost column.
5. **Where the rule would bite, the measurement is a tautology of the cost model.** Beyond ≈ 2 % OTM the median same-day index contract is quoted $0.00 × $0.01 (ETFs) or $0.00 × $0.05 (SPXW) at every minute; single-name 2–5 % OTM contracts carry closing half-spreads of 17–66 % of mid (`execution-costs.md` §2.2). A contract bought at the ask on a zero bid is −100 % at the bid immediately; the "gross return spread at NBBO" between such contracts and 0.45-delta contracts is the spread, which `C1`, `H14` and `GATE-v2` already charge in full. Nothing about skewness preference is learned, and nothing in the engine changes.
6. **The smirk leg has the wrong sign for calls and is not the papers' measure.** Boyer–Vorkink sort on ex-ante *physical* total skewness of the option's own return (from the underlying's return model, horizon and moneyness); Byun–Kim on the *stock's* lottery-likeness (Kumar's MAX / IVOL / low price); Bali–Murray on model-free *risk-neutral* skewness. None uses Xing–Zhang–Zhao's smirk (OTM-put IV − ATM-call IV). The top-quintile smirk is the names whose OTM puts are dearest relative to ATM calls — for a long *call* that is the cheap end, not the lottery end; Heston et al. Table 2 (1996–2019, via the L3 note) puts the IV-smirk-slope sort on straddles at +0.49 % per month high-minus-low (t 0.78): no pricing content. What the smirk does predict is the *stock* (−16 bp per week for the steepest quintile, XZZ 2010), which is `L5`'s underlying-level claim, not a lottery-pricing claim.
7. **The MAX/IVOL leg has no Compass lane to filter and no measurable size.** It is a single-name cross-sectional effect; Compass has no single-name long-option lane (S07 breakouts/pullbacks −31 to −97 bp, S08 no drift, S11-prelim verticals −$193 per spread with controls at −$187). The S07 universe (price ≥ $5, dollar volume ≥ $50 M) already removes Kumar's low-price leg; the L3 refutation already assigned IVOL and price level to L4 as a cost-free default "with no claim of size"; Hu–Jacobs's "calls on low-vol names" is the mechanical elasticity of a call to the stock premium (abstract only; no cost analysis retrieved) and adds no expectancy. On a hypothetical 30–45-DTE single-name call the L3 arithmetic bounds a one-quintile exclusion at ≈ $7 per contract-month against a $36–103 round trip and $90–130 of theta.
8. **The retail-loss leg is a cost statement the registry already prices, and its sign is contested.** Beckmeyer et al.: "roughly 60 % of daily losses are the result of transaction costs" [WP]; de Silva–Smith–So: 9 of the 10–14 points lost on high-EAV earnings trades is the half-spread, and retail there buys *at-the-money* straddles, not lottery contracts [PR]; Amaya et al. (Cboe data grant, conflict flagged): customer SLIM trades net **+$0.34 M per day (t 0.29)** Jan 2020–Jun 2023 and **+$2.07 M per day (t 1.19)** Jan 2020–Jun 2021, with purchased calls positive because the S&P 500 rose 33 % and 38 % over the two windows — "our results provide no evidence that Cboe customer SLIM trades are unprofitable" [WP, read]; its Table 1 (Jan–Jun 2021): 72 % of retail SLIM trades are at the money (quoted 9.2 %, effective 4.0 %), 23 % are 0.1–1 OTM (28.1 % / 12.6 %), and only 0.5 % are beyond 1 OTM (53–62 % quoted, 24–29 % effective). Bogousslavsky–Muravyev (via `retail-performance.md`): the realized skewness of retail option trades is negative, "not the lottery payoff retail is assumed to seek". A retail account that pays the quoted half-spread at the NBBO (GATE-v2) is already charged the whole of what these papers call the loss.
9. **Not testable as written, and the window cannot carry a gate.** The S03 cache has no cross-section (three ETFs: MAX and IVOL quintiles do not exist); "Tier A chains" do not exist (S05 is underlying minute bars; the only single-name option EOD on hand is 876 event-selected chains at 30–45 DTE on 184 roots, 2023-12 →); MAX and IVOL need the stock-EOD lake (2023 → 2026 on disk; 2012–2022 paused); the pull for a real cross-sectional test is the L2/L3 pull (≈ 37 hours for Tier A, 9–13 days for the S07 universe on the single login). With per-contract weekly return standard deviations of 150–300 % for short-dated single-name contracts, a 5-point weekly difference between arms needs ≈ 3,500–14,000 contract-weeks per arm for a positive lower bound — feasible in count, but spent on a rule with no expectancy.

## 1. What the primary sources say about costs

### 1.1 Boyer & Vorkink, "Stock Options as Lotteries", *Journal of Finance* 69(4) 2014, 1485–1527 [PR] — https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v69y2014i4p1485-1527.html (DOI 10.1111/jofi.12152)

- Published abstract (IDEAS, read): "total skewness exhibits a strong negative relationship with average option returns"; "Differences in average returns for option portfolios sorted on ex ante skewness range from 10% to 50% per week, even after controlling for risk"; "these large premiums compensate intermediaries for bearing unhedgeable risk when accommodating investor demand for lottery-like options." No sample period, return convention or cost statement on the page.
- Working-paper details per CXO Advisory [SEC] — https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=13232 (read this session): sample "approximately 1996 through 2009"; end-of-day bid and ask quotes, open interest, volume; 40 portfolios per formation date (eight expiration horizons × expected-skewness quintiles), equal-weighted; "Based on end-of-day bid-ask midpoints, they calculate the return for each option assuming it is held to expiration (ignoring the possibility of early exercise)"; low-minus-high skewness spreads for 7-day options: calls +1.4 % minus −35.4 % = +36.8 %, puts −5.6 % minus −60.6 % = +55.0 %; spreads "always larger for puts than calls" and increasing as the horizon shortens; "Option bid-ask spread increases dramatically with skewness", so investors selling highly skewed options near the bid "generally cannot capture the skewness premium"; returns "ignore transaction fees for opening and closing (when in-the-money) positions", and "including these frictions would lower reported returns".
- Reading for the lens: midpoint, hold-to-expiry, no fees, no spread netting, 1996–2009. The 10–50 % per week is the *gap* between the deep-OTM ≤ 7-day quintile and the rest; the low-skewness 7-day call quintile itself is +1.4 % per week at the mid, i.e. ≈ 0 after a 0.5–1.4 % half-spread each side on an index product and negative after a single-name spread. The paper's own spread finding is that the premium sits exactly where the quoted spread is widest. Under the lens default (no cost netting retrieved) this source is refuted as an edge; as a description of which contracts lose it is consistent with `C1` and `execution-costs.md` §2.2, which already price those contracts at a half-spread equal to the mid.

### 1.2 Byun & Kim, "Gambling preference and individual equity option returns", *JFE* 122(1) 2016, 155–174 [PR] — https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v122y2016i1p155-174.html (DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2016.06.004)

- Abstract (IDEAS, read; paraphrase): call options on the most lottery-like stocks underperform otherwise similar calls on the least lottery-like stocks by roughly 10–20 % per month; put–call-parity deviations grow with lottery-likeness in the direction of overvalued calls; stronger in high-sentiment periods. No sample period, return convention or cost statement on the page; ScienceDirect full text returned 403.
- Reading for the lens: a cross-sectional single-name result on *stock* lottery characteristics (MAX, IVOL, price); the implementable side is writing calls on those names (Zhan et al.'s −Ln(PRICE) sort is the same end of the universe, and the L3 note shows that leg at 1.01 % per month before retail margin financing, on the lowest-priced stocks whose options sit in the p75 28.6 % closing half-spread tail). Cost treatment unverified → refuted as an edge by default.

### 1.3 Bali & Murray, "Does Risk-Neutral Skewness Predict the Cross-Section of Equity Option Portfolio Returns?", *JFQA* 48(4) 2013, 1145–1171 [PR] — https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109013000410 ; https://optionmetrics.com/research/t-g-bali-and-s-murrary-does-risk-neutral-skewness-predict-the-cross-section-of-equity-option-portfolio-returns

- Abstract (Cambridge Core, read): "skewness assets comprised of two option positions (one long and one short) and a position in the underlying stock", delta and vega removed; "a strong negative relation between risk-neutral skewness and the skewness asset returns, consistent with a positive skewness preference"; not explained by market, size, book-to-market, momentum, reversal, volatility or option-market factors. Full article "temporarily unavailable" on the publisher page; no cost statement visible.
- Reading for the lens: the tradeable object is a three-leg, delta- and vega-neutral position rebalanced on the stock — two option spreads plus a share leg, i.e. three to four spreads per round trip on single names (S10-rerun: "verticals pay two spreads and are worse"); no retail expression exists. Cost treatment unverified → default.

### 1.4 The moneyness/maturity dimension: Frazzini & Pedersen, "Embedded Leverage", NBER w18558, Nov 2012 [WP, read] (published *Review of Asset Pricing Studies* 12(1) 2022) — https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18558/w18558.pdf

- OptionMetrics January 1996 – December 2010; 11,327,382 equity option-months on 7,179 stocks; monthly buy-and-hold with daily delta hedging, "option returns are computed from the bid/ask midpoint"; filters drop zero bids and sub-tick spreads. Results: "high-embedded-leverage options have negative and significant alphas and … alphas are almost monotonic in embedded leverage"; long-low/short-high across maturities 7.5–11.5 % per month (t 6.7–12.0); across moneyness "the alphas of the OTM options are negative and significant", long-short 5.2–7.4 % per month (t 3.79, 3.84) on the 4-factor model and "not statistically significant" on the 5-factor model that includes a straddle factor; "the return on such a strategy mostly reflects the return of the short side, while the return of the long portfolio is swamped." Transaction costs: the paper motivates monthly rebalancing because "trading costs tend to be higher for options than the underlying spot markets" and nets none; the authors are AQR employees (disclosed).
- Reading for the lens: the "cheap, short-dated, OTM options lose" result is the same object as Boyer–Vorkink's and sits in the short leg; the long leg (low embedded leverage = long-dated, in-the-money) is "swamped". Table A2 (open-interest and volume shares) shows the deep-OTM 1-month bucket is 0.21 % of equity-call open interest and 1.36 % of volume in 1996–2010 — the lottery corner is small.

### 1.5 The retail-loss leg

- Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, *JF* 78(6) 2023 [PR] (text carried in `retail-performance.md` §3.1): aggregate retail −$2.1 bn net over Nov 2019–Jun 2021 at a 10-day horizon, gross +$10–13 M per day; indirect costs $6.4 bn vs ≈ $0.9 bn commissions; < 1-week options 12.6 % quoted spread, slightly-OTM 29 %. The loss is the cost, not the forecast.
- Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, SSRN 4404704, Dec 2023 [WP] — https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf (carried in `0dte-microstructure.md` / `retail-performance.md`): identified retail SPX 0DTE −$241 k per day; "Roughly 60 % of daily losses are the result of transaction costs"; > 25 % of single-leg trades expire at −100 %; median single-leg −0.7 %.
- de Silva, Smith & So, *Review of Finance* 30(2) 2026, 489–535 [PR] (the hypothesis dates it 2025) — https://academic.oup.com/rof/article-abstract/30/2/489/8301159 ; draft https://ide.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Retail_Options.pdf (carried in `retail-performance.md` §3.2): retail "primarily purchase at-the-money options" before earnings; 5–9 % lost per position, 10–14 % on high-EAV events, of which 9 points is the half-spread and 9 points the post-event hold. Not a lottery-contract result: ATM, event-driven, cost-dominated.
- Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson & Vasquez, 7 Apr 2025 [WP, read; "supported by a data research grant from The Options Institute, the educational division of Cboe Global Markets"] — https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf ; SSRN 4404704 is Beckmeyer's number, the hypothesis's SSRN link for Amaya is therefore wrong. Cboe/EDGX SLIM trades with trading capacities, open/close flags and true directions, Jan 2020–Jun 2023: net customer SLIM performance +$0.34 M per day (t 0.29); Jan 2020–Jun 2021 +$2.07 M per day (t 1.19); "Purchases of calls and sales of puts display positive performance during both sample periods, while purchases of puts and sales of calls are not. This pattern is to be expected: stock prices, as measured by the S&P 500 index, increased by 33% and 38%"; BPS's −$1.75 M per day for options held to expiration becomes +$0.36 M per day when expiration values replace the last NBBO midpoint; 42.4 % of SLIM trades are closing trades, so "only 25.5 % of opening trades are held to expiration". Net performance "computed using the actual trade price"; "Neither the gross nor net performance reflect any commissions paid." Table 1 (Jan–Jun 2021, frequency-weighted): time to expiry 0 days 13.8 % of trades (quoted 11.7 %, effective 7.1 %), 1 day 22.3 % (8.7 / 4.8), 2–5 days 26.0 % (7.7 / 4.0), 6–10 days 14.9 % (9.6 / 4.3); moneyness at-the-money 72.1 % (9.2 / 4.0), 0.1–1 OTM 22.9 % (28.1 / 12.6), 1–2 OTM 0.32 % (52.8 / 24.2), beyond 2 OTM 0.20 % (61.8 / 29.1); trades below $250 39.5 % (23.7 / 10.8).
- Reading for the lens: the one paper with true directions and open/close flags finds the retail sign follows the market and is statistically zero; the contracts the hypothesis calls "exactly these" (beyond 1 OTM) are 0.5 % of retail trades; and the cost column of those contracts (53–62 % quoted) is what makes them lose. The engine prices that column already.

Not one of the 818 fills has an ask below $0.10 (S01: 0 of 2,886; S09: 0 of 1,229). The retained group's larger dollar mean is a larger premium (2× the ask) in the high-IV years, not a better return per dollar; per unit of premium the two groups are inside ±1 point of each other and both are inside zero. To put a 95 % lower bound above zero on a −$4.36 difference with these standard deviations would need ≈ (1.96 × 55 / 4.36)² ≈ 600 pairs per arm if the sign were right; it is not.

### 3.3 The 0DTE book the rule exempts

S01's 2,886 fills have a median entry premium of 0.19 % of spot (p90 0.35 %; median ask $0.815); S09's 1,229 SPXW fills 0.16 % (p90 0.28 %). Every one is "cheap and short-dated" by any threshold, and the hypothesis's "≥ 5-day" qualifier exempts them — the class nearest Boyer–Vorkink's ≤ 7-day high-skew bucket, and the class where the registry measured the losses (S01 −$4.19, S09 −$46.18 per contract). The exemption is consistent only if the rule is understood as a proxy for the delta band Compass already applies (0.30–0.60, ask ≥ $0.05, spread < 12 %), in which case it adds nothing.

### 3.4 Upper bound on the benefit where the rule is not redundant

On single-name 30–45-DTE contracts the exclusion of one IVOL quintile is worth ≈ $7 per contract-month at Heston et al.'s Table 2 spread (+0.57 points; L3 §3.2) against a $36–103 round trip and $90–130 of theta; the price-level and MAX legs are removed by the S07 universe filter before any option is considered; the smirk leg has no option-pricing content (Heston Table 2: +0.49 % per month, t 0.78). On the far-OTM contracts (ask < $0.10) the avoided loss is the spread the engine already charges, so the marginal value of the rule to a GATE-v2 backtest is zero by construction.

### 3.5 Power of the suggested measurement where it could run

Boyer–Vorkink's 7-day deep-OTM portfolios average −35 % (calls) and −61 % (puts) per week at the mid on equal-weighted portfolios of many contracts; a single short-dated OTM single-name contract held to expiry has a weekly return standard deviation of the order of 150–300 % (most expire at −100 %, a few at several hundred percent). A 5-point weekly difference between an excluded and a retained arm therefore needs ≈ (1.96 × 150 / 5)² ≈ 3,500 to (1.96 × 300 / 5)² ≈ 14,000 contract-weeks per arm for a positive lower bound — attainable only from the single-name chains we do not hold (§4), and only to confirm that contracts quoted at a half-spread equal to their mid lose their mid.

- The rule's only non-redundant content is already in the engine: ask ≥ $0.05, delta 0.30–0.60, spread < 12 % of mid (S01 config), open interest > 250 and intraday volume > 100 (S03), and `H14` on the exit side. - **Disposition:** do not open an L4 hypothesis id; do not adopt rule (a) as written (it would remove 80 % of the index weekly book for a −$4 ± 19 difference); do not spend pull budget on (b) or (c) ahead of the S11 completion and the Tier A record. If the owner ever opens a single-name long-option lane, the free default is the one L3 already states (exclude the top quintile of one-month IV − HV at decision time) plus the existing universe filter (price ≥ $5, dollar volume ≥ $50 M) and `execution-costs.md` X4 (closing near-ATM half-spread ≤ 4 %); none of the three is a lane and none is to carry a size claim. The hypothesis's own SSRN link for Amaya et al. (4404704) is Beckmeyer's; the Cboe PDF above is the correct source.

## 6. What would reverse the verdict

A pre-registered test on single-name chains at 10:00 NBBO marks showing that, *within* the contract set Compass would actually select (0.30–0.60 delta, spread < 12 %, universe filters), the top quintile of a stated skewness measure (ex-ante physical skewness or risk-neutral skewness, not the smirk) underperforms the rest by more than one round trip with a positive day-clustered lower bound, in a window of ≥ 7 calendar months with the same sign in every year — and, separately, a Compass entry with positive expectancy for the rule to protect. Neither exists; the first is a multi-day pull for a filter, the second is what S01–S16b have not found.
