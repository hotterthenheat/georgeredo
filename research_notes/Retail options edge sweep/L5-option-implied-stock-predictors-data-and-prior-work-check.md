## 0. Verdict

**Refuted on this lens — not because the published sorts are false, but because the test the hypothesis names cannot be run on the data on disk, cannot be run on the data the vendor holds within a window that reaches the gates' statistical bar at any plausible post-2011 effect size, and has no promotion path in the registry even if it clears the A2 hurdle, since the hypothesis itself concedes the option expression fails and the protocol has no stock-side lane.** Six data-and-window findings:

1. **Not closed by id; closed in substance by the scope and by S14's window ruling.** No registry entry mentions the smirk, skew, put–call parity, an IV spread, a volatility spread, ΔCVOL, or any of the three papers (grep over the 46 entries: 0 hits). The nearest entries are H2/H3 (flow-based option predictors on Unusual Whales data, closed — a different, non-price claim, as `academic-anomalies.md` §5 already notes) and S07/S08/S13/S14 (the daily A2 screens on 2024 → 2026). `our-evidence-ledger.md` §2 item 19 closes S14 — cross-sectional month-end deciles — as "un-resolvable on three years; do not re-propose until the 2012–2022 daily years are in the lake", on exactly the sample structure L5 proposes (a cross-sectional sort rebalanced weekly or monthly, 140 weeks / 34 month-ends). And the protocol's objective (§1: "long single-leg options") and the specification (`09_final_specification.md`: underlying-level findings are "candidates", option expressions are what gets promoted; `08_port_spec_3abida.md` §41: stock-sized effects are "outside the long-option scope until the owner decides") leave no gate that a stock-side screen can pass. S13 is the precedent: it cleared A2 on both parts and was "promoted at the underlying level only", pending an option expression (A5.2) — which L5 says in its own last sentence does not exist. §1.
2. **The inputs on disk cannot form the signals for a universe, and one of the three signals cannot be formed at all [LOCAL].** The only single-name option history in the repository or its caches is the S11 event cache: 1,239 files on 498 roots, file starts 2024-01-02 → 2026-09-01, one 30–45-DTE expiration per event (median 27 trading days per file, DTE at the file's first day median 38, range 8–73), event-selected names (20-day breakouts, top-tercile beats, pre-earnings) plus their random-date controls, closing NBBO only, **about 36 root-dates per calendar month** (min 3, max 69 over 34 months) against XZZ's ≈ 840 firms per weekly sort and AABC's 1,261–2,312 per month. The smirk and the call−put spread are formable per file at the close (at a quote-implied half-width of 4.6 vol points on the median name, the sibling's measurement, against 2–3 points between adjacent XZZ quintiles). **ΔCVOL is not formable**: it is the one-month change in a 30-day constant-maturity ATM IV, which needs two observations of the same name a month apart; across files only 170 of the 1,239 file-starts have a same-root observation 30 calendar days earlier (and at a different expiration), and within a file the "21-session change" is on one expiration whose DTE falls from ≈ 38 to ≈ 10 — a term-structure roll, not AABC's quantity. The Unusual Whales lake holds a per-ticker daily 30-day `implied_volatility` for ≈ 6,300 tickers, but only **2025-10-14 → 2026-10-13 (251 sessions)**, one number per ticker-day (no call/put split, no strike slice): it can form a ΔIV decile sort on 12 month-ends and nothing else. ThetaData's own IV/Greeks are absent 2012–2014 and start between 2015 and 2020; the engine's solver (`greeks.py`, `implied_vol(P,S,K,t,right,r=R_DEFAULT,q=Q_DEFAULT)`) uses r = 0.04 constant (06 known defects: rate series not wired) and no per-name dividend input, which biases the call−put IV spread — the Cremers–Weinbaum signal, and the one Muravyev–Pearson–Pollet say is a borrow-fee transform — by a per-name constant we cannot separate from the signal. No smirk/IV-spread/ΔCVOL code exists in `engine/`. §3 items 2–6.
3. **The universe exists for 2024 → 2026 only; the window the hypothesis names ("2012-06 onward as option EOD per name is pulled") is a vendor floor, not a lake.** Stock EOD on disk: `year=2023 … 2026` (216 parts); 2022 is paused at part 005 (`pull_chain3.log` 22:10:39) behind the S11 pull on the single ThetaData login; 2012–2021 are queued at lowest priority. The S07 universe is point-in-time on the prior year's dollar volume, so 2024 is the first usable year now, giving ≈ 140 weekly or 34 monthly formation dates. Of the 1,238 (2025) / 1,542 (2026) roots in an S07-sized universe, 837 / 946 had a listed expiration by 2013-01 and 977 / 1,119 by 2017-01 (the expirations list covers all 15,817 roots including delisted ones, so a point-in-time 2012–2022 universe *can* be built once the bars land — the obstacle is the bars, not survivorship). No single-name option EOD before 2023-12 has been verified on this key (the 2012-06-04 floor is verified on SPY); no single-name half-spread is measured before 2023-12 (closing) or 2025 (intraday, C1). §3 items 1, 7–9.
4. **The pull that would create the window blocks the pulls the daily studies are waiting on, and it is large [LOCAL].** The S11 chain is the only measurement of the vendor's option-EOD rate: 262 files in 2,661 s after the 22:10 restart (≈ 350 per hour, 4 workers; 619 per hour at the earlier 6-worker peak by file mtimes), one `option_history_eod` request per root-expiration-window. The closing-quote screen needs ≈ 1,200 roots × 1–2 expirations (XZZ uses 10–60-DTE puts *and* calls; CW matched pairs across maturities) × 36 months ≈ 43–86 k requests for 2024–2026 (**70–250 login-hours**) and ≈ 207–413 k for 2012-06 → 2026-10 (**330–1,180 login-hours**, 14–49 days), plus the open-interest endpoint (0.1 req/s, `06` 17:00 row) for XZZ's OI > 0 screen, which the EOD rows do not carry. The hypothesis's own preferred input — the 15:45 1-minute NBBO "not the close" — is one whole-chain request per name per session at the ≈ 0.2 req/s observed on the S03 pull (`06` 17:05): ≈ 840 k requests ≈ 1,160 hours for 2024–2026 alone. A 100-name Tier A subset for 2012–2026 is 55–98 hours but gives quintiles of 20 names. Bulk flat files — every contract's closing NBBO for every root in one file per day (≈ 2.05 M contracts, 29 MB) — exist only from **2026-10-06** ("first access date 20261006"; five sessions on disk), i.e. they are the cheap path for a *forward* table, not for history. §3 item 10, §4.
5. **No available window reaches the statistical gate at any size the literature now supports [LOCAL arithmetic].** From XZZ's Table 3 (16 bp/week, t 2.19, 520 weeks) the weekly long-short standard deviation is ≈ 167 bp. On 140 weeks the 95 % half-width is 27.6 bp: a lower bound above zero needs a mean ≥ 28 bp/week, 1.7× the published long-short and ≈ 3× the long leg's 10 bp FF3 alpha; the full published 16 bp needs 417 weeks (8 years), the long leg's 10 bp needs 1,066 weeks (20 years), and the one-third that survives Muravyev–Pearson–Pollet's borrow-fee adjustment (≈ 5 bp) needs 3,800 weeks. The longest window the vendor can supply (2012-06 → 2026-10, 745 weeks) gives a lower bound of +4 bp at the full 16 bp long-short, −2 bp at the 10 bp long leg and −7 bp at 5 bp. AABC (1.09 %/month, t 3.45, 192 months → sd ≈ 4.4 %): 34 month-ends give a half-width of 1.47 % (lower bound −0.38 % at the full size); 172 months give +0.44 % at the full size and −0.29 % at one-third. A2's absolute part (+30 bp lower bound at 5/10/21 sessions) is passed by market beta alone in 2024–2026 (S13's unconditional long earned ≈ +77 bp over 10 sessions), so the adjusted part binds, exactly as it did for S07/S08/S14. The A5 batch rule (same sign in every year, Bonferroni across cells) on three years and three signals × three horizons × two signs cannot be met by an effect of this size. GATE-v2's forward gate (≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 calendar months) is feedable from the flat files from 2026-10-06 — earliest completion ≈ 2027-05 — but it validates a lane that must first exist. §4.
6. **The option expression is closed by the hypothesis's own arithmetic, and the stock expression is outside every gate.** The hypothesis states the long-option case fails (14–16 bp of spot in C1 half-spreads per round trip plus theta against a 0.5-delta capture of ≈ 12 bp; 28–32 bp plus ≈ 51 bp theta at 30 DTE); nothing in the data check changes that, and `execution-costs.md` §9 puts the minimum underlying edge for a single-name weekly at 22–35 bp per session and 45–70 bp per five sessions for 30 DTE, against a signal of 10–25 bp per week gross in 1996–2011 samples. The stock long leg is retail-accessible (marketable orders, ≈ 2–5 bp round trip on liquid names) but is not an options edge, is not in the Compass scope, and at the sizes above is inside its own measurement noise on any window we can build. §5.

Net: the hypothesis's `testable_with_our_data: true` is true of the *inputs the vendor holds* and false of *the test*: the universe is three years, the per-name option history on disk is an event sample of ≈ 36 names a month at closing marks, ΔCVOL is not formable from anything held, the IV solver lacks the rate and dividend inputs the call−put spread needs, the pull that would fix this costs 70–1,180 login-hours that the S07/S08/S13/S14 backfill is already waiting on, and the window it would produce cannot clear a lower bound of zero at the post-2011 effect size. Expected size after costs: negative for every option expression; ≈ 0–5 bp/week on the stock long leg before a 2–5 bp stock round trip, unmeasurable here. Nothing to register.

## 2. Prior-work check: what each construction needs from data, and what we hold

### 2.1 The signals as published

- **Xing, Zhang & Zhao, *JFQA* 45(3) 2010 [PR]** — https://www.pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn/__local/A/24/EA/815CC33800CAA0F7D894E9292A1_6CC8B38B_27B25.pdf?e=.pdf (full text read by the sibling; the construction below is from its §1.1). SKEW = IV of the OTM put with strike/spot in (0.80, 0.95) closest to 0.95 minus IV of the ATM call with strike/spot in [0.95, 1.05] closest to 1; options with 10–60 days to expiration, **positive volume and positive open interest**, IV in (0.03, 2.0), price > $0.125; OptionMetrics vendor IVs (binomial tree, dividend-adjusted); averaged over the week; ≈ 840 firms per weekly sort, large-cap tilted; value-weighted quintiles. Inputs we lack: vendor IVs with dividends (engine solves from the mid with r = 0.04, q constant), per-contract OI on the same request (OI is a separate throttled endpoint), and a universe of ≈ 840 names per week with chains.
- **Cremers & Weinbaum, *JFQA* 45(2) 2010 [PR]** — https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v45y2010i02p335-367_00.html ; DOI 10.1017/S002210901000013X (the Semantic Scholar record this turn carries no abstract; 524 citations). Signal: the IV difference between matched call and put pairs (same strike and maturity), aggregated per stock; "stocks with relatively expensive calls outperform stocks with relatively expensive puts by 50 basis points per week"; "the degree of predictability decreases over the sample period". Sample period and the pair-weighting rule were not retrievable this turn; An et al. (NBER 19590 §V.B, read by the sibling) place the sample at 1996–2005 and the effect as insignificant in 2001–2005. Inputs we lack: a dividend- and rate-consistent IV per leg (the call−put IV gap at one strike is, by put–call parity, a function of the dividend yield, the rate and the borrow fee — Muravyev–Pearson–Pollet's point — so a solver with q = constant and r = 0.04 folds a per-name error into the signal).
- **An, Ang, Bali & Cakici, *JF* 69(5) 2014 [PR]; NBER 19590** — https://www.nber.org/papers/w19590.pdf (read by the sibling). Signal: the one-month change in the OptionMetrics Volatility Surface's **30-day, 0.5-delta** call IV (ΔCVOL) and put IV (ΔPVOL) at month end; equal-weighted deciles, 1,261–2,312 stocks per month, 1996–2011. Inputs we lack: a constant-maturity interpolated surface per name per month (we hold one expiration per event in the S11 cache; UW's 30-day IV for 251 sessions). This is the only one of the three with t > 3 and sub-period support through 2011; it is also the one the data on disk cannot form at all (§3 item 4).
- **Goncalves-Pinto, Grundy, Hameed, van der Heijden & Zhu, *Management Science* 66(9) 2020 [PR]** — https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v66y2020i9p3903-3926.html (abstract read this turn); Semantic Scholar record for DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2019.3398 (open-access pointer https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2695145 → SSRN, HTTP 403). Abstract, verified: the cross-market discrepancy's predictability "is especially strong when accompanied by stock price pressure, and it does not depend on trading in options"; "stock price pressure [is] the primary driver of the option price-based stock return predictability". **The hypothesis's "85 bp next-day alpha" and the "DOTS" label are not on any page reached this turn or by the sibling**; treat the number as unverified. Inputs for a replication: an option-implied stock price per name per day from put–call parity at the close (formable from a chain mid, at single-name closing spreads of 10.8 % median), a one-day stock holding at the next open, and stock order-imbalance data (not held) for the mechanism test.
- **Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet, *JFE* 172 (2025) 104153 [PR]** — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2025.104153 (Semantic Scholar DOI record read this turn: title, authors, venue, open access CC BY; the Elsevier page did not load). Abstract: option prices reflect stock borrow fees, which predict returns; "abnormal return predictability from options signals decreases by about two-thirds" after adjusting returns for borrow fees; excluding high-fee stocks does the same. Sample period not in the record. Inputs for the decomposition: securities-lending fees (Markit-class data) — **not held in any form**; so even the diagnostic that would tell an information signal from a borrow-fee transform cannot be run here.
- **Chen & Zimmermann, *CFR* 11(2) 2022 [PR]** `SignalDoc.csv` (read by the sibling): `skew1` 2_likely / 2_fair ("Screens in appendix are important"); `dVolCall` 1_clear / 1_good; `CPVolSpread` (Bali–Hovakimian 2009) 1_clear / 1_good. The post-sample return series per predictor are in the OpenAP data release (not fetched by either lens).

### 2.2 What was not found (search budget; stated, not evidence of absence)

No post-2011 replication of the weekly smirk or IV-spread long leg, no test at a 5–21-session horizon with a next-open entry, no test on non-OptionMetrics data or intraday quotes, and no cost-adjusted stock-side result were retrievable this turn (zero searches ran). The two latest peer-reviewed statements on the family that were reached — Goncalves-Pinto 2020 (price pressure) and Muravyev–Pearson–Pollet 2025 (borrow fees, two-thirds) — both reassign the mechanism to things a retail long stock position at the next open does not collect, which is the evidence-quality lens's finding; on this lens it matters because it sets the effect size a test must be powered for at ≈ one-third of the 1996–2011 numbers (§4).

## 4. Window length and power against the gates [LOCAL arithmetic]

- **What gate applies.** Protocol §7 (≥ 150 out-of-sample trades over ≥ 3 months per cell) and §10 / GATE-v2 (positive net expectancy with a day-clustered lower bound above zero, out of sample, after multiplicity; forward ≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 months) apply to option lanes; A2 (absolute lower bound ≥ +30 bp at 5/10/21 sessions **and** adjusted lower bound > 0) is the underlying-level hurdle that decides whether an option test is run. L5 concedes the option test fails, so clearing A2 would produce, at most, a second S13: an underlying-level candidate with no expression.
- **Weekly sorts (XZZ, CW).** XZZ Table 3: Low − High 16 bp/week, t −2.19, 520 weeks → weekly long-short sd ≈ 16 × √520 / 2.19 ≈ **167 bp**. Available now (2024-01 → 2026-10): ≈ 140 weeks → SE 14.1 bp, 95 % half-width 27.6 bp; a 16 bp mean has a lower bound of −11.6, the 10 bp long-leg alpha −17.6, the ≈ 5 bp that survives MPP's two-thirds −22.3. Weeks needed for a lower bound above zero: 417 (8 years) at 16 bp, 1,066 (20 years) at 10 bp, ≈ 3,800 at 5 bp. With 2021–2022 landed (≈ 300 weeks): half-width 18.9, lower bounds −2.9 / −8.9 / −13.6. Full vendor history 2012-06 → 2026-10 (≈ 745 weeks): half-width 12.0 → +4.0 at the full published long-short, −2.0 at the long leg, −6.7 at one-third. A long-only adjusted quintile (XZZ's low-SKEW quintile ≈ +9 bp/week over the cross-quintile mean; single-leg adjusted sd taken as two-thirds of the long-short's, ≈ 112 bp) has a lower bound of −9.5 on 140 weeks and +1.0 on 745. CW's 50 bp/week is 1996–2000 in its own data (insignificant 2001–2005 per AABC's description) and gives no post-2005 size to power against.
- **Monthly sorts (AABC).** 1.09 %/month, t 3.45, 192 months → monthly decile-spread sd ≈ **4.4 %**. 34 month-ends: SE 0.75 %, half-width 1.47 %, lower bound −0.38 % at the full size, −1.11 % at one-third. 172 month-ends (2012-06 → 2026-10): half-width 0.65 %, lower bound +0.44 % at the full size, −0.29 % at one-third; months needed for a lower bound above zero: 62 at 1.09 %, 568 at 0.36 %. S14 already produced intervals "hundreds of bp wide" on 20–34 month-ends with the same engine and universe; the ledger's ruling on it (§2 item 19) is the ruling here.
- **The A2 absolute part is not the binding one.** In 2024–2026 an unconditional 10-session long in the S07 universe earned ≈ +77 bp (S13 reading), so the +30 bp absolute lower bound is met by beta; the adjusted part (lower bound > 0 against the same-date universe mean) is what S07/S08/S14 were read on and what the arithmetic above addresses.
- **Multiplicity.** Three signals × three horizons × two signs × (deciles or quintiles) under A5's batch rule — the same sign in every year and a Bonferroni-adjusted interval across the batch — on three calendar years with year signs that already flip in S07/S08/S14's cells.
- **Forward gate.** From 2026-10-06 the flat files give the whole universe's closing chain per day at no pull cost, so a paper lane could log ≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 months by ≈ 2027-05; GATE-v2 requires the historical statistical gate first, and §4 says it cannot be met on any window at the post-2011 size.
- **Data integrity (GATE-v2: full chains incl. extreme-move days, hashed).** The S11 cache is one expiration per event, event-selected; the flat files are full but start 2026-10-06; no per-name full-chain history exists for the window.
