# L2 — cross-sectional option momentum and reversal in single-name ATM straddles: cost-realism lens (2026-10-14)

Hypothesis under test (`academic-anomalies.md` §2 and §11, sweep id **L2**): equity ATM straddles with high past 2–12-month returns outperform low ones by +6.22 %/month (t 7.97), one-month reversal −4.09 %/month (t −6.18), 12-month momentum Sharpe 1.53, stable in every 5-year subsample 1996–2019; but every long-straddle quintile has a negative mean (best −1.55 %/month) and the long-short is about zero at the full quoted spread (−6.78 % main sample; +1.12 %, t 0.75 for low-spread deciles). Suggested test: replicate Heston Table 2 on Tier A then the full universe with 10:00 NBBO marks, 2012-06 → 2026-10 (≈ 172 months), long leg alone and long-short at bid/ask fills with $0.70/side, SPAN-style margin haircut. Proposition to refute: *a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates.*

Lens: cost realism — does the effect survive the measured spreads and fees of registry `C1` and `execution-costs.md`, with the default of refuted when the source did not net out bid-ask. Grades: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[WP]** working paper, **[EX]** exchange/regulator publication, **[PRAC]** broker or practitioner document stating its own rules, **[SEC]** secondary summary (not evidence), **[LOCAL]** this repository's measurements or this note's own arithmetic, **[VEND]** vendor material (not evidence; none used). Local context read first: `research/compass/02_coverage_matrix.md`, `07_coverage_report.md`, `05_results.md` (incl. the S11 preliminary row added today), `registry.jsonl` (GATE-v2, RULE-separation, C1, H14, H16, DATA-1..3, S10/S10-rerun, S11-prelim, S13/S13-R, S14, S15), and the sibling notes `academic-anomalies.md`, `execution-costs.md`, `our-evidence-ledger.md`, and the evidence-quality note on the same id, `L2-option-momentum-refutation.md` (its paper-reading is not repeated here except where a number is load-bearing; this note adds the cost ledger, a new local measurement at the paper's tenor, and the cost-side arithmetic). Primary texts were read from the session scratchpad (`l2_dl/`: the September 2021 and December 2021 drafts of Heston et al. converted with `pdftotext`; `l2_mp_dl/`: the Muravyev–Pearson working paper). No paid API was called; no repository data was modified; the one new measurement runs on a CSV the execution-costs sweep had already produced from cached chains.

## 0. Verdict

**Refuted.** The paper did net out bid–ask (Table 13), and at the column a retail marketable order pays the long-short is zero; everything a retail account adds on top of the paper's cost model (Reg-T margin, approval tier, one-lot granularity, assignment and after-hours exercise on the short leg, today's wider single-name closing spreads) moves it further down. Four independent reasons, any one sufficient:

1. **The paper's own after-cost table.** At the "quoted" column (one-way cost = 100 % of the closing quoted half-spread, no stock-liquidation cost) momentum is −6.78 %/month (t −9.63) in the main sample and +1.12 % (t 0.75) in the cost-optimised version (extreme deciles, spreads < 10 % of mid); reversal +1.99 % (t 1.64); composite +1.98 % (t 1.47). With the cost of liquidating the stock acquired at expiry (half the stock's closing spread, which is also what a retail account pays after auto-exercise) these are −0.61 % (t −0.40), +0.06 % (t 0.05) and −0.30 % (t −0.21). The authors: "the main momentum and reversal strategies are not profitable if one assumes that the entire quoted spread must be paid" (§5.6). GATE-v2 prices marketable orders at the NBBO; `C1` disallows mid fills.
2. **The intermediate columns the paper survives on are, by the cost benchmark's own account, not available to retail.** Muravyev & Pearson (RFS 2020), whose Table 5 supplies Heston's "algo / adjusted / effective" fractions, write: "At most only a few retail investors have the resources and ability to time their option trades based on high-frequency changes in stock prices … Retail and other investors who are not able to time executions will on average trade when the option price is expected to stay the same and their costs will equal the conventionally measured effective spreads." The best retail-claimable column is therefore "effective" (75.8 % of quoted; the same 0.74 effective/quoted ratio Hendershott et al. measure for marketable orders in continuous trading in 2020), where low-cost-decile momentum is 2.60 % (t 1.75) before margin and 0.88 % (t 0.58) with exercise costs; reversal 3.46 % (t 2.85) / 1.53 % (t 1.27); composite 3.48 % (t 2.59) / 1.20 % (t 0.87).
3. **Margin and approval turn a small number into a smaller one.** The short leg is naked single-name straddle writing (Fidelity Level 4, Schwab's $25,000 uncovered-equity minimum, TradeStation Level 5 for naked stock calls). Under FINRA 4210 a short ATM straddle on the median name in our cache ($103 stock, $6.64 straddle) requires $27.26 per share of margin, a ratio of **4.1 × premium** against the paper's SPAN-style "slightly above 2"; the paper's own haircut halves the return, Reg-T cuts it to ≈ 24 %: the quoted-column +1.12 % becomes ≈ +0.3 % per month on margin capital, +1.99 % reversal ≈ +0.5 %, and the t-statistics do not change. Portfolio margin (±15 % equity shocks, ≥ $100–110 k equity) is the only route to the paper's ratio.
4. **The after-cost series cannot be resolved by any sample we can assemble.** t 0.75 on 282 months implies a monthly standard deviation ≈ 25 % of notional for the after-cost momentum deciles (≈ 20 % reversal, ≈ 23 % composite). A two-sided 95 % lower bound above zero — GATE-v2's statistical gate — needs ≈ 1,900 months for momentum, ≈ 400 for reversal, ≈ 500 for the composite at the quoted column, and is unreachable for any exercise-cost row. Our window holds ≈ 159 holding months (first holding month 2013-07 after a 12-month formation window → 2026-10), on a universe whose liquid tail holds 30 names per quintile at best, which adds ≈ 10 points of idiosyncratic standard deviation to the long-short.

The long leg alone — the only expression inside the Compass long-option scope — is −1.55 %/month gross for the best quintile (every quintile in Table 2 is negative) and ≈ −3 to −5 %/month after one entry at the intraday half-spreads of the liquid half of the single-name book, ≈ −13 % at the 2024–2026 closing median of the names we hold chains for (the design holds to expiry, so the exit is an exercise, not a second spread). Execution cost is not what kills the long leg (the sign does); execution cost is exactly what kills the long-short, because the paper's gross edge (6.1–13 % of premium per month on the two straddles) equals the two entries' half-spreads. What survives is the ranking use `academic-anomalies.md` §2 already records.

### 1.2 Muravyev & Pearson, "Options Trading Costs Are Lower than You Think", *RFS* 33(11) 2020, 4973–5014 [PR]; working-paper draft read (mql5 mirror, https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/209/Option%20Trading%20Costs%20Are%20Lower%20than%20You%20Think.pdf) [WP for the quoted text]

Publisher page https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i11p4973-5014..html. Sample: "tick-level data for 39 stocks including 2 ETFs … provided by Nanex … 882 trading days from April 2003 through October 2006"; the stocks "had the largest option trading volume during March 2003"; "the reduction in tick sizes to pennies occurred after the end of the sample period". Table 5 (averages across the 39 stocks, cents per share, full spreads): quoted 8.4, quoted at the time of trade 6.6, effective 6.4, realized 3.7, adjusted (BSM) 4.2, adjusted (regression) 4.5, adjusted/quoted 54 %; "more than 80 % of option trades are executed at the NBBO quotes". Execution timers' adjusted effective spread 1.3 ¢ versus the conventional 6.2 ¢ for non-timers ("overstates their trading costs by a factor of almost five"); the share of trades reflecting execution timing rose from 27.5 % to 54 %. The passage that matters for this lens, verbatim: "At most only a few retail investors have the resources and ability to time their option trades based on high-frequency changes in stock prices and other market information. Thus, our finding that 40 % of option trades exploit ability to time executions indicates that a large proportion and perhaps most option trading is done by sophisticated proprietary traders or institutional investors who either possess execution algorithms or have access to brokerage firm execution algorithms … Retail and other investors who are not able to time executions will on average trade when the option price is expected to stay the same and their costs will equal the conventionally measured effective spreads." The $0.026 / $0.128 ATM half-spreads Heston et al. cite are from the published version's tables (not retrieved); the draft's effective/quoted ratio of 6.4/8.4 = 76 % matches Heston's 75.8 %. Reading: Heston's "algo" and "adjusted" columns are institutional columns by the benchmark's own definition; "effective" is the ceiling for a retail marketable order, and it is a 2003–2006, pre-penny, 39-mega-cap ceiling.

### 1.3 Hendershott, Khan & Riordan, "Option Auctions", *RFS* 39(3) 2026 [PR] (via `execution-costs.md` §2.1, https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/39/3/783/8193725)

OPRA, Aug–Dec 2020, ≈ 79 M single-leg trades: quoted half-spread 10.45 ¢ (1.21 % of a $8.66 average option), effective 7.02 ¢, price improvement 3.43 ¢; effective/quoted 0.74 in continuous trading (77 % of single-leg trades) and 0.38 in auctions (23 %); on exchanges where the DMM is a PFOF wholesaler continuous trades are improved by only 0.98 ¢; "an investor using market orders would have to generate returns of greater than 6 % to break even on a round-trip trade". ### 1.4 Goyenko & Zhang, "Price Pressures and Noise in Option Returns", McGill WP 2020 [WP] (via `academic-anomalies.md` §0.2)

Closing quoted spreads on S&P 500 single-name options are ≈ 50 bp wider than intraday; closing-midpoint straddle returns are biased upward by up to 100 bp per day. For L2 the bias touches the entry price of both legs; the hypothesis's 10:00 marks are the right response, and the only measured single-name spreads at the paper's tenor that we hold are closing quotes (§2.2), so the retail cost estimates below are upper bounds for a 10:00 entry by roughly that margin.

### 2.3 Margin ratio under Reg-T for the cache-median name [LOCAL arithmetic on FINRA 4210]

Spot $103.1, ATM one-month mid $3.32 per leg → straddle $6.64 per share ($664 per lot; implied σ ≈ 28 % from 6.64/103.1 ≈ 0.8 σ√(30/365)). Short call requirement = premium 3.32 + 20 % × 103.1 = $23.94 (above the 10 % minimum of $13.63; no OTM deduction at the money); short straddle = greater side $23.94 + the other side's premium $3.32 = **$27.26 per share, $2,726 per lot, ratio 4.1 × premium**. For a 35 %-IV name the same rule gives ≈ 3.5. The paper's "margin-adjusted" column divides by ≈ 2; Reg-T divides by ≈ 3.5–4.1, so the retail margin-adjusted returns are ≈ 24–29 % of gross rather than 50 %: quoted-column low-cost momentum +1.12 % → ≈ +0.3 % per month on margin capital, reversal +1.99 % → ≈ +0.5 %, composite +1.98 % → ≈ +0.5 %, with exercise costs ≈ −0.15 / +0.01 / −0.07 %. Portfolio margin (FINRA 4210(g): ±15 % equity shocks; IBKR $110 k to upgrade) is the paper's scenario set and the only way to its ratio; its capital is then set by GATE-v2's tail gate rather than the house requirement (Table 1's 90th-percentile straddle return is +94 %, so a one-lot short loses ≈ $620 on a $664 credit at the 90th percentile and is unbounded beyond; the paper's gross lag-2–12 factor had a 55.7 % maximum drawdown in its factor-statistics table, as transcribed in the evidence-quality note).

The first column is what the names we actually hold chains for would cost if executed at the close (the S07/S08/S11 design); the second is the quarter of roots that pass the paper's own filter at the close; the third is the paper's quoted-column cost and is the best estimate for a 10:00 marketable entry on the liquid half of the single-name book (C1's intraday 3.5–4.1 % on the mixed book sits on it); the fourth exists for a few dozen names, whose gross spread is the Table 12 liquid-tercile 4.35 % and whose leg count cannot support deciles. Reg-T margin then multiplies every long-short cell by ≈ 0.24–0.29. The registry's S10-rerun (defined-risk substitutes pay two spreads and are worse) rules out an iron-butterfly proxy of the short leg, which would pay four single-name spreads of 5–11 % each.

Minimum gross edge per month for a retail long-short to clear zero: 2 × half-spread + 0.4 % + exercise ≈ 7–9 % at C1's intraday mixed-book level, ≈ 23 % at the closing median — against a gross of 4.35–7.29 %. The paper's cost-optimised gross (7.23 %) clears the paper's own low-cost cost (6.1 %) by one point; it does not clear ours.

## 4. Power under GATE-v2 [LOCAL]

GATE-v2: positive net expectancy with a day-clustered 95 % lower bound above zero on complete data, out of sample, after multiplicity; ≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 calendar months of forward paper trading; tail survival under ±10 %/±15 % shocks with Reg-T and portfolio margin; a signal lane must beat random entry by more than one round trip. Monthly observations here are the unit (one entry per expiration cycle).

- **Standard deviation of the after-cost strategies.** From Table 13 with N = 282 months (the low-cost rows are assumed to span the full sample): momentum low-cost deciles at quoted, SE = 1.12/0.75 = 1.49 → SD ≈ 25 %; reversal 1.99/1.64 = 1.21 → SD ≈ 20 %; composite 1.98/1.47 = 1.35 → SD ≈ 23 %. (The gross quintile factor's SD is 14.1 %, Table 4; the decile/low-cost versions hold fewer names.)
- **Months for a two-sided 95 % lower bound > 0 at the paper's own quoted-column means:** momentum (1.96 × 25.1/1.12)² ≈ **1,930**; reversal (1.96 × 20.4/1.99)² ≈ **400**; composite (1.96 × 22.6/1.98)² ≈ **500**. One-sided: ≈ 1,360 / 280 / 350. With exercise costs (means −0.61 / +0.06 / −0.30) no finite sample gives a positive lower bound. Our window: 2012-06-04 option-NBBO floor + 12 formation months → first holding month 2013-07 → 2026-10: **≈ 159 months**, of which 87 are after the paper's sample. The gross 6.22 % needs ≈ 20 months and is resolvable; the retail version is not, on this or any realistic window.
- **Concentration on our universe.** Tier A is ≈ 100–150 roots (`07_coverage_report.md` §1; the S06 Tier A screen used 34). With 150 names, quintile legs of 30 and decile legs of 15. Single-straddle SD 80.6 %: a 30-name leg carries ≈ 14.7 % of idiosyncratic SD if independent (4.9 % at the paper's 274), so the long-short SD rises from ≈ 14 % to ≈ 24 % (√(14.1² + 2 × (14.7² − 4.9²))). The liquid-tercile gross of 4.35 % then needs ≈ 117 months for a lower bound above zero (resolvable on 159, before multiplicity) and the after-cost +1–2 % needs ≈ 550–2,200. Deciles on 15 names roughly double the noise again.
- **Forward validation.** Seven calendar months of a ≈ 25 %-SD monthly series is seven observations; the gate cannot discriminate a +1 % mean from zero in a decade of forward paper.
- **Multiplicity.** The suggested test pre-specifies at least 3 signals (momentum, reversal, composite) × 2 universes (Tier A, full) × 2 marks (10:00, close) × long-leg and long-short; a Bonferroni z of ≈ 3 raises every month count above by ≈ 2.3×.

## 5. Testability with our data [LOCAL]

- **What exists.** ThetaData option 1-minute NBBO, option EOD and open interest from 2012-06-04 (verified on SPY only; single-name floors assumed equal, unverified per root — `02_coverage_matrix.md` §1); expirations per root for all history; stock EOD from 2012-06-01 for UTP-tape names and only 2017-01-03 for CTA-tape names, so the hold-to-expiry payoff for roughly half the universe in 2013–2016 must be inferred from the chain at expiry by put–call parity, on quotes whose median half-spread is 11 % of mid. The stock-EOD lake holds 2023 → 2026 (2022 paused at part 003 behind the S11 pull); the point-in-time universe before 2023 waits on those years. No half-spread has been measured on any chain before 2023-12 (C1 is 2025–2026 intraday; §2.2 is 2023-12 → 2026-10 closing), so a 2013–2019 result carries an unmeasured cost until the pulled NBBO is summarised (one pass).
- **Pull size at the observed throttle.** The S11 pull ran at ≈ 0.17 requests/s (`05_results.md`, S11 preliminary: 876 of 7,166 chains in the first window) on the single login (DATA-3). Tier A: 150 roots × 160 expirations ≈ 24,000 chain-month requests ≈ **39 hours**; the paper's universe (800–1,700 names) ≈ 130k–270k requests ≈ **9–19 days** of the one login, plus open interest per root-year (≤ 365 days per request) and the stock-EOD backfill it displaces. Unusual Whales adds nothing (option history from 2023-11-13, DATA-2).
- **What a test could still show.** A gross replication at 10:00 marks on Tier A (≈ 39 h of pull) would be the first intraday-quote test of monthly straddle momentum and the first 2019-07 → 2026-10 reading; the gross 4.35–6.22 % is resolvable in 20–120 months. It would also measure the one-month ATM 10:00 half-spread by root for the first time (the number §2.2 could only bound from the close). That is a data-quality and selection-filter exercise: it cannot change §3 or §4, because the after-cost mean is bounded by the paper at ≈ 0 and the sample needed to verify any positive residual exceeds the data by an order of magnitude.
