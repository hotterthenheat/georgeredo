Scope: what Cboe's option-strategy benchmark indices (PUT, WPUT, BXM, BXMD, BXY, CMBO, CNDR, BFLY, PPUT, CLL, BXMW), AQR, Parametric, J.P. ## 0. The short answer

1. **The index providers document a low-beta equity substitute, not an edge.** Cboe's PUT (monthly at-the-money SPX put, fully collateralized by T-bills) returned 7.2 %/yr with 10.7 % volatility, −32.7 % maximum drawdown, beta 0.6 and a Sharpe of 0.52 from 2007-01-03 to 2026-09-30 against 11.0 %, 15.4 %, −50.9 % and 0.61 for the S&P 500 total return (Cboe fact sheet, [EX]). Over our own data window, 2012-06-04 → 2026-10-13, PUT compounded at 8.6 %/yr with 8.8 % volatility (excess-of-T-bill Sharpe 0.75) and a −28.9 % drawdown in 23 trading days of 2020 [COMPUTED]; the S&P 500 price index did 13.4 %/yr (plus about 2 %/yr of dividends not in the price series). PUT lost more than the S&P 500 in March 2020 (−13.4 % vs −12.5 %), was in drawdown for 9.5 months after it, and trailed the price index by a median 5.8 points per rolling year. Its monthly beta since 2012 is 0.54, with a down-market beta of 0.82 and an up-market beta of 0.42 [COMPUTED]. That is the shape of every option-selling index: the loss arrives with the market and the upside is capped.

2. **The decomposition work (AQR, [PR]) says the short-volatility component has a Sharpe near 1.0 but is a small fraction of the risk.** In an at-the-money overwrite mimicking BXM from 1996-03-25 to 2014-12-31, short volatility earned 1.9 %/yr with 1.9 % volatility (Sharpe 0.98) and contributed 7 % of the strategy's risk; passive equity earned 3.5 % at 8.5 % (Sharpe 0.41, 67 % of risk); the remaining 26 % of risk was uncompensated "equity timing" from the option's changing delta (0.5 %/yr, Sharpe 0.10). In 2002–2008 the short-volatility Sharpe was 0.40 (Israelov–Nielsen 2015). The delta-hedged short 5 %-OTM monthly put that AQR uses to illustrate the VRP earned 1.5 %/yr at 2.2 % volatility (Sharpe 0.68, maximum drawdown −10 %) from 1996 to 2016 *net of AQR's own transaction-cost estimates* (Ang–Israelov–Sullivan–Tummala 2018, [PRAC]). The Chicago Fed paper in the companion note (Dew-Becker–Giglio 2025, [WP]) finds that this alpha component converged to zero around 2010–2020. Neither the indices nor the decompositions were written for a retail account.

3. **Costs assumed by the indices: none.** The Cboe methodologies deem the new option sold at the volume-weighted average of OPRA-reported trade prices in a 30-minute (PUT, WPUT, PPUT, CNDR, BFLY) or two-hour (BXM, BXMD, BXY, CMBO since 2010-11-19) window on roll day, settle expiring options at the SOQ, mark daily at the bid-ask midpoint, and carry no fees, no bid-ask crossing and no taxes (the fact sheet says so in the disclosures). AQR's published numbers are "net of estimated transaction costs" at AQR's realized institutional cost levels and gross of fees. The funds carry 0.35 % (JEPI), 0.46–0.52 % (Parametric EIVPX), 0.7–1.2 % plus an anti-dilution levy of 0.3 % rising to about 1 % in stress (Nomura NEVRP). A retail account replicating PUT on SPY pays roughly 0.10–0.15 % of notional per year in half-spreads and fees (C1 half-spreads, 12 rolls) — small — but gives up the T-bill yield on the collateral unless it holds bills, which over 2012–2026 was 1.7 %/yr, a fifth of the index's return [COMPUTED].

4. **2018 and 2020 are the documentation of the risk, not an argument against the premium.** Feb 2018 (XIV termination; Nomura's variance-swap fund −23 % in a day, +21 % the next) cost PUT −7.6 % and BXMD −8.5 % peak to trough versus −10.2 % for the S&P 500; Q4 2018 cost them −15 % to −18 % against −19.8 %; 2020-02-19 → 03-23 cost every short-premium index 25–32 % against −33.9 %, and the defined-risk indices (CNDR −9.2 %, BFLY −3.2 %) and the put-protection indices (PPUT −11.8 %, CLL −14.9 %) much less [COMPUTED]. The year 2020 as a whole: PUT +2.1 %, BXM −2.8 %, WPUT −7.2 %, PPUT +29.1 %, S&P 500 TR +18.4 %. The tail-hedging side has its own 2018–2024 record: Universa's 3.33 % allocation claim (+3.6 points of CAGR over the S&P 500 across 2008–2020, per Universa) against Aaron Brown's arithmetic (0.22 % of portfolio per month in normal months), CalPERS's termination of its programme weeks before March 2020, AQR's 35-year put series losing money in every decade, and the Simplify Tail Risk ETF (CYA) liquidating in March 2024 after losing 99.8 % since September 2021 (Bloomberg via [SEC]).

5. **What is retail-reproducible and testable with our data.** Monthly cash-secured ATM/2 %-OTM put writing and 30-delta call overwriting on SPY (one contract per $65k of notional, PM-settled American options, early-assignment risk on the quarterly ex-dividend third Fridays for calls) are exactly reproducible from 2012-06-04 at the 1-minute NBBO with measured C1 costs: 172 monthly rolls, 748 weekly rolls. XSP (1/10 SPX, cash-settled European, Section 1256) is listed in our expirations file from 2012-06-16 (1,950 expirations) but its NBBO depth was not probed. Iron condors/butterflies pay four half-spreads a month for a documented ≤ 1 %/yr return since 2007 and are not worth a pull. Delta-hedged short puts (AQR's illustration) are reproducible with SPY shares as the hedge but the published net Sharpe was earned before the post-2010 convergence; it is the companion note's L1 in another form. Variance swaps (Nomura, SG), VIX futures (SVOL) and ELNs (JEPI) are not retail option products; the option strategies inside them are.

## 1. The Cboe benchmark rules (every number from the methodology documents, [EX])

| Index | Position | Strike rule | Tenor / roll | Collateral | Entry price on roll day | Settlement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PUT (S&P 500 PutWrite) | short 1 SPX put | listed strike closest to but not above the last SPX value before 11:00 ET | monthly, third Friday (preceding business day if holiday); held to maturity | one- and three-month T-bills; number of puts sized so the strike × N is covered | VWAP of OPRA trades 11:30–12:00 ET (late/cancel/spread trade codes excluded); last bid before 12:00 if no trades | SOQ (AM), max(0, K − SOQ) |
| WPUT (One-Week PutWrite) | short 1 SPX put | first strike below the SOQ (AM-settled expiry) or below the last value before 16:00 (PM-settled) | weekly, every Friday | T-bills equal to the strike | first bid quote after 09:30 ET (AM case) or last bid before 16:00 (PM case) — a bid, not a VWAP | SOQ, or bought back at the last ask before 16:00 for PM-settled weeklies |
| BXM (BuyWrite) | long S&P 500 TR + short 1 ATM SPX call | strike at or above the last value before 11:00 ET | monthly, third Friday | the index itself ("covered") | VWAP 11:30–13:30 ET (two hours; 30 minutes from 2004-05-21 to 2010-11-19) | SOQ |
| BXMD (30-Delta BuyWrite) | long S&P 500 TR + short 1 OTM call | strike with Black delta closest to 0.303 from inputs before 11:00 ET; ties go to the higher strike | monthly | covered | VWAP (same table) | SOQ |
| BXY (2 % OTM BuyWrite) | long + short call | strike closest to 102 % of the last value before 11:00 | monthly | covered | VWAP | SOQ |
| CMBO (Covered Combo) | per Cboe's index description: long S&P 500 + short 2 % OTM call + short cash-collateralized ATM put (its methodology document was not read today; unverified here) | — | monthly | covered / T-bills | VWAP | SOQ |
| CNDR (Iron Condor) | short 0.20-delta put and 0.20-delta call, long 0.05-delta put and call | Black delta closest to ±0.20 / ±0.05 before 11:00 ET | monthly | one-month T-bills equal to 10 × the maximum loss of the four-leg position (max loss ≈ 10 % of the account) | VWAP | SOQ; daily mark at the bid-ask midpoint before 16:00 |
| BFLY (Iron Butterfly) | short ATM put and ATM call, long 5 % OTM put and call | ATM = first strike above the last SPX value; wings at ±5 % | monthly | one-month T-bills = 10 × the worst payoff | VWAP | SOQ |
| PPUT (5 % Put Protection) | long S&P 500 TR + long 1 monthly 5 %-OTM SPX put | — | monthly | — | VWAP 11:30–12:00 ET | SOQ at 09:30 |
| CLL (95-110 Collar) | long S&P 500 + short monthly 110 % call + long quarterly 95 % put | — | monthly call / quarterly put | — | VWAP | SOQ |

Sources: `https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/governance/Cboe_SP_500_PutWrite_Indices_Methodology.pdf` (PUT; also defines PWT, the same index priced at a time-weighted average 11:30–12:00 — of bids per its §1.1, of trades per its §2.2; the document is internally inconsistent on this), `.../BXM_Methodology.pdf` (BXM, BXMD, BXY, BXD, BXR, BXRD family), `.../CNDR_Methodology.pdf`, `.../BFLY_Methodology.pdf`, `.../WPUT_Methodology.pdf` (PUTR, PUTY, PTLT, WPTR, WPUT), `.../PPUT_Methodology.pdf` (PPUT, PPUT3M), `.../CLL_Methodology.pdf`. Three details matter for replication: (i) every index except WPUT and PTLT (sold at a bid) is *sold at a trade-weighted average price*, which sits inside the quoted spread; a marketable retail order sells at the bid; (ii) every expiring option is cash-settled at the SOQ with no exit cost; SPY/QQQ options are PM-settled and American, so the "exit" is either expiration (no cost) or assignment (shares to unwind at a one-cent spread) or a buy-back at the ask; (iii) the T-bill account earns the 4-week and 13-week bank-discount rates from the Treasury's website; the index's "income" includes this.

## 3. AQR

### 3.1 Covered calls, decomposed [PR]

- Israelov & Nielsen 2014 (above): "Covered calls provide long equity and short volatility exposure." Table 2 (1996-04-01 → 2013-12-31, ATM calls written on expiration dates at closing midpoints, not delta-hedged): covered call 5.0 % excess return, 14.1 % vol, Sharpe 0.36; long-equity half 3.3 %, 9.9 %, 0.33; short-straddle half 1.8 %, 6.7 %, 0.27, skew −0.5, kurtosis 21.9, beta 0.14; risk contribution 64 % / 36 %. The stylized example: with implied 18 % and realized 16 %, 11 % of the ATM premium ($0.23 of $2.07) is VRP compensation, 2.76 %/yr; if implied equalled realized the covered call would earn exactly what a 49 % equity position earns, "no different from what would have been earned by simply reducing the index position size by 51 %." Myth 3 ("covered calls generate income"): "Income is revenue minus cost."
- Israelov & Nielsen, "Covered Calls Uncovered", *FAJ* 71(6), 2015 [PR] — `https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/Covered-Calls-Uncovered.pdf`. Table 1 (BXM-mimicking, 1996-03-25 → 2014-12-31): strategy 5.9 % excess, 11.4 % vol, Sharpe 0.52, skew −1.7, alpha 1.7 %, beta 0.62 (up 0.46, down 0.86); passive equity 3.5 %/8.5 %/0.41, 67 % of risk; short volatility 1.9 %/1.9 %/0.98, 7 % of risk, alpha 1.7 %, beta 0.03; equity timing 0.5 %/4.8 %/0.10, 26 % of risk. Table 3 (BXY-mimicking, 2 % OTM): 0.53/0.41/0.98/0.13, risk 83/5/12 %. Sub-periods (Table 5, short-volatility Sharpe): 1996–2001 1.49; 2002–2008 0.40; 2009–2014 1.39. Hedging the timing exposure raised the covered call's Sharpe from 0.37 to 0.52. "Shorting volatility has provided one-third of the covered call's average return."
- Israelov, Klein & Tummala, "Covering the World", *Journal of Risk* 2017 [PR, not retrieved]: 11 global indices, covered-call Sharpe 0.45 vs 0.33 to Sep 2015 per a secondary summary [SEC].

### 3.2 "Understanding the Volatility Risk Premium", Ang, Israelov, Sullivan & Tummala, May 2018 [PRAC] — `https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/White-Papers/Understanding-the-Volatility-Risk-Premium.pdf`

Rules: sell a front-month S&P 500 put 5 % out of the money, sized to unit leverage (notional = NAV), held to expiry, *delta-hedged daily* with S&P 500 futures; 1996-01-04 → 2016-12-31; "net of estimated transaction costs, gross of fees, and excess of cash." Exhibit 4: 1.5 %/yr, 2.2 % volatility, Sharpe 0.68, maximum drawdown −10 %, correlation to the S&P 500 0.07 over the full period but losses "during more extreme market moves, both positive and negative, such as in March 2000 and September 2008." Exhibit 3, the buyer's side (same put, unhedged, on a long S&P 500): S&P 500 5.1 %/16.1 %/0.32/−62 % becomes 1.8 %/12.7 %/0.14/−57 %. The paper's motivation is the Yale crash-confidence survey (two-thirds of investors believe a > 10 % chance of a 1929/1987-type crash within six months; realized frequency ≈ 1 %). It cites Israelov & Nielsen 2015 for "the VRP persists across volatility regimes" (selling in low-vol regimes still profitable on average) and Israelov & Tummala 2018 (which options to sell: front-month, near-the-money and moderately below — see the companion note). AQR's cost assumptions are "based on AQR's historical realized transaction costs" — an institution crossing at or inside the spread in size, not a marketable retail order (companion note §10, Muravyev–Pearson).

A note on "Risk Premia Harvesting": no AQR paper with that title was found; the phrase appears in Vontobel, MSCI and arXiv (Lempérière et al., "Risk Premia: Asymmetric Tail Risks and Excess Returns", 2014; "Trends and Risk Premia", 2017 [WP]) material. The arXiv papers report Sharpe ≈ 1.5 and skew ≈ −4.6 for short SPX options and a risk-managed short variance-swap Sharpe of 1.26 across underlyings 1996–2014, i.e. "investors are rewarded for skewness risk, not volatility risk" — institutional variance swaps, not read in full here.

### 3.3 Fallon, Park & Yu, "Asset Allocation Implications of the Global Volatility Premium", *FAJ* 71(5), 2015 [PR] — `https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v71y2015i5p38-56.html`

Global volatility-carry portfolio across 34 markets in four asset classes (swaptions, variance swaps, options on futures), 1995-01 → 2013-05: Sharpe "approximately 1.0", worst month −2.3 % (more than twice its annual standard deviation); "transaction costs played a significant role, reducing gross returns by 47 % on average in our sample"; small allocations raised portfolio Sharpe by up to 12 %. Institutional instruments and sizes; the 47 % cost haircut is the number to remember when a gross option-selling Sharpe is quoted.

### 3.4 Tail hedging from the seller's side

- Israelov, "Pathetic Protection: The Elusive Benefits of Protective Puts", *Journal of Alternative Investments* 21(3), Winter 2019 [PR] — `https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/Pathetic-Protection-JAI-Wint19.pdf`. PPUT 1986-07-01 → 2016-05-19, excess of 3-month LIBOR: "Investing 36.5 % in SPX and holding 63.5 % in cash provided the same 2.5 % compound annualized excess return as PPUT." Worst-1 % peak-to-trough drawdowns: 20-day −9.6 % (PPUT) vs −6.6 % (divested); 250-day −32.1 % vs −20.9 %; divesting had the better drawdown 97 % of the time over the shortest windows and 100 % over the longer ones. PPUT beta 0.74, volatility 13.5 % vs 6.6 % for the return-matched divested portfolio. An ex-ante implementable divested portfolio (average 84 % equity) returned 5.9 % vs PPUT's 3.2 % with about 10 % more volatility, "a 60 % improvement in Sharpe ratio." Regression alpha of PPUT −15 bp per month (t = −2.0). Cambridge Associates (2021, [PRAC]) paraphrases the result as "buying put options with a stock portfolio effectively delivered 35 % of the stock market return in exchange for about 75 % of the stock market risk" — `https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2021-05-Portfolio-Protection-Challenges-with-Equity-Put-Options.pdf`.
- Ilmanen, Thapar, Tummala & Villalon, "Tail Risk Hedging: Contrasting Put and Trend Strategies", *Journal of Systematic Investing* 1(1), Feb 2021 [PRAC, published in a practitioner journal] — `https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/Journal-of-Systematic-Investing-Vol-1-Issue-1--Tail-Risk-Hedging-AQR.pdf`. "Put" = buy a 5 %-OTM one-month S&P 500 put at mid-month (S&P 100 before 1996), re-initiate at expiry, gross of trading costs and fees, 6 % unlevered volatility, 1985-01 → 2020-03: "OTM put prices were high enough to give negative returns each decade" including 1987, 1998, 2001, 2008 and 2020; six OTM put specifications 1996–2020 (1-month and 1-year, 5–20 % OTM, delta-hedged and not) "share the same pattern of persistent losses, interspersed by temporary spikes." Hit rates: Put profitable in 7 of 8 slow drawdowns and 10 of 10 worst months; Trend 6/8 and 9/10. The live CBOE Eurekahedge Tail Risk Index since 2008: "around −2 % per annum, but −8 % per annum during the bullish 2010s," with survivorship bias noted. "None of the worst ten months was the first one within its broader drawdown episode" (e.g. Oct-1987 was month 2 of 3, Oct-2008 month 12 of 16, Mar-2020 month 3 of 3).

## 5. Tail hedging: the Universa claims and the critiques

- Universa's own framing (decennial letter 2018-03-16, available only as a Scribd upload [SEC], and the 2020 letter as reported by Institutional Investor): a hypothetical 3.33 % allocation to its tail-hedge fund with 96.67 % in SPY; 10-year CAGR 12.3 % (2008-03 → 2018-02) versus 9.7 % for a 25 % TLT / 75 % SPX portfolio; "11.5 % since inception in March 2008 vs 7.9 % for SPX" per the 2020 letter as reported; CAGR "beat the S&P 500 by 3.6 percent" over 12 years, "a 47.9 % gain in terminal wealth" (Lagnado, in `https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxbe3qsygknlfwk81z4/culture/calpers-cio-called-out-by-ex-head-of-tail-risk-program`, 2020-05-01). Business Insider/Yahoo report a "life-to-date average annual return on invested capital" above 105 % for 2008–2019 ([SEC], not fetched). None of these is audited in public; the firm says its returns "speak for themselves" and declined to comment on them (Bloomberg, [SEC]).
- Aaron Brown (Bloomberg Opinion, 2020-05-29, `https://www.wealthmanagement.com/alternative-investments/those-astronomical-returns-aren-t-what-they-seem`): the 4,144 % March 2020 figure is "computed on 10 % of the 3.33 %" that clients fund at a time; on the whole portfolio it is a 12.8 % gain, "about five years of premium payments"; the cost in non-crash months is about 0.22 % of portfolio assets per month; "the 4,144 % figure isn't useful information" and Universa's numbers "are not completely transparent."
- CalPERS terminated its tail-risk programme in early 2020 "because of their high cost, lack of scalability" (Meng) and was reported to have forgone more than $1 bn in March 2020; Universa's Lagnado replied that conventional diversifiers "provide so little relative protection in bad markets" while the hedge "becomes an immense drag in good markets" because "large allocations to the strategy are required." Both sides agree on the drag.
- The evidence that does not depend on either side: AQR's 35-year Put series (negative in every decade, §3.4), PPUT's 1986–2016 record (equal to 36.5 % equity / 63.5 % cash, §3.4), the Eurekahedge Tail Risk Index (−2 %/yr since 2008, −8 %/yr in the 2010s), CYA (−99.8 % in 30 months, §4.3), and the Cboe PPUT numbers above (2012–2026: 11.9 %/yr vs 13.4 % for the SPX price index, i.e. the 5 % OTM monthly put cost about 1.5 points of price return plus the dividends PPUT keeps — roughly 3–4 %/yr of drag against the total-return index — in exchange for a −22.1 % rather than −33.9 % worst drawdown, which came in 2022, a slow drawdown the monthly put barely helped). Taleb's 2020 dispute with Asness (Twitter; trade-press coverage) contains no numbers that can be checked.

Cost of replicating PUT on SPY, per $100 of notional per year [arithmetic on C1 and Bondarenko's premium rates]: gross premium 16–24 % of notional per year (12 sales of 1.4–2.0 %); half-spread at 0.5 % of a premium of 1.4–2.0 % = 0.007–0.010 % per sale → 0.08–0.12 %/yr; fees 12 × $0.70 on a ~$65,000 contract = 0.013 %/yr; expiry handling ≈ 0 for OTM, ≈ 0.01 % for ITM buy-backs or assignment. Total ≈ 0.10–0.15 %/yr of notional, about 1.5 % of the gross premium — an order of magnitude below the 47 % haircut Fallon et al. report for institutional multi-asset volatility carry and far below the 3–6 % round trips that killed the 0DTE lanes (H7, H9, S15). The material retail cost is elsewhere: (a) the index earns bills on 100 % of the strike, 1.71 %/yr over 2012–2026 — a retail cash-secured account forfeits whatever its broker does not pay; (b) a margin account can write the same put on ~20 % collateral (Reg T), which is how a −29 % index month becomes a margin call; the index numbers are for 1× notional. For a 30-delta call overwrite on 100 SPY shares the arithmetic is the same (premium ~0.7–1.0 % per month, cost ≈ 0.05–0.10 %/yr) with one extra hazard: SPY goes ex-dividend on the third Friday of March, June, September and December (State Street distribution schedule, `https://www.ssga.com/library-content/products/fund-data/etfs/us/distribution/SPDR_Dividend_Distribution_Schedule.pdf`), the same day the monthly roll happens, and an in-the-money short call is assigned the evening before when the dividend exceeds its remaining extrinsic value; the replication must roll ITM calls on the Thursday or accept assignment. XSP (Mini-SPX, 1/10 of SPX, European, cash-settled, Section 1256 60/40; `https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/mini_spx_options/european_style`) removes assignment and dividends and is the index's own instrument at ETF size; its spreads are not in C1 and were not measured.

## 10. Hypotheses carried forward (ids VC1–VC4; nothing here is a lane until it passes GATE-v2)

- **VC1 — Monthly ATM cash-secured put writing on SPY (PUT replication) is a positive-carry, beta-0.5 equity substitute with ~0.1–0.15 %/yr of retail execution cost; it is not an edge over holding SPY.** Evidence: Cboe fact sheet and methodology [EX]; Black–Szado, Bondarenko, Wilshire 2016 [PRAC]; Israelov–Nielsen 2014/2015 [PR]; Dew-Becker–Giglio 2025 [WP] (alpha ≈ 0 since 2010); own computations [COMPUTED]. Expected after costs over 2012-06 → 2026-10: 8.4–8.5 %/yr with bills as collateral, 6.8–7.0 % without, 8.8 % volatility, −29 % worst drawdown, against ≈ 15 %/yr for SPY total return; year-by-year sign identical to the index. The test is cheap and its first output, the tracking error to PUT at the bid, is itself the measurement of the retail cost of the whole family. Variants (VC1b): 2 % OTM (PUTY), and the Parametric 50/50 strangle. Registry: distinct from H9/H11/H13/S15 (0DTE ATM straddles, closed) and H15 (14:05 SPXW strangle, open) by tenor (one month) and collateral (1× notional); the companion note's §12 line "naked index put writing … nothing new proposed" was a judgement, not a registry closure, and is superseded only in the sense that the replication is proposed as a benchmark measurement.
- **VC2 — 30-delta monthly call overwriting on SPY shares (BXMD) and its JEPI-style weekly-laddered form is reproducible and earned 10.8 %/yr vs 13.4 % for the SPX price index since 2012 with beta 0.75 and the same drawdowns; the short-volatility component is worth ≈ 1.7–2 %/yr at Sharpe ≈ 1 but is only 5–7 % of the risk.** Evidence: BXM methodology [EX]; Israelov–Nielsen 2015 Tables 1/3/5 [PR]; JEPI N-CSR and manager transcript [EX/PRESS]; own computations. Expected after costs: index minus 0.05–0.10 %/yr minus any assignment slippage on quarterly ex-dividend rolls; a positive but small alpha to SPY (AQR's 1.7 %/yr over 1996–2014, 0.40-Sharpe short-vol leg in 2002–2008), probably lower post-2010. Not a Compass "signal"; a structural overlay a retail account can run on shares it holds anyway.
- **VC3 — AQR's delta-hedged short 5 %-OTM monthly put (1.5 %/yr at 2.2 % vol, Sharpe 0.68, 1996–2016, net of institutional costs) is reproducible with SPY shares as the hedge; expected post-2010 return 0 to +0.5 %/yr of notional before margin, with the −10 % drawdown arriving in a fast month.** Evidence: Ang et al. 2018 [PRAC]; Dew-Becker–Giglio [WP]; companion L1 (same question for the 30-DTE straddle). Low prior; run only if VC1's chain pull is already on disk, as the same contracts with a hedge leg.
- **VC4 — Weekly ATM put writing (WPUT) is worse than monthly before any cost (4.6 % vs 8.6 %/yr since 2012; Sharpe 0.34 vs 0.75; in drawdown from January 2018 to March 2020) and pays four times the spread crossings; proposed only as a cost measurement riding on the S03 chains, never as a lane.** Evidence: WPUT methodology [EX]; Bondarenko 2016 [PRAC]; own computations.

Expected sizes are stated in index-return terms because that is what the sources document; none of them is a per-contract expectancy against a matched random control, which is what GATE-v2 asks of a Compass lane. A fair prior is that each of VC1–VC3 reproduces its index minus 0.1–0.3 %/yr, carries the index's 2018/2020/2022/2025 drawdowns in full at 1× collateral, and shows no alpha to its own equity beta after 2012 that a day-clustered interval would separate from zero.

- 0DTE short straddles/strangles, unconditional or VRP-timed: H9, H11, H13, S15 (closed); the 14:05 SPXW strangle carry lane H15 stays as it is (open, forward paper only, fails the shock gate) — nothing here changes it.

- Put protection as a strategy (PPUT, CLL, PPUT3M, Universa-style always-on puts, CYA): documented negative carry in every source that publishes numbers (AQR 1985–2020 in every decade; PPUT 1986–2016 = 36.5 % equity + cash; Eurekahedge −2 %/yr; CYA −99.8 %); Universa's own numbers are unverifiable and, by Brown's arithmetic, a 12.8 % portfolio-level gain in March 2020 against 0.22 %/month of drag. The only use for a put leg here is as a GATE-v2 stress overlay whose cost is measured, not as a lane.
- Variance-swap VRP products (Nomura NEVRP, SGI Vol Premium): OTC; the Feb-2018 one-day −23 % mark is the documented behaviour of unhedged implied-vs-realized carry at fund leverage; inapplicable to an options-at-the-NBBO account.
