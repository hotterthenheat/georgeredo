# Execution economics of a retail options account, 2024–2026: fees, spreads, price improvement, settlement, margin, and the edge each structure must clear (sweep of 2026-10-14)

Scope: what a one-contract retail account actually pays and is exposed to when it trades SPX/SPXW, SPY, QQQ, IWM and a liquid single name, so that every registry hurdle (`research/compass/registry.jsonl`, GATE-v2 "execution realism") rests on measured numbers rather than the flat $0.70 + per-root half-spread it uses today. ## 0. The numbers that matter, in one place

- **Fees.** Full-service brokers (Schwab, Fidelity, E*TRADE, IBKR Lite/Pro ≤ 10k contracts) charge $0.65 per contract; with the OCC clearing fee ($0.025), ORF (Cboe $0.01248 per side; Robinhood's blended ORF+OCC $0.04), CAT ($0.0003) and, on sales only, FINRA TAF ($0.00329) and the SEC fee ($20.60 per $1 M of premium from 2026-04-04), the all-in is **$0.69–0.70 per contract per side** on ETF and stock options; the engine's $0.70 is right. Zero-commission brokers (Robinhood, Webull) pass through only the regulatory layer, **≈ $0.04–0.05 per side**. tastytrade is $1.00 to open, $0 to close, plus $0.10 clearing and $0.02 ORF per contract (≈ $0.62 per side averaged). **SPX/SPXW** add the Cboe customer transaction fee, $0.36 (premium < $1) or $0.45 (≥ $1) per contract, which brokers pass through at $0.45–0.66 (E*TRADE $0.54, tastytrade $0.60, Robinhood $0.50 contract fee + exchange fee): **≈ $1.00–1.25 per contract per side**, with no SEC fee or TAF on index options.
- **Spreads, measured (quoted half-spread at the fill minute, % of mid, medians).** 0DTE at 10:00: SPY ATM 0.61 % ($0.005), QQQ 0.86 % ($0.01), IWM 1.35 % ($0.01), SPXW 0.96 % ($0.10 on an $11.5 option); 0.1–0.5 % OTM: 1.06 / 1.01 / 1.72 / 1.45 %; 0.5–1 % OTM: 3.1 / 2.1 / 3.2 / 2.9 %; 1–2 % OTM: 14 / 7 / 11 / 10 %; beyond 2 % OTM the median option is quoted $0.00 × $0.01 (ETFs) or $0.00 × $0.05 (SPXW), i.e. the half-spread equals the mid. Weekly 4–7 DTE at 10:00 (2017–2026): SPY ATM 0.64 %, 0.1–0.5 % OTM 0.78 %, 1–2 % OTM 1.42 %; QQQ 0.75 / 1.31 / 1.99 %; IWM 1.34 / 0.96 / 1.82 %. Single names at the close (S11 cache, 8–30 DTE, near-ATM): median root 10.8 % (p25 6.1 %, p75 23.8 %); MSFT 2.6 %, GOOGL 1.4 %; the intraday fill-time medians of C1 for the same class are 0.8–1.3 % (MSFT, AAPL, NVDA, AMZN) and 3.5–4.1 % for the mixed single-name book. These agree with C1 (SPY/QQQ 0.5 %, IWM 0.7–1.0 %, SPXW 0.9 % at fill time, 2025–2026) and are far below the 5–13 % quoted spreads of the 2020–2023 academic samples: spreads collapsed between 2020 and 2023 (Fu et al.), so any cost assumption lifted from those papers overstates the 2025–2026 regime by 3–10×.
- **Time of day.** In dollars the ETF half-spread is one half-tick ($0.005) at every minute from 09:31 to 16:00 near the money; in percent of premium it is lowest 09:35–11:00 (SPY ATM 0.47–0.61 %), doubles by 15:00 (1.18 %), and is 4–6 % at 15:55–16:00 because the premium has decayed, not because the quote widened. SPXW's dollar half-spread halves from $0.10 (morning) to $0.05 (11:00–15:45) to $0.025 (last 15 minutes). The opening minute (09:31) is not wider than 10:00 in percent terms for 0DTE on any of the four products; it has more two-tick markets (SPY ATM one-tick share 61 % at 09:31, 57 % at 10:00, 85 % at 11:00).
- **Price improvement.** For marketable retail-sized options orders the published evidence (OPRA, Aug–Dec 2020) is price improvement of 3.43 ¢ on a 10.45 ¢ quoted half-spread (0.79 % of the option price), effective/quoted 0.74 in continuous trading and 0.38 in auctions; auctions take 23 % of single-leg trades. No retail broker publishes an options-specific per-contract improvement statistic; Robinhood's 606 report shows what it is paid instead: $0.30–$1.20 per contract on single-leg orders of 1–100 contracts, scaled to the symbol's average spread, and nothing on index options. The only retail-accessible route that halves the cost is the resting limit order at the midpoint (Fu et al.: $0.021–0.028 expected cost vs $0.05 marketable on sub-$3 SPXW options, 45–70 % fill probability); GATE-v2 prices the marketable side.
- **Settlement.** SPX: European, cash, Section 1256; AM-settled third-Friday series settle to SET from component opening prices and stop trading the prior day; SPXW settles to component closing prices and stops trading at 4:00 pm ET on expiration while the index itself trades to 4:15 pm. ETFs: American, 100-share delivery, OCC auto-exercise at $0.01 in the money, exercise instructions accepted until 5:30 pm ET, options trade until 4:15 pm ET, shares settle T+1. The after-hours window (4:00 → 5:30 pm) is a real cost for ETF short premium held to expiry: the registry's own H16 re-valuation cut the SPY hold-to-close gain from +$30.60 to +$6.30 per trade.
- **Margin.** Reg T/FINRA 4210: short equity option = premium + 20 % of the underlying − OTM amount, floor 10 %; broad-based index 15 % / floor 10 %; a defined-risk spread is margined at its maximum potential loss (strike width − net credit); a straddle at the greater side plus the other side's premium. Portfolio margin (≥ $100–110 k equity in practice) stresses broad-based indexes −8 %/+6 % and equities ±15 %. A 0.30-delta 30-DTE SPY put is $13.8 k of Reg-T margin naked versus $1.5 k as a 20-point vertical; the same put on SPX is $100 k naked under 15 %.
- **Day trading.** The $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum was in force through 2024–May 2026 (options day trades count); FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10 (published 2026-04-20) replaced it, effective 2026-06-04 with a phase-in to 2027-10-20, by intraday margin standards: no PDT designation, no $25 k floor, no day-trading buying power; firms must compute an intraday margin deficit and restrict accounts that habitually fail to cover it.
- **Per-structure (§9).** At the measured medians and $0.70 fees, a 0.45-delta 0DTE SPY single costs $2.71 per round trip (2.5 % of a $108 premium; 0.35 bp of notional) and needs +0.8 bp of favourable underlying move to pay the cost alone and +4 bp to pay cost plus one hour of theta; the same contract on SPXW costs $23 (2.1 %, 0.30 bp of notional). A 0DTE 0.30/0.15 credit vertical on SPY costs 14 % of its credit; a 0.20/0.10 iron condor 23 % (46 % on IWM). Weekly and 30-DTE structures cost 1.4–4.5 % of premium on SPY/SPXW/QQQ, 3–9 % on IWM and MSFT, 11–15 % for condors, and the long structures' theta over a one- to five-session hold is 10–60 bp of notional, an order of magnitude above the spread-and-fee cost. For short premium the exit side dominates: buying back near-worthless legs costs 3.3 % of the credit on a 0DTE ETF straddle (C1) and the median OTM leg after 15:00 is quoted at a 100 % half-spread.

### 2.3 Reading across the sources

The 2020–2023 academic samples (Hendershott 1.21 % quoted half-spread on $8.66 options; Bryzgalova 12.6 % full spread on sub-week retail trades; Beckmeyer 5–6 % effective on SPX 0DTE; Doshi 9 % on 0DTE SPX 2004–2020) and the 2025–2026 measurements (C1 and the tables above: 0.5–1.5 % half-spread near the money on the four index products at mid-morning) differ by the compression Fu et al. document (one-tick SPXW markets 0.5 % → 65 % of the time between July 2020 and July 2023) and by moneyness: the published averages pool far-OTM and late-day contracts whose half-spread is the whole mid. For a cost model the right inputs are the bucketed, time-stamped medians, with p75 as the adverse case; the sibling notes' reading that 2021–2022 cost estimates overstate the current regime by 3–10× stands. On SPX versus SPY per unit of notional: at 10:00 the ATM 0DTE half-spread is 0.96 % on SPXW vs 0.61 % on SPY (SPXW's $0.05 tick on an $11.5 option is a coarser grid than SPY's $0.01 on $1.14), but fees per notional are 10× lower on SPXW, so the round trip of a 0.45-delta single is 0.30 bp of notional on SPXW against 0.35 bp on SPY (§9); the crossover is one contract, and above ten SPY contracts SPXW is cheaper on both legs.

## 3. Price improvement and the retail execution routes

- **What marketable retail orders get** [PR]: Hendershott et al. (2020 sample) — 3.43 ¢ average price improvement on a 10.45 ¢ quoted half-spread (0.79 % of price); 7.62 ¢ when the order goes through an auction (23 % of single-leg trades), 2.2 ¢ in continuous trading, and only 0.98 ¢ in continuous trades on exchanges where the DMM is a PFOF wholesaler. The EQ ratio of 0.74 in continuous trading is the number to use for a marketable one-lot that is not auctioned: it pays ≈ 74 % of the quoted half-spread. Ernst & Spatt, NBER w29883 [WP] — https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29883/revisions/w29883.rev0.pdf — estimate from Rule 606 reports that routing a 100-share-equivalent options order earns the broker ≈ 40 ¢ (20 ¢ for a stock order), that auctions are 15 % of trades and generate $200–600 M per month of improvement, and that a PFOF-paying specialist is associated with a price ≈ 100 bp (2 ¢ on a $2 option) worse per share than a non-PFOF one. Bryzgalova et al. [PR]: > 95 % of retail options orders are purchased by wholesalers (as summarised by Hendershott et al.).
- **What brokers disclose**: Robinhood Securities Rule 606, Q2 2026 (https://cdn.finra.org/606/2026/287900_606_NMS_2026_Q2_V1.pdf) [PRAC]: options order mix market 2.3 %, marketable limit 26.8 %, non-marketable limit 53.6 %, other 17.3 %; venues Citadel 42.2 %, Dash/IMC 29.7 %, Wolverine 11.5 %, Jane Street 9.0 %, Global Execution Brokers 7.5 %; "RHS received a per contract rate for orders of 1–100 contracts of $0.30, $0.38, $0.56, $0.70, $0.90 or $1.20 depending on the symbol's average spread", $0.15–0.60 for 101+ contracts, $0.30–0.90 for multi-leg, and "RHS does not receive payments for orders in index options"; curb-session (4:15–5:00 pm ET) and GTH orders route to Wolverine. Schwab's Order Execution Advantage page (https://www.schwab.com/execution-quality/quality-statistics) reports equity statistics (97 % of orders price-improved, $966 M in Q2 2026) and, for Q2 2025, "$1.9 billion in price improvement on their equity and option orders" with no options breakdown; Fidelity (https://www.fidelity.com/trading/execution-quality/overview) reports "$3.2 billion saved … on all Fidelity equity, options & ETF orders in FY 2025", counting "marketable option orders (1–100 contracts)", again with no per-contract option statistic. No retail broker publishes an options-only improvement rate; Rule 605 does not cover options.
- **The retail-accessible lever**: the non-marketable limit order (Fu et al.: 53.6 % of Robinhood's options orders are non-marketable). Expected cost at the midpoint of a two-tick SPXW market $0.021–0.028 per contract versus $0.05 marketable, fill probability 45–70 %, non-fill penalised at one second. This is an execution model, not an edge: it halves the spread side of the round trip and leaves the entry's expectation untouched, and GATE-v2 requires NBBO fills until measured fill rates exist (C1's fill-realism finding — 58 % of GeorgeFi Epoch-1 rows priced better than the market, 27 % below the minute's lowest bid — is the reason).
- **Institutional-only**: initiating AIM/PIM auctions (a broker/wholesaler function; the auction initiator's contra side earns the Cboe "AIM Agency/Primary" surcharges and SCORe rebates), DMM priority in continuous trading, realized-spread capture (Fu: ≈ 2.5 ¢ early in the day, < 1 ¢ after a minute), market-maker sliding scales ($0.24–0.28 per contract on SPX with volume tiers), exchange fee caps per order (20,000 contracts), and PFOF itself.

## 4. Behaviour at the open and the close

- **Opening minutes.** On the four index products the 0DTE half-spread in percent is *lowest* at 09:31–09:35 and rises through the day (table §2.2); in dollars it is one half-tick for the ETFs from the first quote. The one-tick share is lower at the open and at 10:00 than at 11:00–15:00 (SPY ATM 61 % / 57 % / 85 %), so a marketable order at the open pays a second tick more often; the 09:30:00 row is empty on every product (no quote has been posted). Chan–Chung–Johnson [PR] found the same shape for equity options in 1995 (sharp fall after the open, then flat). The SPX index value itself is stale for the first minutes: the published index uses the previous close for constituents that have not opened and almost always starts at the prior close, with ≈ 95 % of stocks open within 15 minutes (CME, "Understanding the Special Opening Quotation" [EX/PRAC; page blocked the fetch, statement from the search summary] — https://www.cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/understanding-the-special-opening-quotation-soq.html); the AM settlement value SET uses each component's opening price and is published only once all have opened, so an SPX 9:30–9:45 print and SET can differ by the whole opening gap. Cboe's opening rotation (Rule 5.31; https://www.sec.gov/file/34-87682) starts after the first primary-market trade after 9:30; a series that fails the composite width check can be force-opened after a class-specific period (https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2025/34-103184-ex5.pdf).
- **Closing minutes.** ETF options trade until 4:15 pm ET while the shares stop at 4:00 (Cboe: SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM close at 3:15 pm CT — https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/cboe-trading-hours-to-change-on-february-13-2006-2011124/ [EX notice via a news mirror]; Rule 6.1 interpretation quoted in SEC 34-50840 — https://www.sec.gov/file/34-50840); SPXW stops at 4:00 pm ET on its expiration day and otherwise at 4:15 with a curb session to 5:00 pm and global trading hours 8:15 pm–9:25 am ET (Cboe spec — https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/specifications/). Our cache ends at 16:00; after 15:00 the median OTM leg is quoted at a half-spread equal to its mid (100 %) on all four products, ATM legs at 4–14 % at 15:55–16:00 on the ETFs and 6 % on SPXW at 15:55 — this is H14's rule (leave ≤ $0.05 ETF / ≤ $0.10 SPXW legs to expire, +$0.3 / +$2.7 per trade) measured at the chain level. Goyenko–Zhang's +50 bp closing-spread jump applies to single-name closing quotes (the S11 table). For PM-settled SPXW the settlement value is the Cboe close, which the ThetaData EOD close matches to the cent on 1,105 of 1,105 sessions, while the 16:00 minute print differs by 0.63 pts on average and up to 18.32 pts (2026-06-26) (`research/thetadata/settlement_audit/README.md`) — any SPXW hold-to-settlement study must use the official close (DATA/GATE-v2 already require it).

## 6. Early-exercise risk

- Pool, Stoll, Whaley, "Failure to exercise call options: An anomaly and a trading game", *Journal of Financial Markets* 11(1) 2008, 1–35 [PR] — https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v11y2008i1p1-35.html (abstract restricted; magnitudes from Vanderbilt's summary of the 2007 study: call holders forwent ≈ $491 M over ten years by not exercising dividend-paying stock calls at the ex-date — https://blogs.owen.vanderbilt.edu/vanderbiltbusiness/failure-to-exercise/). The counterparty that benefits is the short-call holder who is *not* assigned; market makers run the "dividend play" to capture it.
- Barraclough, Whaley, "Early Exercise of Put Options on Stocks", *Journal of Finance* 2012 [PR] (OptionMetrics listing https://optionmetrics.com/?p=1026 ; Vanderbilt summary above): Jan 1996–Sep 2008, 3.96 million put contracts (3.7 % of puts outstanding) not exercised when the interest on the strike exceeded the option's time value; > $1.9 bn forgone (≈ $1.82 bn after trading costs), captured by short-put holders including market makers.
- Jensen, Pedersen, "Early Option Exercise: Never Say Never", *JFE* 121(2) 2016, 278–299 [PR] — https://ideas.repec.org:443/p/cpr/ceprdp/11019.html (open-access text https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/files/44936283/1_s2.0_S0304405X1630099X_main_1_.pdf): early exercise is optimal when it saves short-sale, transaction or funding costs; the model explains ≈ 98 % of market-maker early exercises and ≈ 67 % of customer early exercises.
- Reading for the registry: assignment on a retail short ETF option arrives when the counterparty's funding or dividend arithmetic says so — deep ITM puts when rates are high (the 2024–2026 regime at 4–5 % bills makes a 5 %-ITM 30-DTE SPY put worth exercising about a week before expiry), ITM calls the night before SPY's quarterly ex-date — and otherwise at expiration through the $0.01 rule. For spreads whose short leg is OTM at entry (the §9 structures) the probability is small and the cost on assignment is the share spread plus financing for one day; the material exposure is the one H16 measured (after-hours moves on expiration day), not dividend arbitrage. For a long option the account holds, the two papers say the holder is the one who forgets: an ITM long put should be exercised when its extrinsic value is below the strike's one-day interest, and an ITM long SPY call should be exercised (or sold) before the ex-date if its extrinsic is below the dividend.

## 8. Pattern-day-trader constraints

- **2024 → 2026-06-03** [EX]: FINRA 4210(f)(8)(B) pattern day trader: four or more day trades in five business days (> 6 % of trades) in a margin account → $25,000 minimum equity, day-trading buying power 4× maintenance excess, 90-day freeze on a missed call; options day trades count. The Cboe Margin Manual still prints "a $25,000 minimum account equity requirement for pattern day trading". Cash accounts are outside the rule but can spend only settled funds (options settle T+1), which for a $5–25 k account is the binding limit on 0DTE round trips.
- **From 2026-06-04** [EX]: FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10 (published 2026-04-20; SR-FINRA-2025-017, 91 FR 1580; https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10 ; https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/SR_FINRA-2025-017-91-FR-1580.pdf) "replaces the outdated requirements in their entirety": no pattern-day-trader designation, no $25,000 minimum, no day-trading buying power; instead each member must determine the **intraday margin deficit** of every margin account on any day with an intraday-margin-level-reducing transaction ("real-time monitoring is not a requirement"; one calculation a day is permitted), require it "to be satisfied as promptly as possible" (outstanding until satisfied or the close of the 15th business day), and enforce a 90-day restriction on new short positions or debit balances for customers who regularly fail to cover by the fifth business day (deficits ≤ the lesser of 5 % of equity or $1,000 excluded); phase-in allowed until 2027-10-20; portfolio-margin accounts below $5 M must keep intraday margin substantially similar to end-of-day margin. Cboe's comment letter (https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/government_relations/Cboe-Response-to-FINRA-PDT-Proposal-February-2026-.pdf) [EX comment] supports the change. Practical effect for a retail options account after June 2026: the constraint is the broker's house intraday margin on the option positions themselves (long options fully paid; short premium at the Reg-T rates of §7 at the moment of the trade), not a trade count; brokers may keep house $25 k floors, and none of the five schedules above states its post-June policy.

How to read the table against the registry. (i) The 0DTE SPY single's $2.71 round trip matches S01's measured $2.79; its break-even move of 0.8 bp is trivially small and the hurdle that matters is the 3.2 bp of theta per hour plus the −6 to −8 bp the entries actually deliver (S04): execution cost did not decide S01, S02 or S09, and a zero-commission broker (+$1.30 per contract) does not change their sign. (ii) Defined-risk structures pay two or four spreads on premiums that are a fraction of the single's: a 0.30/0.15 0DTE credit vertical gives up 14 % of its credit on SPY and 27 % on IWM to execution, a 0.20/0.10 condor 23 % and 46 % — S10-rerun's "verticals pay two spreads and are worse" and S12b's −$2.56 vertical are this arithmetic. (iii) For short premium the entry-side cost is 1.5–3.3 % of credit on SPY/SPXW at every tenor, so a carry lane's economics are set by the exit rule (expire vs buy back: H14) and by capital (§7), not by the opening spread; on IWM and QQQ 0DTE the cost is 2–3× SPY's. (iv) Index products: SPXW costs 0.30–0.69 bp of notional per 0DTE round trip against SPY's 0.35–1.24, i.e. SPX is already the cheaper product per dollar of exposure at one contract, and the gap widens with size; XSP is unmeasured. (v) Single names: at the assumed 1.5 % half-spread MSFT costs 3–9 % of premium for the delta structures and the break-even moves are 8–49 bp per hold — the same order as the S07/S08 effects (−22 to −97 bp over 5–21 sessions), so at the median single name (closing half-spread 11 %) no S07/S08-sized effect survives execution; only the top-decile names (closing half-spread ≤ 4 %, intraday ≤ 1 %) can carry one. (vi) "Minimum underlying edge per holding period" for a long structure = break-even move + theta; for 0DTE singles on the index products that is ≈ 4 bp per hour (SPY, SPXW), 6 bp (QQQ), 7 bp (IWM); for weeklies ≈ 22–35 bp per session; for 30-DTE ≈ 45–70 bp per five sessions; for credit verticals the structure *earns* theta of the same order, so its minimum edge is a realized move smaller than the implied one by the break-even column (4–50 bp depending on tenor and product) — the S10/S12b/S15 tests all cleared the spread hurdle and failed on the entry, which is consistent with this table.

## 10. Institutional-only versus retail-accessible

Retail-accessible: marketable orders at the NBBO with the §9 costs; resting limit orders at the midpoint (Fu et al.; halves the spread side at 45–70 % fill probability; needs a measured fill model before GATE-v2 can accept it); exit by expiration where the product allows it (SPXW cash settlement; H14's leave-to-expire rule on ETFs with the after-hours caveat); product choice (SPXW over SPY for notional ≥ one SPX contract; XSP untested); broker choice (≤ $0.65 per side swing; index pass-through $0.45–0.66); Section 1256 on index options; entering 09:35–11:00 rather than after 13:00 on 0DTE (half the percentage cost near the money). Institutional-only: auction initiation and the AIM/PIM contra-side economics, DMM priority, PFOF receipt, maker rebates and MM sliding scales ($0.24–0.28 SPX), per-order fee caps (20,000 contracts), realized-spread capture at sub-second horizons, cross-product arbitrage (ES vs SPX, SPY vs SPX), term financing at bill rates on 100 % of collateral (the Cboe benchmark assumption; sibling note vrp-carry §7), and the ability to be short gamma into the close without the 4:00–5:30 pm exercise exposure (a cash-settled book).

## 11. What this changes for the registry, and what is testable

Nothing here reopens a closed hypothesis: every negative result in `05_results.md` was negative *before* costs or lost to the entry rather than to the spread (S01 gross −$2.79; S04/S06 flat-to-negative underlying; S07/S08 negative adjusted returns; S10/S12b/S15 short premium flat or negative at an entry cost of 2–5 % of credit). The measurements above refine the cost side and define what an execution-realistic test must assume.

- **C2 (cost measurement, adopt):** replace the per-root flat half-spreads of C1 with the bucketed medians of §2.2 (symbol × tenor × moneyness bucket × minute), p75 as the adverse-fill scenario, one-half-tick floor, and the measured §1 fee layer ($0.70 ETF/single, $1.15 SPXW; $0.05/$0.99 zero-commission). Re-pricing S01/S09/S10/S12b/S15 under C2 is a one-pass job on cached chains and is expected to move the ETF results by less than $1 per contract and no sign. (Testable now: yes; data: cached 0DTE and S03 chains.)
- **X1 (execution model, not a lane):** measure the fill probability and realized cost of a one-lot limit order resting at the midpoint (and at one tick inside the touch) on SPXW and SPY 0DTE and weeklies, from 1-second NBBO and trade prints with the quote at the print (ThetaData `trade-quote`, 2012-06 →; the 0DTE cache holds 1-minute NBBO, so 1-second quotes and prints must be pulled for a sample of sessions), replicating Fu et al.'s $0.021–0.028 vs $0.05 and 45–70 % fill rates on 2024–2026 data. Outcome: a documented alternative fill model for GATE-v2 with a measured non-fill rate; it halves the spread side of §9 and nothing else. Retail-accessible; peer-reviewed/WP support.
- **X2 (product substitution):** measure XSP 0DTE and weekly half-spreads by moneyness and minute against SPY's (XSP root in ThetaData, not cached). Expected from the only snapshot (TradeStation, vendor): 3–6× SPY's dollar spread per unit of notional; decides whether an ETF-sized lane could take SPX settlement and tax treatment. Retail-accessible; no academic source.
- **X3 (exposure diagnostic for ETF short legs):** on the S03 weekly chains 2017 → 2026, count the sessions on which a 0.30Δ short SPY call is in the money on the session before an ex-dividend date with extrinsic value below the dividend, and the expiration days on which the 16:00 → 17:15 move crosses a 0.20–0.30Δ short strike (H16's measurement extended from 0DTE to weeklies, requires the post-16:00 quotes, which are not cached). Outcome: an execution rule (close short legs inside the after-hours range by 15:50; never hold ITM short calls into SPY's quarterly ex-date), not a lane. Retail-accessible; PR support (Pool–Stoll–Whaley, Barraclough–Whaley, Jensen–Pedersen).
- **X4 (single-name liquidity screen for S11):** restrict any single-name option test to roots whose closing near-ATM half-spread is ≤ 4 % (top decile of the S11 cache; §2.2) and price them at the intraday C1 medians rather than the close; at the cache median (11 %) the S07/S08 effects (−22 to −97 bp) are inside one round trip. Testable now (CSV `single_name_eod_rows.csv`).

## 12. Closed or inapplicable after this sweep

- "A cheaper broker fixes S01/S02/S09": closed by arithmetic — the full-service to zero-commission difference is $1.30 per ETF contract per round trip against a gross expectancy of −$2.79 (S01) and −$24 vs random (S09).
- "SPX is cheap enough to change the sign": closed by S09 (−$46.18 per contract, −$24.09 vs random); per notional SPXW is cheaper than SPY by ≈ 15 % on the single, which is not a sign change.
- "Mid-point fills" as a backtest assumption: disallowed by GATE-v2 and by C1's fill-realism finding; reopened only through X1's measured fill model.
- "Hold ETF short premium to expiry to save the exit spread": closed by H16 (after-hours exercise valuation) unless the X3 rule is applied; H14's leave-to-expire rule stands for ≤ $0.05 legs.
- "Exit after 15:00 on OTM ETF legs": inapplicable as a cost-saving — the median OTM leg's half-spread equals its mid after 15:00 on all four products (H14).
- "Price-improvement statistics justify a lower cost assumption": the published improvement (EQ 0.74 continuous) applies to 2020 spreads 3–10× wider than today's; on a one-half-tick market there is nothing to improve inside the tick, so the measured quoted half-spread is the executable cost for a one-lot ETF order.
- "Single-name option lanes executed at the close" (S07/S08/S11 at closing quotes): inapplicable at the cache median (11 % half-spread); only the ≤ 4 % decile is in scope (X4).
- 30-DTE index spreads: not measured locally; the §9 30-DTE rows carry the weekly ATM assumption and are not evidence until a chain is pulled.
