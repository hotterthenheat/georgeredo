## 1. The claims and their evidence status

### 1.1 Direction: gamma sign conditions intraday momentum versus reversal

**Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 142 (2021) 377–403, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.04.029 [PR].** Full text: https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf; record: https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/. Sample: 60+ futures (equity index, bond, commodity, currency), December 1974 – May 2020. Claim: the last-30-minute return r_LH is positively predicted by the return from the previous close to 30 minutes before the close, r_ROD; equity-index futures timing strategy η(r_ROD) earns 6.86 %/yr, SD 3.96 %, Sharpe 1.73, success 55 % (Table 6, 1/N across index futures, gross); "we do not consider transaction costs... the strategy as presented might not be exploitable to many investors after accounting for transaction costs"; "exploiting the effect in the S&P 500 futures yields a positive net Sharpe ratio when we assume transaction cost equal to a tick (a cost level faced commonly by advanced investors in the S&P 500 futures market)". Dealer-gamma conditioning (§4.1, Tables 7–8, S&P 500 futures, January 1996 – May 2020): NGE = Σ Γ·OI·100·P over calls + Σ Γ·OI·(−100)·P over puts, divided by S&P 500 market value, from OptionMetrics 1996–2017 and SqueezeMetrics to May 2020 ("We have verified the computations of SqueezeMetrics using OptionMetrics data over the 2002 to 2017 period, yielding virtually identical results"); 2,930 negative-NGE days, 3,158 positive. On NGE_{t−1} < 0 days β_ROD = 6.63 (×100; Newey–West t = 4.78; R² 3.58 %); on NGE ≥ 0 days β_ROD = 0.82 (t = 1.03; R² 0.05 %). Interaction form: r_ROD 1.41 (t 1.72) plus I(NGE ≤ 0)·r_ROD 5.05 (t 3.04); continuous NGE·r_ROD −123.04 (t −3.42). The effect reverts over the next days (equity index futures: next-day −14.51, t −1.70; two-day −29.05, t −3.16). Footnote 8 is the caveat that matters for us: "NGE is on average positive for most days... we assume option market makers are short all puts and long all calls. We do not have data on, for example, OTC option positions and precise end-user option positions." Status for a retail account: the conditional effect is 6.6 bp of last-half-hour return per 1 % of rest-of-day move; the instrument in which it cleared costs is the E-mini at one tick, which is a futures-account, institutional-cost result.

**Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi & Moerke, "The Role of Leveraged ETFs and Option Market Imbalances on End-of-Day Price Dynamics", University of St. Gallen Working Papers on Finance 2021/14 (September 2021; later retitled "Liquidity Provision to Leveraged ETFs and Equity Options Rebalancing Flows", SSRN 3925725, Swiss Finance Institute 22-40) [WP, not refereed].** Full text: https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/5a99db31-0d37-4f86-9502-8cb0f3bff4fe/content; record: https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/publications/264338; SSRN https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3925725. Data: exchange-tagged open/close buy/sell volume by participant type (broker/dealer, proprietary, customer) from CBOE C1 (from 2010), ISE (from May 2005), PHLX (2009), GEMX (2013), BX; sample to July 2020; OptionMetrics Greeks; LETF holdings from ETFGlobal, January 2012 – December 2019; 3,428,010 optionable stock-days. Dealer net open interest per series (eq. 3) is −[customer buys − customer sells + proprietary buys − proprietary sells]: the market-maker position is the negative of everyone else's, measured, not assumed. Γ^IB (eq. 4) = Σ netOI × Γ × S_{15:30} × multiplier × S/100 ÷ last-half-hour average dollar volume; Γ^HP interacts Γ^IB with the return to 15:30. Results: last-30-minute return on Γ^HP, β₁ = −11.947 (t = −5.37), stable to IV and realized-vol controls (Table 4); signed last-half-hour volume on Γ^HP, −13.37 (Table 3); the LETF rebalancing effect Ω^LETF is "about 4 times larger" in the joint sample (Table 7); the intraday-window regression finds "the bulk of rebalancing seems to occur between 3:30pm and 4pm, when the coefficient is the largest in absolute magnitude at roughly −10" (Fig. 6); effects revert at the next open. Trading strategy (Table 8, January 2012 – December 2019, deciles formed at 15:30, closed at 16:00, gross): η(Γ^HP) 5.16 %/yr, SD 1.25 %, Sharpe 4.13, success 62.3 % (equal-weighted; 5.20 %, Sharpe 3.83 value-weighted); η(Ω^LETF) 11.82 %, Sharpe 4.65 EW but 5.78 %, Sharpe 2.51 VW ("outsized for smaller stocks"); combined 11.00 %, Sharpe 5.09; "The daily return η(Γ^HP, Ω^LETF) equates to 4.36 basis points on average"; the benchmark Market_end (long all stocks 15:30→16:00) 0.62 %/yr, insignificant. No transaction costs. The 2022 revision's abstract (SFI 22-40, https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-22-40-liquidity-provision-to-leveraged-etfs-and-equity-options-rebalancing-flows-evidence-from-end-of-day-stock-prices) states that "gamma effects are persistent throughout our sample, while LETFs effects have decreased over time". Arithmetic for a retail account: 5.16 %/yr over 252 days is 2.0 bp per day per side of a long-short decile portfolio held 30 minutes; a marketable round trip in large-cap shares costs 1–3 bp; the option expression is dead on arrival (single-name C1 half-spreads 3.5–4.1 % of premium per side against a 2 bp underlying effect).

**Barbon & Buraschi, "Gamma Fragility", working paper, first version January 2019, this version 18 March 2021, SSRN 3725454 [WP].** Full text: http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf; page: https://abarbon.com/papers/gamma-fragility. Data: participant-tagged trade data from ISE, GEMX and PHLX (10–20 % of US equity-option dollar volume), January 2010 – May 2020; OptionMetrics; CRSP/TAQ; RavenPack; final panel 2,700 stocks and four indices (S&P 500, Dow, Nasdaq 100, Russell 2000); 3,666,122 stock-days. Stock-level proxy: Hedgers Inventory = BrokerDealers Inventory − Customers Inventory, following NPPW; Γ^IB = Hedgers Gamma × S / 21-day average share volume. Index proxy (no index options in the exchange data): "we fall back to a different proxy... relying on the assumption that most investors have short positions on calls and long positions on puts (collar strategy)... broker/dealers are mostly long call options and short put options", Γ^IB₁ = (Γ$_Call − Γ$_Put)/ADV × 100 — the SqueezeMetrics convention. Results: |daily return| on lagged Γ^IB, t from −5.07 (IV control only) to −12.08 (asset and month FE); "one standard deviation in the gamma imbalance is associated to a decrease between 5 and 25 basis points in the absolute value of the daily return... absolute daily returns average at 180 basis points"; 70 % of stock-level time-series t-statistics negative; indices (21,434 index-days) −14.8 to −5.5, t −4.4 to −3.3, "more than 20 basis points (about 20 % of a standard deviation)"; illiquid stocks (below-median Amihud) carry an extra ≈ 9 bp per sd (Table V); intraday autocorrelation of h-minute returns (h = 5…60) falls 1.5–2 pp per sd of Γ^IB (§III.C), the momentum/reversal claim of the title; flash crashes 1997–2015 (10,572 events) more frequent and 20–200 bp deeper at low Γ^IB. No trading strategy, no costs. The direction claim is a sign-of-autocorrelation claim in a stock panel, strongest in illiquid names, which is where retail option spreads are 25–43 % (C1, CRML).

**Adams, Dim, Eraker, Fontaine, Ornthanalai & Vilkov, "Do S&P500 Options Increase Market Volatility? Evidence from 0DTEs", draft of 15 July 2026, SSRN 5641974 [WP].** Full text: https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf. The draft states that it "subsumes two working papers '0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation' [SSRN 4692190] and 'The Market for 0DTE options: The Role of Liquidity Providers in Volatility Attenuation' [SSRN 4881008]", so Dim–Eraker–Vilkov and Adams–Fontaine–Ornthanalai are no longer separate sources. Data: Cboe intraday Option Quotes and Open-Close Volume Summary at 10-minute intervals by participant category, SPX options, January 2018 – December 2024; E-mini futures, SPY and cash-index minute data from DTN IQFeed. Direction-relevant findings (§4, "This evidence is not causal"): with elevated market-maker net gamma "a negative 10-minute return is followed by positive net order flow during the next interval... this relationship strengthens significantly... remains statistically significant for about 30 minutes and decays toward zero within the hour"; "intraday momentum returns from one hour to the next tend to be positive when market makers' hedging needs are low, but these returns tend to be negative—reversal returns tend to be positive—when hedging needs are elevated"; "we cannot directly link E-mini order flow with specific option market makers". Positioning: "The market makers' net gamma is positive on average and throughout most of our sample. Figure IA2 shows that Net Γ_t is positive for 88 percent of all 10-minute trading intervals." Which positions carry the hedging need: "Stale 0DTE positions account for the larger share of market makers' total 0DTE net gamma. The daily average Stale 0DTE Net Γ_t on expiration days was 0.29 and 0.39 percent before and after May 2022, respectively, but 0.08 and 0.16 percent for new 0DTE Net Γ_t"; "about half of the Stale positions were opened one day before the expiration date, close to 90 percent were opened during the week before expiration". This is the one finding in the 0DTE literature that favours public open interest: the hedging need that matters on an expiration day is in positions that were open at the prior settlement, which is what OI records.

**Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?", version 15 December 2023, SSRN 4404704 [WP].** Full text: https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf. Cboe SPX trade data with trader-type codes, February 2021 – September 2023. Positioning claim: "the 0DTE order imbalance of option market makers is consistently negative. It hovers between −0.5 % and −1 % up until the introduction of a daily expiration calendar in May of 2022. Afterwards... has since then consistently dipped below −1 %. End consumers have net-long demand for these options... Option market makers have negative Gamma exposure, as a result of their large and negative order imbalance in 0DTE options. To rebalance their hedges, they therefore need to invest in the same direction as the previous market move, which potentially increases the magnitude of intraday market swings." This is a net-contract imbalance of about 1 % of volume, and the amplification sentence is a conjecture, not a test; it is contradicted on net gamma by Cboe's own position-level measurement (below) and by Adams et al.'s 88 % positive net gamma on the same exchange's data. The paper's measured content on this topic is the imbalance figure, nothing more.

**Pearson, Poteshman & White, 31 July 2006 draft of what became Ni, Pearson, Poteshman & White, "Does Option Trading Have a Pervasive Impact on Underlying Stock Prices?", Review of Financial Studies 34(4), 2021, 1952–1986, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhaa082 [PR; numbers from the 2006 draft, labelled preliminary].** Draft: https://ou.edu/dam/price/Finance/CFS/paper/pdf/pearsonPoteshmanWhite.pdf; record: https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v34y2021i4p1952-1986..html. Data: CBOE daily open interest by origin (purchased and written, public customer and firm proprietary; market-maker position inferred as the negative of the sum), every CBOE-listed equity option series, 1990–2001. Result (Table 2, τ = 5): the coefficient of |return| on market-maker net gamma is negative with t = −7.624; "a one standard deviation shock to the market maker net position gamma is associated with a −0.000543×6.6772 = 36.8 basis points change in absolute return... on the order of 11.8 percent (=36.8/310) of the daily absolute return"; decile sort: "the average daily absolute return of the low net market maker gamma group is 100 basis points greater than the average absolute return for the high net market maker gamma group". A volatility result on origin-coded data; no direction, no strategy.

### 1.2 Volatility: dealer gamma compresses or expands realized variance

Beyond NPPW and Barbon–Buraschi above:

**Adams et al. 2026 [WP]** (same text): "on days when 0DTE contracts expire, the 10-minute realized volatility of S&P 500 index returns is lower by 61 annualized basis points (bps) on average—which is about 7 % of the average realized volatility in our sample" (mean 10-minute RV 9.10 % annualized; identified from Tuesday/Thursday availability before May 2022; the weekday gap disappears once 0DTEs trade daily); market makers' hedging needs ≈ 1 % of S&P 500 shares, +0.15 pp when 0DTEs are present; two-stage multiplier ≈ −4 ("a 1 bp increase in the... hedging needs... is associated with a 4 bps decrease in realized volatility"); "Shifting 0DTE net gamma in a 10-minute interval by one standard deviation reduces predicted S&P 500 realized volatility by about 23 basis points (annualized) in the next 10-minute interval"; "high-frequency hedging of market makers' portfolio with positive net gamma can be profitable" at low cost (p. 888 of the text), which is a market-maker statement.

**Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson & Vasquez, "0DTE Index Options and Market Volatility: How Large is Their Impact?", 25 January 2025 [WP; data provided by Cboe Global Markets].** Full text: https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/gammasqueezes.pdf. Cboe SPX/SPXW trade records by capacity and open/close flag, January 2020 – June 2023 (models fit July 2020 – June 2023), one-minute market-maker gamma; GARCH and linear models with lagged OMM gamma, simulated under the counterfactual of no gamma effect. "The results show that the conditional variance is negatively related to the lagged OMM gammas"; average impact "to reduce daily volatility by 0.08 percentage points"; "the maximum impact of OMM gamma on annualized volatility is to increase annualized realized daily volatility by 3.3 percentage points... well within the range of daily changes in annualized realized volatility" (SD of daily changes 4.5 pp; changes ≥ 3.0 pp occur on about 20 % of days); 30-minute maximum +6.4 pp against an all-cause SD of 4.6 pp. OMM gamma "is negative for the entire day on at least 1 % of the days" and the 25th percentile of daily minima is −94.9 (scaled units).

**Cboe, Mandy Xu, "Volatility Insights: Much Ado About 0DTEs", 8 September 2023 [EX].** https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options. 0DTE 5 % of SPX volume in 2016, > 40 % after Tue/Thu listings, ≈ 50 % in August 2023 (≈ 1.23 M contracts, ≈ $500 bn notional per day); the exchange sees "for every transaction whether it's customer or market maker, buy or sell, opening or closing"; 15 August 2023, 4440 put: > 100k traded, "market makers were left short only about 3k contracts (52k buy vs. 55k sell)"; average net gamma across 30-minute buckets $170 mm – $670 mm per 1 % move = 0.04–0.17 % of ≈ $400 bn/day S&P futures liquidity; at 15:30 median +$173 mm, IQR −$1.1 bn to +$2.4 bn, whiskers −$5 bn to +$7.7 bn (1.3–1.9 % of futures notional); on 15 August MMs were ≈ +$2 bn gamma at 15:00 and −$500 mm (0.13 % of futures volume) at 15:30; YTD close-to-close minus intraday vol spread 2.7 points, equal to the 10-year average. **"0DTEs Decoded: Positioning, Trends, and Market Impact", 2 May 2025 [EX].** https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact. ≈ 2 M contracts/day; retail 50–60 % (47 % in early April 2025); > 95 % of 0DTE trades limited-risk, 4 % naked short; "customer activity continues to be extremely balanced in 0DTE options, and as a result, net market maker gamma hedging remains de minimis, representing at best, just 0.2 % of the SPX daily liquidity". Both are exchange research with a commercial interest in the product; the position-level numbers are nonetheless the only direct measurements of SPX 0DTE dealer gamma in print.

**Maurer, "Dealer Gamma Exposure and Overnight Gap Risk: Incremental Information in Low-Volatility Regimes", 20 May 2026, SSRN 6650858 [WP; abstract only, via OptionMetrics].** https://optionmetrics.com/research/dealer-gamma-exposure-and-overnight-gap-risk-incremental-information-in-low-volatility-regimes/. Against a Corsi HAR baseline with a credit control, S&P 500 dealer gamma predicts overnight gap magnitude "only in low-VIX regimes"; pooling regimes "washes out the effect"; calm-regime 0.99-quantile loss ≈ 15 % lower; robust to "stress threshold, training cutoff, tenor, and sign convention" and a Holm correction over 100 Clark–West tests. Data source and sample not on the page.

**Soebhag, "Option gamma and stock returns", Journal of Empirical Finance 74 (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.jempfin.2023.101442 [PR; publisher abstract only].** https://tinbergen.nl/publication/168397/option-gamma-and-stock-returns. Monthly cross-section: stocks with high net gamma exposure (a hedge-adjusted, gamma-weighted sum of open interest, OptionMetrics, January 1996 – December 2021 per the CXO summary [SEC], https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=43574) earn lower returns; net gamma predicts future realized volatility through a non-informational channel. No decile spread or cost figure was retrievable; a monthly stock-selection anomaly, outside this note's intraday scope (see §6).

**Buis, Pieterse-Bloem, Verschoor & Zwinkels, "Gamma positioning and market quality", Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control 164 (2024) 104880 [PR].** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/dyncon/v164y2024ics0165188924000721.html. A zero-intelligence simulation, not an empirical test: positive dealer gamma sustains liquidity in stress, negative gamma depletes it. Listed because it is sometimes cited as evidence; it is theory.

**Shi, "0DTE Options and Intraday Volatility", Princeton ORFE senior thesis, 8 April 2026 [student thesis, restricted access; abstract only].** https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/61115cd1-cb09-4a8b-9230-ff488fc2a386. "The gamma-driven hedging feedback channel adds no meaningful predictive power for intraday variance forecasts" by QLIKE or rank correlation, while other option risk exposures do. Not evidence of the grade needed here; it is the only study found that frames the question as incremental forecast value over a volatility baseline, which is also how H5 was framed.

**Brogaard, Han & Won (0DTE volume and volatility) [WP; no primary text located in either sweep].** Not used.

### 1.3 Levels and pinning

Ni, Pearson & Poteshman, "Stock price clustering on option expiration dates", JFE 78 (2005) 49–87 [PR], https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v78y2005i1p49-87.html: closing prices of optionable stocks cluster at strikes on expiration dates; returns "altered by an average of at least 16.5 basis points" per expiration, ≈ $9 bn aggregate; attributed to market-maker hedge rebalancing and firm proprietary manipulation; sample 1996–2002. Golez & Jackwerth, "Pinning in the S&P 500 futures", JFE 106 (2012) 566–585 [PR], https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p566-585.html: futures "gravitate toward the at-the-money strike" on serial expirations and are repelled from the carry-adjusted strike before SPX index-option expiration ("anti-cross-pinning"); mechanism is delta-hedge decay (charm) plus early exercise/resale of ITM options; ≥ $115 m notional shift per expiration day; pinning occurs only on expiration dates. Both pre-date weeklies on most names and daily index expirations; no post-2022 pinning study was found in either sweep. H6 (below) tested vendor levels, not strike open interest, and not expiration days specifically.

### 1.4 Practitioner constructs and their evidence status

- **SqueezeMetrics (Prior Analytics LLC), "Gamma Exposure (GEX): Quantifying hedge rebalancing in SPX options", March 2016, revised December 2017 [PRAC; mirror https://slopeofhope.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/GEX-white_paper.pdf].** Four stated assumptions: all options are facilitated by delta hedgers; "Call options are sold by investors; bought by market-makers"; "Put options are bought by investors; sold by market-makers"; dealers hedge exactly to delta. GEX = Γ·OI·100 (calls), Γ·OI·(−100) (puts), summed over every strike and expiry. The single empirical statement is a quartile comparison with no sample period, standard error or control: next-day SPX return SD 0.55 % after the highest GEX quartile versus 0.85 % after the second-highest, against 0.51 %/0.66 % for the two lowest VIX quartiles; and "An investment process that selected... by screening for a GEX near zero yielded excess returns" with no numbers. Its sign convention is the one Baltussen adopted and verified against OptionMetrics for 2002–2017 as a computation, not as a statement about who holds what.
- **SpotGamma [VM].** "SPX long gamma backtest", 10 December 2019, https://spotgamma.com/spx-long-gamma-backtest: a Twitter chart (@PatHennessey) with no dates, returns, drawdowns, trade counts, costs; the page's own caveats: "You will underpeform the SPX if you only use 'market gamma' as your buy/sell indicator"; "you can get 'chewed up' at times where the indicator flips back and forth"; "All gamma data we've seen has been collected during a very large bull market. Therefore any backtest from this period shows you cannot beat being fully invested in the SPX." The "Volatility Trigger" is proprietary and undisclosed (2026-10-13 note, https://spotgamma.com/what-is-gex-gamma-exposure/); the help-centre "Net Gamma" page (https://support.spotgamma.com/hc/en-us/articles/15413527450899-Net-Gamma) returned 403. No published out-of-sample test of gamma flip, call wall, put wall or volatility trigger exists in either sweep.
- **Unusual Whales [VM for the levels; documented construction].** From the API docs quoted in the 2026-10-13 note (https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-levels.md, .../gex-exposures-per-1min.md, .../greek-exposure.md): GEX "assumes that market makers are part of every transaction and that the bulk of their transactions are buying calls and selling puts"; `gex-levels` defaults to `source=vol` (ask/bid-sided volume) with `oi` as the alternative; the docs themselves warn that zero-gamma crossings "often cluster within a dollar or two" and that `gamma_flip` "can be `null` or far from spot, so do not use it for regime detection". History: `gex-levels` non-null from 2024-05-13 for SPY and SPX (605 days to 2026-10-09; `tb_gex_availability.csv`); option history endpoints 403 before 2023-11-13; the ticker-level `greek-exposure` series starts 2025-10-14 regardless of the date requested (DATA-2). The 2026-10-14 `docs` root fetch confirmed the base URL and an `llms.txt` index only.
- **FlashAlpha, "GEX/DEX/VEX/CHEX 8-year backtest with VIX control", 23 April 2026 [PRAC; from the 2026-10-13 note, https://flashalpha.com/articles/gex-dex-vex-chex-8-year-backtest-spy-vix-control].** SPY 2018-04-16 to 2026-04-02, 1,972 EOD snapshots: Spearman ρ(GEX, next-day RV) −0.36 raw, −0.14 after VIX, −0.03 (p 0.18) after VIX + ATM IV; top-minus-bottom GEX quintile RV spread −10.63 vol points raw, −0.99 (p 0.25) after controls. The only vendor-adjacent test with a control, and it says the OI-GEX/volatility link is the IV regime.
- **LambdaFin, "Dealer gamma flip predictive track record" [third-party page; could not be opened, timed out twice; search snippet only, https://www.lambdafin.com/articles/dealer-gamma-flip-predictive-track-record].** "15 cleanly identified positive-to-negative flips shown; full dataset of 47 flips", 11 followed by a ≥ 5-point RV rise within 20 sessions, 5-day return negative in 14 of 15; a hand-selected subset, no control, no test. Flagged, not evidence.
- **Vanna/charm "flows" [VM].** Vendor pages only (2026-10-13 note). The academic analogue of charm is the Golez–Jackwerth expiration-day mechanism, which is a pinning claim, not a drift claim.

### 1.5 What the evidence adds up to

1. Dealer gamma is a measured, modest volatility variable: one sd ≈ 37 bp of a 310-bp average |daily return| in origin-coded 1990–2001 stock data (NPPW), 5–25 bp of 180 bp in 2010–2020 stock data and > 20 bp for indices (Barbon–Buraschi), ≈ 23 annualized bp of next-10-minute RV per sd of 0DTE net gamma on SPX 2018–2024 (Adams), a maximum of +3.3 pp annualized daily vol on 2020–2023 Cboe data (Amaya). The one practitioner test with controls finds the OI version absorbed by VIX and ATM IV (FlashAlpha); the one working paper that splits regimes finds it survives only in low-VIX (Maurer).
2. The direction claim is a last-half-hour, negative-gamma-state effect in futures (Baltussen) and a last-half-hour cross-sectional effect in stocks (BBBM), both with the hedging concentrated 15:30–16:00, both reverting the next session, both measured gross of costs, and in BBBM's case 2 bp per day for the gamma leg. Post-2022 SPX evidence says the negative-gamma state is the minority state (net gamma positive in 88 % of 10-minute intervals; Cboe median positive) and that elevated gamma predicts reversal, not momentum.
3. Nobody has published an options P&L for any of this. Every strategy number in the literature is in shares or futures.
4. The sign convention that every public-OI construct uses (dealers long all calls, short all puts) is an assumption stated as such by its users (Baltussen fn. 8; Barbon–Buraschi "although imperfect"; SqueezeMetrics assumption 2–3). The only direct measurements (Cboe, Adams, Amaya, NPPW, BBBM) come from origin-coded exchange data that is sold, not published.

**What public OI can and cannot measure.** It measures the gross gamma of every open contract at the prior settlement, by strike and expiry, from 2012. It cannot sign it. The sign convention turns that into "NGE", which Baltussen's footnote 8 and Cboe's balanced-flow measurements say is a weak proxy for the true dealer position; two UW vendor proxies built on the same convention agreed on the regime only 52 % of days (H10) and the intraday sign agreed with the prior-day flip 54 % of the time (H5). What OI does carry, and what the vendor intraday feeds do not, is fourteen years of history and the expiring-contract inventory that Adams et al. find is the source of most expiration-day hedging need (stale 0DTE net gamma 0.29–0.39 % of shares versus 0.08–0.16 % for same-day positions). The trade-quote feed adds a second, independent handle: a quote-rule-signed cumulative flow per strike that is the closest public analogue of Cboe's buy/sell split, with a known misclassification problem (Muravyev & Pearson, "Options trading costs are lower than you think", RFS 2020, document that a large share of option trades execute inside the quote; mid-price prints are unsignable).

**Institutional versus retail.** The channel itself is market making. What a retail account can trade is the predicted price behaviour, in two instruments: shares (SPY round trip ≈ 0.4 bp at the NBBO, the cost assumed in H10; large-cap single names 1–3 bp) and listed options at the C1 half-spreads (SPY/QQQ 0.5 % of mid per side, IWM 0.7–1.0 %, SPXW 0.9 %, single names 3.5–4.1 %, 0DTE straddle exit legs 3.3 % median of credit) plus $0.70 per contract per side. The literature's effect sizes (2–7 bp of underlying per event; 7 % of RV; 1.5–2 pp of autocorrelation) are of the order of the share round trip and one to two orders of magnitude below the option round trip. Everything that cleared costs in print cleared them in futures at one tick (Baltussen) or in gross share portfolios (BBBM).

## 4. Cost hurdles the proposals are measured against

- Shares: SPY 0.2 bp per side (H10 assumption), 0.4 bp round trip; large-cap single names 1–3 bp round trip at the NBBO; no rebates.
- Options: SPY/QQQ ATM 0DTE round trip $2.79 per contract (S01) to $3.5–4.6 (H7) ≈ 3–6 % of a ≈ $1 premium; 0DTE straddle round trip $12.93 (C1, SPY/QQQ) and $68.9 (SPXW, H11); weekly 0.45-delta contract ≈ 25–40 bp of spot per round trip incl. a day of theta (S16a arithmetic); single-name half-spreads 3.5–4.1 % of premium per side.
- Promotion (GATE-v2; S06 reading): for a signal lane, an absolute day-clustered 95 % lower bound ≥ +3 bp at 30–60 minutes **and** a positive paired lower bound against the matched random control, before any option is attached; carry lanes are labelled carry and need the tail gates.

## 5. Proposed tests (none duplicates H4/H5/H6/H10; each states the registry id it differs from)

**P1. Public-OI dealer gamma (sign convention) as a 14-year volatility input, with the IV control that the vendor tests failed.** Construct NGE_t at the open from ThetaData prior-settlement OI × engine gamma (NBBO-mid IV) × S² × 0.01 for SPY, QQQ, IWM (and SPX/SPXW once OI is verified for those roots), 2012-06-04 → 2026-10-13 (≈ 3,600 sessions per root); outcomes: same-day realized variance from 1-minute bars (whole day; 10:00–15:30; 15:30–16:00), |close-to-close return|, next-day overnight gap; controls: VIX (free CSV, 1990 →), the root's own ATM IV at the open, trailing 21-day RV, HAR terms; standard errors clustered by date; the Maurer split by VIX tercile pre-registered. Priors: Barbon index −20 bp of |return| per sd before IV controls; FlashAlpha −0.03 partial after VIX + ATM IV; Maurer: survives only in low VIX. Differs from H5 (UW 1-minute modelled exposures, 60 sessions, no IV control) in data, window and specification; it is the input H5's open next step needs. Retail-accessible: not a trade; a sizing/stop-width input (H5's registered use) and the conditioning variable for P2's second stage. Expected size after costs: not applicable as a lane; the deliverable is whether anything remains after ATM IV, which on the practitioner evidence is ≈ 0. Cost of the test: one OI request per root-year (≤ 365 days each), Greeks from cached NBBO; no chains beyond what the engine already prices.

**P2. Expiring-contract OI gamma on expiration days (the Adams "stale 0DTE" channel) → intraday variance compression and last-hour reversal, 2012 → 2026.** On each expiration day compute, from prior-settlement OI, the gamma of contracts expiring that day as a fraction of the underlying's float (Adams' "hedging need" units) for SPY (Friday weeklies from 2012, monthlies throughout, Mon/Wed weeklies where listed, daily from 2022-11-11) and SPXW (from 2012; daily from 2022-05-13), with the sign convention and, separately, unsigned (gross). Outcomes: 10-minute realized variance across the day, the 15:00 → 16:00 return autocorrelation, and the ratio of realized to open-implied straddle variance; design: expiration days versus adjacent non-expiration days (pre-2022 only, where the contrast exists), and within expiration days by tercile of expiring gamma; controls as in P1; CIs clustered by date. Priors: Adams, 61 annualized bp (7 % of 9.10 %) lower 10-minute RV on 0DTE days, −23 bp per sd of 0DTE net gamma next 10 minutes, elevated net gamma → hourly reversal; Amaya, average −0.08 pp daily vol, maximum +3.3 pp when gamma is negative; Cboe, hedging flow ≤ 0.2 % of liquidity. Differs from H5/H10 in construct (expiring inventory only, measurable from public OI because it is stale by Adams' own decomposition), window (2012 →) and outcome (variance and reversal, not direction). Second stage, only if the first-stage top-tercile effect clears a pre-stated threshold (realized/implied ratio lower by ≥ 5 % with a date-clustered lower bound above zero): a short ATM 0DTE straddle on SPY/QQQ at 10:00 conditioned on top-tercile expiring gamma, against S15's unconditional −$15.46 [−32.68, +0.29] and $12.93 round trip. Arithmetic: 7 % less RV on a ≈ 0.8 %/day move ≈ 5 bp of spot ≈ $3 per SPY straddle, below the round trip, so the honest expectation for the option expression is ≈ 0 or negative; the first stage is worth running because it is the only public-data test of the one 0DTE hedging mechanism the literature now agrees on. Retail-accessible: the option expression, yes; expected after costs ≤ 0. The underlying-level result is a carry-lane conditioning input (H15 scope) rather than a signal lane.

**P3. Cross-sectional end-of-day gamma imbalance in single-name shares (BBBM), with the public-OI proxy in place of exchange-tagged net OI.** Universe: Tier A (≈ 100–150 roots) once the S05 minute bars land, 2017-06 → 2026-10; later 2012 → as bars are pulled. Γ^IB per stock at 15:30 = (Σ Γ_call·OI_call − Σ Γ_put·OI_put) × S × multiplier × S/100 ÷ last-half-hour average dollar volume over the prior month (BBBM eq. 4 with the convention sign in place of netOI); Γ^HP = Γ^IB × return to 15:30. Outcome: last-30-minute return and signed last-half-hour volume on Γ^HP with date and stock fixed effects, value-weighted, date-and-stock clustered SEs; decile long-short at 15:30, flat at 16:00, matched random-decile control; shares only. Priors: β₁ = −11.947 (t −5.37); η(Γ^HP) 5.16 %/yr, Sharpe 4.13 EW, 62.3 % success, ≈ 2 bp/day gross (BBBM, 2012–2019, exchange-tagged positions); the LETF leg (4× larger in their data, "decreased over time") is not measurable with our keys. Differs from H4/H10 (index ETFs, vendor flip, direction over half-days) in universe, construct and the stated outcome being the sign of a published cross-sectional coefficient. Retail-accessible in shares: mechanically yes; expected size after costs: 2 bp/day gross against a 1–3 bp round trip per leg → ≈ 0 to negative; a 30-minute hold in 20 names each side is also beyond a single marketable-order account's practicality. The value of the test is diagnostic: if the convention-signed proxy reproduces even the sign of BBBM's β₁ on 2017–2026 data, P1/P2's proxy has external validity; if it does not, every OI-convention construct in this note is downgraded. Not a lane.

**P4. Expiration-day pinning to open-interest strikes, 2012 → 2026, shares first, single-name 0DTE straddles second.** Stage 1 (daily data only): for every optionable name in the S07 universe on every monthly (third-Friday) expiration and, separately, every weekly expiration, the distance of the close from the nearest strike and from the highest-OI strike, versus the same stock on non-expiration days matched on |open − strike| distance and on the day's realized range; also the Golez–Jackwerth test on SPY/SPX: pull toward the ATM strike on serial (non-quarterly) expirations and repulsion before the AM-settled SPX monthly (the Thursday close). Daily bars 2023 → now in the lake, 2022 → 2012 landing; ≈ 1,100–1,500 names × 12 monthly expirations × 14 years ≈ 200k stock-expirations when complete; SPY/SPX alone gives ≈ 170 monthly events (low power, reported anyway). Priors: NPP ≥ 16.5 bp per expiration (1996–2002); Golez–Jackwerth ≥ $115 m per expiration; no post-weeklies estimate exists. Stage 2, only if stage 1 shows a date-clustered pull of ≥ 10 bp on monthly expirations in the top liquidity tercile: a short ATM straddle opened at 13:00 on the expiration day in names whose highest-OI strike is within 0.5 % of spot, closed at 15:50 at the ask, with C1 single-name costs (3.5–4.1 % half-spread per side plus the 3.3 % exit-leg median) and the H14 leave-to-expire rule where the ask ≤ $0.05. Arithmetic: a 3-hour single-name ATM straddle at 30 % IV is ≈ 1.0 % of spot; a 16.5 bp pull is ≈ 16 % of premium against ≈ 11–12 % of premium in costs, so the stage-2 expectation is between −5 % and +5 % of premium, the one cell in this note where the published magnitude and the measured cost are of the same order. Differs from H6 (vendor single-number levels, all sessions, index ETFs, 2025-06 →) in level definition (OI strike), day class (expirations), universe (single names), window (2012 →) and the Golez–Jackwerth repulsion leg, which H6 could not test. Retail-accessible: yes (stage 2 is one marketable straddle per name). Multiplicity: stage 1 is two pre-registered contrasts (monthly pull; index repulsion); stage 2 is one cell.

**P5. Does the Baltussen effect exist in our own 2012-06 → 2017-05 minute data, and does the public-OI NGE sign condition it there? (pipeline validation, not a lane).** SPY 1-minute bars (verified 2012-06-04; not yet cached before 2017-06), SPY OI → NGE with Baltussen's exact definition, VIX from the free CSV; regress r_last30 on r_ROD unconditionally and split by NGE sign; ≈ 1,250 sessions. Priors: cash index β = 5.98 (t 4.78) over 1982–2020; conditional 6.63 / 0.82; our 2017 → 2026 estimates are 1.17 (×100) [−2.40, +4.74] unconditional (H10) and the wrong sign conditional. Differs from H10 in window (the five years no study has touched; `07_coverage_report.md` §3) and proxy (Baltussen's own, from public OI, rather than UW's vendor level). Retail-accessible: shares, yes. Expected size after costs: the paper's gross Sharpe 1.73 is a 1974–2020 average across 17 index futures; even if 2012–2016 SPY reproduces it, the 2017 → 2026 window has already shown ≈ 0, so the live expectation is ≈ 0; the test establishes whether the pipeline detects a known effect and dates its disappearance (Gao et al.'s JFE publication is 2018), which is what a reviewer will ask before believing any null in §3.

**P6. Quote-rule-signed dealer gamma from ThetaData trade-quote, calibrated against Cboe's published position numbers, 2012 → 2026.** For SPY and SPXW (then QQQ/IWM), sign every print at or above the ask as a customer buy and at or below the bid as a customer sell (mid prints and prints with spread-condition codes left unsigned and counted), accumulate per strike and expiry through the session, and compute the implied dealer net gamma (dealer = opposite of the aggressor) per minute; benchmark the construct on the dates Cboe published: 15 August 2023 (4440 put: MM net short ≈ 3k of > 107k traded; MM gamma ≈ +$2 bn at 15:00, ≈ −$500 mm at 15:30) and the 2023 distribution (30-minute average net gamma $170–670 mm per 1 % move; 15:30 median +$173 mm, IQR −$1.1 bn to +$2.4 bn). If the signed construct lands inside those ranges, run the Adams §4 relationships with what we have: next-10/30-minute realized variance, and the last-hour return autocorrelation, on the signed 0DTE net gamma, with the P1 controls; 2022-05 → 2026-10 for SPXW 0DTE (1,106 sessions, chains cached), 2012 → for SPY weeklies. Priors: Adams −23 annualized bp of next-10-minute RV per sd; Cboe "de minimis" (≤ 0.2 % of liquidity); H5's `dir` variant |IC| ≤ 0.03 (UW's directionalisation on 60 sessions). Differs from H2/H3 (flow as a directional entry, UW, 2025-04 →) in purpose (a positioning estimate validated against exchange numbers, not an entry), from H5 in data (ThetaData prints with NBBO, 14 years) and in having an external calibration target. Retail-accessible: the construct is not a trade; its registered use would be the same as H5 (risk input) and the conditioning for H15's carry lane exits. Expected size after costs: none as a lane; the measurable deliverable is whether a public-data signed-flow gamma tracks the exchange's numbers at all, which no published source has checked. Cost: trade-quote volume is 21k SPY prints on a 2012 day and millions per day now across the chain; run SPXW 0DTE days and SPY expiration days first (8 concurrent requests, resumable).

Multiplicity budget: P1–P6 are 6 primary pre-registered contrasts plus two conditional second stages (P2, P4); every CI is reported after a Holm correction across the batch, with the S06 two-part hurdle applied to any cell that would be promoted.

## 6. Closed, inapplicable or not proposed, with the reason

- H4 (UW gamma-flip regime as a direction filter), H6 (first-touch fades of UW walls and magnet), H10 (UW-regime-conditioned last-30-minute momentum in SPY, 2024-05 →): closed in the registry; nothing in the primary texts read here changes them. - H2, H3, H8, S12, S12b, S15, H9, H11, H13, H7: closed; they bound the option expressions of P2 and P4 and are cited as the hurdles.
- Beckmeyer et al.'s "net-short 0DTE market makers amplify swings": a conjecture in the text, contradicted on net gamma by Cboe (median +$173 mm at 15:30) and Adams (positive 88 % of intervals); not a hypothesis.
- Any test needing Cboe Open-Close, OCC origin codes, LETF holdings history or E-mini order flow: data not in our keys (Open-Close is a paid, internal-use licence); the BBBM LETF leg (Ω^LETF), the Adams order-flow-reversal regressions and the NPPW origin-coded net gamma are therefore not replicable; P3/P6 are their public-data approximations and are labelled as such.
- SpotGamma volatility trigger, gamma flip and walls; LambdaFin flip track record; vanna/charm "flows": vendor constructs without a published out-of-sample test; UW's own documentation advises against regime use of `gamma_flip`; H4/H6 are the only controlled tests of such levels and were negative.
- SqueezeMetrics' near-zero-GEX stock screen "yielded excess returns": no numbers, no period; not testable as stated.
- Soebhag (JEF 2023) monthly net-gamma decile returns: a monthly stock-selection anomaly, not intraday or option-expressible; no magnitude retrievable; revisit with S14 when the 2012–2022 daily bars land, not here.
- Buis et al. (JEDC 2024): simulation; Shi (2026): student thesis, restricted; Brogaard–Han–Won: no primary text; Londono–Samadi (Fed IFDP 1376): different question. Not used as evidence.
- Dim–Eraker–Vilkov (SSRN 4692190) and Adams–Fontaine–Ornthanalai (SSRN 4881008): subsumed by the July 2026 Adams et al. draft per its own front matter; cite the latter.
