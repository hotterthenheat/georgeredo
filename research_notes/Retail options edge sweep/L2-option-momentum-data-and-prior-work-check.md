## 0. Verdict

**Refuted.** Not because the registry closes it (it does not, §1), and not because the gross effect is doubtful (it is the best-established result in the single-name option literature, §2), but because the proposition fails on three data-and-window facts that no pull can change:

1. **The window cannot resolve the after-cost cell for any sample we can assemble.** The paper's own after-cost number at the quoted spread — +1.12 %/month, t 0.75 over 282 months — implies a monthly standard deviation of ≈ 25 % for the low-cost-decile long-short; a one-sided 95 % lower bound above zero needs ≈ 1,360 months. Our data give **159 holding months** (first formation window 2012-06 → first holding month 2013-07; last expiration 2026-09) and **141 out-of-sample months** under the protocol's calendar-year walk-forward (first test year 2015). A Tier A replication is worse, not better: 69 single equities in Tier A 2026 (31 of the 100 are ETFs/ETNs, which the paper excludes: "We retain options on common equity only"), so quintiles of ≈ 14 names carry ≈ 21 % of idiosyncratic noise per leg (Table 1 single-straddle SD 80.6 %) and ≈ 30 % for the long-short; even the *gross* liquid-tercile spread (4.35 %/month, Table 12) needs ≈ 130 months at that breadth, and the net +1.12 % needs ≈ 2,000. The gross effect is resolvable only at the paper's breadth (≈ 90 names per quintile: ≈ 21–26 months), which is the full-universe pull of §3, item 9. §4.
2. **The point-in-time universe the test needs does not exist yet, and the payoff series for half of it does not exist in the vendor's data before 2017 (vendor documentation says 2020).** Protocol §3 defines Tier A from the prior year's daily dollar volume; `tiers.parquet` holds 2025 and 2026 only ("2024: skipped: year 2023 bars not in the lake" at build time), because the stock-EOD lake holds 2023 → 2026 and the 2022 → 2012 backfill is "queued at lowest priority behind research pulls" on the single login (DATA-3; `06_implementation_record.md`). Using today's Tier A for 2013 is survivorship: 24 of the 114 names in the 2025–2026 Tier A union had no listed expiration by 2013-01 (PLTR, COIN, META-as-root, HOOD, UBER, CRWD, SNOW, GOOGL, BABA, SPOT, PDD, BKNG …), 20 had none by 2017-01 [LOCAL, expirations file]. And the paper's return is the straddle's payoff from the **split-adjusted stock price at expiry (CRSP)**: ThetaData stock EOD reaches 2012-06 only for UTP-tape (Nasdaq) names; for CTA-tape (NYSE) names our probe measured 2017-01-03 (SPY, IWM, GME) and the vendor's own page says "For symbols only available on the CTA tape, the history is limited to 2020-01-01" [VDOC]. 42 of the 69 Tier A equities are NYSE-listed (my classification, §3 item 4). For 2012-06 → 2016-12 (a quarter of the window, and the only years not already analysed by anyone's 2024–2026 daily screens) the payoff of those names must be inferred from the expiring chain's deep-in-the-money quotes, which is the noisiest quote in the chain and the one Goyenko–Zhang show is most biased at the close. §3.
3. **The two tradeable expressions are both outside what the gates can pass, by arithmetic that the data cannot overturn.** Hold-to-expiry means each leg pays its entry half-spread once (Table 13 implies an average weighted half-spread h_w = (6.22 − (−6.78))/2 = 6.5 % of mid in the main sample and (7.23 − 1.12)/2 = 3.1 % in the low-cost deciles). At our measured 10:00 single-name half-spreads (C1: 0.8–1.3 % for the five or six mega-caps, 3.5–4.1 % median across GeorgeFi's names, 11.1 % median closing across the 254 S11 roots) the long-short's expected net is 4.35 − 2h_w = **+2.3 % at h_w 1 %, +0.3 % at 2 %, −1.7 % at 3 %, −5.7 % at 5 %, −17.9 % at the closing median** — a sign that depends on which decile of liquidity one restricts to, resolvable in no window we hold, and the only positive cases require writing naked single-name straddles on 14–20 names a month (short leg: −7.77 %/month gross is where the profit is; FINRA 4210 margin ≈ 3.5 × premium versus the paper's SPAN ratio ≈ 2). The long leg alone — the only expression in the Compass long-option scope — is −1.55 % gross and **−2.5 to −4.5 %/month at 10:00 mega-cap spreads, −12.7 % at the closing median**: negative before any data are pulled. §2.3, §4.

Cost is therefore not the only binding constraint, and neither is the vendor floor: the binding constraints are the sample size the after-cost gate requires, the missing point-in-time universe and pre-2017 CTA-tape payoff series, and the fact that both legs' signs are set by the paper's own Table 2 and Table 13 before our test runs. What *could* be registered — a gross, diagnostic replication at 10:00 marks — is stated in §6 with its pull cost (≈ 3 days of the single login for Tier A; ≈ 6–8 weeks for the paper's breadth) and its answerable questions, none of which is "is there an edge".

## 2. Prior-work check: what the construction requires, and what exists after the paper's sample

### 2.1 The construction as published, read for its data requirements (Heston, Jones, Khorram, Li & Mo, *JF* 78(6) 2023 [PR]; December 2021 post-acceptance draft, `l2_dl/heston_usc.txt`)

- Universe and source: "We obtain call and put prices from the OptionMetrics database, which provides end-of-day bid-ask quotes for options traded on U.S. exchanges. We retain options on common equity only and discard any options with expiration dates that are outside the regular monthly cycle. Using the WRDS link table, we merge this data with CRSP, which we use as the source of stock prices, returns, trading volume, market capitalization, and adjustments for stock splits. The availability of options data restricts our sample period to the interval from January 1996 to June 2019." (p. 5). 282 months, "1,369 straddles per month on average" (p. 12).
- Selection, point in time: "On each expiration day, we select two matching call/put pairs for each stock, where all calls and puts expire in the following month. One is the pair whose call delta is closest to 0.5. The other uses the same criteria but requires that both the put and the call have positive open interest on the day they are selected. In either case, if the call delta is less than 0.25 or greater than 0.75, we discard the observation." Footnote 1: "Prior to 2015, stock option expiration dates were Saturdays. The de facto expiration date was the prior trading date, which is the date we use." Footnote 2: deltas are OptionMetrics' binomial-tree deltas. Liquidity: "we discard any straddle in which the weighted average bid/ask spread of the call and put is greater than 50% of midpoint prices. If this occurs, we attempt to replace the straddle with another that is further from at-the-money".
- The open-interest filter is applied **at the start of the holding period, not in the formation period**: "We strike a balance between these two concerns by imposing the positive open interest filter only during the holding period. … By dropping the open interest filter in the formation period, which is in some cases several years long, we increase the sample size by up to 50%." and, for the VIX-return version, "only options with positive open interest are included at the start of the holding period." This matters for §2.2 (look-ahead) and for the data check: OI is needed on the selection day only, and the formation signal is computed from straddles that may have had zero OI.
- Return: "Because we hold straddles to expiration, call and put returns are calculated based on the split-adjusted price of the underlying stock on the expiration date, where split adjustment uses data from CRSP. The initial price of each option is taken as its bid-ask midpoint." Early exercise is ignored. The signal is the *average* monthly straddle return over the formation lags (returns near −100 % are frequent; Table 1 median −22.8 %, 10th/90th percentiles −86 %/+94 %).
- Cost table (§5.6, Table 13): "one-way costs that are a fixed fraction of the quoted half spread" — algo 20 %, adjusted 51 %, effective 76 %, quoted 100 % (Muravyev–Pearson 2020 Table 5 [PR]); "with and without a cost to exit the stock position that would result from the exercise of options that expire in the money … When we impose a cost on liquidating stock, we assume that cost is equal to half of the closing bid-ask spread." Two cost-optimised variants: "extreme deciles rather than quintiles" and "strategies that avoid options with bid-ask spreads above 10% of option midpoints". Verdict in the text: "the main momentum and reversal strategies are not profitable if one assumes that the entire quoted spread must be paid." Margin: SPAN scenarios ±15 % price / ±10 % implied vol (Goyal–Saretto 2009), "the returns shrink by 50%-55% … suggesting an average initial margin ratio slightly above 2"; costs and margin are never combined.

What this means for a replication on our data: (i) the universe must be common equity on the regular monthly cycle (third Friday; Saturday-dated before 2015), with a point-in-time list; (ii) one chain snapshot per root on each expiration Friday (closing or 10:00 NBBO) and OI on that day; (iii) the underlying's split-adjusted close on the next expiration Friday; (iv) a corporate-action layer (splits, special dividends, symbol changes) that OptionMetrics/CRSP supply and ThetaData does not expose in the expirations file; (v) the formation signal needs 12 prior monthly straddle returns, so the first holding month is 13 months after the data floor.

### 2.2 Evidence after the paper's sample, and the look-ahead question

- **Käfer, Mörke & Wiest, "Option Factor Momentum", *JFQA* 61(1) 2026, 32–60 [PR, open access]** — https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109025000225 (PDF read, `l2_data/kafer/kafer.txt`). OptionMetrics IvyDB Jan 1996 → **Dec 2021**, CRSP share codes 10/11, price ≥ $5, *daily delta-hedged calls* held month-end to month-end, 379,165 option-months on 7,015 underlyings. Cross-sectional option momentum reproduces on the extended sample (sibling note: lags 2–12 annualized +4.57 %, t 6.98, Sharpe 2.03; alpha on the Horenstein–Vasquez–Xiao factors 1.27 %, t 1.82). Their filter policy is the one a replication should copy: "We follow Bali et al. (2023) and apply filters only at position initiation. This mitigates any forward-looking bias, which can significantly affect option returns, as pointed out by Duarte, Jones, Mo, and Khorram (2024). If no option price is available at the time of position closing, we use the intrinsic value of the option". Subsample analysis is in their Table E.1 (supplementary material, not retrieved); no 2019–2021-only number is in the main text. This is the only peer-reviewed evidence past June 2019, and it is on a different return (delta-hedged call, daily rebalanced) with 30 months of extension.
- **Duarte, Jones, Khorram, Mo & Wang, "Too Good to Be True: Look-ahead Bias in Empirical Option(s) Research", *RFS* forthcoming (accepted 2025) [PR, text not retrieved]** — SSRN 4590083 (abstract page HTTP 403 twice today; the ouci.dntb.gov.ua mirror 403; the authors' pages list it without abstract or PDF: https://faculty.marshall.usc.edu/Christopher-Jones/ , https://sites.google.com/view/haitaomo , https://sites.google.com/site/junbolwang/research ). Rice Business (https://www.business.rice.edu/wisdom/when-research-promises-impossible-investors-pay-price ): the original "daily mean returns of more than 3%" became "much smaller, and the level of risk also increased" once selections used only start-of-day information; the bias came from filters using "each contract's average price over the entire trading day to make selections at the start of that day". Neither summary mentions option momentum or straddles; three of the five authors are Heston et al. co-authors. **Reading for L2:** the paper's data-section text (§2.1) applies its OI, delta and spread filters on the selection day and takes the payoff from the stock, so the look-ahead mechanism Duarte et al. describe is not visible in the published construction; but the *replication* must not apply any filter at the end of the holding period (no "valid quote at expiry" or "OI at expiry" condition), and must take delisted or halted names at their last price, which is where a ThetaData-based rebuild without CRSP delisting returns can introduce a survivorship it did not intend. Not evidence of anything about L2's after-cost sign; listed because it is the newest paper from the same group and may carry post-2019 data — unverifiable here.

### 2.3 What the cost table implies for a replication on our quotes [LOCAL arithmetic on Table 13 and C1]

Hold-to-expiry means each leg pays its entry half-spread once and nothing at exit (payoff at intrinsic; the paper's "nonzero exercise costs" column adds half the *stock's* closing spread on the ITM leg). The cost per month of the long-short is therefore ≈ 2 × h_w, where h_w is the weighted half-spread of the straddle as a fraction of its mid. From Table 13: main sample 6.22 → −6.78 ⇒ h_w ≈ 6.5 %; low-cost deciles 7.23 → 1.12 ⇒ h_w ≈ 3.1 %. Our measured single-name half-spreads: C1 intraday at the GeorgeFi fill second MSFT 0.8 %, AAPL 0.9–1.3 %, NVDA 0.9 %, AMZN 0.8–1.7 %, TSLA 0.9–1.6 %, META 3.5 %, GOOGL 0.9–3.2 %, DIS 7.5 %, RKLB 5.9 %, CRML 25–43 %; the C1 "single names 3.5–4.1 % median" across GeorgeFi's mixed book; closing near-ATM 8–30 DTE across the 254 S11 roots median 11.1 % (p25 5.0 %, p75 28.6 %), per-root median 10.8 %, 10th percentile 4.2 % (`execution-costs.md` §2.2). Against the liquid-tercile gross spread of 4.35 % (Table 12, the right comparator for Tier A rather than 6.22 %):

| h_w (entry half-spread, % of mid) | which names, at which mark | long-short net (4.35 − 2h_w) | long leg net (−1.55 − h_w) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 % | five or six mega-caps at 10:00 (C1) | +2.3 | −2.6 |
| 2 % | Tier A mega-cap/closing MSFT–GOOGL range | +0.3 | −3.6 |
| 3 % | the paper's low-cost decile set; C1 mixed-book median 3.5–4.1 % | −1.7 (−2.7 to −3.9 at 3.5–4.1 %) | −4.6 |
| 5 % | S11 cache p25 at the close | −5.7 | −6.6 |
| 11.1 % | S11 cache closing median | −17.9 | −12.7 |

Three readings. (i) The after-cost sign on our names is set by liquidity decile, not by the signal: positive only inside the top ≈ 10 names at intraday marks, where the breadth (§4) makes it unresolvable. (ii) The long leg is negative in every row — the Compass-scope expression needs no data to be rejected. (iii) These are *entry-only* costs; the retail short leg also pays assignment/liquidation on ITM expiries (the paper's "nonzero exercise costs" column turns the low-cost momentum deciles from +1.12 to −0.61) and early assignment on American single-name calls over ex-dividend dates, which the paper ignores and a retail account cannot.

## 4. Window length against the gates [LOCAL, `scripts_l2/l2_gate_arith.py`]

- **Holding months.** Option NBBO/EOD floor 2012-06-04 → first expiration Friday with a chain 2012-06-15; lags 2–12 need twelve monthly straddle returns → first signal at the 2013-06 expiration, first holding month 2013-07; last complete expiration 2026-09-18 → **159 holding months**. `04_protocol.md` §6: calendar-year folds, first test year = first year with two full prior years of option quotes → 2015 → **141 out-of-sample months** (2015-01 → 2026-09). Protocol §7 minimum evidence is "≥ 150 out-of-sample trades over ≥ 3 months per reported cell": if the unit is the monthly portfolio return (the only unit with independent observations — every straddle in a month shares the same holding window, so day-clustering collapses to month-clustering), 141 < 150, as for L1. Counting straddles instead (≈ 14–270 per month) satisfies the letter of the rule and not its purpose.
- **Power at the paper's breadth.** Gross quintile factor 6.22 % with SD 14.1 % (Table 4): ≈ 14 months for a one-sided 95 % lower bound > 0. Liquid-tercile spread 4.35 % (t 5.40 over 282 months ⇒ SD ≈ 13.5 %): ≈ 26 months. After-cost low-cost deciles 1.12 % (t 0.75 ⇒ SD ≈ 25.1 %): **≈ 1,360 months**; with the paper's "nonzero exercise costs" the point estimate is −0.61 % and no sample size helps.
- **Power at Tier A breadth.** Single-straddle SD 80.6 % (Table 1). Quintiles of the 69 Tier A equities hold ≈ 14 names: idiosyncratic SD ≈ 21.5 % per leg, ≈ 30 % for the long-short (upper bound; a common vol factor cancels partly) → ≈ **130 months** for the gross 4.35 % and ≈ 2,000 for the net 1.12 %. With 20 per leg (the Tier A union's ≈ 83 equities): 93 and ≈ 1,400 months. With 90 per leg (the paper's quintiles of ≈ 274 names, or the liquid tercile's ≈ 450 ÷ 5): 21 and ≈ 310 months. The gross effect is therefore resolvable in our window only at roughly the paper's breadth — the 58-login-day pull of §3 item 9 — and the after-cost effect at no breadth.
- **Forward validation (GATE-v2: ≥ 150 sessions and ≥ 7 calendar months of paper trading with live NBBO logging).** For a monthly strategy, seven observations of a 25–30 %-SD series. Protocol §6.4 for a long-option lane (≥ 150 trades, ≥ 3 months): 150 straddles ≈ 11 months at 14 per month per leg; the count is met by breadth, not by time, and the lower bound is not.
- **Multiplicity and the batch rule (A5).** Two sorts (momentum, reversal) × two legs × two cost models × liquidity cuts; promotion of a follow-up cell "requires the same sign in every year of its window and survival of a Bonferroni-adjusted interval across the batch". Figure 2 of the paper shows positive rolling five-year *gross* means; nothing in the paper or in any later source reports a yearly after-cost sign, and at SD 25 % a single year of twelve months has a standard error of ≈ 7 % against a mean of ≈ 1 %.
- **Tail survival.** Naked single-name short straddles held through earnings (Table 1's 90th-percentile straddle return +94 %, i.e. a −94 % month on a short leg; the short leg's loss is unbounded); the paper's lag-2–12 factor maximum drawdown is 55.7 % gross (Table 4) at ≈ 137–274 names per leg, before costs and margin; Reg-T margin for a short equity straddle is premium + 20 % of the underlying on the greater side plus the other side's premium (FINRA 4210(f)(2)(E); `execution-costs.md` §7), ≈ 3.5 × the straddle's value for a one-month ATM straddle at 35 % IV (sibling §3), versus the paper's SPAN ratio "slightly above 2".

## 5. Retail accessibility (brief; the sibling note has the detail)

Long leg: two marketable orders at the NBBO, $0.70 per contract per side, no margin — retail-accessible, and negative at every cost row of §2.3. Short leg: naked single-name straddle writing on 14–274 names a month, highest approval tier ($25,000 minimum equity for uncovered equity options at Schwab [PRAC], Level 5 at TradeStation [PRAC]), Reg-T margin ≈ 3.5 × premium, assignment risk on ITM expiries and ex-dividend dates — accessible in principle to a large account, outside the Compass long-option scope (H15 scope note) and outside A4's defined-risk opening. Defined-risk substitutes (iron butterflies) add two more entry half-spreads per leg (S10-rerun: verticals pay two spreads and are worse), which at h_w ≥ 1 % removes the only positive row of §2.3. Nothing in the test design is institutional-only; the inaccessible part is the paper's cost column ("algo" 20 % of the quoted half-spread: Muravyev–Pearson's algorithmic traders at $0.026 against a quoted $0.128).

## 6. What could be registered instead (for the owner's scope decision; not a recommendation), and what should not

1. **A gross diagnostic replication, labelled data-quality, not edge search.** Tier A equities (69 names, or the 2013-listed subset of ≈ 54 to avoid survivorship until point-in-time tiers exist), monthly ATM straddle at the 10:00 1-minute NBBO mid on the expiration Friday *and* at the closing EOD NBBO mid, OI at selection, hold to expiry with the payoff from stock EOD where the tape floor allows (Nasdaq names 2012-06 →, NYSE names 2017-01 → as measured) and from the expiring chain's intrinsic elsewhere, flagged; quintile sorts on lags 2–12 and lag 1; CIs clustered by month. Pull ≈ 2.8 login-days (§3 item 9). It would answer two questions no published source answers — whether the monthly straddle effect survives 10:00 marks instead of closing midpoints, and whether it held 2019-07 → 2026-09 — and it would produce the first measured single-name half-spreads on 2012–2024 chains (item 10). It cannot answer the after-cost question (§4), and at Tier A breadth it cannot resolve even the gross spread inside the window (≈ 130 months needed, 141 available, before the 2013–2016 payoff caveat). Pre-register it as a diagnostic with no promotion path, or do not run it.
2. **The ranking use** already stated in `academic-anomalies.md` §2 and the sibling's §5: if Compass ever holds a long single-name straddle (the S13/A5.2 pre-earnings cell via S11 is the only candidate, and S13-R says its mean is a tail), prefer names whose trailing 2–12-month straddle returns rank high and whose lag-1 return ranks low. Gross value ≤ 3.7 % of premium per month against the universe mean (−1.55 vs −5.28), zero execution cost, computed from the same per-root chain history; it belongs with L3/L4 as a filter, and it cannot turn a negative long-straddle cell positive.
3. **Should not be registered:** L2 as a lane in either direction. The long leg is negative before costs on the paper's own Table 2 and at every measured spread; the long-short's after-cost sign depends on a liquidity decile and is unresolvable in 159 months at any breadth we can pull; the short leg is naked single-name writing outside every scope amendment; the point-in-time universe and the pre-2017 CTA-tape payoff series do not exist in our data; and the only post-2019 peer-reviewed evidence is on a different return measure with 30 months of extension. Re-propose only with (a) point-in-time tiers for 2013–2024 built from the landed daily bars, (b) a measured single-name half-spread table on 2012–2024 chains, (c) a published after-cost replication on post-2019 data, and (d) the owner's explicit opening of naked single-name short premium — all four, not one.
