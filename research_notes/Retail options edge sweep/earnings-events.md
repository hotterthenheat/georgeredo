# Option returns around earnings and macro events: what the literature measures, what we measured, and what is left to test (2026-10-14)

Companion to `research/compass/registry.jsonl` (closed ids cited by name), `02_coverage_matrix.md` (data floors), `05_results.md` (S01–S16b) and the sibling notes in this directory (`academic-anomalies.md` §8 already attaches Gao–Xing–Zhang to S13/A5.2; `0dte-microstructure.md` §7 covers FOMC-day 0DTE evidence: Song 2026, Londono–Samadi, Wright, Greekslab). This note does not repeat those sections; it adds the primary numbers that were not yet on file and reads them against S08, S13, S13-R, S15, H17 and the pending S11/A5.2.

Grading: **[PR]** peer-reviewed journal; **[WP]** working paper (SSRN/NBER/Fed/conference); **[PRAC]** practitioner white paper; **[SEC]** secondary summary used only where the primary text could not be retrieved, and said so; **[VM]** vendor marketing, never evidence, listed only to flag. Every number below is either read from the primary text (PDFs converted with `pdftotext` in the session scratchpad: Gao–Xing–Zhang Nov-2017 version, Kelly–Pastor–Veronesi NBER 19812, de Silva–Smith–So (June-2023 draft and the *Review of Finance* 2026 version), Lucca–Moench SR 512, Knox–Londono–Samadi–Vissing-Jorgensen Aug-2024, Knox–Vissing-Jorgensen FEDS 2026-023, Hu–Pan–Wang–Zhu JFE proof, Pan–Peng June-2026, Ernst–Gilbert–Hrdlicka July-2021, Lei–Wang–Yan JBF 2020, Liu–Mao–Tang–Zhou Jan-2023, Barth–So May-2011, Jacobs–Ke–Pan JFQA 2026, the Dubinsky–Johannes–Kaeck–Seeger NYU-2017 slides, the anonymous FMA-Taipei 2025 CAPM-in-options paper, Barber–De George–Lehavy–Trueman June-2011) or from the publisher abstract, and the sentence says which.

Cost model for every after-cost statement: marketable orders at the NBBO (full quoted half-spread each side), $0.70 per contract per side, no rebates. Measured half-spreads from registry `C1`: SPY/QQQ 0.5 % of mid, IWM 0.7–1.0 %, SPXW 0.9 %, single names 3.5–4.1 % median (CRML 25–43 %). A single-name ATM straddle therefore costs ≈ 7–8 % of its premium per round trip (two legs, two sides) plus ≈ 0.5 % in fees; a SPXW straddle ≈ 2 %; a SPY/QQQ straddle ≈ 1 % plus fees, with the 0DTE buy-back of near-worthless legs at 3.3 % of credit (C1).

## 0. The three numbers that frame everything below

1. **The pre-earnings straddle premium is real, small, concentrated in the last two sessions, and largest where spreads are widest.** Gao, Xing & Zhang [PR, JFQA 2018]: individual-stock ATM straddles lose −2.12 % per week unconditionally (t −11.92), but earn +3.34 % held [−3, 0] (announcement at day 0) over 1996–2013, +1.90 % over [−3, −1], +1.88 % over [−1, 0]; the daily pattern with dollar-open-interest weights is +1.11 % on [−2, −1] (t 4.19) and −1.37 % on [0, +1] (t −3.07), all other days insignificant. Sorted by option bid-ask spread, the lowest-spread quartile earns 1.14 % and the highest 4.93 % (difference 3.79 %, t 5.33); by option volume, highest-volume 0.90 % vs lowest 5.82 %. Returns are at closing bid-ask midpoints. In the names a retail account can trade at a 1–2 % half-spread the gross premium is ≈ 1 % and the round trip ≈ 2–4 %.
2. **The event-day straddle is overpriced by a large margin, and retail is the buyer.** Dubinsky, Johannes, Kaeck & Seeger [PR, RFS 2019; NYU-2017 slides]: option-implied earnings-jump volatility exceeds subsequent realized announcement volatility uniformly ("Q-vol > P-vol"), and "straddle returns are highly significant: −8 %/EAD" for the 50 most option-active firms per year, 2000–2015. de Silva, Smith & So [PR, *Review of Finance* 30(2) 2026, 489–535]: on 32,758 announcements 2010–2021 with Nasdaq NOTO/PHOTO clientele data, straddles on high expected-announcement-volatility (EAV) events underperform low-EAV events by 11 % on the announcement day (t 19.44) and a further 9 % over the next 10 sessions (t 8.31); retail buys in [−5, −2], market makers are the counterparty, the half-spread retail pays costs an additional 9 % of the investment in high-EAV names, and retail loses 5–9 % of option investments per announcement on average, 10–14 % for high-EAV announcements, ≈ $3 billion over the sample after spreads.
3. **The pre-FOMC drift is 49 bp in 1994–2011 and ≈ 4 bp in 2012–2018.** Lucca & Moench [PR, JF 2015; SR 512 text]: +49 bp over 2 pm (day −1) → 2 pm (day 0), t > 4.5, ≈ 80 % of annual realized excess returns since 1994, annualized Sharpe 1.14; 20 bp in 1980–1993; nothing in Treasuries or on other macro days. Hu, Pan, Wang & Zhu [PR, JFE 2022] Table 2: pre-FOMC return (close day −1 → 5 min before the release) 35.81 bp in 1994–2000 (t 5.55), 35.55 bp in 2001–2011 (t 4.27), **3.96 bp in 2012–2018 (t 0.74)**; 27.14 bp over 1994–2018. Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert [PR, FRL 2021, abstract]: the drift "essentially disappeared after 2015". Song [WP, 2026; sibling note]: 2016–2023 open-to-release −0.03 %, only the overnight gap +0.19 % survives; structural break at March 2016. Pan & Peng [WP, June 2026]: over the full September 1994 → December 2025 sample the pre-FOMC SPX return (close day −1 → −5 min) still averages 26.84 bp (t 6.74) with the VIX level explaining 14.5 % of its variance; that mean is carried by the pre-2012 years.

## 1. Pre-announcement implied-volatility run-up and long straddles

- **IV path** (Gao–Xing–Zhang, Figure/Panel B, 1996–2013): mean ATM IV 0.467 at day −30, 0.499 at day −4, 0.514 at day −1 (the peak), then collapses to 0.464 the day after and stays flat (0.463 at day +30). So the run-up is +4.7 vol points over the month, of which +1.5 points fall in [−4, −1]; the crush is −5 points in one session. Mean straddle IV 47.4 % vs 43.1 % realized daily volatility across the sample. Average straddle open interest 2,431 lots, daily volume 401 lots; 669 firms per quarter on average (165 in 1996-Q3).
- **Who earns it**: smaller firms, higher volatility and kurtosis, more volatile past surprises, lower volume, wider spreads (all four cuts in Table 4 Panel C go the same way). Simple (non-delta-neutral) straddles earn 0.80–2.25 % with t > 6 across windows and weights. The authors do not report a net-of-spread return; the quartile cut above is the cost evidence.
- **Chung & Louis** [PR, J. Empirical Finance 40 (2017) 220–235, abstract]: straddles bought before announcements earn positive returns; the effect is larger when pre-formation volatility is low and realized volatility into expiry high; traders "overestimate future volatility after recent earnings announcements, particularly after a period of relatively high volatility", and the overestimation "increases with the magnitude of the earnings surprise". No magnitudes on the public page.
- **Chen, Gan & Vasquez** [PR, JBF 149 (2023), abstract]: a straddle decomposes into a vega asset and a gamma (jump) asset; the jump asset's price share (S-jump) rises before announcements and "captures the run-up and run-down return patterns" of straddles around earnings. The run-up is a jump-risk price, not a diffusion-vol price, which is why a vega-neutral, gamma-positive structure carries it and a calendar does not.
- **Alexiou, Goyal, Kostakis & Rompolis** [PR, *Review of Finance* 29(4) 2025, 963–1007]: 2013–2020, the 100 highest-option-volume firms per year (194 firms), options expiring 3–13 calendar days ahead, 2,229 pre-EAD IV curves. 38.4 % of pre-EAD curves are concave on day −1 (4.8 % on non-EAD days); concave names move 5.88 % vs 4.24 % in absolute abnormal return on the EAD (t 7.71) and have 47.49 % vs 37.11 % ten-day post-EAD realized volatility; yet their delta-neutral ATM straddles return −3.74 % vs +0.91 % on the EAD (t −2.16 on the difference), strangles −7.94 % vs +1.12 %, delta- and vega-neutral straddles −10.69 % vs +2.29 %; the delta- and gamma-neutral straddle shows no difference (+1.32 % vs +1.08 %). Average bid-ask spread 5.99 % of mid for 0.98–1.02 moneyness in the concave set; strikes with spreads above 20 % excluded; no net-of-spread returns reported. Reading: concavity is a cheap ex-ante tag for "the event-jump premium is high here", and the premium is paid by the straddle buyer and collected by the gamma seller.
- **The anonymous FMA-Taipei paper "Testing the CAPM in equity options: earnings announcements as catalysts"** [WP, Feb 2025; OptionMetrics 1996–2021, 529,528 option-EAD observations]: sorting calls by Black–Scholes option beta, the quintile-5-minus-1 monthly return is 41.94 % (t 2.29) in months with an announcement and insignificant otherwise; the 5−1 spread over [−5, +5] is 18.02 % at midpoints, 10.16 % at 50 % of the quoted spread and 4.14 % at 100 % of the quoted spread, still significant; "the bulk of the option return premium is realized prior to the EAD. And there is no option return reversal after the EAD". This is a long-short across leverage, not a long; the delta-hedged version also loads on beta. Relevance to A5.2: if the pre-EAD call premium is a leverage (beta) premium, the A5.2 nearest-0.50-delta call is the wrong instrument and the 0.30/0.15 vertical is closer; the test should cut by option beta.
- **Barth & So** [PR, *The Accounting Review* 89(5) 2014; May-2011 WP text]: 45,181 firm-quarters, 2,576 firms, 1996–2007; implied announcement volatility exceeds realized when the announcement risk is non-diversifiable (bellwethers, industry leaders, high COMOVE), and the published abstract says the premium "produces predictable variation in option straddle returns around earnings announcements". Magnitudes of the straddle result are in the TAR version, which the MIT repository rate-limited (HTTP 429) twice; not retrieved.
- **Liu, Mao, Tang & Zhou** [WP, Jan 2023, Washington U./UT Dallas]: 3,812 announcements of 357 S&P 500 firms, 2010–2021; the option-implied upward/downward announcement drift sizes average 246/351 bp with an ex-ante announcement risk premium averaging 15 bp (sd 16 bp). Table 9, one-day holds **at bid/ask** (long bought at the offer, sold at the bid; short the reverse): long straddle −0.58 % (day −1), −0.95 % (day 0), −0.73 % (day +1); short straddle −0.90 %, −0.68 %, −0.73 %. Both sides lose on every day: on S&P 500 names the quoted spread exceeds the one-day event premium in either direction. Conditional on above-median ex-ante premium the short straddle on day 0 earns +0.39 % (n 1,214) vs −0.17 % below the median (n 1,215), difference 0.56 % (t 11.53). Their day-0 convention and return denominator are not fully stated in the retrieved text, so these magnitudes are not directly comparable with DJKS's −8 %; the sign pattern is what matters: unconditional, the round trip eats the event premium; conditioned on ex-ante uncertainty, a small net short edge appears.

**Against S13 / S13-R / A5.2.** S13's underlying run-up is +97 bp absolute, +20 bp [+4, +36] adjusted over 10 sessions (2024–2026), a tail- and Q2-concentrated mean (S13-R: without the top 1 % of events the adjusted mean is −7 bp; the ≥ $1 B names are +45 [−29, +117]). The literature's run-up window for the option is [−3, −1], not [−10, −1]; the vega gain from +1.5 IV points over [−4, −1] on a 30–45-DTE ATM contract is ≈ 0.5–0.7 % of spot (vega ≈ 0.4 % of spot per point at 30 DTE), the theta over 3 sessions ≈ 0.4–0.5 % of spot (premium ≈ 5 % of spot at 35 % IV), the single-name round trip ≈ 7–8 % of premium ≈ 0.35–0.4 % of spot. Net ≈ 0 before the delta term; the delta term is +20 bp × 0.5 over 10 sessions, or less over 3. A5.2 as pre-registered (entry 10 sessions before) pays 7 more sessions of theta for the same vega. `academic-anomalies.md` §8 already recommends adding the [−3, −1] cell and a liquidity-tercile cut; the new evidence here (FMA-Taipei beta cut; Alexiou concavity; Liu's bid/ask result) says also to cut by option beta and by concavity and to expect the long cell to clear only in the top liquidity tercile, if anywhere.

## 2. "Sell the expected move": event-day short premium

- **Peer-reviewed and working-paper magnitudes.** DJKS −8 %/EAD for the buyer (50 most active names, midpoints). de Silva: high- minus low-EAV straddle −11 % on the EAD (t 19.55 in the RoF text, 19.44 in the draft), and the predictive variable is AbnormalIV at day −1, which absorbs AbnormalIV at day −5 because it carries the retail price pressure of [−5, −2]. Alexiou: concave-curve straddles −3.74 % on the EAD. Gao–Xing–Zhang: [0, +1] −1.37 % per day with dollar-OI weights. Liu: net of bid/ask on S&P 500 names, +0.39 % for the high-premium half, −0.17 % for the low half. Chung–Louis: post-announcement overestimation grows with the surprise.
- **Milian** [PR, *J. Risk and Financial Management* 16(5) 2023, MDPI, abstract]: weekly-option straddle returns around earnings are "significantly higher (lower) when the historical earnings announcement move is high (low)" relative to the implied move; no relation to historical volatility, implied volatility or the volatility gap; the author says a trader "will need to carefully minimize transactions costs to profit from it". No magnitudes on the public page.
- **Arjun, Lipkin & Tatevossian** [PR, *Journal of Risk*, Jan 2025; SSRN 4701633 blocked, abstract via publisher listing]: from the pre-earnings IV on day −1 "the options market does a good job of predicting the price impact (in magnitude) of the earnings event, but there are significant numbers of outliers"; the earnings-window return distribution is symmetric and fat-tailed. No containment percentage is given.
- **Vendor and retail-education claims, flagged, not evidence [VM]**: ORATS "volatility around earnings" (no measured run-up/crush, no backtest); Market Chameleon single-name pages (NFLX: market "underpriced" the move 54 % of the time, implied 8.8 % vs realized 11.9 %; Citigroup and Home Depot: options overestimated the move 58 % of the time since 2022); earnings-watcher "beat rates" of 25–88 % per ticker on a peak-move definition; the widely repeated "stocks stay inside the implied move 70–75 % of the time" has no primary source; tastylive's "75 % theory" video is unverified. None reports costs.

**Against the registry.** No study has tested single-name event-day short premium: H9/H11/H13/S15 are index 0DTE straddles (closed; S15's VRP conditioning had the wrong sign because a rich 0DTE implied move forecasts a large realized move). The earnings case differs in two ways that the literature documents and the index case lacks: (a) the ex-ante premium is identifiable per event (AbnormalIV−1, concavity, implied-vs-historical move) and the conditional short edge has the right sign in both Liu and de Silva; (b) the buyer is retail with measured price pressure. The S11 cell "sell a call vertical on top-tercile beats" is directional and enters after the event; the cell proposed below (E2) is non-directional and enters before it. Tail: the earnings jump is unbounded; GATE-v2 tail survival applies, so the structure is an iron fly or a strangle with wings, never a naked straddle, and the stress case is a 25–30 % gap against a 10 % implied move.

## 3. After the event: volatility persistence and drift through options

- **IV stays rich after high-EAV events.** de Silva: a further −9 % (t 8.31) for high- vs low-EAV straddles over [+1, +10], "with little skewness or kurtosis", and retail does "not close their positions until weeks after announcements". Chung–Louis: post-announcement overestimation of volatility grows with |surprise|. This is a non-event-risk carry window that nothing in the registry covers: S08's drift entry is directional stock; S11's vertical is directional.
- **Post-earnings drift in the surprise direction, through options.** No paper tests SUE-sorted option returns directly (searched; none found). The adjacent evidence says the drift is smallest where options are active: Truong & Corrado [PR, *Review of Accounting Studies* 19 (2014), abstract; 1996–2007]: higher abnormal option volume around the announcement "hastens the stock price adjustment to earnings news and reduces post-earnings announcement drift". Lei, Wang & Yan [PR, JBF 119 (2020) 105126; 128,438 announcements 1996–2015]: the call-minus-put IV spread builds monotonically into the announcement, its cumulative abnormal value over [−5, −2] forecasts the [−1, +1] announcement return (OLS and clustered, all significant), raises the earnings response coefficient, and the PEAD over [+2, +91] is smaller where the pre-announcement spread was large; option volume alone has no forecast power. Liu et al.: PEAD "is present only when the risk premia are high. After controlling for the announcement risk premia, the PEAD factor of the literature no longer has any abnormal returns". Atilgan [PR, JBF 38 (2014) 205–215, abstract]: over the two-day announcement window, stocks with relatively expensive calls outperform stocks with relatively expensive puts by more than 1.5 percentage points; stronger with liquid options and asymmetric information. Johnson & So [PR, JFE 106 (2012), abstract]: low O/S outperforms high O/S by 0.34 % per week and O/S predicts firm-specific earnings news.
- **Reading against S08.** S08 conditioned on the realized surprise and found the surprise direction underperforms the universe by −17 to −44 bp over 10–21 sessions in 2024–2026 ("sell the beat"). The literature's live information is ex-ante and option-implied (the [−5, −2] call-put IV spread; O/S), not the ex-post SUE; it forecasts the announcement-window move (≈ 1.5 pp over two days), not a multi-week drift, and in optioned names the drift is already small. So S08's negative is consistent with the literature for this universe and does not need reopening; what has not been tested is the ex-ante option-implied direction for the event window itself (E4 below), which is an underlying-level screen first.

## 4. The retail-specific evidence (de Silva, Smith & So)

Data: Nasdaq NOTO/PHOTO end-of-day files by clientele (retail, professional customer, market maker, broker/dealer, firm), January 2010 → February 2021, 32,758 announcements; retail stock trades identified from TAQ by the Boehmer et al. sub-penny method. Findings with numbers: retail is net long into announcements and market makers net short (Table 2: mean retail position at day −2 +57,574 contracts, market maker −44,316); retail buying is concentrated in [−5, −2] and rises monotonically with AbnormalIV−5; retail trades options over stocks more as EAV rises; high-EAV straddles −11 % on the EAD and −9 % over the next 10 sessions relative to low-EAV; the dollar half-spread retail pays is ≈ 50 % larger before high- than low-EAV announcements and costs an additional 9 % of the investment; retail loses ≈ 5 % of its non-announcement dollar volume on a typical announcement and ≈ 50 % on a top-quintile-EAV announcement (≈ $6K and ≈ $60K per announcement on a $134K average), ≈ $360 M across ≈ 6,000 high-EAV announcements in the sample, ≈ $1.5 B grossed up to the market before spreads and ≈ $3 B after spreads at a conservative 20 % price improvement (Ernst & Spatt put retail price improvement near 10 %). Return predictability is absent around pseudo-announcements and holds in the OptionMetrics universe outside the Nasdaq sample.

Implications for a retail account: (1) any Compass long-option earnings play that enters [−5, −1] in a high-EAV name is the trade this paper measures as a 10–14 % loser; (2) the counter-trade (short the event gamma in high-EAV names at day −1) is what market makers earn, and its net size for a marketable-order account is bounded by the spread the paper says is widest exactly there; (3) the 10-session post-event underperformance is the one window where the retail buyer's sluggishness leaves premium on the table without event risk.

## 5. Underlying-level earnings-announcement premium versus S13

Frazzini & Lamont [WP, NBER 13090, 2007]: long expected announcers / short non-announcers earns over 60 bp per month, 1972–2004 (NBER digest summary; working paper). Barber, De George, Lehavy & Trueman [PR, JFE 2013; June-2011 text]: 46 countries, 1991–2010, announcer-minus-non-announcer 66.8 bp per month (8.02 % annualized; announcer portfolio 118.3 bp, non-announcer 51.6 bp), over 11 % annualized after controls, positive in 16 of 20 years and in 16 of the 20 largest countries (significant in 9), smallest-quintile premium exceeds the largest by ≈ 6 % per year. S13's +20 bp adjusted over 10 sessions (≈ +40 bp per month-equivalent) is the same sign and about half the 1991–2010 global magnitude, on 2024–2026 only, and S13-R shows it is a tail. The literature's premium is a calendar-month long-short on stocks; it is not an option result and the port-spec arithmetic in `05_results.md` S13 stands.

## 6. Macro events: FOMC, CPI, NFP; elections

**Pre-FOMC drift and its decay (all underlying-level).** Lucca–Moench 49 bp (1994–2011; the SPX "is about 50 bp higher than on the previous day's open" by the release; realized volatility and the VIX are low in the window; a one-sd higher VIX two days before adds 31 bp; drift 29–52 bp in other developed indices; housing starts the only other announcement with a positive pre-return, 13 bp). Hu–Pan–Wang–Zhu Table 2: FOMC 35.81 / 35.55 / 3.96 bp in 1994–2000 / 2001–2011 / 2012–2018; NFP 10.10 bp (t 3.63), ISM 9.14 (t 2.10), GDP 7.46 (t 2.08) over 1994–2018, realized overnight (close → 8:25 am) and "stable and significant across all three subperiods" (ex-FOMC 6.98 bp in 2012–2018, t 2.27) against a non-announcement overnight mean of 0.69 bp; the VIX builds over up to six sessions before FOMC and falls before the release. Martello & Ribeiro [WP, SSRN 3286745, 2018; via Quantpedia, SEC]: pre-FOMC drift 109 bp when implied volatility is above its prior median, 9.7 bp below it, ≈ 0 in the bottom quintile. Kurov–Wolfe–Gilbert: gone after 2015 with or without press conferences. Knox & Vissing-Jorgensen [WP, FEDS 2026-023, Aug 2025]: in April 2011 → December 2023 the surviving piece is "a substantial average overnight return of about 20 bps the night before" the FOMC; Boguth, Grégoire & Martineau (2019) find the post-2011 drift only on press-conference meetings. Ignatieva & Ohashi [PR, *Applied Economics* 57(17) 2025, abstract]: on press-conference meetings the excess return is positive before the announcement and the VIX return is significantly negative both before and after. Song 2026 (sibling note): break March 2016; 2016–2023 open-to-release −0.03 %, overnight +0.19 %. Johannes, Kaeck & Seeger "FOMC Event Risk" [PR, *Journal of Finance* forthcoming per SSRN 4484011, abstract]: FOMC event risk is priced with a significant variance risk premium, but "no evidence for an expected return premium related to FOMC event risk in the pre-FOMC period or spanning the announcement"; pre-FOMC drifts relate to the VIX, not to event-risk resolution; as quoted by Song, straddles lose over the statement window and earn ≈ 0 over the press conference. Jacobs, Ke & Pan [PR, JFQA 61(2) 2026, 640–672; SPX options 1996–2021, 207 meetings]: 7-DTE option-implied abnormal skewness is 86.21 % lower and kurtosis 52.50 % higher before FOMC than on other days; abnormal volatility predicts the pre-FOMC drift and announcement-day return; skewness and kurtosis predict post-FOMC returns up to a week (adjusted R² up to 13 %), stronger for expansionary shocks.

**Macro-day equity premium, ex ante versus realized.** Savor & Wilson [PR, JFQA 48(2) 2013, abstract]: 1958–2009, 11.4 bp on inflation/employment/FOMC days vs 1.1 bp otherwise, Sharpe ≈ 10×, > 60 % of the premium. Ernst, Gilbert & Hrdlicka [WP, July 2021]: January 1990 → June 2018, the announcement sets of Savor–Wilson, Lucca–Moench and Cieslak–Morse–Vissing-Jorgensen combined deliver 7.7 vs −1.9 bp per day, ≈ 150 % of the equity premium; four variables already give 108 %; with day-of-month fixed effects and the full distribution of macro series, expected announcements explain about half the premium and Sharpe ratios are similar on announcement and non-announcement days; the CAPM's fit on those days is a by-product of their high ex-post returns. Knox, Londono, Samadi & Vissing-Jorgensen [WP, Fed, Aug 2024; daily SPX expirations October 2016 → December 2023]: ex-ante abnormal forward equity premia are 0.65 bp per day for FOMC (t 5.73), 0.38 bp CPI (t 3.20), 0.25 bp NFP (t 4.14); FOMC forward premium 7.93 bp per period (63 bp per year on the IEP); CPI premia rose sharply June 2022 → June 2023, peaking at 4.98 bp for the 12 Jan 2023 release; periods spanning CPI/FOMC/NFP are 17 % of periods and 23 % of expected returns, "quantitatively far from fully explaining" the realized-return results; realized excess returns over the longer sample are +24 bp per FOMC, +23 ISM, +16 NFP, insignificant for CPI and PPI. Ai, Bansal & Guo [WP, NBER 31923, 2023] survey the announcement premium; a 1961–2023 figure of ≈ 44 announcement days earning ≈ 71 % of the premium is quoted in secondary coverage and was not verified in the abstract.

**FOMC/CPI/NFP option returns.** DeSimone [dissertation, Univ. of Delaware 2017] and DeSimone & Laux [WP, 2018; OptionMetrics summary]: announcement days account for > 77 % of the negative annualized SPX delta-neutral straddle return; CPI, NFP, industrial-production days −1.3 % to −2.5 % per day vs −0.1 % on non-announcement days; FOMC days "highly positive" (no figure on the public page), attributed to a "government put"; sample period not stated on the public pages. This conflicts in sign with JKS (statement-window straddles lose). Wright [PR, J. Time Series Analysis 2025; NBER 28306]: event-eve variance risk premia positive, larger for FOMC than employment; about half the FOMC-eve rise in implied variance is premium. Londono & Samadi [WP, IFDP 1376]: FOMC-spanning expirations carry 134 bp higher ATM IV and a 44 % larger variance risk premium than neighbours (sibling note §7). Locally, H17: FOMC afternoons move 2.08× [1.56, 2.68] event-free afternoons; CPI/NFP afternoons 0.94–0.96× (the 08:30 release is in the price by the 0DTE open); S15 skipping FOMC/CPI/NFP/month-end moved the 10:00 straddle from −$15.46 to −$11.78 (still negative). Zhong [WP, arXiv 2606.12872, June 2026; PM-settled SPX May 2022 → August 2025]: scheduled FOMC/CPI/NFP jumps calibrated from event-spanning expiries improve held-out pricing of straddles and strangles, identification strongest for CPI and FOMC; no return results.

**Elections and summits.** Kelly, Pastor & Veronesi [PR, JF 2016; NBER 19812 text]: 271 events in 20 countries (OptionMetrics, longest sample 1990–2012 for the S&P 500), options spanning the event carry 1.43 % per year higher mean-adjusted implied volatility (ATM one-month puts 5.1 % more expensive; 5 %-OTM 9.6 %, 10 %-OTM 16.0 %), a 0.0107 per year higher variance risk premium (t 2.61) and a 0.0173 steeper slope (t 3.59); ≈ 8 % more expensive in weak economies vs 1 % in strong ones. The paper measures prices, not realized option returns; the one country-index with a retail-tradeable chain is the S&P 500 with one election every four years. Not a lane.

## 7. Institutional-only versus retail-accessible

Institutional-only: market making against retail event demand (de Silva: market makers are the primary beneficiaries; requires quoting, inventory netting and the Nasdaq clientele data to see it); the Liu et al. ex-ante premium model (needs their recovered-distribution estimator; a public proxy is AbnormalIV−1 or concavity); informed pre-announcement option flow signing (Lei–Wang–Yan use closing IV spreads, which is accessible, but their volume variable is unsigned and useless); Bernile-type informed trading ahead of macro releases; international index options for KPV; sub-second FOMC statement-window trading (Song's 5-second ES data).

Retail-accessible and measurable with the owner's data: single-name EOD NBBO chains and open interest from 2012-06-04 (ThetaData), the Unusual Whales earnings calendar with report time 1996 →, 1-minute SPY/QQQ/SPXW chains for the macro-day overnight structures (SPXW Friday weeklies throughout; SPY Wednesday expirations from 2016; daily expirations from 2022-05/2022-11). Closing bid-ask midpoints (every paper above) are not an executable price; the engine prices leg by leg at the NBBO (A4/A5 conventions).

## 8. What differs from what we tested

## 9. Testable hypotheses (new ids proposed; none duplicates a closed id)

**E1 — refine A5.2 before S11 runs (attaches to S13; not a new lane).** Add cells: (a) ATM straddle and 0.50-delta call bought at the close of session −3, sold at the pre-event close ([−3, −1]); (b) the same from session −2 ([−2, −1], the Gao–Xing–Zhang +1.11 %/day cell); (c) cut every A5.2 cell by measured half-spread tercile (ThetaData closing NBBO), by option beta (Black–Scholes Ω of the chosen contract) and by pre-EAD concavity (sign of the second difference of the 3–13-DTE IV curve across the three strikes around ATM); (d) record the entry-to-exit ATM IV change and attribute P&L to delta, vega and theta. Data: UW earnings calendar (1996 →, report time) × ThetaData option EOD with closing NBBO and OI (2012-06 →) on the S07 universe; window 2012-06 → 2026-10 once the daily-bar years land, 2024 → 2026 now. Expected after costs: top-liquidity tercile ≈ 0 to +1 % of premium per event for [−3, −1] (gross ≈ 1–2 % at 1–2 % half-spreads), negative elsewhere (gross 1–3 % against a 7–8 % round trip); the [−10, −1] cells negative outside the S13-R tail. Promotion rule as A4/A5.

**E2 — single-name event-day short gamma, conditioned on ex-ante announcement variance (new; defined risk).** At the close of session −1 (BMO reporters) or of day 0 (AMC reporters), sell the nearest-expiry (≥ 2 sessions past the event) ATM straddle with wings at ±(2 × implied move) (iron fly), buy back at the close of the first post-event session; condition on (i) AbnormalIV−1 top quintile (ATM IV at −1 minus its [−35, −6] mean, cross-sectionally ranked each quarter), (ii) pre-EAD concavity, (iii) implied move ÷ median of the last 8 realized announcement moves in the top tercile (Milian's sign); universe = names whose measured half-spread is ≤ 1.5 % of premium; controls = the same structure on the same names on random non-event dates and the unconditional event set; fills leg by leg at the closing NBBO, $0.70 per contract per side per leg, adverse one-tick scenario; CIs clustered by event date; same-sign-every-year and Bonferroni across cells. Data: as E1; 2012-06 → 2026-10 is ≈ 50,000 quarterly reports in the optionable universe, of which the ≤ 1.5 %-half-spread set is perhaps 100–200 names per year. Expected after costs: gross ≈ +5–8 % of straddle premium per event in the conditioned set (DJKS −8 % for the buyer; de Silva −11 % high-vs-low; Alexiou −3.7 % for concave), round trip 2–4 % in the liquid set, wings cost ≈ 1–2 %; net ≈ +1–4 % of premium per event before the tail, ≈ 0 outside the top liquidity tercile, and one 25 %-gap event against a 10 % implied move costs the full width of the fly (≈ 100–150 % of the credit). Win rate will be 60–70 % by construction (H16); it is reported, not a gate. Tail survival (GATE-v2) must be stated per name against the worst historical gap in the sample.

**E3 — post-event volatility persistence in high-EAV names (new; carry, defined risk, no event jump).** At the close of the first post-event session, in names that were top-quintile AbnormalIV−1 (or concave) and whose realized event move was ≤ the implied move, sell a 10-session-to-expiry ATM straddle with wings, hold to the close of session +10 or expiry; controls = same names, same structure, on non-event dates and in bottom-quintile-EAV names. Mechanism: de Silva's −9 % (t 8.31) over [+1, +10] for high- vs low-EAV straddles and the retail disposition effect; Chung–Louis's post-announcement overestimation rising with |surprise|. Expected after costs: the −9 % is relative (high minus low), so the absolute is smaller and unmeasured; at a 2–4 % round trip in the liquid set, net ≈ 0 to +3 % of premium per event; ≈ 0 or negative at single-name median spreads. The attraction is the absence of jump risk; the risk is the second-day continuation moves (S08: moves against the surprise reverse weakly; moves with it do not continue).

**E4 — ex-ante option-implied direction for the earnings window (underlying-level screen first; option expression only if the screen clears).** Signal: cumulative abnormal call-minus-put ATM IV spread over [−5, −2] (Lei–Wang–Yan) or the Atilgan spread at −2; target: the signed [−1, +1] return (≈ 1.5 pp top-minus-bottom in 1996–2011 samples). Run on the S07 universe with ThetaData closing NBBO IVs (engine `greeks.py` from the mid) and the UW calendar, 2012 → 2026 as years land; A2 hurdle on the underlying (absolute lower bound ≥ +30 bp and adjusted lower bound > 0) before any option cell. Option expression if it clears: a 0.30/0.15 vertical in the signal direction bought at −1, sold at +1 (the crush on both legs partly nets); expected after costs ≈ 0 (≈ 75 bp of spot on a 0.5-delta exposure against a 5-point IV crush on the long leg and a 7–8 % round trip). The honest expectation is that the underlying screen clears in a subset and the option cell does not.

**E5 — event-eve overnight short gamma on SPXW/SPY into NFP and CPI (record-only; cannot reach GATE-v2's 150 forward sessions at 12 events per year).** Sell the SPXW straddle expiring on the release day at the prior close (Friday weeklies exist throughout 2012 → 2026 for NFP; daily expirations from 2022-05 for CPI), buy back at 09:31–09:35; also the FOMC variant at the day −1 close bought back at 13:55 (sibling L4 covers the 14:00 statement window). Expected: DeSimone's −1.3 % to −2.5 % straddle return on CPI/NFP days and Wright's "half the event-eve variance rise is premium" give a gross of ≈ +1–2.5 % of credit per event; SPXW round trip ≈ 2 % (C1); net ≈ 0 to +0.5 %, with the 2022–2023 CPI prints (moves of several percent against a ≈ 1.6 % implied move) as the tail. Pair it with the long-side mirror (buy a 1-DTE SPY ATM call at the day −1 close, sell at the open) to measure whether the surviving ≈ 20 bp pre-FOMC overnight return and the 7–10 bp NFP/ISM/GDP overnight returns are worth anything through an option: 0.5 delta × 20 bp = 10 bp of spot ≈ 20–25 % of a 1-DTE ATM premium gross against an overnight decay of ≈ 20 % of premium and a 1–2 % round trip; expected ≈ 0, n = 8 per year. Both are measurements that bound H17's event treatment for long-option lanes, not lanes.

## 10. Closed or inapplicable after this sweep

- Post-earnings drift in the surprise direction as a stock or option position: S08 (closed); the literature agrees for optioned names (Truong–Corrado; Lei–Wang–Yan; Liu).
- Long index 0DTE and index short straddles, unconditional or VRP-timed: S01/S02/S09, H9/H11/H13, S15 (closed); nothing here reopens them.
- The Lucca–Moench 24-hour pre-FOMC drift as a lane: ≈ 4 bp per event in 2012–2018 (HPWZ), gone after 2015 (KWG; Song); only the overnight ≈ 20 bp survives and n = 8 per year; measured in E5, not promotable.
- Savor–Wilson / Cieslak-type announcement-day and FOMC-cycle stock calendars: stock-only, outside the options scope, and Ernst–Gilbert–Hrdlicka show the > 100 % concentration is sample selection; Knox et al.'s ex-ante premia are 5–14 bp per year for FOMC and CPI.
- Kelly–Pastor–Veronesi political-event option premia: international index options and one U.S. election per four years; a pricing fact, not a trade.
- Market-making against retail event flow, the Liu ex-ante premium model, informed-flow signing, statement-window microsecond trading: institutional.
- Vendor implied-vs-actual "containment" statistics (ORATS, Market Chameleon, earnings-watcher, tastylive): no costs, no sample definition, per-ticker cherry-picks; not evidence.
- DeSimone's positive FOMC-day straddle returns: a dissertation result that conflicts with JKS (forthcoming JF) and Song; the FOMC statement-window short straddle stays as the sibling note's L4 record-only cell.
