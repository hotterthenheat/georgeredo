# L4 — lottery-like options and the "retail exclusion rule": evidence-quality refutation (2026-10-14)

Lens: evidence quality (is the cited effect real, replicated, not a data-mining artefact; checked against the primary text where it could be retrieved). Hypothesis under test: registry-sweep id **L4** as stated in `academic-anomalies.md` §4 and §11 — "ex-ante total-skewness quintile spreads of 10–50 % per week (Boyer–Vorkink), calls on the most lottery-like stocks underperform by 10–20 % per month (Byun–Kim), risk-neutral skewness negatively priced (Bali–Murray); retail is the buyer of exactly these contracts and loses (Bryzgalova et al.; Beckmeyer et al.: 60 % of 0DTE retail losses are transaction costs; de Silva–Smith–So). Retail-accessible expression is a hard exclusion rule, not a lane." Suggested test: drop contracts with ask < $0.10 or premium < 1 % of spot for ≥ 5-day expirations, top-quintile smirk, top-quintile MAX or IVOL; measure the gross return spread between excluded and retained contracts on the S03 cache and Tier A chains. Proposition to refute: *a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates.*

## 0. Verdict

**Refuted as a retail-accessible edge, and the hypothesis says so itself ("Does not create a positive lane"). Beyond that, the exclusion rule as drafted is defective against the Compass contract rules, and the evidence behind its three filters is weaker than the summary suggests.** Six findings:

1. **The ex-ante skewness sort is a moneyness sort.** Boyer–Vorkink's own Internet Appendix (Table IA.II, Fama–MacBeth ranks, seven-day options): "moneyness alone explains on average about 92 % of the cross-sectional variation in ex-ante skewness for calls … and about 93 % for puts"; adding volatility raises it to 97 %, all inputs 98 %. The 10–50 % per week "lottery premium" is therefore, to first order, the known relation between moneyness/maturity and expected option returns (Coval–Shumway 2001). Compass's contract rule (delta 0.30–0.60 nearest 0.45, ask ≥ $0.05, spread < 12 %; `config/compass_config.v0.baseline.json`, S01/S03) already fixes moneyness and never selects a top-skewness-quintile contract. The exclusion is already in force; adding it excludes nothing.
2. **The "calls on lottery stocks underperform 10–20 %/month" effect is of the same size and sign as the mechanical volatility effect.** Hu–Jacobs (JFQA 2020; January 1996–July 2013, ATM one-month calls held to expiry, midpoints): call quintiles sorted on the underlying's 30-day realized volatility earn +14.7 % (low) to +0.9 % (high) per month, H−L **−13.8 % (t −3.42)**; after double-sorting on expected stock return the spreads are −11 % to −24 % per month; at the ask price "of a similar order"; "not due to cross-sectional variation in expected stock returns". The mechanism is Black–Scholes elasticity: the call on a low-volatility stock is cheaper per unit of delta, hence more leveraged, hence a higher expected return per dollar. A MAX/IVOL "lottery" sort on raw call returns picks up this leverage difference whether or not any mispricing exists. Byun–Kim's put-call-parity evidence is the part that is specific to mispricing; its magnitude net of the mechanical component is not stated in the abstract and the text could not be read.
3. **Skewness characteristics carry no alpha once moneyness and volatility are controlled, and no alpha in a factor model.** Aretz–Lin–Poon (RoF 2023; 1996-01 → 2019-06; Table 8 Panel C): with moneyness and systematic/idiosyncratic volatility in the regression, Bakshi–Kapadia–Madan implied skewness prices calls at −0.20 [t −1.85] (deep OTM), −0.06 [−1.12], −0.05 [−1.43], −0.05 [−1.71] — "weakly". Goyal–Saretto, "Can Equity Option Returns Be Explained by a Factor Model? IPCA Says Yes", RFS 38(6) 2025 (1996-01 → 2022-12, 46 characteristics incl. model-free implied skewness, realized skewness and moneyness): 39 of 46 long-short delta-hedged strategies are significant gross, 16 net at a 30 % effective/quoted spread, the average IPCA alpha is ≈ 6 bp/month (7 % of the raw 80 bp), the skewness characteristics' IPCA alphas are insignificant, and "no strategy has a significant IPCA alpha after transaction costs; net IPCA alphas are negative for all strategies". The moneyness long-short itself is +0.38 %/month (t 3.60) gross and −0.86 % net.
4. **The overpricing of OTM single-stock calls is sample-specific.** Félix, Kräussl & Stork (Tinbergen 16-022, 2016/2018; *J. Behavioral Finance* 2019; 1998-01-05 → 2013-03-19, S&P 500 constituents' OTM calls priced through cumulative prospect theory): overweighting of small probabilities is present on 97 % of days 1998–2003-01-30, *under*weighting on 65 % of days 2003-01-31 → 2008-02-21, and "less pervasive" 2008 → 2013; "overpricing of single stock options is sample specific and not structural". Boyer–Vorkink's and Byun–Kim's samples (1996–2009 and, per the JFE abstract, an OptionMetrics sample ending before 2016) sit mostly in the first regime. No replication of the quintile spreads on post-2013 data was found.
5. **"Retail is the buyer of exactly these contracts" is contradicted by the three retail datasets the hypothesis cites.** Beckmeyer et al. Table 1 (identified retail SPX 0DTE, Feb 2021–Sep 2023): ATM and 0.5–2 % OTM contracts are 29.9 % + 16.4 % (calls) and 28.1 % + 19.2 % (puts) of retail volume; contracts more than 2 % OTM are 1.4 % (calls) and 3.2 % (puts) — "more than 90 % of their transacted volume" is ATM or slightly OTM, and the 60 %-of-losses-are-transaction-costs finding is a finding about ATM/near-ATM 0DTE contracts, not lottery strikes. de Silva–Smith–So (RoF 2025; Jan 2010–Feb 2021): "ahead of high EAV announcements, retail investors primarily purchase at-the-money options, and are equally" split between < 1-month and > 2-month maturities; they control for the Boyer–Vorkink skewness measure and conclude "our findings are not likely to be driven by a preference for lottery-like payoffs". Bryzgalova et al. (companion note `retail-performance.md` §3.1): the retail concentration is in "slightly OTM" (23 % of trades) and sub-week contracts, i.e. the Compass delta band, not the lottery end of the chain. Retail loses on the contracts Compass trades, to spread and theta on uninformed entries (S01/S04/S06) — that is the registry's finding, and the lottery literature does not add to it.
6. **The exclusion rule as drafted would exclude the Compass contract itself.** Rule (a) "premium < 1 % of spot for ≥ 5-day expirations": a 0.45-delta SPY weekly at 15 % IV is ≈ 0.7–0.85 % of spot by construction (`execution-costs.md` §9: $550 on spot 777 = 0.71 %). On the actual S03 fills [LOCAL]: entry ask ÷ spot has median 0.67 % (p25 0.44 %, p75 0.93 %); **80.1 % of the 818 fills are below 1 %** (SPY 85 %, QQQ 79 %, IWM 73 %; 91 % at 1 DTE, 58 % at 7 DTE); zero fills have ask < $0.10 (minimum $0.24). Premium/spot scales with IV × √T, so a fixed 1 % threshold removes every low-IV index contract and keeps 10 %-OTM contracts on 60 %-IV names — the opposite of a lottery screen. Rule (b), the top-quintile smirk, is Xing–Zhang–Zhao's *stock-return* predictor (L5), appears in none of the three lottery papers, and as a straddle sort is insignificant in Heston et al.'s Table 2 (+0.49 %/month, t 0.78); it belongs to L5, not here. Rule (c), top-quintile MAX/IVOL, is the Hu–Jacobs leverage sort (point 2).

What survives: nothing that is not already in the registry. The Compass delta band (0.30–0.60) and the ask floor are the exclusion; H14 already leaves sub-$0.05/$0.10 short legs to expire. The local evidence on "more OTM within the band loses more" runs the other way and is inside noise: S02's delta grid has delta20_40 as the *best* lower bound (−$3.76 [−4.60, −2.94]) and delta40_60 the worst (−$4.42 [−6.22, −2.69]); within the S03 fills the lowest-|delta| tercile (mean 0.42) earned +$4.42 per contract against −$1.23 for the highest (0.47), and the lowest premium/spot tercile −$0.62 against +$2.81 (mid) and +$1.63 (high) — all within the study's ±$5 interval. There is no lane to attach the rule to (S01–S16b) and no gate it could pass (it has no expectancy of its own). Expected size after costs: zero.

## 1. The claims as cited versus the primary texts

### 1.1 Boyer & Vorkink, "Stock Options as Lotteries", *JF* 69(4) 2014, 1485–1527 [PR]; Internet Appendix read [PR supplement]

Abstract (IDEAS https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v69y2014i4p1485-1527.html ; BYU record https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8927): "total skewness exhibits a strong negative relationship with average option returns. Differences in average returns for option portfolios sorted on ex ante skewness range from 10 % to 50 % per week, even after controlling for risk … premiums … compensate intermediaries for bearing unhedgeable risk when accommodating investor demand for lottery-like options." Main text not retrieved (§7). Design and magnitudes per CXO's summary of the March 2011 draft [SEC] — https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=13232: OptionMetrics end-of-day quotes ≈ 1996–2009; portfolios formed on the first trading day and the second Friday of each month; 8 expiration horizons × 5 ex-ante-skewness quintiles; equal-weighted; returns from the end-of-day bid-ask midpoint, held to expiration; calls expiring in seven days +1.4 % (low skew) vs −35.4 % (high), spread +36.8 %; puts −5.6 % vs −60.6 %, spread +55.0 %; spreads "up to about 60 % per week" in the draft (50 % in the published abstract), larger for puts and for shorter horizons; "Returns reported ignore transaction fees for opening and closing (when in-the-money) positions"; "Option bid-ask spread increases dramatically with skewness", so "selling highly skewed options near the bid generally fails to capture the premium."

Internet Appendix (author-hosted, https://boyer.byu.edu/00000180-910c-dc15-abb6-d9dfb7040001/boyer-vorkink-appendix-pdf, read in full): (i) Table IA.II — moneyness alone explains ≈ 92 % (calls) / 93 % (puts) of the cross-sectional variation in the ex-ante skewness rank; moneyness + volatility 97 %; all inputs 98 %. (ii) Table IA.I — open interest in contracts rises with skewness (seven-day calls: 2,400 per option low-skew vs 3,937 high-skew) while *dollar* open interest falls ($441 M vs $19 M across the bin) "because options with high skewness are relatively cheap". (iii) Early exercise "has little effect"; results robust to coskewness sorts, instantaneous betas, the raw third moment and a peso-problem simulation.

Evidence-quality reading: a peer-reviewed, well-executed sort whose signal is 92–98 % moneyness and volatility — the Coval–Shumway moneyness pattern restated; returns are gross, from midpoints of contracts whose quoted half-spread is the mid (our 0DTE/weekly chains: beyond 2 % OTM the median same-day option is $0.00 × $0.01, `execution-costs.md` §2.2), so neither side of the "premium" is executable by a marketable order. The Duarte–Jones–Wang noise bias (JF 2024, in `L3` §2) is largest for exactly these cheap contracts and biases measured returns *upward*, so the high-skew quintile's true return is lower still — which strengthens "never buy them" and changes nothing for a rule that already does not.

### 1.2 Byun & Kim, "Gambling preference and individual equity option returns", *JFE* 122(1) 2016, 155–174 [PR]; abstract only

https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v122y2016i1p155-174.html ; https://pure.kaist.ac.kr/en/publications/gambling-preference-and-individual-equity-option-returns : "Call options written on the most lottery-like stocks underperform otherwise similar call options written on the least lottery-like stocks by 10–20 % per month … the more lottery-like the underlying stocks, the further and more frequently the options deviate from the put-call parity in the direction induced by overvalued calls … stronger during periods of high investor sentiment … optimism-induced gambling preference causes lottery like options to be overvalued." The lottery-likeness definition (the sweep note's "MAX, IVOL, price" attribution), the sample period, whether returns are raw or delta-hedged, the holding period and any cost treatment could not be verified (ScienceDirect 403; no repository file). Treated here as: raw call returns sorted on a lottery index, sign and order of magnitude as stated, mechanism partly identified through put-call parity.

### 1.3 Bali & Murray, "Does Risk-Neutral Skewness Predict the Cross-Section of Equity Option Portfolio Returns?", *JFQA* 48(4) 2013 [PR]; abstract, 2011 discussion and supplement only

Abstract (OptionMetrics https://optionmetrics.com/research/t-g-bali-and-s-murrary-does-risk-neutral-skewness-predict-the-cross-section-of-equity-option-portfolio-returns): skewness assets = two option positions plus stock, delta- and vega-neutral; "a strong negative relation between implied risk-neutral skewness and the returns of the skewness assets, consistent with a positive skewness preference." Gruber's EFA 2011 discussion of the February 2011 draft (https://people.lu.usi.ch/gruberp/static/files/gruber_discussion_bali_murray.pdf): decile returns of the long skewness asset fall from ≈ −0.15 to ≈ −1.5 per month across RN-skew deciles, "driven by the left (loss) side"; the discussant's open questions are the ones that matter for retail use — "Why restrict to large cap stocks?", "Options still traded at midquote → problem?", "Short positions require lots of margin → over-estimate returns". Supplement (Cambridge, read): physical (historical) skewness has no predictive power; the risk metrics of the deciles run opposite to a risk explanation. Sample period not retrieved. Instrument: a three-leg delta/vega-neutral structure rebalanced monthly on large caps — an institutional skew trade, not a long-option exclusion criterion.

### 1.4 Hu & Jacobs, "Volatility and Expected Option Returns", *JFQA* 55(3) 2020, 1025–1060 [PR]; January 2016 draft read [WP]

https://www.mcgill.ca/desautels/files/desautels/hj_jan29_2016.pdf (SSRN 2695569). Sample: OptionMetrics January 1996–July 2013, 247,859 calls / 188,046 puts, standard monthly expirations, positive OI and volume, held to expiration from the midpoint. Table 3 (moneyness 0.95–1.05, sorted on 30-day realized volatility): calls +14.7 % (low) → +0.9 % (high) per month, H−L −13.8 % (t −3.42); puts −14.6 % → −7.5 %, H−L +7.1 %; at 0.975–1.025 moneyness −13.8 % / +7.7 %; Panel C at the ask price "of a similar order of magnitude". Table 4 (double sort on the Boyer–Vorkink expected-stock-return proxy): call H−L −11 % to −24 % per month in every quintile. Fama–MacBeth with beta, size, B/M, momentum, reversal, option skew, VRP, IV term slope, moneyness and Greeks: the volatility coefficient implies −14.3 % per month per 0.6 of volatility. Theory: "In the Black-Scholes and stochastic volatility models, the expected return from holding a call (put) option is a decreasing (increasing) function of the volatility of the underlying" — "This reflects leverage: low volatility options are cheaper and therefore constitute a more leveraged position." §6.1 reconciles with Cao–Han: the delta-hedged IVOL effect is a different object; raw returns carry the elasticity effect by construction.

Reading for L4: the hypothesis treats "calls on high-MAX/high-IVOL stocks lose 10–20 %/month" as mispricing to be avoided. Hu–Jacobs shows a raw-return spread of the same sign and size arises in a frictionless model from elasticity alone and is not a stock-return effect. For a long-call program that already has a view on the stock, the elasticity difference is a *sizing* fact (a lower-volatility name's call delivers more expected return per premium dollar for the same stock expectation), not an expectancy source, and it is captured by the engine's delta targeting.

### 1.5 Aretz, Lin & Poon, "Moneyness, Underlying Asset Volatility, and the Cross-Section of Option Returns", *Review of Finance* 27(1) 2023, 289–323 [PR]; accepted draft read

https://repository.essex.ac.uk/31904/1/AretzLinPoon_OptionReturns_Complete_CondRFAccept_13Dec2021.pdf. Single-stock calls, OptionMetrics January 1996–June 2019, moneyness buckets DOTM/OTM/ATM/ITM, Fama–MacBeth. Table 8: moneyness −5.7 to −0.7 (t −9 to −5) in every panel; idiosyncratic volatility −0.61 to −0.09; Panel C adds the variance risk premium (insignificant) and Bakshi–Kapadia–Madan implied skewness: **−0.20 [−1.85], −0.06 [−1.12], −0.05 [−1.43], −0.05 [−1.71]** across the four moneyness buckets — "in line with Bali and Murray (2013) and Boyer and Vorkink (2014), skewness (kurtosis) weakly negatively (positively) prices calls". The sign survives; the size is a rounding error next to moneyness.

### 1.6 Goyal & Saretto, "Can Equity Option Returns Be Explained by a Factor Model? IPCA Says Yes", *RFS* 38(6) 2025 [PR]; OUP page read

https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/38/6/1783/8010873. OptionMetrics January 1996–December 2022; 46 characteristics (including MFskew, Rskew, moneyness, IV slope, RV−IV), each a long-short delta-hedged call portfolio and an IPCA conditioning variable; costs at 30 % of the quoted spread at initiation. Results as given on the page: 39 of 46 significant gross (RV−IV 2.87 %/month, t 24.5); 16 significant net; average IPCA alpha ≈ 6 bp/month ("close to zero … contrasts with average realized returns of over 80 basis points per month"); after multiple-testing adjustment only IV slope and RV−IV keep an alpha (≈ 0.34 %/month each); the skewness characteristics' alphas are insignificant; "No strategy has a significant IPCA alpha after transaction costs, and net IPCA alphas are negative for all strategies." Moneyness long-short +0.38 %/month gross (t 3.60), −0.86 % net. This is the most recent and longest-sample statement on the skewness characteristics and it places them at zero alpha gross and negative net.

### 1.7 Félix, Kräussl & Stork, "Single Stock Call Options as Lottery Tickets: Overpricing and Investor Sentiment", Tinbergen DP 16-022 (rev. 26 Jan 2018) [WP]; *J. Behavioral Finance* 20(4) 2019 [PR]; draft read

https://papers.tinbergen.nl/16022.pdf (listing https://ideas.repec.org/p/tin/wpaper/20160022.html). OTM single-stock calls on S&P 500 constituents, 1998-01-05 → 2013-03-19, priced through cumulative prospect theory's probability-weighting parameter γ: overweighting of small probabilities on 97 % of days 1998-01-05 → 2003-01-30; underweighting on 65 % of days 2003-01-31 → 2008-02-21; "less pervasive" 2008-02-22 → 2013-03-19; "overpricing of single stock options is sample specific and not structural"; the overweighting is "much less pronounced than the 0.61 parameter offered by the CPT", strongest at short maturities, and its variation "is largely explained by a sentiment factor". This is the one paper found that asks whether the lottery overpricing persists across regimes; its answer is that it faded after 2003.

## 2. Evidence-quality reading across the sources

- **Real and replicated as gross sorts:** yes — Boyer–Vorkink, Byun–Kim, Bali–Murray, Hu–Jacobs, Aretz–Lin–Poon, Goyal–Saretto and the BBMW/Heston tables in `academic-anomalies.md` all find the sign; t-statistics of 3–10 on 200–320 months; not data mining in the sense of a single-team, single-sample result.
- **But the content is moneyness and volatility, not "lotteryness":** Boyer–Vorkink IA (92–98 % R²), Hu–Jacobs (the raw call-return/volatility relation is a model prediction and is "not due to … expected stock returns"), Aretz–Lin–Poon (implied skewness t ≤ 1.85 beside moneyness t ≥ 5). Once a contract rule pins delta and tenor, what the lottery sorts measure has been removed.
- **No alpha in a factor model and nothing net of costs:** Goyal–Saretto 2025 (skewness alphas insignificant; every strategy's net IPCA alpha negative); the Heston et al. Table 2 IV-smirk straddle sort is +0.49 %/month (t 0.78).
- **Regime-dependence:** Félix et al. — overpricing of OTM calls present 1998–2003, absent 2003–2008, weak after 2008; Byun–Kim's effect is itself "stronger during periods of high investor sentiment". No post-2013 replication of the Boyer–Vorkink quintile spreads was found (search exhausted); the 2020–2021 retail wave is covered only by the aggregate P&L papers, which show the losses at ATM.
- **Measurement:** midpoint returns on contracts quoted $0.00 × $0.01–0.05; the Duarte–Jones–Wang upward noise bias is largest here; Amaya et al. show the midpoint-at-last-trade valuation of far-OTM contracts flips the sign of aggregate retail P&L by itself. Transaction costs are not addressed in any of the three lottery papers (Boyer–Vorkink: "Returns reported ignore transaction fees"; Bali–Murray: "options still traded at midquote" per the discussant).
- **Retail counterparty:** the "retail buys the lottery contracts" claim is right for aggregate contract counts (Boyer–Vorkink IA: more *contracts* of open interest in the high-skew bin, but 4 % of the *dollars*) and wrong for where the money and the losses are (Beckmeyer: > 90 % of retail 0DTE volume ATM/slightly OTM; de Silva: ATM; Bryzgalova: slightly OTM). The lottery end of the chain is where retail loses pennies on worthless contracts; the Compass band is where it loses dollars on spread and theta.

## 3. The exclusion rule against the Compass contract rules [LOCAL]

| rule in L4 | what it would do to the contracts Compass actually fills | evidence for the filter |
|---|---|---|
| (a) ask < $0.10 | nothing: S01/S03 require ask ≥ $0.05 and delta 0.30–0.60; the minimum entry ask among 818 S03 fills is $0.24; H14 already handles the short side | the lottery sorts themselves (never buy a $0.00 × $0.05 contract) — already in force |

| (b) top-quintile smirk (OTM-put IV − ATM-call IV) | computable per name per day from the chain; a *stock-return* predictor at 1–6 months (XZZ 2010, L5); as a straddle characteristic insignificant (Heston Table 2); not in Boyer–Vorkink, Byun–Kim or Bali–Murray | belongs to L5; no lottery evidence |
| (c) top-quintile MAX or IVOL of the underlying | removes high-volatility names from a long-option program; Hu–Jacobs: the raw call-return gap is the elasticity of a cheaper option; Garcia-Ares–Muravyev (L3 §2): the delta-hedged IVOL effect is −0.21 %/month (t −1.92) ex-rollover in the broad sample and +0.14 % in S&P 500 names | mechanical for raw returns; ≈ 0 delta-hedged ex-rollover; Byun–Kim's PCP evidence not readable |

Within-band local evidence (the only test that bears on a Compass lane): S02 delta grid on SPY/QQQ/IWM 0DTE 2022-11 → 2026-10 — delta20_40 −$3.76 [−4.60, −2.94] (n 3,059), delta30_50 −$4.25 [−5.50, −2.96], baseline 0.30–0.60 −$4.19 [−5.68, −2.81], delta40_60 −$4.42 [−6.22, −2.69] (n 2,624): the more OTM band is the least bad, every band negative in every year. S03 weeklies 2017-06 → 2026-10 (818 fills; this note's cut of `trades.parquet`): by |delta| tercile low (0.42) +$4.42, mid (0.45) +$0.61, high (0.47) −$1.23 per contract; by premium/spot tercile low (0.35 %) −$0.62, mid (0.67 %) +$2.81, high (1.30 %) +$1.63; the study's whole-sample interval is [−3.69, +6.25], so none of these differences is distinguishable from zero and the sign on delta is opposite to the lottery prediction. The S03 cost share is 52 % of gross (`summary.json cost_share 0.524`) — the loss mechanism is spread and theta on an uninformed entry, as S04/S06 established, not strike selection.

Power for the proposed excluded-vs-retained test, if it were run inside the band: the S03 per-contract P&L SD is ≈ $70 (CI half-width $5 on 818 fills); a one-sided 95 % lower bound above zero on a $2 per-contract difference between two groups needs ≈ 6,600 fills per group [LOCAL arithmetic]; nine years of three ETFs produced 818. Outside the band the test measures the Coval–Shumway moneyness pattern on contracts no Compass rule would buy, on midpoints no marketable order can trade — a replication exercise, not an edge test, and GATE-v2's statistical gate has no lane to apply to.

## 4. Testability with our data and the gate

- Stock EOD (MAX, IVOL) from 2012-06 (UTP) / 2017-01 (CTA), years 2023 → 2026 in the lake, 2022 → 2012 downloading (`02_coverage_matrix.md`; `07_coverage_report.md`); option chains and OI from 2012-06-04; the per-name smirk needs a chain per name per day (the S11 event cache has one 30–45-DTE expiration per event date on 415 roots, 2024 → 2026, closing NBBO only). So the inputs exist or are landing — the data check passes — but there is nothing to apply them to: every Compass lane is closed or zero (S01–S16b), and the rule has no expectancy of its own to gate.
- What would be worth one pass, as a data-quality replication and not as L4: on the S03 and 0DTE caches, tabulate hold-to-expiry returns by moneyness bucket at the 10:00 NBBO *ask* (not the midpoint) to show the Coval–Shumway/Boyer–Vorkink pattern on 2017–2026 index chains at executable prices. It would confirm that the sub-$0.10 wing is unbuyable; it cannot promote anything.

**Decision.** Refuted as an edge — by the hypothesis's own statement and on the evidence. Record L4 as: (a) no new rule — the Compass contract band (delta 0.30–0.60, ask ≥ $0.05) and H14 already exclude the lottery end of the chain; (b) strike rule (a)'s "premium < 1 % of spot" from any future draft, and move the smirk to L5 and MAX/IVOL to L3's single "top-quintile IV − HV" exclusion (which is the one characteristic with ex-rollover content); (c) correct the Amaya et al. citation; (d) no registry id. What would change the verdict: a replication on 2014–2026 OptionMetrics- or ThetaData-grade chains showing that, *within* a 0.30–0.60-delta, 1–7-DTE band and at ask prices, a stock-level lottery characteristic separates contract returns by more than the per-trade noise with a positive day-clustered lower bound — nothing in the literature measures that cell, and the two local cuts that touch it run the other way.
