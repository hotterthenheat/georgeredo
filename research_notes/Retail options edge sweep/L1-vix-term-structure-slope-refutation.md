# L1 — VIX term-structure slope as a timing signal for 30-DTE S&P 500 straddles: evidence-quality refutation (2026-10-14)

Lens: evidence quality (is the cited effect real, replicated, not a data-mining artefact; checked against the primary text). Hypothesis under test: registry-sweep id **L1** as stated in `academic-anomalies.md` §1.2 — "the VIX term-structure slope (second principal component) predicts S&P 500 straddle returns at all maturities, low slope → high subsequent variance-asset returns (Johnson 2017)", to be tested as a monthly SPY/SPXW 30-DTE ATM straddle and ±0.5×expected-move strangle, long and short, 2012-06 → 2026-10, cells by quintile of VIX3M − VIX9D and of VIX minus 21-day realized, fills at the 1-minute NBBO with C1 costs. Proposition to refute: *a retail-accessible option edge that is plausibly positive after costs and testable with our data over a window long enough for our gates.*

Grades: **[PR]** peer-reviewed; **[WP]** working paper; **[EX]** exchange/regulator data; **[PRAC]** practitioner; **[SEC]** secondary summary (not evidence); **[LOCAL]** this repository's measurements or this note's own computation on free public data. Every number from a paper below was read from the primary PDF (converted with `pdftotext` in the session scratchpad) unless marked [SEC]. Local context read first: `research/compass/02_coverage_matrix.md`, `07_coverage_report.md`, `05_results.md`, `registry.jsonl` (46 entries incl. GATE-v2, C1, H9/H11/H13/H15/H16/H17, S15, DATA-1..3), and the sibling notes `academic-anomalies.md`, `execution-costs.md`, `our-evidence-ledger.md`.

## 0. Verdict

**Refuted as stated.** The slope effect Johnson documents is real in his 1996–2013 sample and its *ordering* (low slope → better straddle returns than high slope) reappears on free data for 2012–2026 (§3). But the only cell that would make L1 a long-option result — a long 30-day ATM straddle when the term structure is flat or inverted — is **negative before costs in every specification run here** (−18 % of premium per month [−35, −2] on 35 monthly observations; −9 % [−27, +9] with the straddle priced at 0.9 × VIX; negative in 11 of 13 years). What the slope does sort is the size of the *short-premium carry*, which is (a) outside the Compass long-option scope (`08_port_spec` §5; H15 `scope_note`), (b) a carry lane under GATE-v2 (no timing skill needed for the unconditional short to be positive), (c) naked index-straddle margin with a −7.5 × premium month in the window (2020-02), and (d) exactly the component Dew-Becker & Giglio show lost its alpha after ≈ 2010–2012. The suggested test is also under-powered for the registry's gates: a quintile cell holds 34–35 monthly straddles against a monthly return standard deviation of 76 % of premium, so a cell needs a mean of ≥ 26 % of premium to clear a 95 % lower bound; the 150-trade minimum per cell would take 62 years of history for five quintiles; the forward gate (≥ 7 calendar months) is seven trades. The free-data pre-test in §3 is a sufficient reason not to spend the ThetaData pull on L1 as written. A *different* hypothesis — the slope as a filter on a 30-DTE short-straddle carry lane — is stated in §6 for the owner's scope decision, with its tail.

## 1. The claim as cited versus the claim as published (Johnson, *JFQA* 52(6) 2017 [PR])

Primary: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/risk-premia-and-the-vix-term-structure/56572D1F060448571BD8F597C732D9C3 (text read from the author's copy of the JFQA version, https://www.travislakejohnson.com/pdfs/Johnson%20VIXTS%202017%20(JFQA).pdf; SSRN 2548050).

- **Data and construction.** OptionMetrics SPX options 1996–2013, 4,445 daily observations; VIX²_T for T = 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 12 months computed from the chain; LEVEL/SLOPE/CURVE are the first three principal components of that six-point term structure (Table 1). The straddle test assets are *constant-maturity* ATM straddle strategies (a mixture of the two expirations nearest T, re-formed every day, appendix eq. A-2), i.e. daily-rebalanced portfolios, not a straddle bought and held. Returns are computed from **closing bid–ask midpoints** (footnote 9: "I compute the returns of both strategies using midpoints of the bid–ask spread, which can be substantial in the options market, meaning they overstate returns available to real-time investors"). This is the closing-mid measurement that Goyenko & Zhang (2020 [WP], `academic-anomalies.md` §0.2) show is biased upward by up to 100 bp per day for straddles.
- **Unconditional 1-month straddle (Table 2, Panel C).** Daily mean −0.32 %, SD 5.86 %, annualized Sharpe −0.88; monthly mean −5.29 %, SD 35.74 %, Sharpe −0.51.
- **Next-day returns by SLOPE quintile (Table 5, Panel C), 1-month straddle:** Q1 (low) **+0.44 % per day** (SE 0.19, 5 % level), Q2 −0.38, Q3 −0.61, Q4 −0.43, Q5 (high) −0.65 (SE 0.19); High − Low **−1.09 %** (SE 0.27, 1 % level). The 12-month straddle's Q1 is +0.29 %/day (SE 0.04) — the "30 bps per day" quoted in the introduction. Across all 18 assets the extreme-quintile differences are 29–181 bp per day, 7.4 %–36.4 % per month (abstract).
- **Next-month (Table 6, Panel C), 1-month straddle:** High − Low **−21.13 %** (SE 4.83). Robustness rows for next-day High − Low: crisis (2008–2009) removed −0.94 (0.29); bottom 5 % of SLOPE days removed −0.92 (0.28); **SLOPE replaced by the simple exogenous slope VIX²₁₂ − VIX²₁: −0.57 (0.27)**, "statistically significant but muted"; footnote 6: the simple slope "is −68 % correlated with the LEVEL of the term structure, which is unrelated to variance asset returns".
- **Regressions (Table 4, Panels C and F).** Next-day 1-month straddle on SLOPE: coefficient −37.19 (SE 9.97), SLOPE-only adjusted R² 0.38 %, OOS R² 0.30 %; next-month: −6.97 (1.47), adjusted R² 3.80 %, OOS R² 2.29 %. R² rises with maturity (next-month 12-month straddle 11.99 %).
- **Incremental to the level VRP (Table 7, Panel C).** With VIX² − E[RV²] (implied minus expected variance) as a control, SLOPE keeps −38.88 (11.20) for the 1-month straddle; the VRP control itself is **−33.73 (16.43)**, i.e. a rich implied variance predicts *more negative* straddle returns the next day. This is the opposite of the registry's 0DTE finding (S15: rich implied → larger realized, short loses), and in either direction it favours the short side, never a long.
- **The out-of-sample strategy (Figure 2, §IV.F).** 2000–2013, 3,437 days: short the 30-day straddle every day except when SLOPE is in the bottom quintile of its history, then buy; margin 20 % of the index; outperforms the unconditional short "by a factor of 4.8 over the 14-year window, 11.9 % per year". The author: "A substantial portfolio [sic] of the OOS improvement comes from late 2008." §IV.D: "A limitation of my analysis is the relatively short 1996–2013 sample period featuring a historic financial crisis."
- **What the paper does not do.** No transaction-cost analysis; no post-2013 data; no buy-and-hold (to-expiry) straddle; no test on SPY or on a 9-day/3-month proxy. The headline evidence is next-*day* returns of daily-rebalanced portfolios; a daily rebalance of a 30-day straddle pays a round trip every day, which at the lens's own cost estimate (≈ 2 % of premium per round trip on SPY, `academic-anomalies.md` §1.2; unmeasured at 30 DTE, `execution-costs.md` §12) is five times the Q1 cell's +0.44 %/day gross. Only a hold-to-expiry version (the suggested test) can avoid that, and that version is what §3 measures.

Reading: a real, peer-reviewed, in-sample-and-OOS result on a 17-year sample dominated by 2008, measured at closing mids, with the economically interesting part for a long-option program (the Q1 sign change) carried by a 2.3-standard-error cell and attributed by the author in part to late 2008. The proxy the suggested test would use (VIX3M − VIX9D) is of the "exogenous simple slope" type whose predictability Johnson's own Table 6 shows is about half of SLOPE's; its daily correlation with the VIX level is −0.56 (§3, [LOCAL]), against Johnson's −0.68 for VIX²₁₂ − VIX²₁.

## 2. Has the effect been shown to survive after 2010? No published test on straddles; the unconditional premium it sorts has lost its alpha

- **Dew-Becker & Giglio, "The Decline of the Variance Risk Premium: Evidence from Traded and Synthetic Options", Chicago Fed WP 2025-17, 4 Sep 2025 [WP]** — https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2025/wp2025-17.pdf (read in full). Traded SPX options 1987–2022, synthetic options 1926–2022, monthly rolling strategy. "In the period since 2010, in fact, the alphas of the traded options have converged to zero"; "the overall cumulative return on traded puts is zero between March, 2009 and the end of the sample in December, 2022"; cumulative alphas of delta-hedged puts and straddles "have been approximately zero since 2010" (Fig. A.11); rolling 10-year information ratios of delta-hedged 5 % OTM puts and ATM straddles "converging to zero in 2020 (with a complete convergence for the ATM straddles coming even before Covid)"; break test on dealer net gamma identifies **2012m5**; Table 2: traded-option information ratio **−0.6 before → +0.09 after**, significant; "there is still a premium for variance risk, but … no larger than what would be expected from the CAPM beta". Mechanism: a friction model in which non-dealer investors could not *sell* options; as that friction fell, overpricing fell; dealers' net S&P 500 gamma "shifted from being consistently negative to being zero or positive following the financial crisis". **The paper contains no conditional or timing test** — no term-structure, slope, VRP-timing or regime cut (confirmed by search of the text) — so it neither confirms nor refutes the Q1 sign change; it removes the *unconditional* alpha that Johnson's Q2–Q5 short cells harvested. Its model's mechanism (retail now sells options) is also the reason a slope-sorted short cell should not be expected to carry alpha beyond beta today.
- **Dew-Becker, Giglio, Le & Rodriguez, "The Price of Variance Risk", NBER 21182 / *JFE* 123(2) 2017 [PR]** — https://www.nber.org/papers/w21182.pdf (read). Variance swaps 1996–2014: "The Sharpe ratios are negative for the one- and two-month claims (at −1.4 and −0.5, respectively), but all other Sharpe ratios are insignificantly different from zero"; a pure realized-variance shock is priced at −2.72 annualized; dealer bid–ask "1 to 2 percent for maturities up to 1 year". So the 30-DTE tenor is where the (pre-2014) premium sat — and it is the tenor Dew-Becker & Giglio show converging to CAPM-beta compensation after 2010–2012. The hypothesis's framing ("test the slope at the 30-DTE tenor where the only priced variance is") is correct about the tenor and does not change the post-2010 result.
- **Hu & Jacobs, "Expected and Realized Returns on Volatility", *JFQA* (2026 posting) [PR]** — https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Expected__Realized_Returns_on_Volatility.pdf (read). VIX futures 2004-03-26 → 2022-11-23. "We obtain the slope factor data from Travis Johnson's website. The sample for the slope factor ends in December 2017. … Tables A2 and A3 confirm that the slope factor predicts VIX futures returns and show that this also translates into index return predictability." This is the only post-2013 confirmation found, on **VIX futures (not straddles), through 2017-12, gross**. It does not test straddles, costs, or 2018–2022 for the slope factor.
- **Konstantinidi & Skiadopoulos, "How does the market variance risk premium vary over time?", QMUL WP 732 (2014) / *JBF* 2016 [PR; WP text read]** — https://www.qmul.ac.uk/sef/media/econ/research/workingpapers/2014/items/wp732.pdf. OTC S&P 500 variance-swap quotes "from a major broker dealer", **1996-01-04 → 2009-02-13**, maturities 2 m–2 y; the predictors are economic conditions and trading activity, **not the term-structure slope**; the after-cost claim uses 0.5 volatility points of VS bid–ask (robustness at 5 points) on OTC swaps. Institutional instrument, pre-2010 sample; not evidence for L1.
- **Jones & Wang, "The term structure of equity option implied volatility", USC WP 2012 [WP, "very preliminary"; not read in primary form, search summary only — SEC]**: cross-section of single-name straddles; the summary reports "no time-series predictability in average straddle returns". Vasquez (*JFQA* 2017 [PR]) is the published cross-sectional version (single names, 1996–2007 per the sweep note §3). Neither tests the index time series.
- **CXO Advisory summary of Johnson (2015) [SEC]** — https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/vix-term-structure-slope-and-variance-asset-future-returns/ : "Reported returns are gross, not net"; "Trading strategies based on daily VIX term structure signals may be very volatile." Consistent with the primary; not evidence.
- **Searched for and not found** (two extended and two standard searches: "VIX term structure slope straddle returns out-of-sample 2015–2023", "Johnson 2017 SLOPE replication", "volatility term structure slope straddle predictability post-2010", Jones–Wang/Konstantinidi lookups): no published replication of the slope-sorted *straddle* result on any sample ending after 2013; no after-cost test of it on listed options; the related post-2013 literature is on VIX futures (Yoon, *JFM* 2022; Hu & Jacobs) and on VVIX (2007–2014 sample, gross).

Reading: the effect is peer-reviewed and partially replicated on VIX futures to 2017, but it has never been shown on straddles after 2013, never after costs, and the unconditional premium it sorts is the one documented to have converged to zero at the alpha level around 2012. Default under the brief's rule is refuted=true unless a cheap test says otherwise; §3 is that test.

## 3. Free-data pre-test of the suggested study, 2012-06 → 2026-08 [LOCAL]

**Why it can be run now.** The conditioning variables are free: Cboe publishes the VIX family as daily CSVs with no key (downloaded today, 2026-10-14, from `https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/daily_prices/{VIX,VIX9D,VIX3M,VIX6M,VIX1D}_History.csv` [EX]): VIX 1990-01-02 → 2026-10-13 (9,293 rows), VIX6M 2008-01-02 (4,725), VIX3M 2009-09-18 (4,293), **VIX9D 2011-01-04** (3,967), VIX1D 2022-05-13 (1,108). The same endpoint serves `SPX_History.csv` (daily SPX close 1975-01-02 → 2026-10-13, 13,054 rows). This confirms the sweep note's statement that daily regime variables exist over the whole option-NBBO window and lifts the 2017-01 index floor of `02_coverage_matrix.md` for daily-resolution work.

**Proxy.** For each entry date t: 30-day ATM straddle price P = 0.7979 × (VIX_t/100) × √(30/365) × S_t (Black–Scholes at-the-money straddle at zero rate; VIX is a variance-swap rate and overstates ATM implied volatility, so a 0.9 × VIX sensitivity is shown); payoff at the close 21 sessions later = |S_{t+21} − S_t|; return = payoff/P − 1. Midpoint, no spread, no fees, no 10:30 mark, no early exit, SPX rather than SPY. Entries: the first session on or after the 15th of each month, 2012-06-15 → 2026-08-17 (171 non-overlapping straddles with a 21-session exit), plus every session 2012-06-01 → 2026-09 (3,591 overlapping entries) with calendar-month block bootstrap. Slope = VIX3M − VIX9D at the entry close (the suggested test's variable; VIX3M − VIX as a second definition). 95 % intervals are bootstrap (3,000 draws).

**Slope behaviour, daily 2011-01-04 → 2026-10-13 (3,967 sessions).** Correlation of VIX3M − VIX9D with VIX −0.563 (VIX3M − VIX: −0.556; VIX3M/VIX9D: −0.555). Inverted (VIX3M < VIX9D) on 13.3 % of sessions; since 2012-06, 454 inverted sessions in 73 episodes, 33 of ≥ 5 sessions, the longest 2018-10-10 → 2019-01-03 (44), 2020-02-24 → 04-21 (39), 2025-03-03 → 04-28 (31), 2015-08-20 → 09-30 (20). **Reading of §3.** (i) The slope is informative about the *relative* return of a 30-day straddle in 2012–2026, at a magnitude consistent with Johnson. (ii) It does not create a positive long cell: the flat/inverted-slope straddle loses 9–21 % of premium per month before costs in every definition, because the high-VIX months it selects (mean VIX 23–25) still realize only 0.75–0.81 of implied on average and the few crash months do not offset the many post-spike calm months. (iii) The unconditional short carries +15–24 % of premium per month gross in this window — the beta compensation Dew-Becker & Giglio say remains — with a tail of −7.5 × premium in one month; the slope raises that to +44 % in the top quintile and lowers it to +18 % in the bottom one; the top-quintile improvement over the rest (+25 points, lower bound +7) is at the edge of what 34 months can establish and half its months are 2020–2021.

## 4. Retail accessibility and costs

- **Long straddle:** fully retail-accessible (two marketable orders at the NBBO, $0.70 per contract per side; no margin). It is the side that is negative.
- **Short straddle:** listed and fillable, but it is a naked index straddle: Reg-T margin = the greater of the short put or short call requirement plus the other side's premium (FINRA 4210(f)(2)(E); `execution-costs.md` §7), ≈ $16–17 k per SPY straddle at SPY 777 (a 0.30-delta 30-DTE SPY put alone is $13.8 k), ≈ $100 k+ on SPX; needs the broker's highest options approval; under GATE-v2 tail gates a ±10 %/±15 % shock on a 30-DTE SPY ATM straddle costs ≈ 6–12 % of notional per straddle before the volatility repricing, and the realized 2020-02 month was −7.5 × premium. This is the H15 capital profile (`H15`: $261.6 k / $396.2 k per SPXW lot for a 25 % stress budget), i.e. not institutional-only, but not a small-account edge.
- **Costs are not the binding constraint.** The lens's 2.1 % of premium per round trip on SPY (two legs × two sides × 0.5 % half-spread + $2.80) is an *assumption* at 30 DTE: `execution-costs.md` §12 records that no 30-DTE index chain has been measured and the §9 30-DTE rows (SPY short straddle $3,208 premium, $43.87 round trip, 1.4 %) carry the weekly ATM median. Published SPX ATM relative effective spreads at 28–34 DTE were 2.3 % (Doshi et al. 2025 [WP], 2004–2020; `execution-costs.md` §2.1), i.e. ≈ 1.2 % half-spread, so a SPXW version could cost 2–3× the SPY assumption. Either way, a 1–3 % round trip is small against means of ±20–40 % of premium and a 76 % SD; the test fails on sign (long) and on power/scope (short), not on execution.
- **Institutional versus retail separation.** Johnson's synthetic variance swaps (option portfolios at 1–12 months) and Konstantinidi–Skiadopoulos's OTC swaps are institutional; daily rebalancing of constant-maturity straddles (Johnson's actual test asset) is not retail-viable at any measured spread; the retail-accessible expression is the monthly buy-and-hold of §3.

## 5. Testable with our data, and is the window long enough for the gates?

- **Data exist and the pull is small.** ThetaData option EOD (closing NBBO) and 1-minute NBBO for SPY from 2012-06-04, SPXW from 2012 (expirations listed), option OI from 2012-06-04 (`02_coverage_matrix.md` §1; DATA-3); nothing longer than 7 DTE is cached (`07_coverage_report.md` §1), so the study needs ≈ 172 months × 2–4 contracts × 21 sessions of EOD or 1-minute NBBO — a few hundred contract-months, hours not days. SPY 1-minute bars for trailing realized vol exist from 2012-06-04 (cached from 2017-06). The Cboe CSVs cover the regime variables from 2011-01-04 (VIX9D) with no key. "Testable with our data" is true.
- **The window is not long enough for the gates on conditional cells.** GATE-v2 requires a day-clustered 95 % lower bound > 0 out of sample after multiplicity accounting, plus ≥ 150 sessions *and* ≥ 7 calendar months of forward paper trading; GATE-v1's retained items include ≥ 150 trades. A monthly straddle gives 172 trades in 14.3 years unconditionally and 34–35 per quintile; 150 trades per cell is 12.5 years per cell and 62 years for five; the forward gate is seven straddles. Overlapping daily entries raise n to ≈ 3,600 but not the number of independent 21-session blocks (172), and the protocol's day-clustered interval would understate the overlap — the interval must be clustered by month-block (as in §3). The suggested design (2 structures × 2 sides × 2 sorts × 5 quintiles = 40 cells, plus year tables) has no cell that can clear a Bonferroni-adjusted bound at these n unless its mean exceeds ≈ 35 % of premium; on the proxy no long cell is positive and only Q5-short is of that size.
- **Registry fit.** S15 (0DTE, VRP-level, wrong sign), H17 (VIX-level skips lower the carry mean), H9/H11/H13 (0DTE straddles rejected at measured NBBO) are not reopened: L1 was correct that none of them tested the slope at 30 DTE, and §3 now has. H15 remains the only open carry lane and is forward-only. The Compass scope (`08_port_spec` §5) excludes short premium; the long expression of L1 is closed by §3; nothing in L1 as specified should be registered as an experiment.

## 6. What could be registered instead (not a recommendation; for the owner's scope decision)

If short premium is placed in scope, a pre-registered **carry-lane** hypothesis, distinct from L1: "30-DTE SPX/SPY ATM short straddle (or ±0.5×EM strangle) opened monthly at 10:30, held to settlement/21 sessions, only when VIX3M − VIX9D is in its top quintile of the trailing history (steep contango), skip FOMC/month-end per H17". Expected from §3: +44 % of premium per month gross in Q5 (≈ +$1,100 per SPY straddle; ≈ 1.4 % of notional per month), +42 % after the assumed 2.1 %, worst month −46 %, against an unconditional short of +24 % with a −750 % month; the conditional improvement +25 points [+7, +45] rests on 34 months, 16 of them in 2020–2021, with a single 2023 loss of −46 % and Q4's −112 % (2017-12) showing what a one-quintile shift does. It must be labelled carry (it needs no timing skill to be positive), priced at the measured 30-DTE NBBO (unmeasured today — one pass over the pulled chains), stressed under GATE-v2 (±10 %/±15 % and the 2020-02 path), and judged against Dew-Becker & Giglio's point that the remaining premium is beta, not alpha: a short straddle's CAPM beta is large and negative in down months, so a positive mean is not evidence of an edge over a short-market position of the same beta. The long-straddle and long-strangle cells of L1 are closed by §3 and should not be run.
