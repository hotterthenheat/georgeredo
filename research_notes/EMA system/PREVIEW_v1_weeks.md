# EMA system — PREVIEW of v1 on three drawn weeks (up, flat, down)

**PREVIEW — 15 sessions, no verdict.** RULEBOOK_v1.md (commit f171654, committed alone before any v1 signal, trade or
P&L and before the weeks were drawn) run as written, on three weeks drawn by its §4 rule. Fifteen sessions in three
hand-labelled regimes cannot separate a rule from luck; nothing here is a finding and nothing is selected from it.

Labels: **OBSERVED** = read off the data (bars, NBBO, open interest, volume); **CALCULATED** = arithmetic of observed (P&L,
fees, half-spreads, R); **MODELED** = the Black-Scholes delta used to choose the contract. All P&L is bought at the ask,
sold at the bid, fees each way ($0.68748 SPY/QQQ/IWM; SPXW $1.18748 under $1, $1.27748 at $1 and up).

### 1.3 A coding fix found by the verifier, before any result was read (disclosed)
RULEBOOK_v1 §1.1 says "no new entries at or after 13:30" and "entry minutes m = 15..299 (09:45..13:29)". The minute index
is an arithmetic slip: 13:29 is m = 239 (13:30 is 240); m = 299 is 14:29. The first run coded m ≤ 299, so it admitted
entries 13:30–14:29 — after the 14:00 flat, which the engine then "closed" at minute 270, before they were opened. The
plain-loop verifier flagged it (4 differences of 32; trades entered at 13:30–14:28). **That run was discarded: its tables
were written to disk and never opened.** The code now follows the rule's stated clock times (entries 09:45–13:29, m ≤ 239;
an assert refuses an entry at or after the flat), the verifier and the unit test check the same, and the rerun is the one
reported, with 0 differences. RULEBOOK_v1.md is left as committed; this paragraph is the erratum. Nothing else changed
between the two runs.

## 12. Next-version ideas (from these results — untested, not part of v1)

**PREVIEW — 15 sessions, no verdict.** None of these was tried; each would be written into a v2 rulebook before any run.
- **Size the S&P venue by notional, not by contract.** One SPXW contract is ~10 SPY contracts; with VN1 sending ~85% of
  S&P trades to SPXW, the S&P stream's dollars swamp QQQ and IWM. A v2 could trade 1 SPXW against 10 SPY (or report per
  $ of premium only) so the venue rule changes cost, not exposure.
- **A and B collapse to one rule** on these weeks; a v2 could drop one, or make B differ in a way that matters.
- **V-chop** helped on this draw only because the chop days fell in the two losing weeks; worth a proper test on a long
  development span, not a promotion.
- **The direction filter, not the costs, decides the sign.** Fees (~0.4% of premium) and half-spreads (~1.4%) are small
  next to the weekly swings (−29% to +29% of premium); the random baseline with the same minutes and sides lands near zero.
  What the filter adds over random has to be measured over hundreds of sessions in a development span with a held-out
  check, gated on the random baseline per regime, before anything else is tuned.
- **The 14:00 flat ends 2–8 trades a row**; the cut at 13:30 leaves late entries 30 minutes at most — a v2 could stop
  entries earlier (e.g. 13:00) so a trade has room to reach its target, if a development span supports it.

## 13. Caveats

- Fifteen sessions, three weeks chosen by SPY's return alone: a week's sign is mostly the week. The bootstrap is iid over
  15 sessions from three different regimes, printed to show width, not to test.
- The up week barely clears +1.5%; the flat week is flat for SPY and SPX but QQQ fell 1.96% and IWM rose 2.06%.
- SPXW contracts are priced off the SPX print stamped at the entry minute (one print a minute); the stop and targets are
  SPY's, so an SPXW trade's exit can lag or lead the index slightly (the basis is not modelled — the exit is SPY's minute).
- X4 is 10 contracts, so its dollars are ~10× the other exits'.
- §1.3's fix is disclosed; RULEBOOK_v1.md stays as committed.
