# Compass Audit Finds Frictions, Not Edges

Not one rule in the sixteen-module Compass specification that picks a direction or opens a trade survives out of sample once a contract is bought at the ask, sold at the bid and charged **$0.65 each way** — on 2022–2024 (fit) and 2025–2026 (check) alike, across ThetaData's 1-minute NBBO of every same-day SPY, QQQ, IWM and SPXW contract (**980 / 980 / 621 / 1,106 sessions**), one year of Unusual Whales dealer books (2025-10-15 → 2026-10-13) and daily bars on 23 liquid names (2020/2022 → 2026-10-13). What the data does support is narrower and cheaper than the spec: the **15:58 flat on SPY/QQQ/IWM** (riding the nearest in-the-money contract loses $7–22 a contract, CI clear of zero every year), the **−32 % premium backstop** (inert inside the stack, the best stand-alone level), the **breakeven lock when a T1 target is on** (change its activation from +6 % to +0 %: +1.0 % of debit OOS, CI clear on the sweep universe), a **trailed remainder on the scale-out** (+0.7–1.0 %, CIs clear), the **Method A flip** and the **three-book slicing**, the **r,q pricer with r − q read off put-call parity**, a **net/gross confidence** in place of the hard sign gate, the **MOC magnitude score** as a volatility-of-the-close gauge (ρ +0.16–0.21 with |move| in both periods on every name) and an **ATR > 1.5 block on counter-trend fades** (hit rate 30–44 % against 47–53 %). Everything that assigns a direction — the nine-window chase/fade/pin table, the charm multipliers, the sweep fade, the FVG/OB/OTE geometry, the grade score, the DEX/VEX/CEX drift rules, the straddle-boundary fade, the king, the sweep-premium gate, the swing four-boolean gate — is either neutral, negative after fees or untestable, and several of the spec's "proven" claims run the wrong way (the sweep continues, not reverses; UW's greeks are r = 0, not r,q; the ITM distortion is in puts, not calls; the −15.2 % ledger figure is an average realised loss, not a stop). Six blockers cap what any further audit can say: no OPRA print tape, sweep flags or dark-pool history on disk; no strike-level open-interest history before October 2026; one year of book-level dealer greeks; no multi-day option paths; the ThetaData API not called in the owner's terminal hours; and the stored UW token answering 401. The honest build is boolean gates documented as not positive EV, frictions kept, telemetry recorded daily until a year of flow and strike-level history exists.

## One data set, one split, six blockers that cap what can be said

Every number in this report comes from a script under `research_notes/Compass engine audit/` and its `out/` folder; nothing was called from a vendor API, and no key appears in any file. The conventions are uniform across the six groups: minute t is New York's hour × 60 + minute; a 1-minute bar stamped t closes at t + 1; a chain NBBO row stamped t is the quote at t; half days are dropped; a contract is bought at the ask and sold at the bid, **$0.65 a contract each way, no fee on a worthless expiry**; fit (pick, train, "disc") is 2022–2024, out-of-sample (check, OOS) is 2025-01-01 → 2026-10-09/13, and nothing in the check period was read while a definition or threshold was chosen; the stress test on every headline is a session bootstrap (1,000–2,000 draws), the mean without the best five sessions ("wo5") and the mean by year ([clock_session.md](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session.md), [sweep_trigger.md](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger.md), [exits_sizing.md](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing.md)). Two groups re-derived random samples of their trades from the raw files with separate plain-loop code: 240 sweep events with one difference (an entry at 15:58 with no minute left to sell, excluded), and 80 exit paths with none after a real bug was fixed (float32 storage read a bid of 1.05 as 1.04999995 and missed a 1.05 threshold; prices are now rounded to four decimals on load) ([verify.py, sweep](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/verify.py); [verify.py, exits](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/verify.py)). Percent-of-premium is the cross-product yardstick wherever SPXW (10× the ETFs' notional) is pooled with the ETFs.

The blockers are plain and they bound the verdicts. **No OPRA tape, sweep flag, trade condition or dark-pool print is on disk**; the local per-contract bars carry volume and OHLC within ±2 % of the 09:31 price, so a "sweep" cannot be told from any other print and Module 11 is untestable as written. **No strike-level open interest or gamma history exists before 2026-10** (the UW levels file has 1,521 rows with every wall column empty; the house's recorder began in October 2026), so the gross pin, the gamma walls, "positive gamma between walls" and the swing engine's B4 are untestable. **UW's book-level greek-exposure covers one year** (250 rows a name), so every book split is read once on the same year and is descriptive, not a train/check result. **No multi-day option price paths are on disk** (same-day chains plus five October-2026 EOD sessions), so every Weekly/Swing/LEAPS option figure is a Black-Scholes model on the underlying's path, not a backtest. **The ThetaData API was not called** (the owner's terminal is in use 13:00–21:00 UTC), so the spec's named greek benchmark at 7/14/30/180 DTE and any daily option NBBO history are blocked, and the bulk EOD files on disk carry OI but no greeks. **The one UW token on disk answered 401 Unauthorized** to three GET calls on 2026-10-21, so whether UW's live chain endpoint is r = 0 like its history could not be settled ([greeks_books.md](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books.md)). Two smaller gaps recur: SPXW's sweeps and levels are detected on SPY's bars (SPX has one print a minute, no bar high/low), and the SPXW settlement is approximated by the 16:00 print (the official series is not on disk; about $0.50 of precision).

## Modules 1 and 10: the clock routes nothing, but the 15:58 flat and the MOC magnitude hold

The nine-window chase/fade/pin table does not hold on the underlying. Four pre-registered measures were read on SPY, QQQ and IWM 1-minute bars pooled (fit 2,218 window-sessions a window, OOS 1,309, context 2012/2017–2021 4,777): a continuation share (the window's first third against its remainder, null 0.5), the lag-1 autocorrelation of 5-minute returns, an efficiency ratio scaled so a random walk reads 1.0, and the share of sessions whose window reverses the one before. On 2022–2024 **every window reads neutral** by the rule fixed before reading; on 2025–2026 only the power hour reads chase (continuation 0.562 [0.524, 0.598]) and it was 0.490 on 2022–2024 and 0.486 in context, so it is not a property of the clock. The dealer hour the spec calls chase leans mean-reverting out of sample (efficiency 0.940 [0.882, 0.999]; QQQ continuation 0.418) and in context (ac5 −0.043). None of the three "reversal-prone" windows reverses the one before (0.48–0.51, CIs through 0.5). The one robust feature is that the **opening drive trends more than a random walk** (efficiency 1.137 fit, 1.133 OOS, the only positive ac5 in context), yet its first third does not predict its remainder (0.508 / 0.499), so no entry follows from it ([t1_window_summary.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session/out/t1_window_summary.csv)). The replacement table is every window neutral, membership a boolean flag with no direction attached, the "block lunch unless fading to the gross pin in positive gamma" clause untestable for want of strike-level OI, and pre-market and after-hours untestable for want of bars. The charm multipliers 1.0 → 3.5 are a monotone function of the clock with no window behaviour behind them and should scale nothing that sets a trade.

The owner's task (a), charm_pressure × GEX sign in 795–958, is answered no. With |charm| scaled by its trailing-20 median and × 3.0 at the 15:00 entry, the spec's "> 2.4×" is true on 57–71 % of sessions, so it only removes unusually quiet-charm days. Trend trades (the side of the 14:00 → 15:00 move, nearest OTM same-day strike, 15:00 ask to 15:58 bid, fees in) won **23 % above the threshold against 26 % below** pooled over the four names, and lower above it on three of four names (SPY 20 vs 29 %, QQQ 26 vs 30 %, IWM 17 vs 23 %, SPXW 27 vs 22 %). Every power-hour long-premium trade loses on 2022–2024 with CIs clear of zero (trend −27.7 [−48.5, −4.9], fade −32.2, pin −38.6 $ a contract, n ≈ 1,887; −18 to −24 % of premium) and trend still loses on 2025–2026 (−19.5 % of premium [−31.5, −6.9], n ≈ 1,759); the fade and pin cells that turn to +$2–28 in the book year or under GEX < 0 (n 614) have CIs through zero and are negative without their best five days (−19 to −48). The one cell that looks best — fade or pin under negative gamma and high charm — is a 2×2 read on one year of book with 95–236 trades a cell and needs a second year before it is anything ([t3_summary.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session/out/t3_summary.csv)). Keep charm_pressure and the GEX sign as display fields, never as gates. A note on the book itself: in UW's sign net gamma is positive on 74 % of SPX sessions but on 32 % of SPY's, 36 % of QQQ's and 7 % of IWM's, and net charm is positive on 76 % of SPY sessions against 21 % of SPX's — two products on the same index disagree most days, so a GEX_book sign from this feed is not a stable regime label.

The 10:00 inside-opening-range rule is vacuous as written: the 10:00 spot is the 09:59 close, one of the thirty bars that define the range, so it is inside on **99.6–99.8 % of ETF sessions** (SPXW 91–93 %). The three ways in it leaves — first breakout, sweep-and-return, post-sweep breakout — were traded with real quotes (nearest strictly OTM strike, the ask one minute after the signal bar, 50 % trailing stop or hold to 15:58). No way in has a positive OOS expectation whose CI excludes zero; the only CI that does is the sweep-and-return fade held to the close, and it is negative (−12.3 % of premium [−23.5, −0.7] pooled over 1,537 OOS trades, −14.9 % on 1,644 fit trades); every "without the best five days" figure is negative for every name and way in, and waiting for the sweep then taking the breakout (−$8.5 pooled OOS, n 1,516) is not better than the first breakout (−$30.5, n 1,645) ([t2_summary.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session/out/t2_summary.csv)). Drop the check; an opening-range breakout is a boolean event flag with no EV claim.

The settlement rule splits by product. On SPY and QQQ the 15:58 bid of the nearest in-the-money contract stands above its eventual intrinsic by $0.07–0.22 on average (it still carries two minutes of time value; the contract ends worthless on 11–18 % of sessions), so **riding loses $7.4 [−8.8, −6.1] fit and $14.8 [−17.1, −12.6] OOS on SPY** (n 1,060 / 882), $10.0 and $21.5 on QQQ, $1–2 on IWM, negative in every year and growing: keep the flat rule. On SPXW the nearest ITM contract's bid sits below intrinsic on average (+$21.2 fit, +$16.3 [+5.0, +27.5] OOS, n 1,324 / 888) but the median ride is −$28 to −$42, riding loses on 55–56 % of contracts and the 5th percentile is −$390: a variance trade, not an edge. "Delta-aligned" separates nothing (aligned +14.9 [−14.1, +43.3] against not aligned +17.6 [−11.1, +48.8] OOS), so drop that condition and keep a boolean "may ride" for SPXW, never a "should ride". By moneyness a deeper ITM contract (intrinsic 0.2–1 % of spot) rides positive on every name (+$1 to +$14 on the ETFs, +$37 to +$73 on SPXW), so the flat rule's exception is the first strike only ([t4_summary.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session/out/t4_summary.csv), [t4_by_moneyness.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session/out/t4_by_moneyness.csv)).

Module 10's normalization, the owner's task (b), is defined exactly and deterministically. For a name, an input x and a checkpoint t ∈ {905, 910, …, 950}, mean₂₀ and sd₂₀ are the mean and standard deviation of x at that minute over the twenty sessions strictly before the day (at least fifteen present, else no score):

```
x_vol   = ln(1 + volume_{15:00→t})               x_range = ln(1e-6 + (high − low)_{15:00→t} / close_{14:59})
x_move  = (close_{t−1} − close_{14:59}) / close_{14:59}      x_sweep = (call_bought − put_bought)/(call_bought + put_bought)_{15:00→t}
z_i     = clip((x_i − mean₂₀(x_i,t)) / sd₂₀(x_i,t), −4, +4)       σ(z) = 1/(1 + e^{−z})       u_i = 2σ(z_i) − 1
moc_score (signed, unit weights) = u_move + u_sweep + sign(x_move)·(u_vol + u_range)        ∈ (−4, 4)
moc_mag  (magnitude)             = [σ(z_vol) + σ(z_range) + σ(z_|move|) + σ(z_|sweep|)] / 4  ∈ (0, 1)
```

The closing-cross imbalance (NYSE/Nasdaq messages from 15:50) is not on disk and enters as the fifth unit-weight term the day a feed exists. What the score predicts is the size of the last ten minutes' move, not its direction: at t = 950 against the 15:49 → close move (pooled n 1,836 fit, 1,767 OOS) the **magnitude score's Spearman ρ with |move| is +0.213 fit and +0.157 OOS**, +0.14 to +0.25 on every name in both periods, the top quintile moving 17–18 bp against 9–10 bp in the bottom, carried by the late-day volume and range norms; the **signed score's ρ is +0.069 fit and −0.010 OOS**, sign hit rate 48.7 % (47.1 % when |score| > 1), and the +1.2 bp of fit became −0.4 bp ([t5_predictive.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session/out/t5_predictive.csv)). The power-hour buy rule with real 0DTE quotes (first checkpoint beyond the pre-15:00 high/low, nearest OTM, out at 15:58 or on a 50 % trail) loses **19–26 % of premium a trade OOS** with or without its sweep and score gates (full rule −19.1 % [−38, +1] held, n 427; the gates move the pooled mean by +$0.7 to +$1.5, inside the noise) ([t5_rule_summary.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/clock_session/out/t5_rule_summary.csv)). Implement the normalization as written, keep moc_mag as an "expect a move" gauge for sizing, drop the directional use and keep the long-premium power-hour rule out of the engine; the one direction the data points to — selling the close's optionality when moc_mag is low — was not tested and is a new hypothesis for the owner's go-ahead. The literature is about the underlying's last half hour, not about buying a same-day option into it ([Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens 2021](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Gao, Han, Li, Zhou 2018](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)).

## Modules 2 and 9: the sweep has no direction, the geometry no lift, the score no rank

The formal sweep definition the owner asked for is exact, has no look-ahead, and is what every test below used. A level L is one of ORH/ORL (the 09:30–09:59 bars, valid from bar 31), PDH/PDL (the prior session's regular-hours extreme), PWH/PWL (the prior ISO week's) or SH/SL (the running session extreme, counted only once it has stood ten bars); the parameters are X (bps beyond the level) and k (bars allowed for the close back inside):

```python
def sweep_high(bars, L, e, X=0, k=1):          # bars indexed by the minute they CLOSE
    thr = L * (1 + X / 1e4)
    if not (bars.high[e] >= thr and bars.close[e-1] < L and bars.high[e-1] < thr):   # beyond the line, from inside
        return None
    for r in range(e, e + k):                                                        # close back inside within k bars
        if bars.close[r] < L: return r                                               # r = the entry minute
    return None
# sweep_low mirrors it. Fade: PUT after a high-side sweep, CALL after a low-side one; entry at the ask stamped r,
# nearest strike at or beyond spot with ask > 0, bid > 0, bid <= ask, (ask − bid)/mid <= 0.05.
```

Across **160 definitions (4 levels × 5 excursions × 4 windows × 2 confirmations), 225,390 event rows**, the underlying's forward return in the fade direction is between +0.1 and −1.8 bps at 30 minutes in both periods (48–50 % positive; the share moving ≥ 10 bps in the fade direction within 30 minutes is 24–28 % at every level), so the option's result is the option's cost. The merged trigger's fade (X = 0, k = 1, nearest OTM, 50 % trailing stop) loses **SPY −6.1 % [−11.4, 0.0] of premium on 2022–2024 and −8.0 % [−13.2, −2.5] on 2025–2026; QQQ −12.6 / −11.5 %; IWM −18.6 / −13.7 %; SPXW −4.5 / −5.1 %**, win rates 0.18–0.20; the +25 %/−32 % exit and the hold to 15:58 are worse everywhere ([grid.log](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/logs/grid.log), [underlying_fwd.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/out/underlying_fwd.csv)). The definition chosen on 2022–2024 by the largest dollar mean with ≥ 500 events (PD, X = 2, k = 3, confirmed: +$27.2 on 621 events, already failing its own stress test at −$44 without the best five days) read **−$56.4 on 565 events in 2025–2026**, −19.7 % of premium; of the ten best fit definitions five flipped sign and the four that stayed positive in dollars are −1.9 → +0.6 %, −1.7 → +1.8 %, −1.2 → −2.3 % and −3.8 → −6.9 % of premium with every range through zero and every one negative without its best five days ([extra_stress.log](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/logs/extra_stress.log)). A sweep fade on SPY is indistinguishable from buying a near-the-money 0DTE at a fixed time (−8.7 % with a 20 % trail in this repository's step 4). The published mechanism runs the other way: stop clusters beyond round numbers produce cascades through the level ([Osler, NY Fed SR 150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)), and the one controlled fair-value-gap test found a real-minus-placebo difference under one percentage point ([TradingView FVG placebo study](https://fr.tradingview.com/scripts/backtest/)).

The exclusions the spec calls "proven negative EV" are true but not discriminating: what they keep loses as much as what they exclude. Displacement-only continuation (9,023 / 8,146 bars) loses −10.2 / −10.8 % of premium against the sweeps' −8.1 / −9.4 %; ORH sweeps within 0.2 % of PDH/PDL lose less than the rest in both periods (−4.6 % against −7.5 % and −10.3 %) — the only exclusion with a stable ordering — but its OOS +$70 rests on five SPXW days (−$33 without them); "block QQQ" ranks correctly (−12.6 / −11.5 %) but IWM, which the spec approves, is the worst product (−18.6 / −13.7 %); the direction-by-window rule fails in every window (fades lose in the fade windows, continuations in the chase windows, the underlying within ±3.4 bps in every cell); and by UW's net gamma sign (245 sessions, 3,774 events, one year) the fade is −9.6 % on positive days against −9.3 % on negative ([exclusions.log](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/logs/exclusions.log), [direction_by_window.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/out/direction_by_window.csv)). Module 9's geometry was implemented exactly (FVG: a three-candle gap in the fade direction; order block: the last opposite-colour candle in [r − 5, r − 1], body, a doji takes its full range; OTE: the first bar reaching 0.62–0.705 of the displacement leg measured back toward the sweep extreme, leg ≥ 1 bp, voided through the extreme, 30 bars) on 16,980 (k = 1) and 25,028 (k = 3) episodes. The OTE fills 55–63 % of the time and loses −8.9 / −10.4 % against the sweep close's −7.9 / −9.4 %; the order block fills 59–68 % and loses −8.1 / −10.1 %; **an order block is present on 99.7 % of sweeps, so it is not a condition**; an FVG is present on 4.5 % and marks events whose underlying continues against the fade more (−1.0 / −5.7 bps against −0.4), with win rates 0.199 / 0.185 against 0.193 / 0.193; the confirmation bar's apparent OOS lift (+$6.1) is a selection effect, since the close entry one bar earlier on the same episodes made +$16.1 / +$35.5 ([geometry.log](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/logs/geometry.log)). Draw the three as objects, never as entry conditions; "triple confirmation upgrades an A setup" is rejected.

The score cannot rank what has no order. A logistic regression on the spec's features (depth, bars outside, level type, minute, window, OR position, relative volume, VIX, strike distance in straddle units, straddle/spot, bar range, FVG, OB, day's move in ATR, spread, ask, product, side) fitted on 7,989 fit events and scored once on 6,928 check events has **AUC 0.563 fit and 0.528 check** for "the fade trade nets > 0" (base rate 0.19); a gradient-boosted model goes 0.773 → 0.513. The top decile by the fit threshold makes +$64 on 465 check trades, 95 % [−44, 217], −$39 without the best five days, 38 of 164 sessions up, SPXW +$718 on 44 trades and QQQ −$83 ([score_summary.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/out/score_summary.csv)). The one thing a model does learn is volatility: "the underlying moves ≥ 10 bps within 30 minutes" scores AUC 0.68 / 0.71, driven by straddle/spot, VIX, relative volume and depth, and its trade dollars by decile are unordered. There is no empirical win probability for a 0–100 grade to reflect; removing SWEEP_FLOOR, BONUS_CAP and CONFIDENCE_CAP is right (invented weights) and replacing them with a fitted score is not — leave boolean gates and document them as not positive EV. What survives are frictions: quote hygiene (0.1–0.2 % of sweeps had no clean candidate), "missing score blocks the trade" (a guard), and spread/mid ≤ 5 %, which binds hard — it rejects 30–44 % of IWM picks at sweep minutes and most picks after 15:30 everywhere (SPY's pass share falls 0.999 at 09:30 → 0.478 at 15:30; IWM 0.945 → 0.042), and the rejected candidates lose more of their premium (SPY −28.5 / −24.7 % against −4.1 / −7.2 %) ([spread_by_halfhour.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/sweep_trigger/out/spread_by_halfhour.csv)). Add "no eligible contract → no trade" as an explicit outcome. The 0.30–0.45 delta pick is unavailable 36–78 % of the time after 15:30 and 59 % on IWM 13:45–15:30, and its fade is as negative as the nearest OTM's (−5.5 / −7.4 % pooled). The product ordering the data supports is SPXW ≈ SPY > QQQ > IWM, an ordering of losses.

## Modules 3 and 4: exits shape the loss, the lock at +0 % beats +6 %, the breaker is a coin flip

The exit engine was replayed on two 0DTE universes with every trade's full minute path stored: **A, the sweep fade (3,433 trades, 1,826 fit / 1,607 check, 1,034 sessions, median ask $1.25, median spread 1.4 % of the ask)** and **B, one random minute a session in 10:00–15:00 with a random side (3,672 trades, 1,900 / 1,772, median ask $0.90)**; a stop, trail, time stop or flat sells at the bid of the minute it first triggers, a target fills at its price, hard flat at 15:58, fees in ([summary.json](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/summary.json)). No exit configuration turned either universe positive in either period. The spec stack as written loses **−4.3 / −4.5 % of the debit on A and −6.3 / −6.0 % on B** (fit / check; win 25.6 % / 31.4 %; every bootstrap interval below zero; 100 of 429 and 123 of 444 check sessions up); the three-contract variant is 0.6–1.0 points better ([t0_spec_stack_A.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t0_spec_stack_A.csv)).

The owner's task (a) resolves the −15.2 % arithmetic and settles the stop question. In the replayed stack the −32 % backstop is the exit on **0.7 % of A trades and 6.3 % of B trades**; the structural stop takes 58.8 % of A (at −11.3 %), the breakeven lock 11.9 % (−4.7 %), the time stop 7.4 % (−5.7 %), and the average losing trade is −10.5 to −16.3 % by universe and −10 to −23 % by product — exactly where **0.886 / 5.83 = 0.152** sits if lossPercent is the average over losing trades. There is no hidden −15 % stop, and the backstop level inside the stack changes nothing (−15, −20, −25, −32, −50 % give −4.5 % on A to the second decimal; paired −20 vs −32 +0.05 % [−0.01, +0.11]). Stand-alone, −32 % is the best premium level OOS in both universes (A −2.6 %, B −0.6 % against −4.5 / −2.3 % at −15 %), the tighter levels fire on bid-ask noise (**25.5 % of −15 % stops within three minutes of entry against 2.4 % at −32 %**) and every level realises 3–5 points past its nominal because the first bid at or below it is already through. A −15/−20 % underlying-triggered stop fires on 77 / 66 % of random entries at −38 / −49 % realised and does not beat the premium stop (paired −20 vs −32, flat base: A −1.9 % [−7.8, +2.6] OOS). The structural stop at the swept level fires on 90–96 % of sweep fades — the underlying re-tests the level almost always — and sets the loss distribution without moving EV (paired ±0.6 % inside the stack) ([t1_stops_A.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t1_stops_A.csv), [contrasts.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/contrasts.csv)). Keep −32 %, never widened; keep the structural stop with a 0–2 bp buffer (5 bps is worse in fit and no better in check).

Task (b), the +6 % high-bid lock, is the one exit change that passes the house rule. The lock is set on 83.5 % of trades and, with a T1 +25 % target on, sells 46.8 % of those; of the trades it sells **64 % would have reached +25 % and 52 % +45 %** — a real choke, but not a bid-ask-bounce artefact: with a 1.4–2 % spread a bid that has been +6 % and returns to the entry ask has moved three to four spreads, and the choke share is flat from +0 % to +12 % (62.5–63.4 %), so a higher threshold sells fewer trades, not better ones. With a target in the rule, lower is monotonically better: **+6 % beats none by +2.0 % [+0.7, +3.3] (A) and +1.0 % [+0.07, +1.9] (B) OOS; +12 % loses to +6 % by −1.2 % [−1.9, −0.4] and −0.6 % [−1.2, −0.0]; +0 % beats +6 % by +1.0 % [+0.1, +1.8] on A (+0.6 % without the best five days) and +0.3 % [−0.2, +0.9] on B**. Without a target the lock's sign flips between periods (+4.1 % then −2.0 %) ([t2_belock_A.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t2_belock_A.csv), [contrasts.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/contrasts.csv)). Exact replacement: keep the lock only while a T1 target is on, and change its activation from +6 % to +0 % — once the bid has traded at or above the entry ask, sell at the first bid at or below the entry ask. It is a smaller loss, not a profit.

The rest of the stack is parameter noise or arithmetic. The VIX-tiered trail (6/8/11/14 %) is indistinguishable from a flat 10 % trail from the peak once +6 % (paired A −0.05 % [−0.7, +0.7] fit, +0.3 % [−0.2, +0.9] check; B −0.1 / +0.1 %), within each tier the tier's own rate never beats the best flat rate in both periods, and where VIX is 20–30 or above 30 a tighter 6 % trail does better in 2025–2026 — the opposite of the spec's widening; a trail of any kind against none flips sign between periods (+3.2 % then −1.7 %). The whole spec stack against "−32 % stop plus a flat 10 % trail from +6 %" is −0.06 / −0.6 % on A and **−2.3 % [−3.8, −0.9] on B OOS** ([t3_trail_A.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t3_trail_A.csv)). Change to the flat trail as a simplification with no EV claim. The 5-minute time stop fires on 47 % of trades and sells them at −13 % where holding would have averaged −12 to −13 %; 10, 15, 30 and 60 minutes are the same within noise: keep or drop at taste. The targets fail their own break-even: **T1 +25 % all-out needs a 60.8–62.8 % win rate against its −37 % realised loss and gets 46.8–49.4 %**, so it loses 8–10 % of debit in both periods and is worse than no target OOS (B −7.7 % [−14.2, −2.0]); T2 at +45–50 % needs 43–46 % and gets 34–37 %. A scale-out that keeps a trailed leg beats the one-contract T1 exit by **+0.6–0.7 % (A) and +0.7–1.0 % (B), CIs clear of zero**, and 1@+25 + 1@+50 with no trailed leg is as bad as all-out; two, three or four contracts are immaterial (−5.4 to −5.6 %); every target leg had bid_size ≥ 1 at its exit minute ([t5_scaleout_A.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t5_scaleout_A.csv)). If a target is wanted: one contract at +25 %, the rest trailed 10 % from the peak once +6 %, no T2.

The size cap and the breaker are ledger outcomes. The displayed NBBO covers 2 contracts on 95–96 % of trades at both entry and exit, 3 on 92–94 %, 5 on 89–92 %, 10 on 80–85 % (SPXW thinnest, 84 % at 5), and per-contract EV by quoted size is non-monotonic; the only size effect visible runs the other way (the largest quoted sizes sit on the cheapest contracts, where $1.30 of fees is 2.3 % of a $0.56 debit). "≥ 5 contracts had negative EV" is a losing engine on its largest tickets unless the vendor's size was set by something that itself predicts the result, which this data cannot see; size by the dollar risk budget, never by a count read off the ledger ([t6_liquidity_A.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t6_liquidity_A.csv)). The rolling 30-trade profit-factor breaker (< 1.05 → paper) keeps a losing stream in paper 66–100 % of the time per product but **raises live-only EV in only 1 of 8 product × universe cells in 2025–2026** (A SPY −3.7 % all against −8.2 % live-only; IWM −7.6 against −16.3 %; pooled B −6.0 against −5.0 %), and the trades it skips win as often as the ones it takes (22–36 % against 25–37 %). Its sampling noise is the finding: on 2,000 streams of 200 i.i.d. trades shaped like the spec stack's outcomes and shifted to a true EV of +5 % of debit, **the breaker trips in 49 % (A) to 85 % (B) of runs**, median first trip at trade 71–99; on the flat-exit shape (13 % wins, +240 % average win) it trips in ~100 % of runs at +10 % and 95–98 % at +20 % ([t7_breaker_noise_A.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t7_breaker_noise_A.csv)). Do not use it as a lane-quality test; if a governor is wanted, state a drawdown budget in dollars with a re-entry rule, and recompute the noise table for the actual lane's win-rate shape before any threshold is chosen.

The Weekly/Swing/LEAPS exits are a blocker on this data, and the arithmetic says why they will misbehave: by Black-Scholes at IV 20 %, r 4 %, **a −20 % premium stop on a 7-DTE ATM option is a 0.47 % adverse move at entry, 0.20 % after one trading day, and is tripped by time decay alone by the third day**; at 14 DTE 0.67 / 0.48 / 0.08 %; at 30 DTE 0.98 / 0.85 / 0.58 %; at 90 DTE 1.70 %; a −25 % stop on a 365-DTE LEAPS is a 4.3 % move; a 4–5 vol-point IV drop trips −20 % at entry at every tenor to 90 DTE. The spec's 2.4–4.6 % underlying level stops cannot bind at 4–14 DTE; the premium stop fires first ([t8_bs_drag.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/exits_sizing/out/t8_bs_drag.csv)).

## Modules 5 and 6: the pricer stands, the vendor benchmark is r = 0, dealer signs predict nothing

Module 5's pricer is sound — delta, theta, charm, vanna and rho agree with finite differences to 1e-7 and put-call parity to 1e-12 — and 60 bisections on [0.01, 3.00] give 2⁻⁶⁰ of the bracket, which never bound on these chains; r = q = 0 on 0DTE is immaterial (rτ ≤ 0.011 % of K); the 0.01 skip rule is benign (on 980 SPY sessions and 8.6 million contract-minutes within ±1 % of spot it removes 4.7 % of rows in the first half hour and 32.5 % in the last, mostly rows with mid ≤ intrinsic that no threshold can fix, and costs ≤ 0.6 % of near-money gamma before 15:30 and 2.8 % after; 0.005 changes nothing and 0.02 skips 47 % of near-money rows in the last half hour) ([q2_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q2_results.txt)). The consequential finding is about the benchmark. On **68 UW option-contracts snapshots of SPY (34 dates 2023-11-20 → 2026-08-24, 26,307 rows, 17,332 with IV and delta)**, UW's own greeks are reproduced from UW's IV only at **r = 0, q = 0, τ = calendar days/365**: median |Δdelta| 0.0015 (p90 0.0096), gamma +0.1 %, theta/365 0.0012, and the best-fit r per snapshot is 0.0000 with an interquartile range of 0–0; at r = 4.15 % the delta error is 0.0106 (p90 0.043). Against the market itself the r,q engine is the right one: same-strike call and put vols from the mids agree to −0.17 vol points under r = 4.15 %, q = 0 (sign random, 48 % positive) and are **+4.04 points apart under r = 0 in 100 % of 67 snapshots**; the forward-implied r − q from the ATM pairs is 4.07 % median (3.6–4.7 % by year). So the spec's r,q is correct for pricing and wrong as a way to match UW, and UW's 1.15–1.25 put/call gamma asymmetry the house saw is the arithmetic of inverting at r = 0 when the forward carries 4 %. The ITM claim is reversed: ITM calls more than 3 % in (n 2,879) have **0.0 % of mids at or below intrinsic under r = 0 and 5.7 % under r = 4.15 %, q = 0** (the floor S − K e^{−rτ} is higher), while deep ITM puts (n 848) have **32.0 % at or below K − S under r = 0** and 0 % under r = 4.15 % ([q1_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q1_results.txt), [q1b_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q1b_results.txt)). Exact replacements: keep r, q in d1 but write it as the forward — **r − q := ln(F/S)/τ from the ATM put-call pair of the expiry being priced, r := the SOFR print for the discount factor only** (no SOFR series is needed for d1); change the benchmark from "vendor greeks" to put-call parity on the chain, or ThetaData greeks when the terminal is free; and rewrite the ITM sentence as "under r = 0 a deep in-the-money put is often quoted below K − S (32 % at the close on SPY monthlies), so no vol solves; the forward-consistent floor restores one; in-the-money calls are unaffected under r = 0 and more often unsolvable under r > 0 with q = 0". The house's trailField prices UW's chain-read vols at r = 4.15 %; if the live chain endpoint is r = 0 like its history, every put is priced 1–2 vol points too rich and every call too cheap — one GET of `option-contracts` with a working token settles it.

Module 6's arithmetic is standard and its sign is a belief. The house's gamma × OI × 100 sum on ThetaData full chains stands within ±0.05 of UW's book-level net/gross on 12 of 15 name-days, the sign agrees on 12 of 15, and **every disagreement sits at |net/gross| < 0.07** ([q4b_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q4b_results.txt)). The dealer-sign tests, the owner's task (a) for this module, found nothing. On the cross-section (836,160 ticker-days, 5,103 names, 249 sessions, no earnings within a day) the next open-to-close return by quintile of the name-scaled exposure is GEX +0.0 bp (t 0.01) in the first half and +2.0 (t 0.62) in the second, DEX +2.6 / +3.0, VEX −2.4 / −3.3 and **CEX +6.5 (t 1.38) / +7.4 (t 1.74), +7.0 (t 2.20) over the year** — the one same-sign-both-halves result, 7 bp a day gross, below any options round trip and resting on six months of confirmation; on SPY, QQQ and IWM every correlation with the next session's first- or last-hour return is below 0.23 and sign-flipping across halves, the hit rate of sign(X) against sign(o-c) is 0.43–0.59, and IWM's GEX split has NW t −1.72 in one half and +2.16 in the other ([q3_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q3_results.txt)). Raw against negated is moot: a sign that predicts nothing predicts nothing flipped. "Never pick call vs put from the regime sign" is the one spec rule this audit confirms outright. The regime claim itself is mostly implied volatility in disguise: GEX_z predicts lower next-session realized vol after a trailing-vol control (t −3.7 / −4.2 SPY), but **with UW's 30-day IV added the GEX term is +0.011 (t +0.55) / −0.063 (t −3.17) on SPY, −0.010 / +0.001 on QQQ, −0.033 / −0.043 on IWM** ([q3b_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q3b_results.txt)). Demote the regime to a descriptive label; no weight. The published effects are short (10 minutes to an hour, or the last half hour) and conditional on illiquidity or low VIX ([Adams et al. via Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/); [Barbon & Buraschi](https://abarbon.com/papers/gamma-fragility); [Maurer, OptionMetrics 2026](https://optionmetrics.com/research/dealer-gamma-exposure-and-overnight-gap-risk-incremental-information-in-low-volatility-regimes/)), and Cboe's observed SPX 0DTE positioning is small and balanced ([Cboe, 0DTEs decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)); outside Cboe any "dealer" book is customer OI with a convention, so the right response is a confidence, not a firmer sign.

The levels need their names and their books. Method A (the linear zero crossing of the per-strike profile, the house's rule) is found in 67 of 67 snapshots, 66 within 3 %, |F_A − S| median 33 bp; Method B (re-pricing the net book on a 0.05 % spot grid) has a zero on ±4 % in 56 of 67, and where both exist **|B − A| has a median of $11.37 (188 bp), p90 $22.88, differs by more than one strike step in 95 % of cases and sits on the same side of spot only 57 % of the time** — different objects, never both "the flip". The 1 / 5 / 12 / 25 bp tiers are 1/20, 1/4, 2/3 and 1.4 of SPY's $1 strike step (18 bp): "breached" and "critical" are noise at a 1-minute print; put the tiers in strike steps or in the day's 1σ budget. Walls depend on which expiry or book is summed (the 25- and 60-DTE expiries on one date give the same call wall on 8 of 33 dates, the same put wall on 4; the 0DTE book's call wall never matched the Macro book's on 15 name-days, the Near book's did on 9), and on 34 dates the next session reached a monthly wall no more often than its mirror strike (call wall 3 % against 10 %, put wall 4 % against 4 %, king 10 / 10 %) — small-N and single-expiry, saying nothing about intraday 0DTE walls near spot, where the terminal's own track record is the right instrument ([q4_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q4_results.txt)). Keep A, the wall and king definitions and the three-book slicing; label every level by its book; report B under its own name or drop it. Task (b), the continuous multiplier, has an exact answer with no window and no free weight:

```
conf = (Σ GEX_call + Σ GEX_put) / (Σ |GEX_call| + Σ |GEX_put|)      ∈ [−1, 1], one conf per book (0DTE, Near, Macro)
conf_agree = min(|conf_UW|, |conf_house|) × [sign agrees]            where both engines give a book
```

Downstream multiplies by conf (or |conf| with the sign carried separately) instead of testing sign(GEX_UW) == sign(GEX_house); a dead zone, if wanted, is |conf| < 0.10 — the band in which two engines on one open interest disagree — not a tuned constant. On SPY 40 % of the year's sessions had |conf| < 0.10 and 64 % of the 56 day-to-day sign flips happened there (QQQ 60 %, IWM 71 %); conf is 0.95–0.96 correlated with the house's tanh-of-p85 display scale, which can stay as the display. It is a gate-behaviour replacement, not an edge: nothing in this audit shows the sign carries EV ([q5_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q5_results.txt)). The unit choices are cosmetic (365.25 against 252 is a factor 1.450; against the house's 365, 0.07 %): keep CEX per calendar day = CEX_year/365, VEX per vol point = VEX/100, print the unit on every figure; UW's book-level charm and vanna could not be reproduced from the chains (ratios 0.01–6), so their unit stands on UW's documentation alone ([q6_results.txt](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/greeks_books/q6_results.txt)).

## Modules 7, 8 and 11: the straddle band is a ruler, ATR > 1.5 is a ledger fact, flow is unrecorded

The straddle against straddle × 1.2533 is answered in the second moment and the quantile. On 3,654 sessions × 14 scans the RMS of |close − spot| / straddle at 09:35 is **1.15–1.29**, close to the theoretical 1.2533 ([Brenner & Subrahmanyam via Gundersen](https://gregorygundersen.com/blog/2023/08/19/atm-approximation/)), but the distribution is fat-tailed, so the **68.3 % quantile is 1.09–1.19 × straddle at the open and 0.70–0.95 × in the last 90 minutes** on the ETFs; 1.0 × holds 59–65 % of closes and 1.2533 × 70–75 %, more than its nominal 68.3 % ([em_contain.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/em_contain.csv)). Neither boundary is a fade signal: at the first touch of spot ± M the close is back inside **0.46–0.52 of the time at both multipliers** (upper touches 0.42–0.43 on SPY/QQQ/SPXW), the underlying continues 0.2–1.6 bps against the fade over 30 minutes, and the nearest-OTM fade loses **−11 % to −29 % a trade in every product, period and multiplier** (SPY M = straddle −$5.27 [−7.66, −2.35] fit on 4,076 trades, −$9.14 [−15.6, −3.3] check on 3,408; SPXW −$46 / −$91), win rates 16–19 %, every year negative, and the best fit-period scan of each product × multiplier read negative in 2025–2026 in all eight cases ([em_fade_stress.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/em_fade_stress.csv)). Change the band to M = straddle, labelled "about 63 % of closes inside at the open" — if a 1-SD band is wanted, 1.10–1.15 × at the open tapering to 0.9 × after 14:00, never 1.2533 × — and delete any fade at upper_1 / lower_1. SPXW's late-day containment (58–60 % of sessions beyond ±1.0 × at 15:50) is unresolved — the 16:00 print against a 15:50 straddle — and should not be used until the print is checked against the official settlement. The king scanner's |dist_em| ≤ 1 gate is a sound reachability filter that validates the distance, not the king: with the king proxied by the strike with the most same-day volume so far (gross gamma by strike is not on disk), a level within 1 M is reached 63–67 % of the time, at 1–2 M 14–21 %, and **the mirror strike at the same distance is reached at the same rates** (SPY ≤ 1 M: 0.671 against 0.680 fit, 0.666 against 0.673 check) ([king_reach.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/king_reach.csv)). Keep the gate, show the reach odds by distance band (≤ 0.5 M 65–87 %, 0.5–1 M 41–45 %, 1–1.5 M 18–26 %), and drop the claim that the king carries extra reach; the true king needs the recorded per-minute strike book.

The ATR exhaustion filter is the one directional rule with support on the underlying, and it must be stated as a ledger finding. After a sweep of the session extreme on a day already extended more than 1.5 ATR14 in that direction, the close is back inside the extreme only **0.439 / 0.301 of the time on SPY (41 fit / 83 check sweeps) and 0.413 / 0.310 on QQQ (75 / 84)** against 0.47–0.53 otherwise, and the underlying runs a further 8–35 bps to the close against the fade; IWM does not show it (0.600 / 0.556). The option fade loses in every bucket anyway (−3.5 % to −53 % a trade), so the filter removes a loss-maker from a loss-maker. The "negative gamma" qualifier is untestable at the available sample (5–47 events, 2–14 sessions a cell, signs inconsistent) and would barely bind, since UW's net gamma is negative on 64–68 % of days ([atr_sweep.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/atr_sweep.csv), [atr_gamma.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/atr_gamma.csv)). Change to "block counter-trend fades of the session extreme when |move_atr| > 1.5 in the trend's direction, whatever the gamma sign". The swing-side "prefer pullback over range_position > 0.95" has no stable edge: on the ETFs' daily bars 2012/2017–2024 pullback and near-mean entries beat chase entries on 20-day returns with ranges that include zero (SPY pullback +74 bps [−79, +142], n 69), 2025–2026 reversed it (SPY chase_20 +162 bps [+142, +292], n 83), and the 3,300–3,900-name cross-section shows no pullback premium (t −1.0) and a weak 5-day reversal after 20-day-high closes (−24 bps, t −2.2) that an 8–28 DTE swing would not capture after the spread ([swing_etf.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/swing_etf.csv), [swing_uw.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/swing_uw.csv)). Keep it as an entry-price preference with no expected-return claim.

Module 11 cannot be tested as written and its testable proxy contradicts the spec's sign. The proxy — per-contract 1-minute volume × price, bought when the bar's close is above its mean mid, summed over every same-day contract within ±2 % — leads the next 5/15/30-minute underlying move with the **opposite sign** (more buy-side call premium → a lower return), robustly by session (SPY −0.29 bps per SD at 5 minutes, t −7.9; −1.04 at 30, t −9.5 fit; −0.26 / −0.93, t −6.5 / −7.2 check; QQQ, SPXW and IWM the same sign at t −5 to −14) but by a fraction of one option spread, and the pooled quintile spreads are inconsistent across products (hit rates 0.50–0.54). Requiring "trade-side premium > other side" made the sweep fade worse in fit (SPY −$30.1 [−42.0, −19.8] on 53 trades; QQQ −$28.5 [−35.5, −20.8] on 125) and produced an OOS positive mean on 41–68 trades that rests on five sessions (−$51 to −$57 without them); the condition holds on 3–4 % of sweeps ([flow_lead.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/flow_lead.csv), [flow_sweep.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow/out/flow_sweep.csv)). Do not implement the premium balance as a gate; the dark-pool signature (print ≥ ask = buy) and the ≥ $100k whale filter and RVOL rotation are untestable here. The published flow evidence that survives review is signed, opening, account-typed flow at daily-to-weekly horizons in single stocks — 40 bps the next day in Pan–Poteshman, 0.34 % a week in Johnson–So ([NBER w10925](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10925); [Johnson & So 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i2p262-286.html)) — none of it 0DTE index flow at the minute scale. UW carries the inputs today (flow alerts with `has_sweep`, `has_floor`, ask- and bid-side premium; dark-pool prints with `price`, `nbbo_bid`, `nbbo_ask`; `net-prem-ticks`; `ohlc/1m`; `spot-exposures/strike`) but answers them as current windows, so the prerequisite is a daily recorder after 16:15 ET, append-only, per name on the Compass universe: every flow alert ≥ $25,000 (so the $100k threshold itself can be tested), every dark-pool print with its NBBO, the day's `net-prem-ticks`, the day's minute bars and the stock screener at each scan time, and the per-minute king from the strike book — picked on the first recorded year and read on the second ([move_atr_flow.md §Q6](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/move_atr_flow.md)).

## Modules 12–16: the four-boolean swing engine fires 20 times and loses to random

On 23 names (the twenty largest non-ETF option roots by October-2026 contract volume plus SPY, QQQ, IWM; daily bars 2020/2012 → 2026-10-13, nine splits back-adjusted) the booleans were defined in code from the close of day t with entry at the next open: B1 trend (close > EMA21 and > EMA50 and the last completed week's close > its 10-week EMA), B2 the doji-safe OTE pullback (an impulse on closes over 30 sessions, ≥ 2 ATR14, the high ≥ 2 sessions old; the day's low reaching 0.62 and the close not below 0.705), B3 volume ≥ 1.5 × the prior 20-session mean, B4 off (no strike-level book before 2025-10-15). **B1 fires on 41 % of name-days, B2 on 5 %, B3 on 10 %, and their same-day AND fired 14 times in 2022–2024 and 6 in 2025–2026 — N = 20, not 500**; no boolean ranks forward returns out of sample (AUC 0.49–0.51), B2 alone is negative in train (−0.41 % at 5 sessions, t −1.6, n 872), and B1 under-performs the unconditional baseline in train at every horizon (+0.49 % against +1.55 % at 10 sessions, n 6,933) ([fixed_hold_results.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/horizons/fixed_hold_results.csv)). N > 500 is not reachable with the spec's booleans on this universe at daily resolution: the strictest reading gives 20 entries, the loosest sequenced reading 218. A pullback day that touches the OTE zone and prints 1.5 × volume inside an uptrend is, by construction, a distribution day; two of the spec's conditions fight. The literature points the same way: momentum is a 3–12-month effect and the 1–4-week sign is reversal ([Jegadeesh & Titman 1993](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v48y1993i1p65-91.html); [Lehmann 1990](https://www.nber.org/papers/w2533)), abnormal volume's premium is a monthly one ([Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/9901.pdf)), and no peer-reviewed support exists for 0.62–0.705.

To reach a readable N the gate was read as a sequence declared before any return was looked at — the OTE touch within the last five sessions, B1 today, 1.2 × volume today, an up close (SEQ5_12, **N 105 train / 71 check**). With the spec's swing exits (prior-week-low stop, 1 × expected-move target, 20 sessions) it returned **−1.95 % a trade in train and −1.72 % in check on the underlying, gross**, while 200 draws of count-matched random entries with the same exits returned +0.99 % (sd 0.78 %) and +1.15 % (sd 1.15 %): the gate is below 200 of 200 and 199 of 200 draws. The weekly reading is +0.81 % / −0.48 %; the short side mirrored is +1.68 % train and −6.86 % check (t −2.4); the one positive-looking cell (63 sessions, +6.70 %, t 2.6 in train) is below the unconditional +7.12 %, +3.16 % without its best five trades and +2.36 % in check (−4.63 % without its best five). The level stop is not where the spec says: after an entry 62–70 % down an impulse the prior-week low sits a **median 8.25 % below entry (q25 −12.1 %, 10th percentile −17.7 %), not 2.4–4.6 %**, against a 1 × EM target a median +10.3 % away at a 35–40 % hit rate; the spec's "median 3.45 %" is a fact about 35 vendor trades, and a fixed 3.45 % stop is hit on 74–78 % of trades ([seq_rule_exit_results.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/horizons/seq_rule_exit_results.csv)). Repriced as the spec's calls by Black-Scholes on the entry's path (UW's IV where the one-year table has it, else the name's median; spreads 3 % ETF / 8 % single name, $0.65 each way; a model, not a backtest), the sequenced gate loses at every horizon: **weekly 7 DTE at 0.425 Δ −13.3 % train / −14.9 % check with the −20 % premium stop (87 of 105 and 56 of 71 stopped on premium); swing 14–30 DTE at 0.475 Δ −17 to −26 % train and −3 to −17 % check; LEAPS 180 DTE at 0.70 Δ +15.0 % train on five trades (+0.9 % without them, median −8.7 %) and −4.4 % check**. The −20 % / −17 % backstop fires before the level stop every time (three sessions of pure decay take 42 % off a 7-DTE premium and 19–20 % off a 14-DTE one), so the exact change is to size it to the tenor — **≥ 45 % at 7 DTE, ≥ 25 % at 14 DTE, or drop it below 21 DTE and keep the level stop alone** (hold-only −0.3 % against −17.4 % with the stop at 21 DTE in train); the scale-out ladder, trail lock and RSI(14) exit stay within ±2 points of the plain stop, the ladder needs four contracts to exist (two run the 50 % tier only, one has no ladder), and the LEAPS roll at 90 against 60 DTE is within ±0.3 % of spot a cycle ([option_model_results.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/horizons/option_model_results.csv)). A logistic regression on the booleans fitted 2022–2024 has OOS AUC 0.507–0.508 and the wrong sign on B2 (−0.29 at 5 sessions): no weights; keep the strict gate as a setup description, not a predictor ([09_logistic.py](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/horizons/09_logistic.py)).

The picker and the earnings gate are frictions and variance rules. On the five October-2026 bulk days the delta bands are liquid on the ETFs (median spread/mid 1.0–1.2 % at the weekly and swing bands) and expensive on single names (5.1–5.3 % median, **8.5 % at 8–14 DTE**, 90th percentile 18–21 %); **the OI ≥ 500 floor leaves a qualifying 0.40–0.55-delta call in about half of the 8–28 DTE expiries** (SPY 59 %, QQQ 34 %, AMD 17 %, MU 12 %, HYG 17 %) because daily-listed expiries carry little open interest; LEAPS at 0.60–0.80 Δ cost 3.7–4.4 % to cross ([picker_spread_by_band.csv](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/horizons/picker_spread_by_band.csv)). Keep the bands; change the OI rule to "the nearest expiry with a qualifying contract"; prefer 15–28 DTE over 8–14 on single names (half the spread). Earnings sessions move the names 2.4–5.5 × a normal session (medians 2.5 % AAPL to 12.1 % PLTR against 0.9–3.0 %, 1,515 reports 1996 → 2026), yet the sequenced swing entries that crossed a report did not do worse on the underlying (+0.91 % against −3.04 % train, n 29 / 76; +1.97 % against −2.85 % check), consistent with the stock-side announcement premium ([Frazzini & Lamont, NBER w13090](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13090)); the option-side cost — retail option buyers lose 5–9 % on average before announcements with spreads around 20 % ([de Silva, Smith & So via Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-retail-losses-money-in-option-trading/)) — is not priceable here. Keep the gate as a risk rule; never present it as EV. The B4 proxy on the one year of book totals (5,738 name-days) carried nothing (38 positive-gamma entries, 6 negative; unconditionally negative-gamma days returned slightly more, +2.54 % against +1.83 % at 20 sessions), and the real B4 — a wall within one expected move, the stop beyond it — needs `spot-exposures/strike?date=` per name per day (≈ 250 calls a name a year on a 3-concurrency plan) or the recorder's files once they cover a year ([08_b4_proxy.py](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/horizons/08_b4_proxy.py)).

Modules 15 and 16 and the Helix isolation are code-review items with no data test, and the checks are exact. Cents normalization: exactly one boundary where vendor prices enter and are converted to one unit (UW's numeric strings in dollars read by `num()`, ThetaData's `strike`/`bid`/`ask` strings in dollars, the house's book in dollars to the cent), a schema test asserting unit and scale for every engine input (spot, strike, bid, ask, mid, premium, target, stop, expected move — a strike "808.0" is dollars, never mils; a premium "0.56" is dollars a share, the contract × 100), a round-trip of one known contract (SPY 20261030 808 C at 0.55 / 0.56 on 2026-10-09 from the bulk file) through the picker, the stop, the tiers and the P&L to the cent, and any `/100`, `*100` or `Math.round(x*100)` outside that boundary a finding. Dead-code quarantine: every removed cap and floor listed by name — the old premium caps and floors, the delta floors and ceilings the 0.35–0.50 / 0.40–0.55 / 0.60–0.80 bands replace, any old OI floor, min/max-DTE constants the 4–7 / 8–28 / 90–365+ windows replace, and any `weight`, `score`, `confluence` or `grade` constant still imported (those words are banned on screen by `npm run words:check`) — with the test static (`tsc` with the exports deleted plus a grep for names and literal values) and the engine's output on a fixed replay day byte-identical before and after except where a removed cap used to bind, each difference listed. Helix: its fields written by the engine and read by the UI and logs, never by a decision — the four booleans, the picker, the stops, the scale-outs and the sizing pure functions of market inputs and config with no Helix argument; an import-graph check that nothing under the decision modules imports Helix; decisions on a fixed replay day identical with Helix's fields null; and Helix writing to its own sink, never to the alert store, the paper engine's orders or the Journal ([horizons.md §10](../research_notes/Compass%20engine%20audit/horizons.md)).

## Conclusion

The audit's deepest result is not that the spec loses but that its losses have a single shape: every long-premium rule, however triggered, returns the premium's decay plus the spread, and every exit redistributes that loss between many small ones and a few large ones without moving its mean. That is why the rules that survive are frictions and loss-shapers — the spread rule, the 15:58 flat, the inert backstop, the lock at +0 %, the trailed remainder — and why the breaker, the grade and the size cap are artefacts of a ledger in which everything long premium is negative. The spec's vocabulary of direction (chase, fade, pin, king, sweep, triple confirmation, dealer sign) describes states the market is in, not states it leaves predictably; the terminal can draw all of them, and should, as long as none of them sets a trade. Two conventions the house relies on also turned out to be beliefs: UW's contract greeks stand on r = 0 while the house prices its vols at r = 4.15 %, and "dealer" gamma anywhere outside Cboe is customer open interest with an assumed sign — which is exactly why net/gross, the quantity on which two honest engines disagree, is the right thing to carry downstream.

What would change the picture is data, not modelling. A year of recorded flow alerts, dark-pool prints with their NBBO, net-prem-ticks and the per-minute strike book — the recorder already exists for six names — would make Modules 6, 7, 11 and the swing engine's B4 testable for the first time; a working UW token answers the r = 0 question in one call; a ThetaData pull outside the owner's terminal hours gives the greek benchmark and the daily option NBBO history the Weekly/Swing/LEAPS exits need. Until then the only hypotheses the data points toward are short-premium ones — selling the close's optionality when moc_mag is low, and the 0DTE short structures this repository tested earlier — and those were not part of this audit's scope.
