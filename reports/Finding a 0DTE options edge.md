# Costs swallow every tested 0DTE signal

None of the six input families Slayer Terminal can source from Unusual Whales (per-strike flow bursts, dealer gamma/charm/vanna, dark-pool prints, market tide, session effects, exit engineering) produces a positive cost-adjusted expectancy in long 0DTE options on SPY, QQQ or IWM, and the published literature says the same thing for the index products it studies. Across **339 sessions (2025-06-02 to 2026-10-09)** our tests ran **62 main predictive cells, 576 exit configurations and 144 GeorgeFi-style strategy variants**; zero of them clear a 1–2 % half-spread plus $0.70 fees, and the best of each family is statistically indistinguishable from random entries managed by the same exit engine. The reason is structural rather than a matter of tuning: every flow and positioning aggregate is 0.5–0.7 rank-correlated with the move that has already happened and at most 0.02 with the move that follows, while an ATM 0DTE contract priced near $1 costs 3–6 % of premium per round trip and bleeds roughly 4 % per 15 minutes in theta. The one premium that is real on our data sits on the short side: selling the ATM straddle earns +$7 to +$17 per straddle at mid (2–10 % of credit) at every entry time, with the 13:00 entry the strongest, but after modelled spreads every pooled confidence interval spans zero and two days (2025-10-10 and 2025-11-20) cost more than sixteen months earned. The directional effects with replicated academic support, last-half-hour momentum on negative-gamma days and noise-band intraday momentum, live in the underlying where a round trip costs ~0.15 bp, not in the option. For Slayer this means no long-premium lane is promotable; the defensible build is a measured short-premium afternoon lane with live NBBO capture and tail sizing, plus underlying/SPX lanes for anything directional.

## Published evidence supports volatility forecasts, not direction

The academic record on dealer positioning is consistent and modest. Barbon and Buraschi find that one standard deviation of gamma imbalance lowers the absolute daily index return by **more than 20 bps (about 20 % of a standard deviation)** and lowers the within-day autocorrelation of 5–60-minute returns by **1.5–2 pp**, a volatility and path-shape effect rather than a directional one ([Barbon & Buraschi](http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf)). The single replicated directional result at the index level is Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens: the last-30-minute S&P futures return loads on the rest-of-day return with **β = 6.63 (t = 4.78, R² 3.58 %) on negative-net-gamma days versus 0.82 (t = 1.03) on positive-gamma days**, a timing strategy earning Sharpe 1.73 gross on equity index futures and a positive net Sharpe at one tick of cost ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). That effect is a close-of-day phenomenon tied to hedgers squaring up by 16:00; nobody has documented a mid-day 15/30/60-minute momentum conditional on gamma sign.

The post-2022 0DTE evidence moves the prior further away from long premium. Cboe's position-level data shows SPX 0DTE customer flow is "extremely balanced", more than **95 % of 0DTE trades are limited-risk structures**, and net market-maker gamma hedging is "at best 0.2 % of SPX daily liquidity" ([Cboe, 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)); at 15:30 the median 0DTE market-maker gamma was **+$173 mm with an interquartile range of −$1.1 bn to +$2.4 bn**, i.e. dealers are usually long ([Cboe, Much Ado About 0DTEs](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)). Adams et al. find that higher net gamma predicts lower S&P volatility over the next 10 minutes, stronger E-mini order-flow reversals and negative high-frequency momentum, fading within an hour, with market makers keeping a positive net gamma throughout expiry day ([Quantpedia summary of Adams et al.](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)); Dim, Eraker and Vilkov find 0DTE volume shocks "do not amplify recent past index returns" ([Oxford seminar abstract](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/node/67396)). An eight-year SPY backtest of the vendor-style aggregates shows why raw GEX looks predictive: its Spearman correlation with next-day realized volatility is **−0.36 raw, −0.14 after VIX and −0.03 (p = 0.18) after VIX plus ATM IV**, and DEX/CHEX have no return predictability at all ([FlashAlpha GEX backtest](https://flashalpha.com/articles/gex-dex-vex-chex-8-year-backtest-spy-vix-control)). Gamma flip, call wall and put wall have no published out-of-sample test anywhere, and Unusual Whales' own documentation warns that `gamma_flip` "can be null or far from spot, so do not use it for regime detection" ([UW gex-levels docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/api/gexgreeks/gex-levels.md)). Pinning, the best-supported level effect historically ([Golez & Jackwerth](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p566-585.html)), has reversed: a 2016–2025 study of 1.6 million near-expiry SPY contracts finds no pinning across five tests and **~16 % wider daily ranges** on high-ATM-open-interest days ([Harbourfront on Elms](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/from-pinning-to-amplification-evidence)).

Signed option flow has even less support at the index level. The richest dataset ever used, CBOE open-buy volumes with trader-type codes, produces **40 bps/day of predictability in single stocks and nothing in OEX/SPX/NDX index options** ([Pan & Poteshman](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10925/w10925.pdf)); the informative component is opening customer buys, which OPRA-based vendors cannot see, and quote-rule side-tagging is right only **83 % of the time** even with true direction known ([Savickas & Wilson](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v38y2003i04p881-902_00.html)). For the option leg the documented short-horizon effect of aggressive flow is adverse to a follower: dealer inventory pushes the option's price up on the day of buying pressure and it reverts by **~1 % per standard deviation the next day** ([Muravyev](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/359812)). The realized outcome of following flow into 0DTEs is in Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda: retail long 0DTE positions lost **53 % of their value (61 % after daily expirations began), with ~60 % of losses attributable to transaction costs**, while short positions returned 48–56 % ([WealthManagement on Beckmeyer et al.](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)). Vilkov's SPX 0DTE study puts the median 10:00-to-close variance premium at **≈0.0011 % of the underlying, "too small to trade after friction"**, with only conditional short-premium rules reaching net Sharpe 0.39–0.93 and a 1 % expected shortfall of 0.58–1.58 % of the underlying per day ([Vilkov results summary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/docs/agent-context/results-summary.md)). The literature therefore sets a clear prior: dealer gamma is a volatility-regime variable, index option flow is noise plus hedging, long-premium 0DTE pays the spread and the premium, and the seller collects a thin, tail-exposed carry.

## Flow, tide, dark pool and bursts describe the last 15 minutes, not the next 30

We built a 1-minute panel of **117,265 ticker-minutes per ticker over 339 sessions** from `net-prem-ticks`, a 22,372-observation market-tide panel, a fully paged dark-pool sample of 90 sessions × 3 tickers and a 40-session per-strike sample, and measured pooled Spearman information coefficients with daily-block bootstrap intervals against 15/30/60-minute forward returns ([flow_ic_main.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/flow_ic_main.csv)). All 36 flow cells (signed net premium, signed ask-minus-bid volume share, net-delta change, unsigned aggressor share × three tickers × three horizons) have ICs between **−0.011 and +0.020 with every confidence interval covering zero** (smallest p = 0.064) and top-minus-bottom quintile spreads of −0.7 to +2.1 bp; sign agreement between feature and forward return is 49.2–50.5 % in every cell. The diagnostic that explains this is the column `ic_vs_trailing15`: the features' rank correlation with the trailing 15-minute return is **0.51–0.67 for SPY and QQQ**, so the "signal" is a restatement of the move that just happened ([flow_ic_main.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/flow_ic_main.csv)). The 216 regime-and-time-of-day conditional cells contain three nominal p < 0.05 against an expectation of ~11 by chance, and none survives Bonferroni or Benjamini-Hochberg ([flow_ic_conditional.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/flow_ic_conditional.csv)). A placebo on simulated random walks shows that within-day ICs are mechanically negative (−0.038/−0.063/−0.114 at 15/30/60 minutes), so the apparent mean reversion a naive within-day analysis would report is an artefact, not a finding ([placebo_daily_ic.py](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/placebo_daily_ic.py)).

Market tide is the clearest demonstration that these aggregates are coincident rather than predictive. The 5-minute `market-tide` row stamped T holds the cumulative value through T+4:59, so it is only known at T+5; aligned correctly its 30-minute change has an IC of **0.007 (p ≥ 0.41)** against SPY's next 30 minutes, whereas the same feature joined contemporaneously shows **IC 0.072 (p ≈ 1e-7) and a 3.3 bp quintile spread** ([tide_ic_main.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/tide_ic_main.csv)). A naive join would have produced a spurious edge of exactly the size vendors advertise. Dark-pool prints behave the same way once the feed is paged completely (median 13.8k SPY prints per day; `tracking_id` is null for Sep–Dec 2025 sessions, which silently truncated a first pass that de-duplicated on it): buy-initiated share and net notional over the trailing 30 minutes have ICs between −0.065 and +0.037 with every interval spanning zero, and where the point estimate is largest (SPY, 60 minutes, IC −0.065, p = 0.051) the sign is contrarian ([dp_ic_main.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/dp_ic_main.csv); [darkpool_coverage.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/darkpool_coverage.csv)). This is what the literature predicts for an ETF whose off-exchange tape is ~83 % wholesaler internalization of retail orders ([Cboe 2024 review](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/north-american-equities-year-in-review)) signed against NBBO, where midpoint signing is the only method with ~5 % error ([Barber et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4202874)).

Per-strike premium bursts are the direct analogue of GeorgeFi's `sus-strike` detector (a 2-minute window, `baselineMultiple` of 2.3×–100×, PREMIUM_BURST / VELOCITY_SPIKE triggers), whose contracts already showed **−0.9 % mean and 36 % positive at +60 minutes** on the public tape ([GEORGEFI_SIGNAL_RESEARCH.md](/home/user/georgeredo/GEORGEFI_SIGNAL_RESEARCH.md)). Defining a burst as ask-side premium ≥ 5× or 10× the trailing 20-minute median at a strike within 1 % of spot, 3,123 bursts over 40 sessions are followed by underlying moves of **−0.9 to +0.1 bp in the burst direction (hit rate 49–50 %)**, no different from matched random minutes, and the burst contract's own 15/30-minute return is ≈ 0 raw and **−6 to −8 % after costs versus −4 to −6 % for random ATM entries** ([bursts_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/bursts_summary.csv)). The four burst-contract cells are the only ones in the 62-cell multiple-testing table that pass Bonferroni, and they are significantly negative (p ≤ 3e-5) ([mt_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/mt_summary.csv)). The test is generous to the feature because `flow-per-strike-intraday` returns the day's top-8 strikes by a full-day filter, a strike set a live trader cannot reproduce and one that selects on outcome ([bursts_coverage.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/bursts_coverage.csv)). Expressed as the nominally best rule (SPY net premium, top/bottom quintile every 5 minutes, ATM 0DTE, `trail` engine), the feature returns **−2.38 % per trade after costs against −2.26 % for random entries matched per day** ([trade_rule_SPY_F1_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/flow_predictors/trade_rule_SPY_F1_summary.csv)). Nothing in the UW flow stack times entries better than a coin.

## Dealer gamma marks volatility regimes, while walls and magnets behave like random prices

The prior-day gamma-flip regime that GeorgeFi's `posgamma_fade` gate and Slayer's paper spec both key on does not separate continuation from mean reversion. On 1,016 ticker-days, the PM-on-AM return slope is −0.014 in the positive regime and −0.045 in the negative regime, a difference of **+0.030 with a daily-block interval of [−0.122, +0.185]**, sign agreement is 51–52 % in both, and the in-sample split (POS +0.067 vs NEG −0.096) reverses out of sample (POS −0.130 vs NEG +0.028), which says the point estimates are noise ([q1_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q1_summary.csv); [q1_pos_minus_neg.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q1_pos_minus_neg.csv)). The only regime differences reaching nominal significance are volatility effects: negative-gamma days have a 0.11 pp wider range (1.30 % vs 1.19 %) and 0.5 pp fewer 1-minute reversals, neither surviving correction across ~197 interval tests. The `gex-levels` snapshot is post-close for its date (`time` 20:00Z or later on all 1,044 records, `source` always `vol`), so the prior-day regime is free of look-ahead and the null is not a data-alignment artefact ([q0_gex_levels_meta.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q0_gex_levels_meta.csv)).

The 1-minute `spot-exposures` feed (60 SPY sessions, 21,001 RTH minutes) confirms the literature's volatility reading and rejects the directional one. Net OI-based gamma has a pooled IC of **−0.211 [−0.300, −0.125] against the absolute 30-minute return**, with mean |r30| of 12.6 bp when gamma is negative versus 8.7 bp when positive, and a partial IC of −0.171 after residualizing on trailing realized volatility; against the signed return the IC is −0.064 with an interval covering zero ([q2_ic_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q2_ic_summary.csv); [q2_extra.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q2_extra.csv)). Charm and vanna add nothing directional (charm_oi is negative at all 21,001 minutes and grows ~20× into the close; vanna_oi is always positive, so both are level effects), the `dir` variant has |IC| ≤ 0.03 against everything, and the 30-minute change in volume-gamma, the largest signed IC at −0.06 to −0.10, is one of 56 tests, marginal, and worth about 1.7 bp of underlying, roughly one round-trip cost on an ATM SPY 0DTE ([q2_ic_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q2_ic_summary.csv)). Two further facts undercut "the regime" as a concept: the intraday gamma sign at 10:00 agrees with the prior-day flip regime on only **31 of 57 sessions (54 %)**, and intraday OI gamma is negative at 10:00 on 65 % of sessions while the prior-day flip labels 54 % of days positive ([q2.log](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q2.log)). Any strategy keyed on one definition would not key on the other, and GeorgeFi's ledger statistics by `gexRegime` (AT_PIN 58 % win rate, POSITIVE_GAMMA 44 %) are confounded by its SPX concentration and by the `GEX:NEGATIVE_GAMMA` tag itself showing no outcome difference (p = 0.81) ([GEORGEFI_SIGNAL_RESEARCH.md](/home/user/georgeredo/GEORGEFI_SIGNAL_RESEARCH.md)).

Walls and the gamma magnet fail the cleanest test we can run: five pseudo-levels per real level placed at the same distance from the open, with identical touch, forward-return and pin computations. The call wall is touched on 49.9 % of ticker-days with a 30-minute "through" return of +0.6 bp [−2.9, +4.1] and a 0.25 % pin rate of 37.1 % versus 35.9 % for controls; the gamma magnet is touched on 59.0 % of days and **34.5 % of touched days close within 0.25 % of it versus 34.6 % for random levels**, a difference of −0.1 pp [−4.7, +4.6]; the put wall is, if anything, anti-pinning (−6.3 pp [−12.2, −1.2]) ([q3_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q3_summary.csv); [q3_real_minus_control.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q3_real_minus_control.csv)). The maximum favourable excursion through a level after a touch averages 44–66 bp for real and control levels alike, so the levels are not barriers either. The lone directional candidate, put wall touched from below (−9.4 bp [−15.8, −3.0] over 30 minutes, n = 103), is one of ~51 comparisons and at 9 bp of underlying is about one round-trip cost on an ATM put. The `avoid_pin` gate in the strategy spec is therefore guarding against an effect that does not exist in this data, and the 144-variant backtest already showed that adding CALL_WALL/PUT_WALL to the level set changed expectancy by less than $1 per trade ([BACKTEST_RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/BACKTEST_RESULTS.md)).

## Session effects and exit engineering cannot outrun a 3–6 % round trip

The session-effect family is empty on this sample. A 1-minute close beyond the 15-minute opening range occurs on 1,016 of 1,017 ticker-days, continues 50–54 % of the time at +30/+60 minutes, moves −0.5 bp [−7.0, +5.8] to the close, and is back inside the range within 30 minutes on 78 % of breaks; buffers of 0.05–0.25 % and three-close confirmations change nothing ([t3_variants.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/t3_variants.md)). No half-hour of the day has a mean return with |t| ≥ 2, the first half hour predicts the last half hour with **correlation −0.036 and sign agreement 46.7 %** (the published SPY R² of ~2 % from 1993–2013 ([CXO on Gao et al.](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=25481)) is absent here), and gaps larger than 0.3 % fill by the close only 38 % of the time, falling to 20 % above 1 % ([summary.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/summary.md)). Every option expression of these loses: ORB breakout −$5.15 [−7.11, −3.24], ORB fade −$1.78 [−4.19, +0.61], 15:00 momentum held to 15:55 −$4.17 [−11.33, +4.21], gap-fill at 09:35 −$3.78 [−7.12, −0.29], against random-time random-direction baselines of −$2.70 and −$3.77 ([csv/summary_all.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/csv/summary_all.csv)). The only pooled |t| above 2 is the 15:00–15:30 → 15:30–16:00 link (QQQ correlation 0.172, t = 3.20), which cannot be traded with a 15:55 hard close and whose sign-conditioned mean is +0.89 bp [−1.29, +3.18].

Exit engineering, the one component the GeorgeFi research credited with value, turns out to shape the option's path rather than add expectancy. Over **576 configurations** (stop −15 to −60 % × breakeven arm × guardian give-back 25–50 % at +15/+30 % × partial take × time stop) applied to 5,215 strategy entries and 3,000 random entries, **zero configurations have a positive expectancy under the spread model on any entry set**, the White reality-check p-value for the best is 1.0 in every set, and the in-sample-selected configuration loses out of sample in every set ([q4_group_heads.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q4_group_heads.csv)). The best pooled configuration (stop −60 %, breakeven +6 %, 25 % give-back armed at +15 %, no partial, no time stop) lifts expectancy from −$3.84 to **−$1.78 [−2.80, −0.73] on strategy entries and to −$1.34 [−2.23, −0.46] on random entries**, with the same exit mix (STOP 12 %, BREAKEVEN 44 %, TRAIL 44 %) and the same 43 % win rate; configuration by configuration, 0 of 576 strategy-minus-random differences have an interval above zero and 51 have one entirely below ([q4_grid_summary.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q4_grid_summary.csv); [q4_strategy_minus_random.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q4_strategy_minus_random.csv)). The ceiling is arithmetic: the best cost-free expectancy any engine extracts is **+$2.45 per contract (1.5 % of premium) against a measured round trip of $3.5–4.6 (3–6 % of premium)**, and the engine would need roughly double its gross edge, or costs would need to halve, to reach zero ([q4_report.log](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q4_report.log)). This is the same ordering the original backtest found (`trail` −$3.2 > `ladder` −$4.0 > `bracket` −$6.1 > `hold` −$14.8) and the same conclusion: the engine's value is in losing less ([BACKTEST_RESULTS.md](/home/user/georgeredo/BACKTEST_RESULTS.md)). Kaminski and Lo's result that stop rules lower expected return under a random walk and add value only under positive serial correlation ([Kaminski & Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)) is the theory behind the observation; with 1-minute reversal rates of 50.2–50.8 % in every gamma regime there is no serial correlation for a trailing stop to harvest.

The cost hurdle deserves to be stated on the option rather than the underlying because that is where every long-premium idea dies. With a $0.01 half-spread on a ~$1 ATM contract plus fees, the round trip is 2–4 % of premium, and theta on an ATM option with three hours left is ≈ 4 % per 15 minutes and ≈ 17 % per hour, so a long needs roughly **2–3 bp of favourable underlying move in 15 minutes or 6–7 bp in 60 minutes** before gamma helps; the strongest documented conditional effect, ≈ 6.6 bp of last-30-minute return per 1 % rest-of-day move on short-gamma days, is of that order only on large-move days ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). The same 1 bp of underlying edge on SPY shares clears a 0.15 bp round trip seven times over.

## The only real premium is short vol, and it is one spread wide

Selling the ATM 0DTE straddle is the one construction on our data whose mid-price expectancy is positive at every entry time: **+$14.40 at 09:35, +$11.35 at 10:00, +$12.05 at 11:00, +$16.69 [5.36, 27.71] at 13:00 and +$6.98 at 14:30 per straddle** (3–10 % of credit), and a random-time short straddle earns +$15.74 [2.49, 27.73] at mid, an interval that excludes zero ([summary.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/summary.md)). After the spread model the means fall to −$1 to +$8 with every pooled interval spanning zero; the 13:00 entry remains the best (**+$8.36 [−3.15, 19.52], win rate 65.1 %, profit factor 1.22, maximum drawdown −$5,084**), and under adverse fills every entry time is significantly negative ([csv/summary_all.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/csv/summary_all.csv)). The implied round-trip cost of the spread model is $8–11 per straddle, 3.6–4.5 % of credit, which is the same order as the mid-price edge, so the verdict hinges entirely on fill quality that UW option bars (no bid/ask) cannot resolve. The loss distribution is the familiar short-gamma shape: mean win +$131 against mean loss −$179, a 1st percentile of −$716, and the ten worst days summing to −$14,039 against a sixteen-month total of +$714 for the 10:00 hold ([csv/t1_straddles.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/short_vol_session/csv/t1_straddles.csv)). Credit stops at 1.5× are hit on ~35 % of days by intraday noise and lower the mean while cutting drawdown by a third; 2.0× stops are closer to neutral. The 20-delta strangle proxy wins 75 % of the time but collects only $1.02 of credit, of which the modelled cost consumes 7.5 %, and its paired difference against a random-time entry is +$0.23 [−14.01, +12.75]. Scanning all 356 breakdown rows for a positive lower bound after costs returns nine cells, all post hoc (QQQ at 13:00, +$17.07 [1.11, 32.34], one of 15 ticker-time cells whose second-half interval is [−17.46, 34.01]; Wednesday strangles; seven single months), exactly the count one expects from ~25 variants × 15 cells × 5 weekdays × 3 IV buckets.

This matches the outside evidence closely enough to trust the sign. Across 193 SPY Wednesday sessions the 10:00 straddle-implied move over-forecast the realized close-to-close move by about 13 % (median realized/implied 0.589 versus 0.674 if fair) with a 1-sigma breach rate of 26 % ([FlashAlpha straddle study](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study)); Vilkov's conditional strangle/straddle rule reaches a 72 % hit rate and Sharpe 0.39 net while the unconditional structures are "low Sharpe, mixed or negative medians" ([Vilkov results summary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/docs/agent-context/results-summary.md)); practitioner data show 10 % profit targets beating hold-to-expiry (78 % versus 61 % win rate on SPX iron flies) ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/zero-dte-winners-and-losers-for-iron-flies-08-07-2025)) and the profitable half of 230,000 platform 0DTE trades entering near 10:15 and exiting by noon ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)). Our 13:00 result is the mirror image of that early-entry, early-exit pattern: fewer hours remain for a large move, so the seller buys the cheapest decay window, but the second-half-sample mean (+$4.18) is a third of the first-half mean (+$12.77), and the sample spans one volatility regime with two tail days.

Three lanes follow from this, all paper first and each with the random baseline and the three cost models computed automatically as the strategy spec already requires ([STRATEGY_SPEC.md](/home/user/georgeredo/STRATEGY_SPEC.md)). The first is a **13:00 short ATM straddle on SPY and QQQ** (IWM is negative at every entry time), unstopped or with a 2.0× credit stop, flat at 15:55, with an event-day filter for FOMC/CPI/NFP and a sizing rule calibrated to the −$2,000 single-day losses already observed; its promotion test is the one that matters for the whole programme, a lower 95 % bound above zero over at least 150 trades spanning three months on *measured* fills. That requires the one data capture nothing else here needs: live NBBO for the ATM contracts from the UW `option_trades` websocket, because real SPY/QQQ ATM 0DTE quotes are often $0.01–0.02 wide (0.2–0.5 % half-spread on a $2–3 option), which would place the truth between the mid and spread rows above and could by itself move the 13:00 lane's interval off zero. The second is to **move every directional idea to the underlying**: the Baltussen last-30-minute rule conditioned on negative gamma was underpowered here (1,016 ticker-days against the paper's ~6,000 index days, and our prior-day flip is not the paper's OI-based NGE), and the Zarattini noise-band momentum has a net-of-cost SPY record through 2024; both are tests of existence and stability on shares, where a 1 bp effect is tradeable, and only afterwards tests of whether a 15:30 0DTE expression survives the spread. The third is **SPX contracts**, where GeorgeFi's record was concentrated (145 of 489 trades at a $6.55 median premium) and where a $5–10 premium halves the proportional round trip ([GEORGEFI_SIGNAL_RESEARCH.md](/home/user/georgeredo/GEORGEFI_SIGNAL_RESEARCH.md)); that lane is blocked until the account has the CBOE-approved index-bar entitlement, and it should be opened with the short-premium structure, not a long one.

Two upgrades would strengthen the tests themselves. Market tide history reaches back to 2022-09-28 ([UW market-tide docs](https://api.unusualwhales.com/docs/operations/PublicApi.MarketController.market_tide)), so the flow panel can be extended to roughly 1,000 sessions spanning the 2023 rally, the 2024 August shock and the April 2025 spike, which is the minimum needed to detect a 1.5–2 pp autocorrelation shift of the size Barbon reports; and the strike-level `spot-exposures/strike` profile plus same-day intraday `gex-levels` would replace UW's single integer wall with the nearest high-gamma strike, the only form in which a wall test has not yet been run. Neither upgrade is expected to produce a long-premium edge, because the cost arithmetic does not change; they would sharpen the volatility-regime filter that the short-premium lane can use to stand aside.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. Slayer was looking for an input that tells a long 0DTE buyer which way the next 30 minutes go; what the data say is that every Unusual Whales aggregate is a high-resolution description of the present, that dealer positioning forecasts the *size* of moves rather than their sign, and that the option market prices this so well that the premium left over belongs to the seller and is roughly one bid-ask spread wide. GeorgeFi's record fits the same picture exactly: entries at the 50th percentile of random timing, a positive ledger produced by exits that shape the path, and a cost-adjusted expectancy indistinguishable from zero. The exit-engine finding is the most clarifying, because it shows the ceiling: the most any path-shaping rule can extract from a zero-drift 0DTE option is about 1.5 % of premium, and the cheapest realistic round trip is twice that. No entry signal in the tested families moves the drift, so no engine can rescue them.

The forward path is therefore narrow and specific rather than exploratory. The only quantity that can turn the one positive mid-price result into a tradeable lane is the executable half-spread on SPY/QQQ ATM 0DTE contracts in the early afternoon, which a few weeks of websocket NBBO capture will measure; and the only place a directional view is cheap enough to test honestly is the underlying. Until those two facts are in hand, every long-premium 0DTE lane, including the reconstructed GeorgeFi strategy and every flow-, gamma- or level-gated variant of it, should remain a measured paper book whose purpose is to record how much it loses, and the promotion rule in the strategy spec should stay exactly as written.
