# Buying win rate costs 0DTE sellers expectancy

Compass can field a 0DTE lane that wins 70 % of the time or more. What it cannot do, on any evidence we have, is reach that win rate through management rules and keep a positive, cost-realistic lower confidence bound. At measured NBBO, every lever that raises the win rate lowers the mean: wider strikes, take-profits and condor wings all do. The only lever that materially raises both is never buying the position back. Two pre-registered grids test this directly: 1,890 distinct configurations per SPY/QQQ/IWM scope and 2,736 SPXW configurations, each with TRAIN from 2022 to 2024 and TEST from 2025-01 to 2026-10. The median ETF configuration wins 71–73 % and loses money, and SPXW configurations above a 70 % win rate have negative median expectancy in TRAIN. Take-profits cost $6–$45 per trade and wings $6–$58. Only one TRAIN-selected configuration met both targets out of sample: a 14:05 SPXW strangle at ±0.5 expected moves (about 28Δ), held to cash settlement, skipping FOMC and month-end days. It earned **+$162 per lot [+$63, +$258] with a 71.5 % win rate over 397 TEST days**. It still fails promotion. The same strangle sold at a random minute earned as much: the lane minus the random baseline is −$93 [−$185, +$1]. The dataset's ±2 % strike band leaves out large-move days such as April 2025, on which strangles of this kind lose heavily. And the pre-registered T-E test of the 13:00 SPXW straddle held to settlement failed on 2022–2025 at +$26 [−$138, +$170], after one unscheduled day, 2025-04-09, cost $33,667 per lot. The SPY/QQQ hold-to-close results pass more statistical checks, but most of their gain is payment for after-hours exercise risk, which a 16:00 valuation leaves out. The defensible lane is therefore a carry lane, not a signal lane: SPXW naked premium, held to settlement, skipping only FOMC and month-end days, and sized to a max-loss budget. It should be tested forward and on data that covers large-move days in full. Win rate should be reported as a consequence of the chosen strike distance, never optimised as a target.

## A 70 % win rate comes from the payoff shape, and the median configuration already loses money

Where the strikes sit sets most of a short-premium structure's win rate. Practitioners treat delta as the probability of finishing in the money ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/from-theory-to-practice/episodes/3-ways-to-use-delta-probability-approximation-10-02-2025)). On that basis, a single short leg at 10Δ wins about 90 % of the time held to expiry. A 16Δ strangle keeps both legs out of the money about 68 % of the time. The probability of touching a strike during the day is roughly twice its delta ([Options Trading IQ](https://optionstradingiq.com/probability-of-touch-in-options/)). The number that matters is the break-even tail, and it is plain arithmetic. If winners keep the full credit, a 90 % win rate breaks even when the average loser costs 9 credits, and an 80 % rate breaks even at 4. A 25 %-of-credit take-profit at a 90 % win rate cuts the tolerable average loss to 2.25 credits.

Measured hit rates sit only a few points above what the payoff shape implies. The 10:00 SPY ATM straddle closed inside its implied 1σ band on **71.0 % of 193 sessions (2022–2026), against 68.3 % if fairly priced**. The worst miss was 2025-04-09: an 8.72 % move against 3.51 % implied ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study)). tastylive's last-30-minute short premium 20 points out of the money won 82 % and still lost money net, because a few outlier days paid for all the wins ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/0dte-options-opening-vs-closing-trades)).

The academic record has also hardened against sellers. In August 2026 Vilkov found that his code charged the per-leg half-spread at 1/100 of its true size, which is about 2.2 bp of spot. After the correction, no SPXW structure in his 2016–2026 panel keeps a positive net Sharpe: **strangle/straddle −0.97, iron butterfly/condor −2.67** ([Vilkov, KNOWN-ISSUES](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/main/KNOWN-ISSUES.md)). The conditional rules that earlier Compass work cited at a net Sharpe of 0.39–0.93 also turn negative. Chong reaches the same verdict from 2020–2024 data: 0DTEs are "expensive but do not appear to be mispriced" once costs and estimation risk are counted ([HKUST](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2026/04/are-0dte-options-mispriced)).

Our grids reproduce this on measured quotes. The ETF grid crossed seven structure/strike cells with four entry times, three take-profits, three stops, two exits and four event filters. Across the 1,890 distinct SPY+QQQ configurations, the **median win rate is 71.0 % in TRAIN and 72.9 % in TEST, while the median mean is −$2.34 and −$1.39 per 1-lot**. The median profit factor is 0.85 and 0.94. More than half the configurations (1,024) won at least 70 % in TRAIN. Across configurations, win rate and mean are barely related: the Spearman correlation is 0.15 in TRAIN and 0.32 in TEST. The 88 configurations that won 90–100 % in TRAIN had a median mean of −$3.39, and only one of them had a positive lower bound ([wr_vs_expectancy.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/results/wr_vs_expectancy.csv); [ETF RESULTS](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/RESULTS.md)). SPXW behaves the same way. In TRAIN, naked structures earned most in the 50–70 % win bins (median +$35.0 and +$24.1) and went negative above 70 % (−$11.8 at 70–80 %). No configuration winning 90 % or more had a positive lower bound. Every winged bin is negative in both periods. Meanwhile the median credit falls from 14.6 to 2.6 index points as the win rate rises ([winrate_expectancy_tradeoff.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/winrate_expectancy_tradeoff.csv)).

## Settlement is the only lever that materially raises both win rate and mean

The grids hold every other rule fixed and change one at a time, which isolates what each management rule actually does. The ETF columns below are SPY+QQQ medians of matched pairs. The SPXW columns are means of paired differences. Each cell reads TRAIN → TEST.

**Take-profits are the textbook way to raise win rate, and at measured costs they lower expectancy in essentially every matched pair.** On SPXW a 25 % target raised the win rate in 100 % of 912 pairs and cut the mean by about $44. The practitioner evidence supports this reading once its two claims are separated. tastylive reports that managing at 25 % of max profit beats unmanaged 0DTE positions ([tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/success-in-zero-dte-early-management-key-09-10-2024)). Its own iron-fly study shows the trade: a 10 % target won 78 % with a $92 average profit, while a 20 % target won 61 % with $177 ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/zero-dte-winners-and-losers-for-iron-flies-08-07-2025)). Neither episode discloses whether fills were at mid or at the touch. Our grids reproduce the win-rate half of the claim and contradict the expectancy half. Option Alpha's live-fill logs are the only real-fill numbers available. SPY condors and flies won 63–72 % and averaged +4.6 % to +7.9 % of premium ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte)), about one to two measured SPY round trips. That is the same "edge about one spread wide" picture.

**Stops lower the win rate, narrow the confidence interval and do not raise the mean out of sample.** On SPXW a 1× stop lifted the lower bound by $33.2 in TRAIN and $15.0 in TEST while its mean effect flipped from +$8.9 to −$21.5. That is risk shaping, not edge. The literature agrees on direction. tastylive found a 100 %-of-credit stop on 0DTE strangles beat 25 % and 50 % stops on both win rate and average P&L ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/stop-losses-for-0-dte-strangles-04-30-2024)). A tastytrade backtest of 20Δ SPX condors with $20 wings, reported second-hand, won about 72 % unmanaged, under 60 % with a 1× stop and under 50 % with a 0.5× stop. About 48 % of the 0.5×-stopped trades would have recovered ([StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/?p=47372)). ORATS gives a worked example of a 200 % stop that triggered at 14:49, after which the loss nearly doubled again by the close ([ORATS](https://orats.com/intraday-backtester)). Stops cap a single day's loss only when they fill near the trigger, so the ETF grid filled them at the next minute's ask.

**Wings are a net cost at both venues.** Adding them cut the SPXW mean by $42.7 and $58.4 per trade, and raised it in only 4.8 % and 8.3 % of pairs. The fee arithmetic shows why. At tastytrade, a broad-based index option costs $1.00 to open, $0 to close, plus clearing and a $0.60 SPX proprietary-index fee per contract ([tastytrade fee schedule](https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt7dc2e3d4a7071563/blt2b752fef372188fe/commissions-and-fees)). A four-leg SPX condor therefore costs about $9.76 in fees for a round trip before any spread. That matches Vilkov's corrected ranking, which puts four-leg structures last.

**Exit-side spread rules give a small, consistent gain and should be adopted as execution rules.** Leaving legs with an ask of $0.10 or less to settle added $2.7 per SPXW trade, up in 89–91 % of 1,368 pairs. The SPY/QQQ version, at $0.05 or less, added $0.3–$0.35, up in about 95 % of pairs. On SPXW the E1 time exit leaves about 0.7 legs of a straddle and 1.0–1.1 legs of a 0.5-EM strangle to settle ([diagnostics.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/diagnostics.csv)). The mechanism is the tick size. SPXW quotes in $0.05 below $3 ([Cboe SPX specifications](https://ww2.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/specifications)), so closing a leg quoted 0.00 × 0.05 costs at least $5 plus fees, for nothing. Volos estimates that letting expiring NDX options cash-settle would have cut roll costs by more than 50 % ([Volos](https://www.volossoftware.com/insights/improving-0dte-trading-returns)). Roundhill's daily-option funds do not buy back at all ([Roundhill](https://blog.roundhillinvestments.com/0dte-to-buy-back-or-not-to-buy-back)).

**Holding SPXW to cash settlement is the one rule that raises both numbers.** It adds +$6.54 per trade in TRAIN and +$29.34 in TEST, up in 59 % and 82 % of pairs. On the reproduced T-C sample, the 13:00 SPXW straddle earned **+$75.32 [−$69.52, +$216.86] bought back at 15:55 and +$159.86 [+$10.91, +$305.77] held to the official close** ([tc_reproduction.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/tc_reproduction.csv)). SPXW is European and cash-settled on the closing prices of the index's component stocks, with no exercise afterwards ([Cboe SPX specifications](https://ww2.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/specifications)). The saving is the exit spread and nothing else. The grid valued settlement at the 16:00 SPX print, which differed from the official close by a mean of −0.06 bp. The largest gap was 18.3 index points, on 2026-06-26 ([SPXW RESULTS](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/RESULTS.md)).

### On SPY and QQQ, the hold-to-close gain is mostly after-hours exercise risk

The same lever looks larger on the ETFs, and most of that size is an artifact of valuation. Holding a 14:05 SPY straddle to expiry rather than buying it back at 15:55 added **+$30.60 [+$24.59, +$36.66] per trade in TEST**. Measured against the 16:00 close, the 15:55 mid of $89.06 looks rich: the realized |S(16:00) − K| averaged only $52.61. Against the 17:15 after-hours quote, the realized value is $82.76, and the gap shrinks to **+$6.30 [−$3.51, +$14.69]** ([diagnostics.md](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/results/diagnostics.md)). The 17:15 quote matters because SPY holders may exercise until 5:30 pm ET on after-hours prices ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2021-02/information-notice-020321.pdf)), and OCC auto-exercises anything $0.01 in the money ([OIC](https://www.optionseducation.org/referencelibrary/faq/options-exercise)). A partial in-the-money finish leaves the seller with an undefined overnight share position ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/learn/0dte-partial-in-the-money-assignment)).

Re-valuing the pre-registered SPY winner shows how much the 16:00 valuation flatters it. It falls from +$34.82 to +$23.59 under rational after-hours exercise, and to **+$2.58 [−$10.03, +$15.13] in the pessimistic case**. On SPY and QQQ, the settlement lever should therefore mean something narrower: abandon long wings unconditionally, and buy back short legs whose strike lies inside the after-hours move the book can carry. It should not mean leaving everything to expire.

### Entry time inside 10:00–14:30 adds nothing once premium is held to settlement

Across the SPXW grid, later entries averaged more than 10:00 entries: +$25–$59 per trade at 13:00 and 14:05 ([rule_effects.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/rule_effects.csv)). But every selected afternoon lane was matched by its random-time baseline, which drew one minute uniformly between 10:00 and 14:30 with the same structure, rules and filter (see the pre-registered tests below). The practitioner record supports avoiding the last half hour. tastylive's last-30-minute premium lost money, and Option Alpha's SPY condors opened more than two hours after the open did better than early ones ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/0dte-options-opening-vs-closing-trades); [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte)). It says nothing that would favour 14:05 over 11:00. The entry cost lever is better documented. On two-tick SPXW quotes, midpoint limit orders filled within one second about 60–70 % of the time, at a net effective spread less than half a marketable order's ([SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-hope-reasonable-prc-2503.pdf)). Our measured marketable entry already costs only 0.87 % of SPXW straddle credit ([T-C RESULTS](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md)).

## Only the FOMC and month-end skips survive, and regime signals belong in sizing

The cleanest filter evidence comes from a nine-year SPX study: 2,333 sessions from 2017 to 2026, fit through 2022 and tested 2023–2026. **FOMC afternoons move 2.08× [1.56, 2.68] as far as event-free afternoons**, and the stylised short straddle's hit rate at fair premium falls to 32.5 % on those days, against 65.8 % on event-free days. CPI and NFP afternoons are not larger, at 0.94–0.96× with confidence intervals spanning 1, because the 08:30 release is absorbed in the morning. Skipping those days *lowers* the out-of-sample mean ([event_ratios.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/vol_filters/out/event_ratios.csv); [rules_single.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/vol_filters/out/rules_single.csv)). Month-end afternoons move 1.33× [1.06, 1.65] as far. Skipping FOMC or month-end trades 92.4 % of days and lifts the test hit rate by 2.5 points to 70.4 %. It raised the mean by +0.044 morning-range units under both premium yardsticks and was positive in 9 of 10 years. That combination was found after the single-rule results were seen, so it counts as post hoc ([posthoc.log](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/vol_filters/out/posthoc.log)).

On measured option P&L the same filter is real but small. In the SPXW grid, adding month-end to an FOMC skip raised the mean by $6.4 in TRAIN and $20.0 in TEST, up in 80–91 % of pairs. Skipping FOMC alone *lowered* it. On SPY/QQQ the combined filter moved the mean by less than $0.40 per trade ([rule_effects.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/rule_effects.csv); [marginal_effects.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/results/marginal_effects.csv)). The reason is that event days carry higher credits. In the 339-session T-C sample, FOMC/CPI/NFP days averaged +$275 against +$45 on other days, on 45 % larger credits. The worst day, −$8,499 on 2026-06-11, was not an event day ([T-C RESULTS](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md)). An event skip is a tail-budget choice more than an expectancy improver.

The published FOMC evidence points to a timing rule rather than a skip. Option-implied event volatility rises about 30 % into the 14:00 statement and then collapses by more than half, while the pre-announcement hours are quieter than on ordinary days ([Song](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=260400)). Vendor backtests of entries after the statement report 84 % wins for a 16Δ strangle ([GreeksLab](https://greekslab.com/blog/trading-0dte-spx-fed-day)) and 23 of 23 winners ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/weekly-0-dte-breakdown-trading-into-fomc-post-fomc-achieves-100-win-rate-in-the-past-three-years-12-15-2025)). Both are cost-free. At NBBO, "enter at 14:05 on FOMC days" was statistically the same as skipping them: +$0.8 and −$0.1 relative to skip-FOMC on SPXW.

**Volatility-level filters fail as skips because the credit already prices them.** Skipping days when the 13:00 VIX is above its 60-day 80th percentile removes 23 % of test days and lowers the mean by −0.020 [−0.035, −0.005] morning-range units. High-VIX afternoons are 1.97× larger, but the morning range is 1.94× larger too. On the T-C straddles, win rates were flat across VIX terciles (62.8–64.6 %), and the Spearman correlation of VIX with P&L per unit of credit was −0.03 ([rules_single.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/vol_filters/out/rules_single.csv); [T-C RESULTS](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/RESULTS.md)). The train-selected "quiet morning" skip looked good: +0.041 out of sample, with the hit rate up 4.3 points. It turned out to be an artifact of measuring the premium against the morning range, which the afternoon tracks with an elasticity of only 0.5–0.8. Under a trailing-afternoon yardstick it reverses to −0.051 [−0.071, −0.030] ([rules_alt_scale.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/vol_filters/out/rules_alt_scale.csv)).

Dealer gamma follows the same pattern. Its −0.36 correlation with next-day realized volatility falls to −0.03 (p = 0.18) once VIX and ATM IV are controlled ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/gex-dex-vex-chex-8-year-backtest-spy-vix-control)). The largest measured gamma-induced 30-minute volatility increase on Cboe position data is 6.4 vol points, against an all-cause standard deviation of 4.6 ([Amaya et al.](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/gammasqueezes.pdf)).

Two day-selection ideas have priced or peer-reviewed support and have not been tested here. Both are candidates for single pre-registered filters. OptionMetrics sold a 25Δ SPX call at the bid each day and held it to expiry. Selling only when the 10:00 0DTE skew was below its median raised the Sharpe ratio from 1.04 to 1.14 on half the days ([OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/blog/0dte-covered-calls-arent-special-timing-them-is/)). The skew is computable from the ThetaData chains. Lehnert shows that the slope and curvature of the VIX term structure predict straddle excess returns for listed maturities ([Lehnert](https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/65830)). The cache holds no VIX9D or VIX3M to test that for 0DTE. One regime fact does matter for sizing. The realized-to-implied afternoon ratio *rises* with VIX: breaches of the VIX-implied band run 13.1 % in the top tercile against 4.6 % in the bottom ([daily.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/vol_filters/out/daily.csv)). That fits the finding that intraday long-option returns are highest when VIX is high ([Muravyev and Ni](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v136y2020i1p219-238.html)). A fixed-credit rule, which implicitly sizes up when VIX is high, is the wrong default.

## Pre-registered tests leave one SPXW strangle standing, and it is not promotable

Both grids ran on the ThetaData PRO dataset under the same design rules:

- **Data.** 1-minute NBBO of the same-day expiry for strikes within ±2 % of the 09:31 price. SPXW covers 1,097 regular sessions from 2022-05-13. SPY and QQQ daily expiries start 2022-11-11; IWM data is complete from 2024-04-19.
- **Splits.** TRAIN runs to 2024-12-31 and TEST from 2025-01-02 to 2026-10-09.
- **Fills and fees.** Sell at the bid, buy at the ask; $0.70 per contract per side for the ETFs and $1.00 for SPXW.
- **Statistics.** Day-clustered bootstrap intervals.
- **Selection.** Configurations were selected on TRAIN by lower bound and hashed before any TEST trade was computed ([SPXW PREREGISTRATION](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/PREREGISTRATION.md); [ETF PREREGISTRATION](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/PREREGISTRATION.md)).

### SPXW: T-E fails, and the surviving strangle earns no more than random entry

**T-E, the test of hypothesis H13, fails, and H13 should stay closed.** Over three years, the 13:00 SPXW straddle held to settlement earned a mean indistinguishable from zero. It had a −$41,419 drawdown, and one tariff-pause rally day (2025-04-09, −$33,667) erased about two years of income. That day was not an FOMC, CPI or NFP day, so excluding scheduled events does not remove unscheduled tails. In both windows the 13:00 entry earned less than a random entry time held to settlement ([te_results.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/te_results.csv)).

**S2 is the only TRAIN-selected configuration that combines a TEST win rate of at least 70 % with a positive cost-realistic lower bound.** It also survives one tick of adverse fill (+$152.0). Its premium is generic, though. The same strangle sold at a random minute between 10:00 and 14:30 earned between +$85.5 and +$336.2 across the five seeds, pooled at +$165.1 with a 71 % win rate. In TRAIN the lane minus its random baseline was +$2.3 [−58.0, +64.7] ([selected_random_baseline.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/selected_random_baseline.csv)).

The storage band biases these results toward sellers. Strangle strikes beyond ±2 % of the 09:31 price were not stored, so trades on 2025-04-04, 04-07, 04-08, 04-09, 04-10 and 04-11 were skipped. The 13:00 version of S2 shows TEST +$260.2 [+135.5, +379.0] at 75.2 % wins as computed. It would have lost at least $29,167 on 2025-04-09 even under the most optimistic credit, and its lower bound then becomes −$23.4. S2 itself keeps a positive bound (+$81.7) only if the skipped days are given a credit estimated from the straddle. With zero credit on those days it falls to −$12.9 ([band_bias_check.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/band_bias_check.csv)).

Across the whole SPXW grid, 135 of 2,736 configurations have a positive TEST lower bound. A recentred day bootstrap expects 51 under the global null, with a 95th percentile of 232 (P = 0.113). A reality check that studentises within each draw rejects nothing ([multiplicity.json](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/multiplicity.json)).

### SPY, QQQ and IWM: real but generic premium

The ETF grid has a stronger multiple-testing signal, and it is the same family again. In TEST, 293 SPY+QQQ configurations have positive lower bounds, against 32.3 expected by chance (95th percentile 154). A Romano–Wolf step-down rejects 127 configurations; 120 of them are held to expiry and 126 are straddles or strangles. The identical code on TRAIN rejects 133 configurations from the same afternoon family ([grid_test_summary.json](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/results/grid_test_summary.json)). All 116 configurations that met the objective (win ≥ 70 % and LB > 0) in TRAIN stayed positive in TEST. Of those, 84 met it again, and only 19 of the 84 used the realistic 15:55 buy-back.

The two formal passes are weaker than they look. SPY S1 passes on paper, but its edge depends on valuing an American, physically settled option at 16:00. IWM S2's margin over its baseline comes entirely from 61 baseline trades on days when spot had already left the band by 14:05, so the lane had no price. Those trades averaged −$152.62. On the lane's own days the baseline beat it by $1.80. With the realistic 15:55 buy-back, the best TRAIN configuration's TEST lower bound sits at about zero: −$2.63 to +$0.39 across scopes, and negative for 2026 alone ([ETF RESULTS](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/etf/RESULTS.md)).

The genuine finding underneath both grids is that naked premium entered between 10:00 and 14:30, held without a buy-back, and skipping FOMC and month-end, had positive means in both periods. It is not tied to an entry signal. Its lower bound depends on a handful of days the dataset under-records.

## Size to a max-loss budget, and test the carry lane forward

**Sizing changes the shape of the equity curve but cannot create an edge.** On the T-C straddle series, the growth-optimal capital per SPXW lot was $28,000, which produced a 59 % drawdown and a 31 % bootstrap chance of ending below the start. Quarter Kelly, at $112,000 per lot, cut the probability of a drawdown above 30 % to 0.08, but the chance of ending below the start only fell to 0.19. That probability tracks the noise in the mean, not the bet fraction ([tc_straddles.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/index_tests/tc_spxw_straddle/tc_straddles.csv)).

For S2, quarter Kelly estimated on TRAIN needs $39,683 per lot and gives a 0.31 probability of a drawdown above 30 % on TEST paths. **An 8 % max-loss budget sized to the worst TRAIN day needs $89,012 per lot and gives a −14.0 % TEST drawdown, P(DD > 30 %) = 0.003 and P(end below start) = 0.002** ([sizing_test_lbpos.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/sizing_test_lbpos.csv)). The max-loss rule depends less on the estimated mean, which is why it is the better choice when the mean's interval is this wide. The theory agrees. Kelly's sensitivity to errors in the estimated mean makes it "dangerous to those whose estimates are in error" ([MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)). A drawdown-constrained bet beats fractional Kelly at equal drawdown risk ([Busseti, Ryu and Boyd](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf)).

These figures are conditional on TEST days that exclude the band-skipped tail. The stress assumption has already failed once. T-E fixed a stress move of 6.33 % before the run, taken from the largest 13:00→16:00 move between 2017 and 2022. On 2025-04-09 the 13:00→16:00 move was 8.42 % ([te_summary.json](/home/user/georgeredo/research/thetadata/hwr_grid/spxw/results/te_summary.json)). For naked SPX strangles, Reg-T margin of about 15 % of the index binds before Kelly does: about $100,000 per lot at SPX 6,500 ([Cboe strategy-based margin](https://www.cboe.com/us/options/strategy_based_margin)). Cboe's worked examples show portfolio margin roughly five to six times lower for a short straddle ([Cboe margin examples](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/membership/Margin_Requirement_Examples.pdf)).

All historical data has now been used for selection, so the next evidence has to come from forward trading or from data the grids could not see. The candidates differ in what that evidence must show.

The first row raises a question the plan has to answer before any carry lane can pass. The §1 gate requires a lane to beat its random-entry baseline by more than one round trip ([COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md](/home/user/georgeredo/COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md)). That criterion exists to reject entry signals that time no better than chance. A carry lane claims no timing skill. If it is defined as "any minute 10:00–14:30", its random baseline *is* the lane, and the gate cannot be passed in principle. The choice belongs to the user. One option is a carry-lane variant of the gate: a lower bound above zero on a fresh forward window, a lower bound above zero under adverse fills, and a loss inside the max-loss budget on a full-chain stress set that includes April 2025. The other is to keep the gate as written and accept that no premium-harvesting lane can be promoted.

Whichever gate is chosen, three data gaps bound every number in this report. The ±2 % storage band hides precisely the large-move days. After-hours prices exist only for SPY. And the ETF daily expiries begin 2022-11-11, so no SPY/QQQ configuration has faced a 2022-style trending market.

## Conclusion

The question of how to raise win rate without negative expectancy turns out to be mis-posed. On measured quotes, win rate is something a lane chooses when it sets strike distance, and every rule that buys more of it is paid for out of the mean. Only two improvements are free, and neither moves the win rate by more than about three points. One is removing costs: settle instead of buying back, leave minimum-tick legs alone and rest entries at the midpoint. The other is removing a few predictable bad days, FOMC and month-end. The defensible 70 %+ lane is therefore simply a 0.5-EM SPXW strangle held to settlement. Its expectancy is the plain variance premium, its timing is irrelevant, and its fate is decided by roughly one unscheduled day per few hundred. Our dataset systematically under-records exactly those days.

This mirrors what the earlier work found for long premium and for GeorgeFi. In the earlier long-premium study, exit engines raised the win rate from 37 % to 43 % and cut losses, yet zero of 576 configurations had a positive expectancy after spread costs ([q4_group_heads.csv](/home/user/georgeredo/research/gex_regime_and_exits/out/q4_group_heads.csv)), and GeorgeFi's ledger reflected its fills and exits rather than its signals ([COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md](/home/user/georgeredo/COMPASS_RESEARCH_PLAN.md)). On the short side, management likewise reshapes the distribution of outcomes without creating an edge. The next increment of knowledge for Compass will come from data coverage and live fills: full chains on crash days, forward NBBO on the carry lane, and a gate that can tell a carry lane from a signal lane. It will not come from more grid cells.
